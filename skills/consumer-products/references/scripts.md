# Scripts

Exact wording for recurring CPG conversations. Fill the brackets with the brand's real numbers; never invent velocity, store counts or buyer quotes. Sources are noted per script: research (deep research, Nov 2026), operators report (Reddit, with thread score), or written for this skill from those patterns.

## Retail buyer: cold email

Research pattern: the first screen carries economics and proof, not the origin story.

> **Subject:** [Category] doing [X] units/store/week locally: sample for your [month] review?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We make [one-line product: what it is, for whom, the one difference]. Wholesale $[price], MSRP $[price], about [X]% margin for you.
>
> Our two lead SKUs sold [velocity, e.g. 3.1 units/store/week non-promoted] across [N comparable stores, e.g. 12 natural independents] over the last [period], with [repeat proof, e.g. 41% of market buyers reordering within 60 days].
>
> Case pack [12], MOQ [1 case per SKU], shipped via [distributor or self-delivery], [shelf life] shelf life.
>
> May I send samples and a one-page sell sheet before your [category] review?
>
> [Name], [title], [phone]

Rules: one product line, two SKUs, numbers you can document. If you have no velocity yet, lead with the strongest real proof you have (market sell-through, repeat, reviews) and offer a demo.

## Retail buyer: follow-ups

Three days later, only (Mantry founder; one commenter reported two big retailers replied to it; operators report, 272-pt thread):

> Following up!

Twelve weeks after a decline (written for this skill from the 12-week re-pitch pattern):

> Hi [Name], we spoke in [month] about [product]. Since then: [one new proof point, e.g. now in 18 stores at 3.4 units/store/week, or a new SKU]. Is [category] up for review in [month]? Happy to send fresh samples.

## Sell sheet (one page)

Top to bottom (written for this skill from RangeMe, SPS Commerce and buyer guidance):

1. Brand name, logo, one-line positioning (what it is and for whom).
2. Hero photo of each SKU, front of pack, on white.
3. Per SKU: name, size, UPC, case pack, case dimensions and weight, cases per pallet (Ti x Hi), shelf life, storage.
4. Pricing: wholesale per unit and per case, distributor price if any, MSRP, retailer margin %.
5. Proof: velocity in current stores (non-promoted), store list or count, repeat or reorder data, awards, notable press.
6. Claims that matter on the shelf: certifications (organic, non-GMO, kosher, gluten-free) with certifier.
7. Launch support: demos, intro offer, social and creator plan, shelf tags.
8. How to order: distributors (with item numbers), direct ordering link (Faire Direct link if used), contact.

Coffee roasters: one buyer-facing catalog with photo, unit price, suggested retail and minimum per item worked for one roaster (operators report).

## Buyer deck (eight slides)

Research structure:

1. **The shopper or category gap:** who is underserved and how big the gap is in this store's shopper base.
2. **Hero SKU:** product, price, what it replaces or adds.
3. **Comparable velocity:** non-promoted UPSPW in similar stores, against a stated benchmark.
4. **Repeat proof:** reorder or repeat data with its denominator.
5. **Shelf price and buyer margin:** price per ounce versus the set, margin %, penny profit per facing.
6. **Distribution and logistics:** distributor, case pack, lead time, fill rate, insurance, certifications.
7. **Launch support:** demos, promos, social, creators, shelf tags, budget.
8. **Test proposal:** the bounded ask below.

## The 12-week test proposal

Research script:

> "We propose a 12-week test in 25 to 50 stores on our two highest-velocity SKUs. We will report non-promoted units/store/week weekly, fund two demo periods, and review continuation against the category benchmark you use internally. What velocity and retailer margin would make that test a win for you?"

## Questions to ask in the buyer meeting

> "What is the category's median and top-quartile dollars per store per week at my price point, and do you look at promoted and non-promoted weeks separately?"
>
> "Does 'no slotting' mean free fills, a required ad buy, a reset fee or an introductory allowance?"
>
> "When is the next review for [category], and when do you need submissions?"
>
> "What would make you keep this at the next reset?"

## In-store demo script

Research script:

> "Do you usually buy [category]? Try this first. The difference is [one sensory or product distinction]. It is on the shelf in aisle [X] at [price]. If you like it, buy it today and tell us which version we should make next."

Opening line at a table outside a store (operators report, 1,114-pt thread):

> "All natural sugar scrubs today if you'd like to test them."

Rules: same sentence carries taste, location and price. Count samples and units. Never say anything off the claims bank.

## Independent store walk-in

Written for this skill from the Google Maps and adjacent-brand patterns:

> "Hi, I'm [Name], I make [product] here in [town]. You carry [adjacent brand], and our customers overlap. Can I leave a sample and a price sheet for whoever does the buying? Wholesale is $[X], it retails at $[Y], minimum is one case, and I deliver every [day] within [area]. If it doesn't move in 60 days I'll swap it for another flavor."

Pitching a supermarket (operators report, r/sweatystartup): ask for the manager or buyer, show a brand their shoppers already know, and offer a better price or more reliable restocking than the current supplier.

## Broker pitch

> Hi [Name], we're [Brand], [one line]. We sell [X] UPSPW in [N] stores in [region] and are targeting [chains] at their [month] reviews. We're looking for representation in [territory] on [commission %] of net sales, with net sales defined after [returns, MCBs]. Which of those buyers do you call on, and what results have you had in [category]?

## Reorder prompt

Written for this skill from Faire's reorder timing (70% within 90 days, average cadence about 45 days):

> Hi [Name], it's been [30] days since your first order of [product]. How is it selling? If you're under a quarter left, I can get a case of [best seller] to you by [day]. Want me to add [new flavor] as a single case to test?

## Co-packer RFQ

Written for this skill from the co-packer questions in [production.md](production.md):

> **Subject:** RFQ: [product type], [N] SKUs, first run [units]
>
> Hi [Name], we make [product] (shelf-stable, pH [X], [glass 5 oz woozy / pouch / can]). We're looking for a co-packer for a first run of [units] per SKU across [N] SKUs, then about [units] a [month or quarter].
>
> Could you share: minimum run per SKU and whether flavors can share a run; per-unit tolling fee; setup, changeover and onboarding fees; deposit terms; who sources ingredients and packaging; lead time; process authority and scheduled-process support; certifications (SQF or other GFSI, organic, kosher); shelf-life and lab testing; storage and shipping options.
>
> Formula ownership stays with us. Happy to sign an NDA and send samples.

## Subscription box pitch

Operators report (r/ecommerce, 151-pt thread): find boxes on Cratejoy, find the operator's email, send a short direct email naming the products you offer and proposing a supply partnership. Price the order and your labor before replying to a quote request.

> Hi [Name], I run [Brand], [one line]. Your [box name] subscribers look like our buyers. We can supply [product] at [unit price] for [quantity] with [lead time], with your box's branding on an insert. Want samples?

## Creator brief with claims bank

Research brief, restrictive on claims, loose on creative:

> "Show the product being used in the first three seconds. State one concrete product fact from the approved claims sheet. Show texture, taste, preparation or result honestly. Give your real opinion. Do not state or imply disease treatment, guaranteed results, weight-loss outcomes, or any claim not on the approved sheet. Put `Ad` (or the required paid-partnership disclosure) where viewers see it before or with the endorsement."

Attach: approved fact, approved benefit (exact jurisdiction wording), do-not-say list, disclosure wording. For tea or supplements, open with the moment of use ("my 9 pm wind-down"), not a medical problem.

## Review request (every buyer, same message)

Research script:

> "How was your experience with [Product]? We want the good, the bad and anything we should improve. Leave an honest review here: [link]."

Where the platform allows an incentive (never on Amazon):

> "As a thank-you for submitting an honest review, positive, neutral or negative, we'll provide [incentive]. The incentive is not conditional on your rating."

Native's early email, which paired the public review ask with a private feedback route sent to every buyer (operators report, 1,396-pt thread):

> "You got a stick of Native deodorant. Love to know what you think about it. If you love the product, please leave a review on our site. If you don't, reply to this email and tell us what you don't like, and we'll try to fix it."

Check this against the FTC rule before copying: send it to every buyer, and do not route unhappy buyers away from public review.

## Complaint and damage recovery

Opening line (Mantry founder, 25,000+ customers; operators report, 484-pt thread):

> "I'm sorry, how can we fix this for you?"

If they want a refund or stay unpleasant, refund in full at once.

Damaged or defective product: ask for a photo and the lot number, then send more than they lost. Tabasco sent twelve 5 oz bottles for one defective 12 oz bottle; the customer's post drew 1,056 upvotes. Heatonist replaced broken bottles the same day with an extra bottle, and a customer said that was why they kept buying (operators report). A roaster refused to replace $30 of missing coffee for a two-year monthly buyer and threatened a ban over a chargeback; buyers switched and a boycott thread followed (operators report, 794-pt thread).

Delivered but missing (operators report, 43-pt thread):

> "Sorry to hear that. I'll put in a missing mail request with the carrier. Occasionally they'll prematurely mark an order as delivered and then it will show up the next business day."
>
> "I'd recommend that you call your local post office and give them the tracking number. If it was misdelivered on the [date], they can very likely still retrieve it and re-deliver to the correct address."
>
> "If they're insisting that it was in fact delivered to the correct address, I'll put in a mail theft claim with the carrier. After that, we can get the ball rolling on sending a replacement shipment."

First check the address: shoppers sometimes pay with an old address saved in Apple Pay or Google Pay.

## Founder emails

Subscriber anniversary referral (food subscription operator, 10+ years; operators report, 154-pt thread), sent one by one:

> "Hi [Name],
>
> I'm a founder, thank you for being a subscriber for a year, if there is anyone you know who would love [Your Company], I would be happy to send them a [Your Product] (on us of course)."

Keep "who would love." Put a 10% insert in the gift box.

Past-customer check-in (operators report about 4x higher conversion than automated emails, no baseline given; 115-pt thread):

> "Hi [Name]! [Timely local reference]. I just wanted to check in and see if you enjoyed the [product]. We're offering 10% off to past customers because we loved them. I'm the founder if you need anything or want to tear apart the product so I can learn."

## Gift guide pitch

Mantry founder (operators report, 272-pt thread). Find last year's guides with a custom date range the month before the holiday, find the writer's social account or the outlet's email pattern, and send:

> "Saw your post on ___ sending a tip for any Valentine's Day Gift Guides! If included I'll have the social team re-share it!"

Follow up three days later with "Following up!" Run outreach as one ten-hour sprint a quarter.
