# Getting into retail and staying there

Retail is a velocity business. The first purchase order is a test; the reorder after the first reset is the win. Sequence the ladder so each rung produces the evidence the next buyer asks for.

## The ladder

| Rung | Who | How you get in | Evidence you leave with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Independents, cafes, gift and specialty shops | Owner is the buyer | Walk-in or email with sample, sell sheet, delivery promise | Reorders per account, sell-through days |
| 2. Local natural grocers, co-ops, regional independents | Store or category buyer | Sample plus demo offer; often a local-vendor process | UPSPW in a few stores, demo results |
| 3. A chain's local program (Whole Foods local, regional chain local sections) | Local or regional forager or buyer | Supplier portal plus local forager; accelerator applications | Velocity against the category in that region |
| 4. Distributor (regional, then KeHE or UNFI) | Distributor category manager, after a retailer authorizes | A retailer's authorization usually pulls you into the distributor | Doors, fill rate, deduction history |
| 5. Regional chains, then national | Category buyer on a review calendar | Category review submission, often via broker | Non-promoted velocity, repeat, promo lift |

Do not skip rungs with a national deal. One DTC brand's Target program projected about 34,000 units across 1,800 stores, got 3 to 5 units of shelf per store, saw returns around 16% (versus under 1% on Shopify, 3.5% on Amazon, 6% on QVC from the same batch), had no return-to-vendor rights, and cancelled a 10,000-unit reorder (operators report, r/ecommerce 113-pt thread; electronics, but the mechanics are retail-wide).

## Rung 1: independents and cafes

- **Build the list from Google Maps.** One coffee roaster listed 300+ independent cafes and coffee shops within a 40-mile delivery radius, sent free samples with a Canva catalog (photo, unit wholesale price, suggested retail, minimum order) and reported 20 to 30 accounts and about $40,000 a month within four months (operators report, r/Entrepreneur 1,885-pt thread; margin claims were challenged, copy the method).
- **Find stores that stock adjacent brands.** Search the adjacent brand's store locator, Faire listing and Instagram tags; pitch with "you carry X, our customers overlap."
- **Pitch a known gap or better service, not just a better product.** A spice route operator walks into supermarkets with an established brand and wins on price or restocking reliability (operators report, r/sweatystartup). Small producers beat distributors on delivery frequency and shelf care.
- **Ask for the reorder trigger, not just the order.** Faire's 2026 data: 70% of reorders come within 90 days, about 60% when stock is at 25% or less, average cadence about 45 days (marketplace data, biased but operational; see sibling `ecommerce-brand` wholesale playbook).
- **Prepaid first orders.** A candle maker turned down a boutique chain's order (custom labels, net 60, returns of unsold stock within 30 days) after offering a smaller prepaid trial (operators report, 925-pt thread).
- **Corporate gifting is a parallel wholesale channel for food.** The same commissary-kitchen founder reported about $150,000 a year in corporate gifting plus about $350,000 in December gifting (operators report, unverified).

**Trick: check the shelf on day 7.** One maker's local shop reported zero sales after a month; the products had never been put out (operators report, 1,114-pt thread). Visit or ask for a photo within a week of delivery.

## Rung 2: local grocers and natural stores

- **Offer a demo in the first conversation.** Grocers worry about turns, not flavor. "We'll demo two Saturdays in the first month at our cost" lowers their risk.
- **Sample outside the store with permission.** A sugar-scrub maker who found parks needed a city license asked a local natural grocery to set up a table, approached every entering shopper with "All natural sugar scrubs today if you'd like to test them," made a little over $300, sold nearly all stock and was invited back every Saturday (operators report, 1,114-pt thread).
- **Re-pitch on a schedule.** A food and beverage consultant pitched the same small grocer every 12 weeks for two years. When it finally took the line, it became the biggest customer by its third order and within a year bought as much as the other 25 wholesale accounts combined (operators report, r/Coffee). The same consultant warned that losing it before diversifying would have been disastrous: cap any single account's share in planning.

## Rung 3: Whole Foods and chain local programs

- **Whole Foods Market** uses local foragers (team members who find local suppliers by region) and a Supplier Portal; register as a "COGS Supplier" to apply. Expect no individual feedback (Whole Foods supplier pages; secondary coverage).
- **LEAP (Local and Emerging Accelerator Program):** the 2026 Early Growth cohort chose 10 brands from more than 2,000 applicants; 9 of the 10 brands in the 2025 cohort were on shelf by September 2026; GEEM moved from a Midwest launch in May 2025 to four more western states and regions in July 2026 (Whole Foods, 2026). This is a selective readiness pipeline, not a 90% acceptance rate.
- **Local Producer Loan Program:** low-interest loans for local suppliers; Vital Farms received $100,000 (Whole Foods). Check current terms before relying on it.
- Other chains run local sections or vendor fairs. Ask the store manager: "Who is the local or regional buyer for [category], and is there a local vendor program?"

## Rung 4: distributors and brokers

**When you need one:** a chain will only receive through a distributor, or your own delivery stops paying. Distributors are pulled in by retailer authorizations; cold-pitching a national distributor without retailer demand rarely works.

**What it costs:** see the fee stack in [economics.md](economics.md): 20 to 28% margin, 7 to 12% freight allowance, MCBs averaging 8 to 15% of sales a year, 1 to 3% admin, 1 to 3% shrink, $250 to $500 per SKU per DC (Eightx, June 2026).

**Brokers:** 3 to 5% of net sales conventional, 5 to 7% natural for early brands (Eightx, 2026). Some also charge a retainer; one cited example is 6% plus $1,200 a month for exclusive representation in a three-state territory (Racklify, low-grade source). Define "net sales" in the contract: whether deductions, returns and MCBs come off before commission changes the effective rate.

**Tricks:**

- Ask the distributor for the promotional calendar and MCB terms in writing before the first promo. MCBs often hit 60 to 90 days later.
- Dispute deductions inside the window. Every unexplained deduction is margin; log each one by type (promo, spoils, short ship, fines, admin).
- Separate SKUs or pack sizes by channel before entering a retailer that sells online. A Target online discount over Labor Day took one brand's Amazon Buy Box and cost "six figures" in a weekend (operators report). Amazon also normalizes by price per ounce: when Kroger discounted a 24 oz size to $9.99, Amazon asked a seller to price the 32 oz at $13.32 instead of $19.99 to regain the Buy Box (operators report, r/FulfillmentByAmazon). Different pack sizes alone do not escape price matching; different products or bundles may.

## The buyer calendar

- Retailers review categories at different frequencies, sometimes once a year, and may overlook submissions outside the review window (RangeMe help center).
- Startup CPG's 2026 retail tracker compiled about 6,000 review and reset dates across 220 banners and 55,000+ locations. Use a tracker or ask each buyer directly.
- Keep RangeMe or equivalent profiles current before reviews: cost, MSRP, retailer margin, distributors, current placements (RangeMe).

Agent rule: keep a calendar row per target retailer: category, review month, submission deadline, buyer name, last contact, next re-pitch date (12 weeks after a decline).

## What buyers want first

SPS Commerce's 2026 guidance: lead with category fit, expected velocity, retailer margin, promotional support and supply reliability. The compact deck: shopper or category gap, hero SKU, comparable velocity, repeat proof, shelf price and buyer margin, distribution and logistics, launch support, test proposal (deep research, 2026). Verbatim deck, sell sheet and test proposal: [scripts.md](scripts.md).

**Questions to ask the buyer (deep research, 2026):**

1. "What is the category's median and top-quartile dollars per store per week at my price point, and do you look at promoted and non-promoted weeks separately?"
2. "Does 'no slotting' mean free fills, a required ad buy, a reset fee or an introductory allowance?"
3. "Which stores and which shelf position would the test use?"
4. "What velocity at week 12 makes this a keep?"

Also show penny profit per facing, not only margin percentage.

## Demos and velocity

- **Measured lift:** Walmart Canada, five grocery sampling campaigns in 2025: 470% average sales lift during activation, 284% more new-to-brand buyers, 13% sales lift 1 to 3 months later (retailer media case series, selected toward winners). Advantage Solutions: sampled granola 6x lift during the event and 11% brand lift over 26 weeks (NIQ/GfK across 80+ stores, 2023 to 2024 data, vendor).
- **When:** in weeks 4 to 13 after a launch. NIQ's 300,000-SKU innovation analysis saw growing and non-growing launches separate as early as week four (NIQ, 2025).
- **Cost:** vendor sources quote $500 to $1,500 per store per day for agency programs (jetfuel via Makai) and much higher for big activations; one sampling platform example prices a 3-hour demo at $75 and calls break-even "lift at MSRP equals demo cost," with 2 to 2.5x the cost as strong (Promomash). Get two or three agency quotes, or run them yourself.
- **Measure:** samples given, units sold during, same-store units the 4 weeks before and the 4 weeks after, and incremental contribution, not event-day revenue.
- **Incrementality matters to buyers.** Of 91 European launches that beat category benchmarks on trial and repeat, on average 86% of their sales came from existing category demand and 14% was incremental (NIQ, 2026). Show the buyer how you grow the category (new users, new occasion), not just how you take share.

## Terms to negotiate before saying yes

| Term | Ask for |
|---|---|
| Payment | Net 30 or better; cash discount only if the margin can carry it |
| Promotions | Brand approval for every price promotion; written promo calendar |
| Returns | Return-to-vendor rights or a cap; who pays freight back |
| Markdowns | Markdown funding cap; no liquidation without notice |
| Deductions | Itemized backup and a dispute window |
| Free fill | One case per SKU per store maximum, in writing |
| Exclusivity | None, or limited to a region and a period |
| Online pricing | Notice before the retailer discounts online; separate SKU where possible |

## Exit and expansion gates

- **Add stores** only when non-promoted velocity at week 12 is at or above the category benchmark the buyer gave you.
- **Hold** when velocity is near the benchmark: add demos and fix shelf position, price tags and out-of-stocks first.
- **Cut** a SKU that sits below about half the category benchmark after demos; ask to swap it for a stronger SKU rather than lose the facing.
