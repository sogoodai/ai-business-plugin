# Claims and labeling rules that shape marketing

The pack, the product page, the Amazon listing, the email, the founder's video and every creator's caption and pinned comment are all evidence of what the brand claims the product does. Run claims through one ledger. This file is an operating guide, not legal advice: confirm anything new with the regulator's current page or a regulatory consultant, and date every rule you rely on.

## The claims ledger (keep one per brand)

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Exact claim wording | "Supports normal immune function" |
| SKU | Elderberry gummies 60 ct |
| Jurisdiction | US |
| Claim type | Structure/function (supplement) |
| Evidence file | Ingredient studies at the dose used; finished-product test if any |
| Allowed media | Pack, site, Amazon, email, approved creators |
| Required qualifier or disclosure | FDA disclaimer; "Ad" for paid creators |
| Notification or registration | FDA structure/function notice filed [date] |
| Owner sign-off | name, date |

Creators get only the approved claims bank from this ledger, never the brand's internal research notes (deep research, 2026).

## United States: food

- **Required label elements:** statement of identity, net quantity, ingredient list by weight, allergen declaration (the nine major allergens, sesame included since 2023), name and place of business of the manufacturer, packer or distributor, and a Nutrition Facts panel unless exempt.
- **Nutrition Facts exemption for small businesses (FDA):** retailers with annual gross sales of $500,000 or less, or food sales to consumers of $50,000 or less, are exempt without filing. A separate low-volume exemption (fewer than 100 full-time-equivalent employees and fewer than 100,000 units of the product a year in the US) requires an annual notice to FDA. **Any nutrient content claim, health claim or other nutrition information on the label or in advertising voids the exemption** (FDA guidance). A "low sugar" or "high protein" line on Instagram can therefore require a full panel.
- **Nutrient content claims** ("low fat," "high in fiber," "good source of") have fixed FDA definitions. Use them exactly or do not use the words.
- **"Healthy":** new criteria finalized December 2024 (published Dec 27, 2024, effective Feb 25, 2025); compliance date Feb 25, 2028. FDA was still developing a "healthy" symbol in early 2025 sources; check current status.
- **"Gluten-free"** means under 20 ppm gluten under FDA's rule. Test if you claim it.
- **Net quantity:** fill to the stated weight. Underfilled coffee bags became a 712-upvote distrust thread (operators report).
- **Acidified foods:** see [production.md](production.md#acidified-and-low-acid-foods-us).
- **Health claims for food** need FDA authorization or qualified-claim enforcement discretion. "Helps you sleep," "reduces bloating" or "boosts immunity" on a tea or snack moves it toward drug or supplement territory. Taste, origin, preparation and how people use it are safe ground.

## United States: dietary supplements

- **Structure/function claims only:** describe the effect on normal structure or function ("supports normal immune function," "supports restful sleep"), never disease ("prevents colds," "treats anxiety," "reverses insulin resistance," "cures acne").
- **Notify FDA no later than 30 days after first marketing** a supplement with a structure/function claim, and carry the disclaimer, verbatim: "This statement has not been evaluated by the Food and Drug Administration. This product is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any disease." (FDA)
- **FTC governs supplement advertising; FDA governs labeling and safety.** FDA considers promotional context when judging intended use (FDA consumer guidance, Sept 2026). An Instagram caption can turn a supplement into an unapproved drug in FDA's eyes.
- **Substantiation:** FTC expects competent and reliable scientific evidence for health claims, generally well-controlled human studies on the product or an equivalent formulation and dose (FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance, Dec 2022, possibly outdated).
- **Hemp and CBD:** a November 2025 federal law redefines hemp and bans most intoxicating hemp-derived THC products (more than 0.4 mg total THC per container) from November 12, 2026 (marketingskills regulated-goods pack, MIT). FDA also limits CBD in foods and supplements. Treat any hemp product as regulated goods and check counsel.
- **Operators report:** a European supplement founder had to destroy €220,000 of stock when EFSA tightened rules on his ingredients in 2020; the brand name and formula became illegal overnight. Track regulatory changes for every ingredient you depend on.

## United States: cosmetics and the drug line

- **A cosmetic cleans, beautifies or changes appearance.** Claims to treat acne, protect from the sun (SPF), stop sweat (antiperspirant), treat dandruff or "repair" skin structure make the product an over-the-counter drug with its own rules. Keep cosmetic copy on appearance: "looks smoother," "visibly brighter," "feels hydrated."
- **MoCRA (enacted December 2022; registration and listing enforced from July 1, 2024):** facility registration renewed every two years; product listing by the responsible person, updated annually; serious adverse event reporting; safety substantiation; label contact information for adverse event reports (FDA, Feb 2026 update).
- **Small business exemption:** responsible persons or facilities with average US gross cosmetic sales under $1,000,000 a year over the prior three years (inflation-adjusted) are exempt from registration and listing, but not if they make certain products such as those that contact the eye's mucous membrane, are injected, are for internal use or alter appearance for more than 24 hours. FDA issues no exemption certificate; keep revenue records (secondary legal summaries, 2025; confirm in FDA guidance).
- **Never market registration as approval.** FDA warned in September 2026 that a facility registration or product listing number does not imply FDA approval.

## United States: reviews, endorsements, origin

- **FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule, effective Oct 21, 2024:** bans fake reviews and testimonials, buying reviews conditioned on positive or negative sentiment, insider reviews without disclosure, deceptive suppression of negative reviews and fake social influence. Incentives are allowed only if not conditioned on sentiment; Amazon bans incentives entirely.
- **Review gating breaks the rule.** Send the same request to every buyer.
- **Endorsements:** the material connection (payment, free product, affiliate commission) must be hard to miss; "ad," "advertisement" and "sponsored" are generally clear (FTC; the influencer brochure predates 2024, the 2023 Endorsement Guides are current).
- **Practitioner evidence:** staff-written Sephora reviews (Sunday Riley, 6,486-pt thread), a brand rejecting a critical review (CeraVe, 9,639-pt thread) and 20 bot comments for one toner on a two-year-old thread (Jiyu, 576-pt thread) each turned into public backlash; commenters said they would never buy (operators report). Publish critical reviews and reply in public.
- **"Made in USA"** must be "all or virtually all" made in the US under the FTC's 2021 rule; qualify it ("Made in USA with imported cocoa") otherwise.
- **California Proposition 65** warnings apply to listed chemicals (lead in chocolate and spices, acrylamide in roasted foods); businesses with fewer than 10 employees are exempt, but many retailers require Prop 65 compliance from all suppliers. Ask the buyer.

## European Union

- **Food information (Regulation 1169/2011):** mandatory particulars, allergens emphasized in the ingredient list, nutrition declaration. Handcrafted foods supplied directly by the maker of small quantities to the final consumer or to local retail are exempt from the nutrition declaration (Annex V). Check national guidance on what "small quantities" and "local" mean.
- **Nutrition and health claims (Regulation 1924/2006):** only claims on the EU register, used with their conditions of use, are safe commercial copy (European Commission, 2026 portal; legislation predates 2024 but is in force). Botanical claims "on hold" are handled differently by member states; check the target country.
- **Cosmetics (Regulation 1223/2009):** an EU-based Responsible Person appointed in writing, a product information file and cosmetic product safety report, and notification in the CPNP before sale; one CPNP notification covers all member states.
- **Cosmetic claims (Regulation 655/2013):** legal compliance, truthfulness, evidential support, honesty, fairness, informed decision-making. Ingredient-level evidence cannot be extrapolated to the finished product without evidence that the product delivers the effect.

## United Kingdom (Great Britain)

- **Foods and supplements:** only claims on the GB Nutrition and Health Claims register (updated May 19, 2026). Supplements are foods, not medicines, and must not claim to prevent, treat or cure disease (GOV.UK, June 2026).
- **CAP Code:** food ads must not base health claims on an individual health professional's recommendation; CAP highlighted rulings against implied claims such as "crafted for calm" in July 2025. A nutritionist creator saying the product "fixes your gut" is high risk.
- **Influencer labels:** CMA guidance (Sept 2025) wants "Ad," "Advert," "Advertising" or "Advertisement" up front; "gifted," "aff," "collab" or tagging the brand are ambiguous. ASA monitoring of 50,000 pieces of content from 509 UK accounts in 2025 found 57% of influencer ads compliant, 9% unclear, 34% undisclosed.
- **Cosmetics:** a UK Responsible Person and SCPN notification; CPNP does not cover Great Britain and SCPN does not cover the EU. Northern Ireland follows the EU portal (GOV.UK statutory guidance).

## Canada

- **Natural health products** (most supplements) need a product licence and NPN before sale; advertising must match the licensed terms. New NHP labelling rules took effect June 21, 2025, with transition to June 22, 2028 (Health Canada).
- **Food health claims:** some need pre-market approval; disease risk reduction claims must use the prescribed wording (CFIA).
- Food labels are bilingual (English and French).

## Australia and New Zealand

- **Food (FSANZ Standard 1.2.7):** high-level health claims must use pre-approved food-health relationships; general-level claims may use approved relationships or a notified self-substantiation (updated Feb 25, 2025).
- **Listed medicines (most supplements):** only permitted indications consistent with their ARTG entry; no claims about serious illness. TGA requested removal of 13,700+ unlawful digital ads in FY2024 to 2025 and treats paid influencers as part of the supply chain for testimonial rules.

## Translate by jurisdiction, not by language

A US structure/function claim may have no authorized equivalent in Great Britain or the EU; a Canadian claim is tied to its licence; an Australian listed medicine has fixed indications (deep research, 2026). Build a separate claims bank per market.

## Safe and unsafe phrasing

| Product | Safer | Unsafe |
|---|---|---|
| Hot sauce, snacks | "Habanero, mango and lime, 3 of 5 heat," "made in small batches in Des Moines" | "Boosts metabolism," "burns fat" |
| Herbal tea | "A calming evening ritual," "caffeine-free blend of chamomile and lemon balm" | "Cures insomnia," "reduces anxiety," "relieves bloating" (US food: drug-like; GB: unauthorized) |
| Coffee | "Notes of lime, stone fruit, nutty" (plain descriptors) | Printed cupping scores from green-coffee samples presented as what the customer will taste (operators report) |
| Skincare (cosmetic) | "Visibly evens tone," "skin feels hydrated for 24 hours" (if tested) | "Treats acne," "heals eczema," "repairs collagen," "FDA registered" as a badge |
| Supplement (US) | "Supports normal immune function*" with the disclaimer | "Prevents colds," "works like Ozempic," "lowers blood sugar" |
| Supplement (GB/EU) | Only register wording, e.g. "Vitamin C contributes to the normal function of the immune system" (where conditions of use are met) | US-style structure/function wording that is not on the register |
| Any | "Contains 100 mg vitamin C per serving" | Ingredient study results presented as proven finished-product results |

## Platform and ad-account friction

- Supplement ads draw repeated disapprovals on Google and YouTube; one PPC practitioner declines supplement accounts under $10,000 a month in spend without a distinct value proposition (operators report, r/PPC).
- TikTok Shop treats some supplements as regulated goods; list them in the right category (see [channels.md](channels.md)).
- Before-and-after content for cosmetics must not imply a drug effect; use real, unedited photos and the same lighting.
- Intimate-care copy that leaned on slang ("coochie," "girl") drew 537- and 156-upvote objections from existing category buyers (operators report, r/SkincareAddiction). Use clear anatomical terms and adult tone.

## Agent rules

1. Before writing any copy, open the claims ledger for that SKU and jurisdiction.
2. Read creator comments and pinned replies as well as the script; testimonials can become the brand's claims.
3. Never turn regulatory housekeeping (MoCRA listing, FDA facility registration, an NPN) into a trust badge beyond what the regulator allows.
4. If the owner asks for a claim that is not in the ledger, draft the safe version, explain the risk in one line, and let the owner decide with their regulatory adviser.
