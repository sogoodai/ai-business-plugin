# Scripts

Adapt names, amounts, units and dates. Every message comes from a real, named person. Mark any number the owner must confirm as [confirm]. Never invent a beneficiary quote or an impact unit. Sources: research report scripts, operator threads (upvotes noted), and the public skills credited in [sources.md](sources.md); unlabeled scripts are written for this skill from that evidence.

## Receipts and thank-yous

**Instant receipt and thank-you (email)**
> Subject: Thank you, [first name]
>
> [First name], thank you for your gift of $[amount] today. It goes straight to [specific program], where [one concrete thing happening this month].
>
> In the next few days I'll send you a short note showing exactly where gifts like yours go.
>
> With gratitude,
> [Name], [role]
>
> Receipt: [org legal name], [EIN / charity number]. Gift of $[amount] on [date]. No goods or services were provided in exchange for this gift. [UK: Gift Aid declared: yes/no.]

For payments over $75 that include goods or services (gala ticket, auction): "The estimated value of goods and services provided is $[X]; the amount of your contribution that may be deductible is $[amount - X]." (IRS Publication 1771)

**Week-one impact note**
> Subject: Where your gift went
>
> [First name], last week you gave $[amount]. Here is one thing it is part of: [person or place] [obstacle], and on [day] our team [what happened]. [One photo.] [One number: "That's the 214th family this year."]
>
> Nothing to do here. I just wanted you to see it.
> [Name]

**Week-two question (segments the file)**
> Subject: Quick question
>
> [First name], what made you decide to give to [org]? Just click one:
> [A friend asked me] [I read a story] [I came to an event] [We were helped by you] [Faith] [Something else]
>
> It helps us know what matters to people like you.

**Thank-you call (cultivation calls for mid-level, monthly and long-tenure donors; see the evidence on mass calls)**
> "Hi, is this [first name]? This is [name] from [org]. I'm not calling to ask you for anything. I just wanted to say thank you for your gift to [program] last week. Do you have one minute? ... It's going to [specific thing]. Can I ask what got you interested in [org]? ... Is there anything you'd like to hear more about, or anything we could do better? ... Thank you, really. I'll make sure you get the update on [thing] in [month]."

Voicemail: "Hi [first name], it's [name] from [org]. No need to call back. I just wanted to say thank you for your gift to [program]. It's helping [specific thing] this month. Have a great day."

When the donor jumps to "I'll make a donation, where do I send it?" (198 upvotes): "That's very kind. You can give at [short link], or I can send you an envelope. Either way, thank you."

## Monthly giving

**Monthly ask, weeks 4 to 8 after a first gift**
> Subject: Make it dependable
>
> [First name], you've already helped once, and [program] felt it. The hardest part of [the work] is that need doesn't come once a year. It comes every week.
>
> Would you make this work dependable by giving $[15] each month? $[15] a month provides [verified recurring unit, confirm].
>
> You can change or stop your gift any time, in one click.
>
> [Give $15 monthly]
> [Name]

**Confirmation-page upsell after a one-time gift**
> "Thank you. Would you turn this into a monthly gift of $[one-time / 3, rounded]? Monthly gifts let us plan [program] months ahead. [Yes, make it monthly] [No thanks]"

**Annual upgrade (sustainer anniversary)**
> Subject: One year of [outcome], because of you
>
> [First name], a year ago you started giving $[amount] a month. Since then, [specific outcome with number]. Thank you.
>
> Costs for [thing] went up this year. Would you add $[3 to 5] to your monthly gift to keep up? [Yes, add $5] [Keep my gift as it is]

**Failed payment**
> Subject: Your monthly gift didn't go through
>
> Hi [first name], your monthly gift of $[amount] to [org] didn't process this month, probably an expired card. You can update it here in under a minute: [link]. Thank you for being part of [program] every month.

## Appeals

**Appeal letter structure (print or email)**
1. Salutation by name.
2. Opening line is a person or situation now: "Last Tuesday, [first name, with consent] waited two hours in the cold for [service]."
3. The problem in one sentence, with a real number.
4. What the org does, one sentence.
5. The ask with an amount: "Will you give $[ask] today to [outcome]?"
6. Why now: the real deadline.
7. What else the gift does, briefly.
8. Restate the ask.
9. Sign-off by a person.
10. P.S. with deadline, amount and the match if real.
Reply card: three amounts from the donor's history, monthly checkbox, Gift Aid box in the UK.

**Appeal email** (research report template, adapted)
> Subject: [First name], one [specific unit] still needs [specific outcome]
>
> Right now, [one person, community or situation] is facing [concrete problem].
> $[amount] provides [specific, defensible unit of help].
> We need [goal] by [real deadline] because [why the deadline exists].
> Will you give $[amount] today?
>
> [Give $[amount] to provide [unit]]
>
> [Name]
> P.S. [Match donor] will match every gift until [deadline], up to $[match].

**Year-end, 31 December morning**
> Subject: Last day
>
> [First name], today is the last day of [year], and the last day your gift to [org] counts for this tax year [US].
>
> We're at $[raised] of $[goal] for [specific outcome]. [Match donor] is matching every gift until midnight.
>
> [Give before midnight]

**Year-end, 31 December evening (short)**
> Subject: Hours left
>
> [First name], a few hours left. $[remaining] to go for [outcome]. [Give now]

**GivingTuesday launch**
> Subject: Today: [specific goal]
>
> Today is GivingTuesday. Our goal is simple: [specific, finishable goal, e.g. "$5,000 to buy the second freezer so we can take fresh food donations"]. [Match donor] will match the first $[match]. Three neighbors already gave to get us started.
>
> [Give today] or [Share our page]

**Ambassador message for GivingTuesday or P2P (they copy and send)**
> "I'm raising $[goal] for [specific outcome] at [org] today. I'm starting with people I know will understand why this matters. Would you be one of my first three donors? [link]"

**Funding loss appeal (only after the loss is confirmed)**
> Subject: We lost [funding source]. Here's what it means.
>
> [First name], on [date] we learned that [funding] ending on [date]. It paid for [program, with numbers]. Without it, [what stops, when].
>
> We need $[gap] by [date] to keep [program] running. [Give] Every gift goes to [program].

## Lapsed donors

**Lapsed donor email, step 1 (news, no ask)**
> Subject: Since we last heard from you
>
> [First name], you gave to [org] in [year], and I wanted you to know what happened next: [one outcome since then]. Thank you for being part of it.
> [Name]

**Step 2 (the ask)**
> Subject: We'd love to have you back
>
> [First name], your gift in [year] helped [specific outcome]. This winter, [specific current need]. Would you rejoin us with $[last gift amount] today?
> [Give $[amount]]

**Step 3 (permission)**
> Subject: Should we keep writing?
>
> [First name], we don't want to fill your inbox. Would you like to keep getting updates from [org]? [Yes, keep me posted] [No thanks]

**Old big donor with no history** (operator thread on a billionaire's $1,000 gifts, 174 upvotes): ED letter: "Thank you for your gifts in [years]. I'm the new director and wanted you to see what's happened since: [two outcomes]. I'd value 15 minutes to learn what first drew you to our work. [Phone, email.]"

## Major gifts and board

**Discovery opener**
> "Thank you for making time. I mostly want to listen today. What first connected you to [org]? ... What part of our work matters most to you? ... How do you like to hear from us? ... Is giving a decision you make with anyone else?"

**Major-gift ask** (research report, adapted)
> "You've been close to [program] for [time]. We're trying to achieve [specific outcome] by [date]. The total need is $[X]. Would you consider a gift of $[Y] to make [specific portion] possible?"
Then stop talking. If they hesitate: "What would make this feel right for you?" Never offer a range.

**Board quarterly menu** (research report, adapted)
> "This quarter, please choose three: introduce us to two prospective supporters; host a five-person mission briefing; thank ten donors; join one donor meeting; personally share the campaign; open one corporate or foundation door. Reply with your three by [date] and I'll set them up."

## Grants

**Funder pre-call or email**
> "Hi [program officer], I'm [name] at [org]. We [one sentence on the program and its result]. Before we apply to your [cycle], I'd value 10 minutes to check fit: is [program] the kind of work you're funding this year, and is a request around $[amount] in range? Happy to send a one-page summary first."

**Letter of inquiry outline (2 pages max unless the funder says otherwise)**
1. Opening: who you are, the ask amount, the program, the period. One paragraph.
2. The need: local evidence, one human example, why now.
3. The program: what you do, for whom, how many, how it works.
4. Outcomes: last year's numbers and this grant's targets, with how you measure them.
5. Why you: track record, partners, cost per outcome.
6. Budget summary: total program cost, this request, other funders committed or pending.
7. Fit: one line tying the request to the funder's stated priorities, in their words.
8. Close: contact person, thanks, invitation to visit.

**Grant boilerplate adaptation prompt** (practitioner method, 326 upvotes)
> "Here is our boilerplate for [section]: [paste]. The funder asks: '[exact question]'. Limit: [N] words. Keep: [facts to keep]. Cut: [paragraphs to drop]. Merge: [what to combine]. Use the funder's language: [their priority terms]. Do not add any facts that are not in the boilerplate."

**Mid-year funder note**
> "Hi [name], a quick mid-year note on [program]: [one number vs target], and [one short story]. Thank you for making this possible. Full report on [date]."

## Partners and in-kind

**Local business pitch**
> "Hi [owner], I'm [name] from [org], a few blocks away. We'd love to partner on [specific idea: a November drink, a menu item, a dine-and-donate night]. We'll promote it to our [N] local supporters and film a short video in your shop for our socials, and you'd donate $[X] per [item]. Could I bring a one-page plan by on [day]?"

**Declining an in-kind donation** (82 upvotes)
> "Thank you for thinking of us. We can't accept [item] due to our commitment to the health, safety and wellbeing of our clients. Right now we need [current needs list], and [partner org] accepts [item]."

**Corporate group asking what to buy**
> "Thank you. The most useful thing for $[budget] would be [specific items and brands], because [reason]. If it helps, here is a ready-made list: [link]."

## Volunteers

**Volunteer recruitment post or email**
> Subject: 6 spots left: Saturday 9 to 12
>
> We need six people this Saturday, 9 to 12, at [place] to [task]. No experience needed; you'll lift boxes up to 20 lb and work with a shift lead. Bring a friend, kids 12+ welcome with a parent.
> [Sign up in 30 seconds]

**After-shift thank-you**
> "Thank you for Saturday. You packed [number] boxes, that's [outcome]. One question: would you come back, and what would make it better? [reply]"

**Volunteer-to-donor ask (after a meaningful shift, research report)**
> "You've already given something valuable, your time. If you'd also like to help cover the cost of [specific work you saw], $25 provides [verified unit]. No pressure; your time already matters enormously. [Give $25]"

## Churches

**Same-day guest text** (research report)
> "Hi [first name], this is [name] from [church]. Thanks for being with us today. I hope you felt welcome. If you have any questions about the church or would like help finding a group or event for [family, stage, interest], just reply here."

**After a second visit** (research report)
> "Glad you came back. If you'd like, I can introduce you to [person or group] next Sunday so you already know someone when you arrive."

**Before next Sunday**
> "Hi [first name], we'd love to see you Sunday. Services at [times]; kids check in at [place] from [time]. Want me to save you a seat?"

**Six weeks no visit (one text, then stop)**
> "Hi [first name], it's [name] from [church]. We'd love to see you again. And if you've found a church home, we're really glad for you."

**Recurring giving moment in a service**
> "If [church] is your home and you'd like your giving to happen without thinking about it, scan the code on the screen to set up a weekly or monthly gift. It takes a minute, and you can change it any time. Volunteers at the back can help."

## Legacy line (annual update footer)
> "Some of our supporters include [org] in their will. If you'd like the wording, or to tell us you already have, contact [name] at [email]. Thank you."
