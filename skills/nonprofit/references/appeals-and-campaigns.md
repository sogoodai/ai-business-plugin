# Appeals and campaigns: email, letters, year-end, GivingTuesday

Benchmarks are M+R 2026 (2025 data) unless noted. Verbatim copy: [scripts.md](scripts.md). Email build, deliverability and consent law: `email-sms-loyalty`.

## 1. What an appeal has to carry

Every appeal, letter or email, contains six things in this order:

1. **One person, place or situation** facing a concrete problem now.
2. **What the organization does about it**, in one sentence a stranger understands.
3. **The donor's part:** "$[amount] provides [verified unit]". Only units program and finance staff can defend.
4. **A real deadline and why it exists** (match expires, school year starts, winter, fiscal year end, 31 December).
5. **The ask, stated plainly,** with an amount: "Will you give $50 today?"
6. **A button or reply device** that repeats the outcome: "Give $50 to keep the pantry open Saturdays".

Evidence on story frames: identifiable victims alone did not reliably raise gifts (7,996 people, 2024). Identification with the fundraiser (r = .36) and with other donors (r = .23) predicts giving across 89,570 people (meta-analysis 2025). So: write from a named person at the org, show other donors like the reader ("neighbors in [town] have given 214 gifts this month"), and keep the beneficiary story factual. Sad versus hopeful imagery worked differently depending on the perspective the message induced (2024): test the frame, do not assume.

**Tricks most owners miss**

- Ask amounts from the donor's own history: last gift, last gift x 1.25, highest gift. For new contacts, anchor near the file median.
- Personal "from" name beats the org name for most small lists; keep the same sender all season.
- A P.S. restating the deadline and amount is read more than the body; use it.
- Plain-text-looking emails from a person usually beat designed newsletters for appeals; test it on your list.
- Mention a real match or challenge only when it exists. A match raised response 22% and revenue per letter 19% in a 50,000-donor field experiment, and 2:1 or 3:1 ratios did no better than 1:1 (Karlan and List, AER 2007; older but replicated widely). So a 1:1 match is enough: ask the match donor to fund it as "a challenge" rather than seeking bigger ratios.
- Unusual amount framings get attention when they carry meaning: an Australian children's cancer charity's "Donate your age" campaign tied the amount to years of life (107 upvotes, r/DigitalMarketing).
- A confirmed funding loss is a legitimate, specific reason to give (86 upvotes, r/nonprofit federal cuts thread). Say what was cut, what it paid for, what the gap is, and by when.

## 2. Email volume and what to judge

- Median: 31 fundraising emails per subscriber a year out of 50 total, 0.59% click, 0.05% response, $54 per 1,000 sends, $2.40 per subscriber per year, 11% of online revenue.
- Rough yield: 100,000 sends gives about 50 gifts and $5,400. A 3,000-person list sending one appeal gets about 1 to 2 gifts from that email; the campaign, not the single send, is the unit.
- Advocacy and engagement emails click at 2.3% and get 1.4% action. Use them to warm the list before giving season.
- More is not automatically better: 62 messages per subscriber in 2024 came with email revenue -11%, 50 in 2025 with +16% (observational).
- Judge on revenue per 1,000 delivered, gifts, unsubscribes and spam complaints, never opens.
- Do not merge purchased contacts into the main list or replace segmented cadences with blasts: one membership org went from weekly emails to 800 members to three-times-weekly blasts to 8,000 contacts including purchased ones, and lost 30% of membership revenue (109 upvotes, several changes at once).
- Never scold donors. A misfired email chastising donors for not giving enough got 4x the open rate and a trust crisis the owner blamed for years of net losses (258 upvotes).

## 3. Year-end: a six-week campaign

37% of online revenue arrives in December, 10% in the last week, 4% on 31 December (M+R 2026; hunger and poverty orgs 46% in December). GivingTuesday falls on the Tuesday after US Thanksgiving: 1 December 2026, 30 November 2027.

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| September | Pick the year-end story and verified unit; secure a match or challenge gift; segment (monthly, active, LYBUNT, SYBUNT, major, volunteers, never-given subscribers); build the landing page with a progress bar |
| October | Cultivation: impact update with no ask, volunteer or event invitations, board thank-a-thon; clean the list; test the donation page on phones |
| Early November | Major and mid-level donors asked personally first (calls, letters, meetings) so their gifts can be announced as the match or lead |
| GivingTuesday week | Distinct GivingTuesday campaign (see below) |
| 1 to 15 December | Mail letter lands; 1 email a week to active donors; impact story; match progress |
| 16 to 26 December | Story email; "halfway to the goal" update; pause around 24 to 25 December unless the mission is Christmas-specific |
| 27 to 31 December | 4 to 6 emails: 27 or 28 (story), 29 (match deadline), 30 (progress), 31 morning (last day, tax year), 31 afternoon (hours left), 31 evening (final, short). Text and social on 31 December |
| 1 to 10 January | Thank everyone, report the total and what it funds; January giving statements |

Segment rules:

- Monthly donors get a thank-you and an optional "extra year-end gift" note, not the full barrage.
- Donors who already gave this season drop out of the remaining asks automatically (suppression by gift date).
- Major and mid-level donors get personal outreach before the mass campaign.
- Never-given subscribers get the full campaign; it is their best conversion window.
- UK, Ireland, Australia, Canada: tax year differs (UK April, Australia 30 June, Canada 31 December). Use the local deadline for "last chance" language, and December as a giving-season moment, not a tax one, where the tax year differs.

## 4. GivingTuesday

- US 2025: $4.0B, +13%; 38.1M participants; 19.1M financial donors; volunteers +20% (GivingTuesday Data Commons).
- It works for small orgs when it has a specific, finishable goal and a match, and when the existing community is told in advance. It does not work as one email on the day.

Playbook:

1. **Goal you can finish:** "$5,000 to buy the second freezer", "50 new monthly donors". Specific beats total-dollar goals for small lists.
2. **Match or challenge:** a board member, local business or major donor puts up $2,500 to $10,000; announce it in the first email.
3. **Pre-launch (week before):** "save the date" email and post; recruit 10 to 20 ambassadors (board, volunteers, monthly donors) with a ready-made message and link; ask 3 people to give first so the bar is not empty at launch.
4. **The day:** morning launch email, midday progress, evening "hours left"; live progress bar; thank every gift publicly where donors allow; one text to SMS opt-ins.
5. **Facebook fundraisers and P2P:** ambassadors run a page each; check that donor data flows back.
6. **Volunteer and "give time" options** count as participation; capture them as leads.
7. **After:** thank within 24 hours, report the result within a week, then roll straight into the December campaign with a different message.

## 5. Other campaign moments

- **Spring appeal** (March to May): one story, one need, letter plus 2 to 3 emails. Usually the second-best window for small orgs after December.
- **Fiscal year end** (often 30 June): honest deadline for orgs on a July fiscal year.
- **Anniversaries:** tie the amount to the number (a camp's 75th anniversary: $75 per cup, 152 upvotes).
- **Awareness days and months** that match the mission.
- **Emergency appeals** only for real emergencies, sent fast, followed by a full report of how the money was used. Disaster and international aid email lists had the highest unsubscribe rate in 2025 at 21% (M+R 2026); emergencies burn list goodwill.

## 6. Letters (direct mail appeal)

Structure that works for small orgs (practitioner convention, consistent with the M+R mail data above):

- Personal salutation ("Dear Maria"), never "Dear Friend" when you know the name.
- First line is the story, not the org.
- Ask with an amount by the end of page one; repeat it twice more.
- Short paragraphs, underlined key line, P.S. with deadline and amount.
- Reply card with three amounts based on the donor's history, a monthly checkbox, Gift Aid declaration in the UK, return envelope.
- Real signature in blue ink for the top donors; handwritten note on the top 50.
- Mail to arrive in the first two weeks of December; follow by email.
