# Churches: guests, belonging and giving

Most of this skill applies to churches (receipts, recurring giving, year-end, volunteers, major gifts for building campaigns). This file covers what is different: a weekly gathering, members rather than donors, and a guest funnel that runs through physical visits.

## 1. The funnel to measure

```
first visit -> contact captured -> second visit -> third visit -> group or serving -> recurring giver
```

Measure each step monthly. The industry rarely does: 80% of Protestant churches use printed guest cards, 38% an online form, 91% greeters (Lifeway pastor survey via Passport, 2024), but few track second-visit rates.

What the evidence says:

- 71% of people who ended up attending a new church regularly decided within three visits; 20% after one visit (Lifeway via Passport 2026).
- Only 21% recalled being contacted by a welcome team.
- The widely repeated "contact within 24 or 48 hours or lose them" and "85% return if followed up" figures trace to old or undocumented sources; a 2026 source review found no credible modern study behind them (Passport 2026). Follow up the same day because it is cheap and kind, and measure your own second-visit rate.
- US attendance estimates rose to 32% in early 2025 from 28% in 2024 (Gloo/Barna survey, not a headcount). Weekly volunteering 24% from 15%. Pushpay's 14,000+ client churches saw volunteer engagements +19% and group participation +22% in 2025 (vendor). Groups and serving are where people stay.

## 2. Capture contact without being creepy

- One QR code on the screen during welcome, in the bulletin and on seat backs, opening a short browser form (name, phone or email, "first time / been a few times", kids' ages, "I'd like: a coffee with the pastor / info on groups / prayer"). No app download; a church translation tool found QR-to-browser removed the app-and-room-code friction (428 upvotes).
- A small, real thank-you for filling it in (coffee, a mug, a book), handed at a welcome table, which also gets a face-to-face hello.
- Kids' check-in captures parents; ask permission to follow up.
- Livestream viewers: a "first time watching? say hi" link in the stream description and chat.

## 3. Follow-up sequence

| When | Who | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Same day | A named person, by text | Thanks for coming, an open question, no pressure ([scripts.md](scripts.md)) |
| Day 2 to 4 | Pastor, by email or handwritten card | Short welcome, one next step that matches what they ticked |
| Before next Sunday | Text | "Save you a seat?" with service times and parking or kids' info |
| Second visit | In person, then text | Offer an introduction to one person or group so they know someone next time |
| Third visit | Text or call | Specific invitation: newcomers' lunch, a group starting soon, a serving team with a first shift |
| After 6 weeks of no visit | One gentle text, then stop | "We'd love to see you again, and if you've found a church home, we're glad" |

Rules: never add guests to giving appeals; never ask a first-time guest for money; record each step in the church management system so the next person knows what was said.

## 4. Online and recurring giving

- Ministry Brands' 2026 report (2025 transactions): recurring gifts were 35% of digital giving (+5.4%); wallet gifts doubled to 5.1% of digital gifts. Among churches with digital giving, 76% offered wallets, 55% automatic recurring, 24% giving during livestreams (January 2026 survey). Pushpay customers processed 5% more gifts and 9% more mobile gifts per church (2025, vendor).
- **Make recurring the default path:** the giving page opens on "give every week / every month" with the date matched to payday; one-time a tap away.
- **Text-to-give and QR** on screen during the offering moment and on the livestream; Apple Pay and Google Pay on the form.
- **Option to cover fees,** unchecked, with the amount shown.
- **Teach and ask once or twice a year:** a short generosity series, with a "set up recurring giving" moment in the service and volunteers at a table to help on phones.
- **Year-end giving statements** by 31 January, with the "no goods or services" line; an extra year-end offering for a specific project in December.
- **Campaigns (building, mission trip, debt):** use the gift range chart in [economics.md](economics.md); quiet phase with the leadership first; pledges and cash reported separately.
- **Support-raised staff:** many Christian missions and campus ministries require staff to raise their own salary, often $30k to $40k a year, and some take an admin fee from each gift (11% in one case, 79 upvotes). If the church funds missionaries this way, give them the same donor-care tools: monthly-first ask, thank-you journey, failed-payment recovery.

## 5. Local visibility for churches

- Complete Google Business Profile: correct category, service times as hours or posts, photos of the room, the parking and the kids' area, accessibility, a "plan your visit" link. Depth: `local-seo`.
- A "Plan your visit" page answering what to wear, how long, where kids go, where to park, what happens. It is the page guests read the night before.
- Google Ad Grants is available to eligible churches where Google for Nonprofits accepts them in that country; seasonal campaigns for Christmas Eve and Easter services and "church near me" style queries fill the "Church" cluster in [acquisition.md](acquisition.md).
- Local business connections are a two-way street: some trades get most of their customers through church networks (a plumber reportedly 90%, 38 upvotes). A business directory for members can strengthen belonging and attract sponsors for community events.
- Community events (Easter egg hunts, back-to-school drives, Christmas concerts) are acquisition only if they capture contacts and invite to a specific next service or group.
