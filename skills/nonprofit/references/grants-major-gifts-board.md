# Grants, major gifts, board and corporate giving

Why this matters for small shops: gifts of $1,000+ grew 4.7% in 2025 while smaller gifts fell 1.1%; small organizations shrank 6.4% and large ones grew 11.7% (Blackbaud Institute, 7,500+ orgs, vendor). Major and supersize donors were 2.9% of US donors and 76.4% of dollars in Q2 2026 (FEP). A small org that has no personal fundraising is fishing in the shrinking pond.

## 1. Grants pipeline

**Benchmarks.** Typical competitive win rate about 14%, 1 in 7 (Instrumentl 2026, 5,000+ nonprofits, vendor). Instrumentl customers report about 40%, and its 2024 analysis of 91,000 applications showed 43 to 46%, both inflated by software users, invitations and renewals. Keep cold, invited and renewal rates separate. Sustained rates under 10% at high volume mean bad prospect selection (Instrumentl guidance).

**Pipeline stages and metrics**

```
researched -> qualified (fit score) -> pre-call or LOI -> invited / full application -> awarded -> reported -> renewed
```

Track count, dollars and conversion at each stage, separately for foundation, corporate and government.

**Qualification gates** (all four, before any writing):

1. Mission fit: the funder's stated priorities and recent grants match a real program, not a stretch.
2. Eligibility and geography: location, org type, budget size, no exclusions (many exclude churches, individuals, capital, or orgs under a budget floor).
3. Amount fit: typical grant size from their Form 990-PF grant list is within the range you need; ask for the median, not their largest.
4. Proof they fund peers: at least 2 grants in 3 years to organizations like yours. Check the 990-PF (ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer is free; Candid is paid, and practitioners complain its 2026 search is hard to filter, 86 upvotes).

Score with the 100-point fit model in [economics.md](economics.md). Below threshold, no writer time.

**Prospect research tricks**

- Read where peer organizations' money comes from: their annual report donor lists and 990 Schedule B summaries name foundations. Funders of your peers are your warmest prospects.
- Family foundations with no website and no staff often accept a 2-page letter. They are the best first grants for cause startups.
- An LLM is a fine brainstorming tool for prospect lists ("list foundations that fund [mission] in [area] with their guidelines", 116 upvotes), but its data is stale and it invents funders. Verify every name in the 990 before use.
- Community foundations run competitive cycles and donor-advised funds; ask their program officer which DAF holders care about your issue.
- Corporate giving is usually invite-only, but a short cold email to a named giving-team member can still open a door (357 upvotes).
- Corporate and foundation portals multiply: keep one shared password manager and a portal list with renewal and report dates (95 upvotes).

**Pre-call every funder you can.** One grant writer who pre-calls funders landed 23 grants and $600k in year 1; in year 2, funders said the applications were fine but they had more applicants and less money (4 approved of 13, 78 upvotes). Pre-calls tell you whether to apply at all. Script in [scripts.md](scripts.md).

**Writing efficiently.** A development director at a 12-person, $800k nonprofit submitting 35 to 40 grants a year keeps a master boilerplate doc (programs, outcomes, bios, history, budget narrative) and has an LLM reshape sections to each funder's question and word limit, with explicit instructions: "keep the watershed data and the county partnership, cut the history paragraph, merge the last two paragraphs". About 70% done, then human edits. "Make this shorter" gives garbage (326 upvotes). Keep the master doc current every quarter.

**Renewals and reports outrank new applications.**

- Put every report and reapplication date into a shared calendar 12 months ahead, with two owners. An ED who ignored a reapplication email cost the org $200,000 (82 upvotes).
- Track restricted funds in the books by grant. A grant manager whose accountant pulled random expenses at report time ended up $30,000 short of documented spending on a $50,000 grant (73 upvotes). That is how small orgs lose funders and face clawbacks.
- Send funders a short mid-year note with one story and one number. It costs 20 minutes and is what program officers remember at renewal.
- When pitching to a foundation board, rehearse answers on evaluation and budget with a hostile questioner: a 12-year veteran lost a 3-year grant on Q&A about methodology the org knew well (70 upvotes).

**Restricted versus unrestricted.** When accepting restricted gifts, negotiate an unrestricted share; one commenter with experience from $19M to $2B organizations uses at least 20% (16 upvotes, recommendation). Budget real indirect costs into every program budget.

**Big unrestricted windfalls.** A nonprofit that received a $20M unrestricted grant closed two years later after expanding too fast (149 upvotes). Treat windfalls like an endowment draw: spend a fixed share a year, never add permanent staff on one-time money.

**Never** pay grant writers a percentage or commission of the award; the AFP Code of Ethical Standards prohibits percentage-based compensation, and freelance writers report organizations asking for it (72 upvotes). Pay hourly or per proposal.

## 2. Major gifts for a small shop

There is no standard major-gift threshold or close-rate benchmark (Bloomerang). Define "major" relative to the file: often $1,000+ for a small charity, $5,000+ for a community nonprofit with a $1M budget. Track pipeline value, velocity, average gift and retention against your own history.

**Weekly rhythm for one part-time person:**

1. Score donors with RFM plus engagement ([economics.md](economics.md)).
2. Top 25 get a stage (identify, qualify, cultivate, ask, steward) and one named next action with a date.
3. Each week: 3 to 5 discovery calls or coffees, 2 thank-you notes, 1 ask if someone is ready.
4. Monthly pipeline review with the ED: who moved, who stalled, what is the next step.

**Discovery meeting goals:** learn why they give, what they care about in your work, how they like to hear from you, and whether others in the household decide. One operator's 20-year donor had given only because of his late wife and had no interest of his own; prep missed it (157 upvotes). Ask early: "What first connected you to [org]?"

**The ask** ([scripts.md](scripts.md)): a specific amount, tied to a specific outcome and date, then silence. Common practice is 3 to 5x their largest gift, capped by known capacity (wonsukchoi/domain-experts, MIT). Never a range; ranges drift low.

**Steward before re-asking:** report what the gift did, invite them to see it, thank them in a way they value (some hate public recognition). Donor demands can be draining; one major gift officer describes donors upset about event invitations and then not attending (74 upvotes). Keep a "preferences" field per major donor.

**Family dynamics:** after a major donor's death, heirs may each give to honor the parent. One org received a six-figure match from a daughter and a larger DAF grant from a son within two months (70 upvotes). Never disclose one relative's giving to another; thank each separately and ask each what they want to continue.

**Stale big names:** a billionaire gave $1,000 two years running with no record of why (174 upvotes). Send an ED letter with thanks and a short update; ask for a 15-minute call to learn what prompted the gifts. Do not open with a big ask.

**Do you need to be rich to do this?** A development director asked if personal wealth is expected (158 upvotes). No: preparation, follow-through and a clear ask outperform style. Make the ask with a peer (board member) when the donor's network expects it.

## 3. Campaigns and gift tables

Use the gift range chart in [economics.md](economics.md). Rules (wonsukchoi/domain-experts, MIT): size the goal from the prospect list up, not the budget down; solicit top-down; hold the public launch until 50 to 60% is committed; report pledges and cash separately; require board participation before asking outsiders.

## 4. Board fundraising

Data is thin and old: BoardSource numbers on mandatory board giving date from 2021; treat them as possibly outdated. Practitioners report boards that "visioned" instead of fundraising (252 upvotes), boards that set unrealistic goals without resources (130 upvotes) and boards that turned a gala into a source of conflict (117 upvotes). Convert "the board should help" into tracked behaviors.

**Rules**

- **100% board giving, any amount, personally meaningful,** before asking outsiders. Funders ask about it, and it is the board's credibility.
- **Give a menu, not a quota.** Each quarter every member chooses three: introduce two prospective supporters; host a five-person mission briefing or house party; thank ten donors by phone or note; join one donor meeting; share the campaign personally; open one corporate or foundation door; recruit one volunteer leader (adapted from the research report).
- **Track it in the CRM:** introductions, meetings attended, notes written, money influenced. Report it at every board meeting, one line per member.
- **"Give or get" floors** are common but on their own drive members to buy a gala table and stop. Pair the floor with the menu.
- **Onboard new members** with bylaws, budget, the case for support, their menu and a buddy. One new member had to "corner someone" for the bylaws and got no onboarding (105 upvotes).
- **Board events:** lock dates with the board before paying deposits.
- **Annual audit or independent review of the books,** formal or informal; a small-town treasurer's post about embezzlement cases makes the point (82 upvotes). Trust with verification protects every donor relationship.

## 5. Corporate and workplace giving

- Corporate sponsorship is getting more transactional; companies favor "safe" missions (101 upvotes). Pitch employee engagement (a volunteer day), local visibility and a measurable program outcome, not a logo on a banner.
- Matching gifts: ask donors on the thank-you page whether their employer matches, with a lookup tool if the budget allows.
- Workplace giving campaigns and United Way designations: register where your community's large employers run them.
- Employee giving inside the nonprofit: staff resent pressure and tracking (76 upvotes, 122 upvotes). Make it fully optional and never track who gave for evaluation.
