# Retention and recurring giving

The sector's growth problem is retention, not generosity. FEP: overall retention 43.3% in 2025 while donor counts fell 3.6%. M+R 2026: 24% of new online donors gave again versus 66% of prior donors. FEP Q2 2026: donors who gave 7+ times were retained at 90%, one-time donors at 14.4% (year to date). The gradient says: every extra gift a donor makes raises the odds of the next one. Design for the second gift.

Flow mechanics (triggers, exits, deliverability, consent law): `email-sms-loyalty`. Exact copy: [scripts.md](scripts.md).

## 1. The first 90 days after a first gift

Treat it like onboarding in a subscription business. Timing below is an operating design; the objective is backed by data, the exact day counts are not.

| When | What | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| Minutes | Receipt and thank-you from a named person | No second ask. Legal receipt lines (below). Say what happens next. |
| Day 2 to 7 | One concrete proof of impact | A person, a number, a photo. "Here is where your gift went." |
| Week 2 to 4 | One non-money action | Survey ("why did you give?"), volunteer slot, event, tour, prayer request, petition, a 2-minute video from the program lead. |
| Week 4 to 8 | Monthly upgrade or second gift | Specific, small, tied to a dependable outcome. |
| Day 60 to 90 | Impact update | Then into the regular program with a "new donor" tag for 12 months. |

Tricks:

- **Ask why they gave** in the week 2 email with a one-click answer set (a friend, a story, an event, I was helped, faith, other). It segments the file and gets a reply, which helps deliverability.
- **Send a different thank-you to tribute, DAF, stock and P2P gifts.** DAF gifts averaged $1,430 in 2025 (M+R 2026). A DAF donor is often a major prospect; the gift arrives from the sponsor, so look up the advisor's name and thank the person.
- **Handwritten notes for first gifts above the file median,** from a board member if you can get them to do it. Track who did.
- **Score the journey on first-to-second gift rate** at 90 and 365 days, by acquisition source.

## 2. Thank-you calls: what the evidence says

The famous rule says a thank-you call within days lifts retention sharply. The best evidence disagrees: a randomized trial on about 500,000 new donors at public TV stations and a national nonprofit found a precisely estimated null effect of thank-you calls on giving again and on gift size, over one and up to five years. Experts had predicted roughly an 80% lift (Samek and Longfield, AEJ Applied 2023). Control donors gave again at about 28%.

Rules:

- Do not staff mass thank-you calls to all new donors on faith. If the org wants to, hold out a random 10% and compare second-gift rates.
- Calls still make sense for cultivation: major and mid-level donors, monthly donors at month 3, donors with 5+ years of giving, anyone who gave far above their usual. Those calls are discovery, not gratitude theater.
- A board thank-a-thon is a board engagement tool as much as a donor one. It is worth doing for the board's sake.
- The call works best without an ask. One operator opened with "I'm not calling to ask you for a donation" and the donor replied "Look, I'll make a donation. Where should I send it?" (198 upvotes).

## 3. Receipts: legal lines that build trust

- **US:** a written acknowledgment is required for any single gift of $250 or more, stating the amount, whether goods or services were provided and their good-faith value. For a payment over $75 that is partly a gift and partly goods (gala ticket, auction), state the deductible portion (IRS Publication 1771). Add "No goods or services were provided in exchange for this gift" when true. Send a calendar-year summary in January to every donor.
- **UK:** capture a Gift Aid declaration on every eligible UK taxpayer gift: it adds 25p per £1 from HMRC at no cost to the donor (HMRC Gift Aid guidance). Unclaimed Gift Aid is the cheapest revenue a UK charity can recover; audit two years back.
- **Canada:** only registered charities issue official donation receipts, with the CRA-required fields.
- Churches: year-end giving statements by 31 January, with the same no-goods line.
- Never state that a gift "is tax-deductible" as a promise; use "to the extent allowed by law".

## 4. Monthly giving program

Why: 71% of sustainers active at 12 months (M+R 2026). Median org has about 4% of donors on recurring, and half added none last year (GivingTuesday 2025). UK charities get 48% of online revenue from regular giving (M+R and Rally 2025); recurring-first is native there.

Build:

1. **Name it** only if the name means something (a "Pantry Partners" circle); a generic "monthly giving club" adds nothing.
2. **One proposition:** "Make [outcome] dependable. $15 a month provides [verified recurring outcome]. Change or stop any time." The "change or stop any time" line lowers the commitment fear.
3. **Form test, in this order:** (a) monthly preselected and visible, one-time a tab away; (b) one-time preselected with a strong monthly callout; (c) one-time, then a monthly upgrade on the confirmation page. NextAfter's monthly-first test raised recurring conversion from 0.55% to 2.0% and average gift from $22.70 to $27.80 (2026). Watch total revenue per visitor: one-time revenue can drop.
4. **Post-gift upsell** on the thank-you page: "Would you make this a monthly gift of $[one-time / 3, rounded]?" Divide the one-time gift by 3 to 4 for the monthly suggestion.
5. **Separate sustainer journey:** welcome, a quarterly "what your monthly gift did" note, no regular appeals (or a softer version with "you're already doing your part"), a once-a-year upgrade ask.
6. **Failed payments:** 10% of sustainers stop within 2 months (M+R 2026), much of it involuntary. Turn on card updater, retry on days 3, 7 and 14, email and text with a one-tap update link, and call anyone over $25 a month. Report recovered revenue monthly.
7. **Upgrade ask once a year,** at the sustainer's anniversary: a fixed step ($5 more) or a percentage ("add $3 to keep up with costs"). Thank before asking.
8. **Self-service:** a link to change amount, date or card, and to pause. Pause beats cancel.
9. **Convert your best one-time donors by phone or letter:** donors with 3+ gifts in 24 months are the warmest audience for a monthly ask.

Churches: recurring giving was 35% of church digital giving in 2025 (Ministry Brands 2026). See [churches.md](churches.md).

## 5. Upgrade paths

```
one-time -> second gift -> monthly -> monthly upgrade -> mid-level ($1,000+ a year) -> major (personal relationship) -> legacy
```

- **Mid-level trigger:** move a donor out of mass email into personal treatment when annualized giving reaches several times the file median, or they show two engagement signals (volunteer, event, reply, question). Mid-level donors get a named contact, a once-a-year conversation and a program-specific update.
- **Major prospects:** RFM plus engagement score in [economics.md](economics.md); the cultivation playbook in [grants-major-gifts-board.md](grants-major-gifts-board.md).
- **Legacy:** long-tenure donors (10+ years, any amount) are the best bequest prospects. A single line in the annual update, "Some supporters include [org] in their will; here is the wording," and a contact name. Never push.

## 6. Lapsed donors (LYBUNT and SYBUNT)

- LYBUNT = gave Last Year But Unfortunately Not This year. SYBUNT = gave Some Year But not this year. Segment both before writing any acquisition copy: they are warmer and cheaper than any prospect list.
- Lapsed mail returns about $0.90 per $1 and costs $69 per donation (M+R 2026): break-even on gift one, profitable on retention. Mail LYBUNTs at year-end; SYBUNTs older than 3 years only if cheap.
- **Lapse trigger:** 13 months since last gift for annual donors; for donors who give several times a year, 2x their own median gap.
- **Win-back sequence:** (1) news and thanks, no ask; (2) "we miss you" with a specific current need and an easy amount; (3) a short question: "Should we keep sending you updates?" Remove non-responders from mass appeals after that to protect deliverability.
- **Check why they lapsed before writing.** One 20-year donor's giving had been his late wife's; he had no interest of his own (157 upvotes). A billionaire gave $1,000 two years running with no record of why and no stewardship (174). Look for spouse, employer match, memorial, P2P friend or event origin in the gift history first.

## 7. Communication rhythm that keeps donors

- The common "3 value touches per ask" rule is practitioner lore, not a measured ratio; use it as a floor for non-appeal months.
- Report outcomes, not activities: "38 families moved into stable housing", not "we held 40 workshops" (mohitagw15856 pm-nonprofit impact-report pattern, MIT).
- One annual impact report, quarterly short updates, real-time notes to major and monthly donors.
- Send fewer, better appeals outside the giving season; M+R participants raised email revenue 16% in 2025 while cutting messages per subscriber from 62 to 50 (observational).
- Mean donor emails are rising for some teams (86 upvotes). Have a reply template, answer once, flag the donor, and protect staff.
