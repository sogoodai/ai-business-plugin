# Volunteers, stories and impact reporting

## 1. Volunteers are a donor acquisition channel

The strongest causal finding in this skill: a randomized field experiment with 149,000+ previously inactive people found that extra volunteer recruitment messages raised volunteering 10% and donation participation 32% seven weeks later, without changing gift size (Marketing Letters 2026). Volunteering raises the chance someone gives, not how much.

Other data:

- Hunger and poverty organizations had 1.96 direct-service volunteers per donor (M+R 2025, 2024 data). For local service orgs the volunteer file can be bigger than the donor file.
- US church weekly volunteering estimated at 24% in 2025, up from 15% in 2024 (Gloo/Barna survey).
- GivingTuesday 2025 volunteers +20% to 11.1M.
- A volunteer hour is valued at $36.14 in the US (Independent Sector, 2025). Use it in grant applications as in-kind match where the funder allows and in board reports; never book it as cash revenue without accounting advice.

**Sequence:** low-friction engagement, then volunteering, then a first financial ask. Not an ask to every new contact on day one.

**Recruitment that works**

- **Specific roles with a start and end:** "Saturday 9 to 12 pantry shift, lifting 20 lb boxes, 6 spots left" beats "we need volunteers".
- **Low first commitment:** a single shift, a family day, a skills-based one-off (photos, a spreadsheet, a grant review).
- **Recruit through people:** current volunteers bring a friend; congregations, employers, schools with service hours, retiree groups.
- **Volunteer pages and Ad Grants:** "volunteer [city]" queries convert well on the grant; give them their own landing page.
- **Skilled volunteers are prospects too:** freelancers volunteer for charities to build portfolios (81 and 308 upvotes in freelance subs). Offer bounded projects with clear scope; it costs nothing and produces work samples for them and assets for you.
- **Say no when needed.** Volunteer coordinators face safety and fit issues (76 upvotes). A written volunteer policy with roles, screening and a code of conduct protects staff and other volunteers.

**Retention**

- Thank on the day, by name. Send a photo and the outcome of the shift within 48 hours ("you packed 312 boxes, that is 1,250 meals").
- Ask for feedback after the first shift: one question, "Would you come back, and what would make it better?"
- Give a path: shift lead, event committee, ambassador for GivingTuesday or P2P.

**Volunteer to donor**

- Put every volunteer in the CRM with a volunteer tag and consent to email.
- After a meaningful shift (not before the first one), make one explicit ask: "You've already given something valuable, your time. If you'd also like to help cover the cost of [specific work you saw], $25 provides [verified unit]. No pressure; your time already matters enormously." (adapted from the research report).
- Invite volunteers to fundraise rather than give when money is tight: P2P pages and GivingTuesday ambassador roles.
- Track volunteers who become donors per quarter. It is one of the few channels with a randomized trial behind it.

**Do not** make staff "volunteer" at events unpaid or pressure staff to give (several threads, 76 to 122 upvotes). It poisons the culture you want donors to see.

## 2. Story bank

Create one record per story with: person or community, obstacle, what the org did, measurable outcome, date, consent status (written, scope, expiry), photo and video permissions, the cost unit the story supports, and a direct quote if permitted. Refresh quarterly.

Rules:

- Every appeal carries human meaning and an efficacy signal: what changed, by how much.
- Build identity: "people like you", other donors, the fundraiser's own voice. Identification with the fundraiser shows the strongest link to giving (r = .36, meta-analysis of 89,570 people, 2025).
- Do not rely on the "one sad child" formula; a 2024 study of 7,996 people found identifiable victims did not reliably raise donations.
- Dignity first: no stories without consent, no images that strip dignity, no details that identify vulnerable people (domestic violence, immigration status, health).
- Overly polished branding can distance supporters; a fundraiser felt donor fatigue from being "too polished" and wanted more grassroots, open-house style contact (129 upvotes). Real photos from phones and real staff voices often beat agency gloss.

## 3. Impact reporting

Trust is measurable attitude: in England and Wales 57% have high trust in charities; the top drivers are that most money reaches the cause (53%), the charity makes a real difference (45%) and transparency about money raised and spent (39%); 69% say that transparency makes them more likely to support a specific charity (Charity Commission 2025).

**Annual impact report, one to four pages** (structure adapted from mohitagw15856 pm-nonprofit impact-report, MIT):

1. Short letter from the leader: the year in one paragraph and thanks.
2. The need, briefly.
3. Impact by the numbers: outcomes, not activities, with last year for comparison.
4. One story of change.
5. Programs in brief.
6. Money in and money out, simply: a two-bar or pie of income sources and spending.
7. Thanks, what is next, and a light ask.

Tricks:

- Put a one-line version of the money picture on the donation page.
- Send a 90-second phone video from the program lead with the report; it is the most forwarded part.
- Report the same three outcome numbers every year so donors can see the trend.
- Mark any estimate as an estimate. Never round up outcomes or claim causation you have not measured.
- Before publishing, check public filings for personal data: home addresses, phone numbers, names of individual grant recipients, a DV grantee's physical address in Schedule I (89 upvotes). Form 990s are public and scraped everywhere.

## 4. Case for support

The core document every appeal, grant and ask draws from (structure adapted from mohitagw15856 pm-nonprofit case-for-support, MIT): the need with evidence and a human face; the solution and proof it works; why us (track record, reach, cost per outcome); what gifts make possible at levels ($50, $500, $5,000, each with a real outcome); the vision and what happens without support; the ask. Write it before the campaign, not after.
