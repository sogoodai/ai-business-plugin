# Disclosure, platform policy and account risk

Rules an agent must apply before any sponsored post, affiliate link, AI-made video or growth tactic ships. This is marketing compliance, not legal advice: for a contract dispute, a regulator letter or a lawsuit, route to a lawyer.

## FTC (US audience, or any post "reasonably foreseeable" to reach US consumers)

Source: FTC, Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers (2019, still current Nov 2026); FTC Endorsement Guides Q&A (2023 revision); FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (effective 21 Oct 2024). URLs in [sources.md](sources.md).

**When a disclosure is required.** Any "material connection": money, free or discounted product, perks, a trip, an affiliate commission, employment, a family or personal relationship with the brand. It applies to free product even when the brand did not ask for a post, and to tags, likes and pins. No relationship, no disclosure needed.

**Where it goes.**
- With the endorsement itself, hard to miss. Not only on the profile or "About" page, not below a "more" fold, not mixed into a block of hashtags or links.
- Video: in the video, not only in the description. Say it and show it: "viewers are more likely to notice disclosures made in both audio and video."
- Stories and images: superimposed, on screen long enough to read.
- Livestreams: repeat periodically, because people join mid-stream.
- Same language as the endorsement.
- Platform tools (Paid partnership label, YouTube "Includes paid promotion", TikTok content disclosure toggle) are not enough on their own. Use them in addition to your own words.

**Wording that works (FTC examples).** "Ad", "Advertisement", "Sponsored", "Thanks to Acme for the free product". On space-limited platforms "AcmePartner" or "Acme Ambassador". A hashtag is optional.

**Wording that fails.** "sp", "spon", "collab", a bare "thanks", a bare "ambassador", other shorthand.

**Affiliate links.** The FTC's own usable sentence: "I get commissions for purchases made through links in this post." A bare "affiliate link" may not be understood; "paid link" next to the link may be adequate. Put the sentence before the first link, not in the footer.

**Honest endorsement rules.**
- Never describe experience with a product you have not used.
- Never call a product great if you think it is bad, even if paid.
- Never make claims the advertiser cannot prove (health, income, "clinically proven").
- Sponsor reads: "I used/tested it for [real context]" only when true. If the host has not used it, say what they checked ("I went through their onboarding this week") or read it as the sponsor's claim ("they say...").

**Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (2024).** Courts can impose civil penalties for knowing violations. Banned: writing, creating or selling fake reviews or testimonials, including celebrity testimonials; influencers in the business of posting testimonials who lie about using a product; buying or distributing fake indicators of social media influence (bot followers, views from fake or hijacked accounts) for commercial purposes. Never buy followers, views, plays or streams, and never sell "engagement" to a brand.

## UK (ASA and CMA)

Source: ASA and CMA, Influencers' guide to making clear that ads are ads (fourth edition, page dated 23 Oct 2026, updated for the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act).

- Label paid posts, gifted posts the brand controls, and affiliate posts as advertising, upfront, before anyone has to click or expand. "Ad", "Advert", "Advertising", "Advertisement" or "#ad" at the start of the caption or on screen.
- Do not rely on "sponsored", "in association with", "thanks to", "#sp", "#spon", "collab", "gifted" or "#affiliate" alone where the guide says an ad label is needed. Read the current guide when in doubt.
- The 2026 edition adds fake reviews and the CMA's direct enforcement powers under the DMCC Act. Treat UK non-disclosure as a fineable risk, not a warning-letter risk.

## EU and France

- France (loi 2023-451, 9 June 2023, article 5): paid promotion must carry "Publicité" or "Collaboration commerciale", clear and legible on the image or video for the whole promotion; images retouched to slim a silhouette or change a face need "Images retouchées", and AI-generated faces or silhouettes need "Images virtuelles". The law also bans influencer promotion of some categories (for example cosmetic surgery and nicotine products); check the current list before any health or finance deal.
- Other EU countries: national consumer-law rules plus platform labels. Default to the strictest: label upfront in the audience's language.

## Newsletters and podcasts

- Newsletter sponsor block: "Sponsored by [Brand]" or "Presented by [Brand]" above the copy. A sponsored dedicated send carries the label in the subject or the first line.
- Podcast host read: "This episode is sponsored by [Brand]" before the read, not only in show notes. Dynamic ads inserted by the host platform still count.
- Affiliate links in a newsletter: the FTC sentence above, near the first link.

## YouTube monetization policy (faceless, AI, reused content)

Source: YouTube channel monetization policies, "inauthentic content" update of 15 July 2025; AI disclosure update of 27 May 2026.

- **Inauthentic content** (renamed from "repetitious content" on 15 July 2025): mass-produced or repetitive content, content made from a template with little variation, and content easily replicable at scale is not eligible for YPP. YouTube presented this as a clarification, not a new ban on AI.
- **Reused content**: compilations, read-aloud of others' text, clips with minimal commentary fail even with the original author's permission. "Copyright-safe" and "YPP-safe" are separate tests.
- **AI-assisted work can monetize** when the result is original: AI used to edit a script, generate background visuals, visualize an original character.
- **AI disclosure (May 2026)**: required for realistic altered or synthetic scenes, real people shown doing or saying what they did not, altered real events, and AI music that is the main focus. YouTube says the label alone does not reduce recommendations or monetization. Not disclosing can.
- **Channel-level risk**: one policy strike can demonetize the whole channel, not one video. Check every channel the owner runs from the same AdSense account.

QC gate before any faceless video ships (adapted from the research report): original thesis, original script, unique sourcing, meaningful edit or story, no mass template, disclosure decided, rights cleared. Any of the first four is a "no": do not publish at scale.

## Instagram and Meta

- Original content gets recommendation priority. Meta replaces identical reposts with the original and removes serial aggregators from recommendations; visible watermarks from other apps hurt eligibility (Meta, May 2024; 75% of US recommendations were original posts in Q4 2025, Meta Jan 2026).
- Branded content must use the Paid partnership label when there is an exchange of value. Partnership ads (formerly whitelisting) need the creator to grant ad permissions in the app, which is also the moment to agree the fee and window ([brand-deals.md](brand-deals.md)).

## LinkedIn

LinkedIn's 12 March 2026 feed update reduces engagement bait ("comment YES and I'll send it"), generic recycled content, automated comments and engagement pods. Never automate comments or join pods.

## Account safety rules (operators report, r/NewTubers and r/PartneredYoutube)

- Never use an unlisted or private livestream as a working space: one accidental adult site on a zero-viewer unlisted stream got a 25,000-subscriber, 30M-view channel banned for life, appeal rejected in minutes (r/NewTubers, 325 pts).
- Stock footage licenses do not stop Content ID claims: licensed Storyblocks footage drew a claim that took 50% of a $14,000 video's revenue (r/NewTubers, 308 pts). Keep license receipts per clip and dispute immediately on any video that takes off.
- Never buy views or subscribers to reach a threshold: 4,000 watch hours is about 48,000 five-minute views, worth about $48 at $1 RPM, and paid views bring no engagement (r/NewTubers, 1,368 pts). A $100 music ad on YouTube brought 190 views, 110 subscribers and zero likes, comments or other plays (r/NewTubers, 232 pts).
- Music: bot streams get tracks removed and distributor accounts closed; fake-playlist placements are the common source ([music.md](music.md)).

## What to ask the owner before a deal ships

1. Which countries the audience is in (top 3 from analytics). That decides FTC, ASA, France rules.
2. Whether the product was used, for how long, and what they actually think.
3. Whether the contract dictates disclosure wording. If the brand forbids "ad", refuse the clause.
4. Whether any claim in the brief needs proof (health, money, performance) and whether the brand will supply it in writing.
