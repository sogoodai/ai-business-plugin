# Agencies, freelancers and consultants: win better clients, keep them longer

Built from 439 practitioner threads (r/agency, r/freelance, r/freelanceWriters, r/Upwork, r/Entrepreneur, r/digital_marketing, r/web_design, r/graphic_design, plus agency threads from r/copywriting, r/PPC and r/EntrepreneurRideAlong), 2025 to 2026 data (Promethean Research, AgencyAnalytics, Nusii, Upwork, Instantly, Smarter Outbound, Expandi, Clutch, Hinge), platform terms (Upwork, Fiverr, Contra, LinkedIn, Gmail) and the best public skills. "Operators report" or a thread score marks a practitioner anecdote; everything else carries its source and year. Sources: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md). The owner's decisions win over this guide.

## How these businesses actually grow

- **Narrow sellers win.** Agencies that cut services grew 13.0% in 2025 vs 9.8% for those that added; about 30% vs 10% net margin. Specialists report 23% higher margins. Niche expertise wins 59% of pitches; price only 31% (Promethean 2026, AgencyAnalytics 2026; associations).
- **The founder is the sales team.** Studios under 10 people are founder-sold and network-fed; only 6.6% of agency staff are in sales or marketing (Promethean 2026). Systematize founder selling; don't hire it away early.
- **Past clients and referrals are the best channel.** Past-client proposals accept 71.8% vs 43.6% for new (Nusii, 2025 data). Referral clients stay 1.9x longer (Promethean 2026).
- **Discounting loses.** Rate raisers grew 8%, flat 4%, cutters -6% (Promethean 2025). Most freelancers are underpriced against their own required rate.
- **Retention is the profit.** 42% of retainers last 2+ years. Access delays, silence and activity-only reports end them; 55% of clients ask whether the work ties to revenue (Promethean 2026, AgencyAnalytics 2026).
- **AI split the market.** Simple generation: Upwork contracts +90%, pay per contract -13%. Complex AI work pays 45% more (Upwork 2026). Sell outcomes and systems, never "AI".

## First session: ask, then compute

Ask the owner:

1. Services, niche(s), solo or team, country, target buyers' countries.
2. Last 12 to 24 months of clients: source, service, value, hours, tenure, why they left.
3. Current rates, packages, minimums, deposit and payment terms; date of the last raise.
4. Pipeline today: leads, calls, proposals out, win rate, typical time to yes.
5. Channels in use and their cost: marketplaces (fees, Connects), tools, ads, directories, events.
6. Proof available: case studies, numbers clients allow, testimonials, portfolio.
7. Capacity: billable hours available, waitlist, delivery team, utilization.
8. Contract template: scope, revisions, change orders, IP, cancellation.
9. What they will never do (cold calling, free work, discounts, guarantees), and who approves outreach and prices.

Then compute ([references/economics.md](references/economics.md)):

```
effective hourly rate per client = revenue / all hours (sales, calls, revisions, reporting, admin)
required hourly rate = (target pay + overhead + tools + taxes + profit) / realistic billable hours (solo about 1,000 a year)
client concentration = largest client / total revenue                  (over 30% is a red flag)
win rate by source = accepted / proposals sent, per channel             (past client, referral, inbound, outbound, marketplace)
channel CAC = (spend + fees + founder sales hours x EHR) / clients won
retainer LTV = monthly gross margin x average tenure in months
pipeline needed = monthly revenue target / (average deal x win rate)
marketplace effective take = (platform fee + Connects or boosts + payment fees) / gross billed
retainer margin = (fee - delivery cost) / fee                          (below 25%: audit hours vs scope)
```

Report the three biggest gaps in money and founder hours, not percentages.

## Decision rules, in order

1. **Positioning before channels.** If the offer reads like "digital marketing for businesses", fix it first: `[mechanism] for [buyer] when [observable trigger], producing [result]`. One service per campaign: operators report 1 to 2% positive replies vs 0.3 to 0.5% for multi-service pitches ([positioning.md](references/positioning.md)).
2. **Price floor before volume.** Compute the required rate. Win rate above 70 to 80%, a waitlist or "very reasonable": raise for new prospects now (each new quote 20% above your highest current rate), existing clients with 60 days notice ([pricing.md](references/pricing.md)).
3. **Warm before cold.** Reactivate past clients and lost deals, ask for referrals at each win, post availability, then partners. Track past clients as their own channel ([getting-clients.md](references/getting-clients.md)).
4. **Outbound shows, doesn't pitch.** Trigger-based lists of 100 to 250 accounts; the email carries a finding about their asset (audit, mockup, 2-minute fix video). Judge on positive replies per prospect; diagnose list and offer before copy ([outbound.md](references/outbound.md)).
5. **Marketplaces are paid distribution.** Selective bids, a P&L per platform, legal off-platform moves via Direct Contracts. Never one account above half of revenue ([marketplaces.md](references/marketplaces.md)).
6. **Qualify before proposing.** Problem, consequence, trigger, current alternative, decision process, budget range. Price standard work on the call; proposals confirm a deal already understood ([sales-and-proposals.md](references/sales-and-proposals.md)).
7. **Paid entry offers, bounded free work.** Paid audit or discovery over free consults; free work only in capped hours, for people with reach or a clear budget.
8. **Scope and money in writing.** Inclusions, revision rounds, change-order rate, deposit (50% default), launch only after final payment.
9. **Retention system before more acquisition.** Access before kickoff, baseline and 30-day milestone, Friday update, monthly health score, quarterly scope review, next-bottleneck review ([retention.md](references/retention.md)).
10. **Content is proof the founder can only write.** Teardowns with dollar figures, client patterns, own data. Judge on conversations, not impressions ([content-and-brand.md](references/content-and-brand.md)).
11. **Ads for the agency itself last,** on pain keywords, judged on collected revenue and month-2 retention.

## First 30 days for a new freelancer or a stalled studio

1. **Offer:** one positioning sentence, one flagship service, one paid entry offer with fixed scope, price and date. Two portfolio pieces in that niche, spec work labeled.
2. **Warm list:** message every past client, ex-colleague and lost "not now" deal individually; one availability post. "5 warm texts beat 50 new cold emails" (76 pts).
3. **Referral asks** to every current client at their latest win; affiliate programs for the tools you already recommend (about $12,000 a year missed by one consultant, 102 pts).
4. **Twenty trigger accounts a week** researched in one 45-minute batch, contacted in two fixed morning blocks (61 pts).
5. **Partners:** five complementary providers with the same buyers; referral terms in writing.
6. **Terms:** deposit, revision rounds, change-order rate and payment schedule in one contract template.
7. **Skip for now:** the big website, paid ads, boosted posts, tool stacks. One ghostwriting founder reached $50,000 a month on a $10 one-page site (204 pts).

The full sequence with exit checks: [references/plan-90-days.md](references/plan-90-days.md).

## Pricing in one screen

- **Model by risk:** unknown scope = paid discovery or capped hourly; repeatable deliverable = fixed price; ongoing duties = retainer with stated capacity; transformation = discovery, fixed phases, upside fee if measurable. Never "value pricing" as a label (use fell 31% to 18%, Promethean).
- **Productize the door:** "$2,500 five-page website", "$1,000 site audit" (1,655 pts). Name it.
- **Three tiers, what they asked for in the middle** ($2,999 / $3,999 / $4,999+, 437 pts); or a second proposal at 2x their stated budget with real extras (172 pts).
- **Raises:** a whole-agency 30% raise lost 20% of clients and still grew revenue 24.4% (47 pts). Time it for the clients' busy season. Offer upgrade paths, not forced moves.
- **Scope:** two revision rounds; 60-day small-edit window; +30% rush and +30% after-hours surcharges (808 pts); label every freebie "this time".
- **Payment:** 50% deposit; 40/40/20 on web builds with launch after final payment; prepaid hour ladder for consultants (114 pts). Card deposits can be charged back ($15,000 lost, 53 pts).

Depth: [references/pricing.md](references/pricing.md). General break-even and raise math: `offers-pricing`.

## The tricks that move the most money

- **The audit is the email.** A one-page local visibility audit sent only where real gaps exist lifted replies from 2% to 18% (78 pts).
- **Speculative redesign, built after a yes:** 120 manual emails, 3 clients (84 pts).
- **No voicemail:** a LinkedIn request within 5 minutes of an unanswered call, 14.1% vs 2.3% (480 pts).
- **Post-interview mini-build** while they compare freelancers: 2 of 3 decided on the spot (133 pts).
- **End proposals with what you need to start** (logins, keys, content): beat 6 finalists (260 pts).
- **Tell prospects when to DIY:** it closed the ones who stayed (485 pts).
- **Friday 2-minute Loom** even when flat: three silent weeks cost a €2,000 a month client (340 pts).
- **Health score on reply latency:** 52 days of warning before churn (30 pts).
- **Win-back pings at 30, 90, 180, 365 days** without asking when they will return (51 pts).
- **Partner agencies, not end clients:** "Have you ever thought about offering [service] to your clients?" (96 pts).

## When practitioners and the data disagree

| Topic | Practitioners say | Data says | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cold email volume | 200 to 300 a day needed vs relevance wins | Positive replies 0.79% median; manual beat bulk in threads | Prove the offer by hand on 100 to 250 accounts, then scale |
| Loom in outreach | Biggest interview driver vs nobody watches | Vendor cases only (1.5% to 6%; +19%) | Video after a reply or opt-in, showing their asset |
| Low first prices | Cheap first jobs build reviews | Rate cutters -6% (Promethean); $5/h bids still lose | Low price only on tiny scoped jobs, raise after 2 to 3 reviews |
| Ask budget or not | Always ask vs three tiers, never ask | No data | Ask on inbound and referral calls; tiers when pricing live |
| Free samples | Spec work wins vs free work anchors low | No data | Capped (2 to 3 hours or 5% of scope), for qualified buyers only |
| Value pricing | Charge a share of value | Use fell, users grew less | Concrete structure by risk |
| Referral fee | 20% is standard | 20% median is software programs, not agencies | Negotiate on margin; write terms first |
| Upwork boosting | Never boost vs only boosted bids get opened | No platform data | Boost only jobs passing every client signal; track per Connect |

## Never

- Never send outreach, proposals, price notices or client messages without the owner confirming text and recipients.
- Never automate LinkedIn, scrape it, or run engagement bots on client accounts.
- Never send cold email without authentication, an opt-out and the recipient country's rules (CAN-SPAM, PECR, CASL, Spam Act, national ePrivacy).
- Never guarantee results a platform controls, or count replies as "leads".
- Never invent results, testimonials, client names or benchmarks; drafts of client quotes need client approval.
- Never sell AI-assisted work as human-made, or hide affiliate links.
- Never move marketplace clients off-platform against its terms; use the legal route.
- Never start work without a deposit and written scope, or release files before final payment.
- Never gate or incentivize reviews.
