# Retention, delivery and expansion

Retention is where agency profit lives: 42% of agencies report retainers lasting over two years and 30% over three, but referral clients stay 1.9x longer, and formal onboarding, business reviews, an account owner and agreed KPIs go with longer tenure (Promethean 2026, correlational). 55% of agencies say the client's top question is whether marketing connects to revenue; 97% call accurate reporting important to retention (AgencyAnalytics 2026).

## Onboarding: the first 30 days decide

- **Access before kickoff.** 62% of agencies say access delays damage client confidence (Promethean 2026). Make dependencies contractual:
  > "To hit [30-day milestone], we need the following access before [date]: [systems]. Anything outstanding by then moves the milestone date one-for-one. Your first success review is scheduled for [date], where we'll compare [baseline metric] with [target]."
- **Baseline and one goal in writing** before work starts. No engagement begins without a named baseline, target and dependency owner.
- **Deliver something in week 1:** "onboarding velocity is the offer"; agencies that take 6 weeks to start lose to those that ship in week one (coreyhaines31 offers skill, MIT).
- **Discovery that prevents rework:** 3 websites they like before designing (243 pts); 1 to 2 hours of research and moodboard before production (37 pts); all content collected before the build (243 pts); intake questionnaire of 20 to 30 minutes for copy work, decline clients who refuse it (r/copywriting, 397 pts).
- **Local service clients:** a prepopulated service checklist built from search volume, separating services licensed separately; ask both "which services do you want to grow" and "which bring the most inquiries now"; collect every service-area ZIP and the local name for the area; agree a target cost per lead including your fee, and walk through the math if they say $10 (r/agency, 42 pts).
- **Put specialists in front of the client's real users on day one** to cut the manager-to-spec-to-dev chain (comment with 486 pts, 4,012 pts thread).

## Reporting that keeps clients

- **Weekly 2-minute Loom every Friday,** even when results are flat: what was sent, what the data says, what is tested next. Adopted after three silent weeks lost a €2,000 a month client whose campaigns were performing (r/EntrepreneurRideAlong, 340 pts).
- **Tie the report to revenue or pipeline,** not activity. One recruiter client acted on one weekly Slack message with three metrics only (new candidates, interviews, placement ratio) (67 pts).
- **Monthly social reports 15 days after month end** so late engagement counts; one client-approved monthly calendar in a shared sheet that also tracks engagement and lets another operator cover absences (r/socialmedia, 319 pts).
- **Make invisible work visible:** SEO articles delivered as optimization-tool draft links showing the score, not plain docs (186 pts); before and after on their own asset at every handover (1,125 pts).
- **Paid media hygiene that clients notice:** judge Meta on blended CPA, total sales and organic lift together (431 pts); set CPA from lifetime value, not an arbitrary cap ($15 target vs $55 real CAC and $180+ LTV, 139 pts); write patience rules down (campaigns often look bad for 2 days and improve by day 4, 94 pts); a second-person check on budgets and audiences before launch (an extra zero cost $10,000 in 3 days, 44 pts); written sign-off before enabling or pausing anything (29 pts).
- **Cheap fixes before asking a client to pay for new creative:** kill the bad placement, duplicate the campaign unchanged, swap the thumbnail (42 pts). Ship new creative during the engagement: one batch never refreshed lost a client after $15,000 and 3 months (121 pts).
- **Stay in the brief.** An agency hired for top of funnel drifted to bottom of funnel to hit numbers and was fired; that client let 5 agencies go in 6 weeks (53 pts).

## Client health: predict churn 60 days early

- One agency tracked contact frequency, delivery patterns and payment timing, with a Slack alert below 65: warning signs were weekly check-ins slipping to biweekly and same-day replies slipping to 2 to 3 days. Relationships decayed 60 to 90 days before non-renewal (52 days of warning on average); losses fell from about 5 clients a year worth $60K each to 1 in 9 months (r/agency, 30 pts).
- Another scores 0 to 25 on payment reliability, budget realism, responsiveness, trust in strategy and operational friction; average about 20, high-maintenance clients below 14, almost every churned client scored low (48 pts).
- Any client without a substantive conversation in the past month is at risk; 5+ year relationships are normal for the commenter (68-pt comment).
- Call every client monthly. One web agency owner calls 100+ clients: needs, questions, concerns, whether they ask for Google reviews, whether they post photos. "I'm not a vendor, vendors are replaceable. I'm a trusted advisor." (19-pt reply under a 164-pt comment; 20 new clients queued.)
- Tag churn by cause (non-payment, unrealistic expectations, seasonality, too early for paid ads) before calling it a retention problem; some churn is healthy (48 pts).
- Health score template: [economics.md](economics.md#client-health-score-0-to-10-review-monthly). Promethean: 88% of agencies track at least one health measure; make it predictive, not a post-mortem.

## Expansion: the next bottleneck

49% of agencies upsell several times a year and 38% occasionally (Promethean 2026). Expansion should follow the result, not a service menu.

> "We've moved [metric] from [baseline] to [result]. The constraint has now shifted to [next bottleneck]. I see three options: leave it as is, have your team handle it, or let us run [defined next phase]. Want the one-page economics?"

- Propose the adjacent piece at delivery: after an email sequence, supporting articles; after a sales page, SEO content on the same pains (104 pts). "Now that your website is finished, let's get it some traffic" (53 pts).
- Strategy sells through delivery: staff give business advice until the client asks "What should we do next?", then "Let's run a workshop and help decide." Pitching strategy directly rarely worked (72 pts).
- Inspect adjacent systems during routine delivery and quote extras the same day (the landscaper's irrigation check; 1,082 pts). Agency version: note broken tracking, slow pages, missing reviews on every monthly call.
- Monitor past clients' sites for gaps: "Hey, it was great working with you on X! I've noticed your updated website could be improved by adding Y!" (r/copywriting, 246 pts).
- Land and expand with a subcontractor: bring a specialist into the client's work for 2 to 3 months, then propose a bundle. A $144,000 6-month contract was approved verbally within 1 to 2 hours; the freelancer kept $117,000 (159 pts).
- Q4 prepaid hour blocks for budgets that expire (128 pts). AI answer audits for past clients as a re-engagement offer (112 pts).
- Benchmark your output against the client's in-house work before renewal; an expensive creative retainer was cut for underperforming in-house (53 pts).

## Case studies, testimonials and reviews

- Ask at the measurable-win moment, not months after offboarding:
  > "We hit [specific milestone]. Would you be comfortable writing two or three sentences on what was difficult before, what changed and the result? If easier, reply with bullets and I'll draft something for your approval."
  The agent may draft from the client's facts; the client approves the final wording. Never fabricate a testimonial or invent a client quote.
- Concentrate reviews on 1 to 2 platforms (Clutch and Google Business Profile for one agency); clients will not review several (50 pts). On marketplaces, ask constantly to buffer one bad client (21-pt reply).
- Auto-send a feedback form and review request at the outcome moment, then turn testimonials into mini case studies for calls and the blog (67 pts). Send the same request to everyone: Google prohibits selectively soliciting positive reviews, and score-based routing ("gating") can get reviews removed (see `local-seo`).
- First win becomes the proof asset: after 6 months and 1,000+ conversations with no clients, one roofer ranked first in its city sold every next client (1,271 pts).
- Where numbers cannot be shared: process evidence, screenshots, artifacts, approved qualitative quotes.

## Delivery capacity (protects retention)

- Plan at 60 to 80% billable utilization: 2 people = 320 hours a month; at 10 hours per client that is 19 to 25 clients, not 32 (80 pts). Months above 100% capacity are a stop signal (37 pts).
- Waitlists instead of overload: 9 incoming clients staged as 4 now, 4 in 30 days, 1 in 60 days; onboarding paused for six months to write procedures (39 pts).
- Grow 10 to 25% a year; one agency doubled in 2022 and spent 2023 repairing it (37 pts). Accelerate only with 2 of 3 signals: no churn, strong referrals, clients increasing spend (53 pts).
- Structure: 3-person client pods (strategist, specialist, generalist) before shared departments; group managers who still hold clients ($500K to $6.5M agency, 202 pts). A client success manager as default contact and Friday reports from managers took one founder out of 95% of day-to-day client communication (74 pts).
- Check outsourced content at every price: 34% plagiarized or spun and 18% AI nonsense across 359 articles from 17 vendors (1,579 pts).
- Quarterly 90-minute scope review on every retainer: real work drifts 20 to 40% above priced scope within 90 days (59 pts).
- "Paused ads = paused work" written into ad retainers (48 pts).
- Bad-day protocol: reply to one client even without progress ("Got this, I'll come back to you Thursday."), log money owed, then stop. Disappearing loses clients (118 pts).

## Concentration and platform risk

- Cap any one client at about 25 to 30% of revenue and any one platform account at about half. Three multi-year clients ended in the same month for a writer who had stopped prospecting (212 pts); invoices fell from $30K+ to $2K a month when contacts were laid off (132 pts); the largest client at 21% of revenue disappeared in March 2020 (160 pts); an agency lost 40% of MRR in 3 months and kept its sales and marketing staff while cutting other costs (155 pts).
- Keep fulfillment evidence (deliverables, approvals, payment history) outside client-owned email and tools: identical evidence split six chargeback disputes 3 to 3 ($3,900 won, $4,100 lost) (21 pts).

## Win-back

- Friendly "hey" by text, email or DM at 30, 90, 180 and 365 days after a client leaves; never ask when they will come back. One returned after 12 months with another agency and grew from $70K to $350K a month (51 pts).
- Returning clients pay the current rate (128 pts; clients who came back paid double in an offers-pricing thread, 105 pts).
- Former clients reactivated at the higher current price (48 pts).
- Win back through the incumbent: free audit, then offer to coach the current agency on a call; the incumbent exited (51 pts).
- Past-client proposals accept at 71.8% vs 43.6% new (Nusii): reactivation is its own channel with its own tracking.

## Firing clients

- Before firing a low payer, offer a higher rate with a longer turnaround (84 pts).
- Grandfathering early loyal clients can be worth it (a $12 to $29 subscription kept early members at $12, 1,752 pts thread); forcing legacy clients up made "most" leave (55 pts). Offer stay-as-is or upgrade.
- An agency fired an abusive $12,000 a month client within hours and reports better clients, many retained 3 years, arriving soon after (134 pts; causation unproven).
