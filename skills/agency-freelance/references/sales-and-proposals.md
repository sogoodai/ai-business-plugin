# Discovery calls, proposals and closing

Measured anchors: agency proposals accept 47.7%, 37.6% of proposals get no answer, past clients accept 71.8% vs 43.6% for new, under $1,000 accepts 70.2% vs 29.2% at $50,000+, 31.9% are first opened on a phone, median time to yes 4.2 days, proposals sent twice accept 57.6% vs 48.3% (Nusii, 2025 data). Pitch win rates: 47% of agencies at or below 50% (AgencyAnalytics 2026). No credible benchmark exists for discovery-call close rate; measure the owner's.

## Speed to lead

- Four threads agree: respond in minutes. A callback to an unknown number in under 5 minutes (the lead had contacted other agencies) became a $2,250 a month top-tier package paid the next day (r/agency, 67 pts). A TikTok lead-form prospect got an instant reply and a same-day call and paid $1,500 a month the next day (86 pts). A Google Business Profile inquiry met at a coffee shop the same day signed that day (108 pts).
- A sales rep left a ready lead for a month; the owner called and closed it in days. Owners audit stalled pipelines weekly (108 pts).
- Test that inquiries actually arrive: an email hosting failure delayed 5 December inquiries by 3 weeks (95 pts).
- Inbound reply under 100 words, answer their question, offer real slots (anthropics speed-to-lead skill, Apache-2.0). Draft only; the owner sends.

## Qualify before the call, and before any proposal

Fill six fields before writing a full proposal (research procedure): business problem, its measurable consequence, trigger and timing, current alternative, decision makers and process, commercial range. If most are blank, flag the proposal as low confidence.

Disqualifiers from operators:
- The trifecta: history of hopping agencies, hard price haggling, "use our contract". One such client removed access after relaunch and paid $1,000 of almost $15,000 (134 pts).
- "Fee or contingent?" not answered before travel or work (a cab, 2 hours and a cancelled paid meeting lost, 154 pts).
- Budget that cannot cover the niche's cost per result (roofing at $500 to $700 a month when one appointment costs $250+, 154 pts).
- A 3-hour meeting with a note-taker while they compare candidates: your plan goes to the cheapest (89 pts).
- Outbound services: founder must already know the ICP that responds, the angle that books and the close rate "with actual numbers"; one agency turns away clients under $30,000 MRR (88 pts).
- Can they earn a return? $200 to $500 site clients had no cash flow; the profitable $2,000 client would have paid $5,000 to $6,000 (1,854 pts). Conflict: a $40M company haggled over a $6,000 site (63 pts). Revenue is not willingness to pay; ask about budget and the value of the problem.

## The call

Agenda credited with 200+ freelance deals (r/Entrepreneur, 1,199 pts):
1. Two to three minutes of small talk, then "Should we dive right in?"
2. "I'll start by asking a few questions to get a really clear picture of your business... If it sounds like I can definitely help, I'll explain how I work. Sound good?"
3. Inbound: "What prompted you to book this call today?" Outbound: "When I reached out, something in my message must have clicked. What was it that made you decide to take this call?"
4. "Have you worked with another freelancer or agency on this? What was that like?" Pitch against that failure.
5. The prospect talks 80% of the first half.
6. Bring 1 to 3 priced packages and price on the call: "I've done similar projects for around $4,000." Never end with "let me get back to you with a proposal". If they decline or delay, ask why.

Conflict: under pressure on complex work (a client challenging your rate with others on the video call), say you need time to calculate and come back (146 pts). Rule: pre-built packages for standard work get priced live; genuinely custom scope gets a short follow-up with the number.

Budget questions:
- "I can craft a $5,000 solution or a $50,000 solution. Obviously, you get a lot more with a $50,000 solution. So I need a ballpark idea of what resources you have before I can recommend an approach." Fallback: "I really can't propose a solution until I have a ballpark idea how much you can spend", then walk (172 pts, claims 90% get a number).
- "For a project like this, I'd normally charge a flat fee of $X, is that something we could work with?" with X at 2x your last similar fee (88 pts).
- "Well, full price would be $X, but if we dropped a couple of options we could work with your budget" (88 pts).

Trust moves:
- Tell them when to do it themselves: "Before we talk price, do you have 5 to 10 hours over the next month? Because if you do, you should DIY it. HubSpot Academy is free... You'll spend 5 to 10 hours either way, doing it yourself or explaining your business to me." A CRM consultant talked 10+ people out of buying; 2 clients in 2 months credited the honesty (r/Entrepreneur, 485 pts).
- No guarantee, a stop-loss: "There's no guarantees, here's what we'll do to make sure it works as early as possible so we can cut our losses short if it doesn't." (r/agency, 990 pts)
- Sell the process: the same 12 posts a month went from about $500 to $1,500 a month in 4 months by explaining a clear process (134 pts).
- Prerecord an industry demo; the live call becomes customization, "almost onboarding" ($15,000 MRR solo, 53 pts).
- Engineers or specialists on calls pitch business results to the exec, not technical detail (lost deals, 4,012 pts thread).
- Ask for their data under NDA before the first proposal; a former consulting partner now buying said generic market slides count against you (r/consulting, 984 pts).

Research close:
> "Based on what you've said, the expensive part is [problem], because it is creating [business consequence]. You want [outcome] by [time], and [decision process] is involved. I think there is a fit. I'll send a proposal built around [recommended approach] rather than a generic menu. Is there anything that would prevent you from moving ahead if the scope and economics make sense?"

Free consult boundaries: 20-minute exploration, not a 1-hour free strategy session (277 pts); "Happy to help. My consulting rate is €150/hour. First 15 minutes free to see if I can actually add value." (implementation rate from about 10% to 80%+, 190 pts).

## The proposal

Structure (research): 1. their situation in their words; 2. desired result and success metrics; 3. recommended scope, not a catalogue; 4. deliverables and explicit exclusions; 5. timeline and client dependencies; 6. proof for the same problem; 7. investment, payment schedule, options; 8. assumptions and change-order mechanism; 9. acceptance and kickoff step.

Rules:
- **First screen readable on a phone:** problem, result, price context, next step (31.9% open on mobile).
- **One page beats twenty** for most small-business work: summary, cost, payment schedule, contract attached (243 pts).
- **Large proposals confirm a deal already understood.** A $50,000 proposal needs a stronger pre-proposal process than a $750 diagnostic (29.2% vs 70.2% acceptance).
- **Options:** three tiers with what they asked for in the middle (437 pts), or one at their budget and one at 2x with significant extras (172 pts). Never offer a tier you would not want to deliver (anthropics proposal skill).
- **Price from comparable past jobs with the arithmetic shown,** flag quotes more than about 15% off your rate card, and log every quote won, lost or no response with the reason (anthropics proposal-builder, Apache-2.0).
- **Client dependencies and the change-order rate go in the commercial document,** not only the project plan.
- **Speed:** a proposal within 10 minutes of the call from a reusable template filled by automation vs about 3 hours from scratch (37 pts); meeting notes to a 6-page PDF in near zero time (34 pts); a post-call form, AI draft, human review, send for about $0.43 per proposal (31 pts). The owner reviews every number.
- **Reply to an inbound inquiry with a short deck** (rates, services, experience, next steps) instead of "Yes, I'd love to know more!": 4 long-term clients for one writer (95 pts).
- **Resend once** when silent (57.6% vs 48.3%), adding information.
- Track acceptance by source (referral, past client, inbound, outbound, marketplace); a blended rate hides the economics.

## After the yes

- Close in minutes: e-signature contract plus a checkout or invoice link on the same page (448 pts).
- Deposit before kickoff ([pricing.md](pricing.md#payment-terms)); onboarding checklist and the 30-day milestone ([retention.md](retention.md)).
- "Final payment due" is a row in the timeline after "final deliverables" (268 pts).

## After a no or silence

- "I'll close this out so I don't keep filling your inbox. If [problem] becomes a priority later, reply 'teardown' and I'll send the notes." (research)
- Unresponsive mid-project: "Because I haven't heard back from you since [date], I am assuming that your priorities have changed", then offer cancellation with partial payment or a new timeline; reply within an hour in one case (174 pts).
- Price shoppers: "Best go with those guys, then!" One returned a month later (437 pts).
- Audit competitors' sales demos (attend as a prospect) before cutting price: one agency found four competitors with weaker services packaged better (51 pts).

## Tricks most owners miss

- On marketplaces and RFPs, a fixed number now beats a call later (60% of applicants archived for "let's chat", 260 pts).
- A happy client's "what would an agency have charged you?" is the cheapest pricing research there is (1,854 pts).
- Before raising a price objection response, check whether the objection is risk, not price: only 31% of pitch wins cite price (AgencyAnalytics 2026).
