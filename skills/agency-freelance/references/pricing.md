# Pricing: models, packages, raises, scope and payment terms

General price-setting math (break-even tables, value equation, guarantee taxonomy) lives in `offers-pricing`. This file holds what is specific to agencies, freelancers and consultants. Operator numbers carry the thread score; full URLs in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Pick the model by risk, not by dogma

| Work | Structure | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Unknown scope: debugging, inherited systems, audits | Paid discovery or capped time and materials | You cannot price what you have not seen |
| Repeatable deliverable with controlled scope | Fixed project price | Buyer wants certainty; you keep the efficiency gain |
| Ongoing capacity and recurring duties | Retainer with stated capacity and service levels | Matches staffing to revenue |
| Transformation with measurable upside | Paid discovery, then fixed phases, then an upside fee if measurable | Value is unknowable before diagnosis |
| Small standard entry offer | Productized fixed price | Fast yes, and it qualifies the buyer |

Evidence (Promethean 2026): 8% or fewer agencies use one pricing model only, so hybrids are normal. Value-based pricing use fell from 31% (2024) to 18% (2025) and its users grew less. That is an association, but it means: never recommend "value pricing" as a label. Recommend a concrete structure.

## Set the floor first

- **Required hourly rate** = (target pay + overhead + tools + taxes + profit) / realistic billable hours. Solo realistic billable hours are about 1,000 a year ([economics.md](economics.md)).
- **Count acquisition time.** A $100 to $200 article costs 4 to 5 total hours once pitching is included; set a minimum take-home per gig (one writer: $150) (77 pts).
- **Writers' per-word floor:** about 250 finished words per total working hour (500 per productive hour, 3.5 productive hours in a 7-hour day). Per-word floor = target hourly / 250 (126 pts).
- **Agency internal floor:** one agency moved its internal revenue floor from $100 to $125 to $150 per delivery hour. At $150, a $1,000 a month productized client gets at most 6.5 delivery hours (80 pts). Put the hour cap in the internal plan, not the client contract.
- **Fully loaded comparison for objections:** an employee costs 2 to 3x salary, and a freelancer stops costing money when the project ends; $90 an hour freelance vs a $30 an hour employee is not 3x (808 pts).
- **Add a 20% buffer** to internal hour estimates on fixed quotes (1,349 pts).
- **Rank clients by effective hourly, not headline rate.** One writer's $0.20 a word flat client paid $240 an hour; their $0.60 a word white papers paid $125 to $175 (89 pts).

## Market anchors (know which market you are in)

- Agency hourly bands: most common $175 to $199 (29% of agencies, Promethean 2026). Clutch 2026 listings: US, Canada, Australia web and design agencies mostly $100 to $149; UK design $50 to $99; many global web listings $25 to $49.
- Upwork freelancer bands 2026: web dev $15 to $50, designer $15 to $35, writer $30 to $59, copywriter $19 to $45, video editor $10 to $60, digital marketer $15 to $45, AI automation engineer $35 to $60, Zapier developer $40 to $90 (projects $300 to $800 simple, $3,000 to $10,000+ cross-functional).
- Operator price menus (self-reported):
  - Copywriter: newsletter ad $25 to $65; product blurb $75; email $100 to $200 each; video or podcast script $200 to $300; landing page $600 to $2,500; full site copy $2,500 to $4,000; consulting $200 to $300 an hour (215 pts).
  - B2B SaaS writer: blog up to 1,500 words $1,200; long-form up to 3,000 words $2,200; white papers from $2,000; $600 minimum project (110 pts).
  - Writing retainers: $600 a month for 4 emails or posts (1,523 pts); $750 a month for 6 to 8 short emails and 12 social posts (216 pts); $2,000 a month for ad copy, email sequences and product descriptions (225 pts); landing page plus 12 emails about $2,500 (225 pts).
  - Local marketing agency menu 2026: website $1,500 one-off; Google or Meta ads $1,250 a month; ads plus SEO $2,250 a month (55 pts).
  - Content agency end to end (research, writing, design, promotion): $7,000 to $20,000 a month (262 pts).
  - Direct-response sales letter: $15,000 plus 3% royalties, capped at 2 projects a month (187 pts).
  - Funnel copy as a 12-week research-to-launch project: $50,000 and $56,000 fees, one new client a month, only for $1,000 to $2,000 courses with 100+ daily funnel visitors (r/copywriting, 500 pts).
  - Local SEO audits (MIT skill harvest, uncited): GBP audit $200 to $500; local visibility $500 to $1,500; site SEO $800 to $2,500; single-location retainer $500 to $3,000 a month.
- Specialty pays: tax litigation content at $0.80 to $1 a word; general niches (pets, games, outdoors) 2 to 7 cents; 7 cents is low for law, medicine, software, finance (132 and 115 pts).
- **Ask the happy client what an agency would have charged.** A $2,000 website client said she would have paid $5,000 to $6,000 (1,854 pts). Do this after every successful project; it sets the next quote.

## Packages that sell

- **Productize the entry point, not the whole business.** "$2,500 five-page website" instead of $100 an hour; "$1,000 site audit" instead of SEO consulting (1,655 pts). Fixed inputs, fixed output, fixed timeline, written exclusions, a named next step.
- **Name it.** Named offers compete on positioning; unnamed services compete on price (coreyhaines31 offers skill, MIT). Example names: "Landing Page Conversion Sprint", "AI Workflow Opportunity Map", "Technical SEO Recovery Audit", "Webflow Migration Plan".
- **Three tiers, what they asked for in the middle:** Basic $2,999, Professional $3,999, Elite $4,999+, presented live; the poster never asks budget and reports no budget complaints (437 pts).
- **Budget known? Send two proposals:** one at budget, one at 2x with significant extras, and sell the higher one; stated budgets understate real funds (172 pts).
- **Anchor with a premium package:** a $5,000 package makes the $2,500 a month core look right; for smaller budgets strip deliverables, never the rate (134 pts).
- **Split cheap and serious work:** about €1,000 no-code one-pager vs €10,000+ branding plus Figma plus Webflow positioned on client acquisition; the expensive clients were easier (55 pts).
- **Unbundle the website from retainers.** One agency stopped including a site in the $1,000 ads plus SEO package; it became a $1,500 one-off (55 pts).
- **Justify a higher tier with attribution:** $650 tier had landing pages, call tracking and GA conversions; the $1,000 tier added WhatConverts attribution and CRM integration (55 pts). Ties to the research: 55% of clients ask whether marketing connects to revenue (AgencyAnalytics 2026).
- **Lego blocks:** predefined service blocks combined into a recommended plan rather than bespoke packages (316 pts).
- **Drop the weak component.** If 90% of clients get 80%+ of leads from ads and landing pages, drop SEO and its writer cost (990 pts, illustrative). Matches the measured gap: agencies that cut services grew 13.0% vs 9.8% for those that added (Promethean 2026).
- **AI-built sales tools as a product:** small website calculators or evaluators sold at $5,000 to $10,000 each (202 pts).

## Recurring revenue

- **Website as a subscription:** $3,800 upfront plus $25 a month hosting, or $0 down and $175 a month with edits, support and hosting; extra page $100, client blog $250, upfront buyers add unlimited edits for $50 a month (55 pts). A WordPress agency reportedly runs about 600 customers at $500 a month (secondhand, 40 pts).
- **Do not just split a project fee over 12 months:** that is an interest-free loan. Lower the upfront price only against a 12-month commitment priced so you are not underwater early (316 pts). Financing with setup fee and 12 or 24 month balance, source code held until paid: about 10% default (194 pts).
- **Retainer before raising project prices:** 2 to 5 retainers remove the pressure to accept cheap one-offs (118 pts).
- **First small order reveals the monthly package:** clients ask for 3 or 4 images first, then reveal ongoing needs (37 pts).
- **Reserved capacity is priced as reserved:** "I have to be paid for the time waiting for you, otherwise I have other clients who will book my time" (136 pts).
- **Prepaid hour blocks in Q4** for clients with use-it-or-lose-it budgets: a returning client asked to be invoiced 40 hours (almost $3,000) before year end (128 pts).
- **Retainer margin check:** below 25% means audit hours vs scope on the last five retainers. One €4,500 retainer priced on 30 hours hit 47 hours by month 3 (15 out of scope); margin fell from 28% to 11% (59 pts).
- **Build-and-hand-over alternative to forever retainers:** fixed 90-day fee, build in the client's own tools, train their operator in months 2 and 3, exit on day 90, optional QA retainer. "You shouldn't be paying an agency forever for this, so here's the version where you stop." Untested (29 pts).

## Performance and risk-reversal structures

- **Ad management fees:** percentage of spend with lower marginal rates above thresholds; or a monthly minimum plus 10 to 15% of ad-attributed revenue (r/PPC, 563 pts); or $1,500 to $2,000 a month plus an agreed fee per lead (r/PPC, 284 pts). One agency raises fees every 3 years; one offers an optional 10% yearly increase in the contract.
- **Revenue share** only with clean attribution, a cap and a base fee. A 100% dropshipper rev-share roster hit $30,000 a month in Q4 and collapsed in Q1 (303 pts).
- **Guarantees:** month-1 paid pilot with an out ("if you don't see [milestone], you don't pay for month 2 and we end on good terms") or an outcome-or-extension (keep working free until [result]). Never an open "until you're happy" (coreyhaines31, MIT).
- **Never a full money-back guarantee on something a platform controls.** A cold email agency refunded 20 clients at $2,600 each, $52,000, when Google filtering changed (r/startups, 270 pts).
- **Define the unit in any lead guarantee as a booked meeting,** not a reply. A $10,000 pilot counted "send me case studies" as leads (30 pts).

## Raising rates

- **New prospects first, while existing clients keep you busy** (4 threads agree). Test on 5 job posts at the new rate; one developer got 2 offers within days at $50 an hour, up from $35 (334 pts).
- **Quote each new prospect 20% above your highest current rate; an instant yes means raise the next quote.** $0.30 to about $1 a word in two years (110 pts, experienced writer).
- **Raise every 2 to 3 successful projects until inbound slows** (135 pts); copywriters report $300 then $350 for the same page after a happy client (225 pts).
- **Existing client raise, anchored to the new-client rate:** 60 days notice, "new clients pay 50%+ more", ask +20%, offer to revisit in summer. Accepted in under an hour (118 pts).
- **Whole-agency raise:** 30% with 60 days notice, no grandfathering, after 6 flat years: lost 6 clients (20%), gained 9, revenue +24.4% year on year. Timed for the clients' busy season, when cash is good and they have no time to shop (47 pts).
- **Upgrade paths beat flat increases:** $75 maintenance clients offered a $300 plan; $350 Meta clients offered $650 Google plus Meta; legacy $500 to $650 could stay or move to $1,000; high-maintenance clients removed. 126 clients went to 109, then back to 114 (134 pts). Forcing everyone up: "most left" (55 pts).
- **Refused raise fallback:** an on-schedule completion bonus (+12.5% after +25% was refused) (122 pts).
- **Expect slower closes after a big jump:** $1,000 a month multi-platform to $2,000+ per platform made prospects deliberate for weeks (200 pts).
- **Measured context:** rate raisers grew 8%, flat 4%, rate cutters fell 6% (Promethean 2025). Discounting is not a growth plan.
- **Returning clients pay the current rate** and often accept more: clients who left and came back paid double (offers-pricing, 105 pts).

## Scope control

- **Write inclusions and revision counts into the quote:** "the $2,000 includes x, y and z" and the number of revision rounds; anything else gets a new estimate (67 pts, lost $2,300 on one project).
- **Two review rounds included;** extra rounds, image sourcing and CMS upload priced per item (132 pts).
- **60-day small-edit window** after launch (font size, footer phone number); new functionality (an opt-in form) is a new project (243 pts).
- **Out-of-scope hourly rate in the agreement** and a verbal OK before extras (194 pts).
- **Label every freebie:** "this is extra, but I'll do it for you, this time", and issue a $0 quote before doing it (67 pts).
- **Paid change order for every out-of-scope request, including quick fixes** (59 pts).
- **Surcharges that stack:** +30% rush with a 2-hour minimum; +30% outside published hours; 6+ hours requested in a day = day rate (808 pts).
- **Premium for hidden costs:** +25% if the client blocks portfolio use (110 pts); bill story sourcing or interviews hourly.
- **Itemize vague engagements:** a client said 320 hours; itemized deliverables came to 50 hours, priced $5,000 (187 pts).
- **All content before production starts** (243 pts).

## Payment terms

- **Deposit before work.** 50% upfront ended ghosting and sped up approvals (243 pts). "I work on a 50% deposit and 50% on completion basis." "For projects under $50 I need payment up front before I start work" (88 pts). 100% upfront under $500 (72 pts).
- **Premium clients: 75% down;** walk if they push back (385 pts).
- **Phase big projects:** $2,500 for 2 weeks of initial work, then $7,500 to finish after approval (448 pts).
- **Prepaid ladder for new consulting clients:** free 30-minute consult, then 1 prepaid hour, then 2, then 4-hour sprints; decline anyone who insists on paying after delivery. Years with no refund requests and no unpaid work (114 pts).
- **Deposits are reversible.** A $15,000 deposit was charged back at completion despite a contract (53 pts). Prefer bank transfer or staged milestones on large jobs, and keep the site unpublished or files unreleased until final payment clears.
- **Bill by time or stage when delivery depends on client inputs;** invoicing only on finished deliverables stretched 4 to 5 week projects toward two months (52 pts).
- **Collect the previous balance plus the new deposit before any scope expansion,** even from good payers (38 pts).
- Contract clause terms (MIT, alirezarezvani contract skill): liability cap 1x contract value standard; termination for cause with 14-day cure, for convenience at 30, 60 or 90 days notice; 30 or 90 day fix warranty; IP is work-for-hire in the US but needs explicit assignment in the EU and UK, and in Germany usage rights (Nutzungsrechte) must be transferred explicitly. Not legal advice; route contracts over the owner's risk threshold to a lawyer.

## Tricks most owners miss

- Ask budget, then fit scope: "Well, full price would be $X, but if we dropped a couple of options we could work with your budget" (88 pts). Conflict: the three-tier method never asks budget (437 pts). Rule: ask on inbound and referral calls; use tiers when you price live without discovery.
- Pre-load zero-cost add-ons into quotes and concede those, not price, when asked for a discount (134 pts).
- Keep the public rate high and negotiate privately against a hard floor ($70 profile, $40 floor) (129 pts).
- Publish prices or ranges: all inbound became warm or hot (94 pts); research shows 31.9% of proposals are first opened on a phone, so the price has to be readable there.
- Temporary discounts only, with a written end date; hard hagglers become the worst clients (655 pts).
