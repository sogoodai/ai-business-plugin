# 90-day plan for a new or stalled agency, studio or freelancer

Built from the research operating plan and practitioner sequencing. Volumes are starting controls where no benchmark exists. Each phase has an exit check; do not move on until it passes or the owner decides to.

## Days 1 to 7: instrument the economics

- Import 12 to 24 months of clients and leads into one sheet: source, service, industry, value, hours, platform fees, tenure, close reason, churn reason, expansions, referrals given.
- Compute effective hourly rate per client, revenue concentration, average deal size, win rate by source, past-client win rate, retainer tenure, margin by service ([economics.md](economics.md)).
- Ask the owner the first-session questions in SKILL.md.

Exit: every client has source, service, niche, revenue and status. The three biggest money gaps are written down.

## Days 8 to 14: choose the wedge

- Score service x niche pairs on margin, proof, urgency, repeatability, findability, expansion and AI compression risk ([positioning.md](positioning.md)).
- Write the positioning sentence; pick one flagship offer and one paid entry offer with fixed scope, price and timeline.
- Set the price floor and raise the price for new prospects if underpriced ([pricing.md](pricing.md)).

Exit: the sentence passes the "competitor can't paste it" test. Homepage, LinkedIn headline, marketplace title and email signature say the same thing.

## Days 15 to 21: build proof

- One detailed case study, one short before and after, one teardown or demo. Where numbers are private, use artifacts and approved quotes.
- A reporting template tied to revenue or pipeline for the flagship offer.
- Ask happy current clients for a testimonial at their last win ([scripts.md](scripts.md)).

Exit: the owner can answer "why you, why now, why this approach, how will we know it worked" without generic claims.

## Days 22 to 30: mine warm demand

- Relationship inventory; individual messages to past clients (last 2 years), lost "not now" deals, former colleagues, partners. Availability post.
- Referral asks to every client at a recent win.
- Marketplace test in parallel only if relevant: one tightly positioned profile or gig, selective bids, Connects and hours logged ([marketplaces.md](marketplaces.md)).

Exit: introductions requested, made, calls, proposals and wins tracked separately. Past clients are their own source cohort.

## Days 31 to 45: outbound and LinkedIn pilots

- 100 to 250 accounts with an observable trigger, one hypothesis per segment, one service per campaign, 3 to 4 touches that add information ([outbound.md](outbound.md)). Low daily volume, every record inspected.
- Founder LinkedIn by hand: highest-fit accounts, substantive comments, trigger-based first messages. The agent researches and drafts.
- Protected acquisition blocks on the calendar (for example Tuesday and Thursday 8:00 to 9:30).

Exit: compare against Instantly 3.43% average reply and 5.5% top quartile, Smarter Outbound 0.79% median and 1.36% top-quartile positive replies per prospect, Expandi 28.5% acceptance and 10.4% message reply. Diagnose list and offer before copy.

## Days 46 to 60: content engine and proposal discipline

- Three content themes: client pattern, teardown, proof. Each substantial piece reused as a post, email, sales attachment and, when search intent exists, a page or video ([content-and-brand.md](content-and-brand.md)).
- No full proposal without problem, trigger, decision process and budget range. Mobile-readable first screen, options, dependencies and change-order terms ([sales-and-proposals.md](sales-and-proposals.md)).
- Reply to inbound within minutes during business hours.

Exit: proposal acceptance tracked by source against the 47.7% agency benchmark (Nusii). Content judged on conversations and assisted deals, not impressions.

## Days 61 to 75: retention system and partners

- Access checklist, baseline and 30-day milestone for every new client; Friday updates; monthly health score; quarterly scope review; next-bottleneck review on every retainer ([retention.md](retention.md)).
- 20 complementary providers with overlapping buyers and different core services: exchange ICPs, triggers and proof; referral terms in writing before the first introduction.

Exit: no engagement starts without baseline, goal and dependency owner. Partner channel tracked on referrals sent, received, qualified, won and collected.

## Days 76 to 90: kill, double or redesign

- Rank every channel on contribution per founder hour and pipeline quality. Stop vanity channels. Double only where at least two funnel stages are healthy.
- Review the service mix for AI compression: offers with rising volume and falling effective rate move up to workflow and outcome work.
- Raise prices on the next renewals if win rate is above 70 to 80% or a waitlist exists.

Exit: one page for the owner with the next quarter's two channels, the price changes, the offers to drop, and the questions only they can answer.

## Weekly dashboard

Qualified opportunities, positive reply rate, meetings held, meeting to proposal, proposal to win by source, average contract value, contribution after channel fees, sales cycle, founder hours per win, expansion revenue, at-risk clients, referral introductions.

## Weekly decision rules

- Cold replies below benchmark: deliverability and targeting first. Replies fine but few positives: the offer or ICP.
- LinkedIn acceptance weak: targeting and profile. Acceptance fine, no conversations: the first message.
- Calls healthy, proposals losing: qualification, differentiation, proof, structure. Price is the winning factor in only 31% of pitches (AgencyAnalytics 2026).
- Clients stay but do not grow: add the next-bottleneck review.
- Volume up, effective rate down: AI commoditization check.
- A channel that needs a terms or law violation to work is not a channel.
