# Tours and activities

Reddit coverage from tour operators is thin (6 threads in r/Tourguide), so this playbook leans on platform documentation from the November 2026 research pass. Treat platform claims as platform claims. Sources in [sources.md](sources.md).

## How tours grow

- A tour is a time slot with a minimum group size. Revenue per departure, not per booking, is the unit.
- Marketplaces (Viator, GetYourGuide, Airbnb Experiences, Tripadvisor) rank on conversion, availability and recent reviews. New products die without the first reviews.
- Direct and local channels (hotels, rentals, Google Things to do, word of mouth) carry the margin; one guide reports doing "reasonably well through word of mouth and direct clients" but almost nothing online (r/Tourguide 17). Both halves matter.

## Platform mechanics

| Platform | What it ranks on | Money | Must-do |
|---|---|---|---|
| GetYourGuide | Revenue, conversion rate, recent reviews, availability | Commission per booking | Inventory a year ahead and bookable until start; cancellations at or under 1.0% and no-shows at or under 0.2% (rolling 90 days) or temporary removal |
| Viator | Relevance (category, places, title, description, intent), competitiveness (reviews, rating, price, availability, performance), popularity (CTR, views, searches, bookings) | $29 one-time per new experience, commission by location | Accelerate buys extra exposure with commission above the minimum; even 1 point helps, but recency and review credibility still weigh |
| Tripadvisor | Popularity Ranking: review quality, recency, enough quantity; "Featured" sort is commercial | Varies | Steady recent reviews; do not equate it with Viator rank |
| Airbnb Experiences | Search, trip planning, emails, in-trip suggestions | 20% host fee | Guests already staying nearby; good for add-on bookings |
| Google Things to do | Feed-based, photos, prices, booking links in Search and Maps | Ads; Things to do campaigns migrating to Search campaigns for Travel from Q3 2026 | Accurate feed via your booking system |

## Launch a new product

1. Write the first screen to answer: what happens, why this version is different, duration, physical demands, meeting point, cancellation terms.
2. Opening hook template (research pass 2026): "Skip the [generic outcome]. In [X hours], you'll [three concrete outcomes], with a maximum of [group size] guests and [differentiating access or credential]."
3. Load 12 months of availability, bookable until close to start time (GetYourGuide says availability affects ranking).
4. Turn on the GetYourGuide launch offer: first 30 days, 20% recommended, "up to 25% more bookings" (platform claim). Treat the discount as the cost of the first reviews ([economics.md](economics.md)).
5. Use Creator Reviews for transparent early reviews (typically posted within 48 hours).
6. Ask every guest at the end, in person, and let the platform request follow.
7. Track: views, conversion, bookings per departure, reviews per 10 guests, cancellations and no-shows.

Platform research: an activity with 3 reviews is 3x more likely to be booked than one with none (GetYourGuide 2026).

## Pricing and commission

- Price so the departure covers fixed cost at a realistic minimum group. Compute break-even pax per departure.
- Accelerate or Visibility-style programs: test on a subset of products or dates; keep only if net contribution after the higher commission beats the control. Bookings alone are the wrong metric.
- Keep direct prices at or above net OTA payout plus what a direct booking saves you; tour guests compare too.

## Reviews and the experience

- Small, specific moments get remembered: a Yellowstone guide gave a child a bear sticker after his first grizzly; the parent still mentions it (r/Tourguide, 3 upvotes).
- Ask in person at the end, the same neutral way for every group, and never tell guests what to write; Google bans requesting specific content (see `local-seo`).
- Remove the reasons for no-shows: confirmation with map pin, meeting-point photo, reminder the day before, a phone number that answers.

## Direct and local

- Rentals and hotels: offer guest-booklet codes paid per redemption ([partnerships.md](partnerships.md)); the helicopter-tour example paid $15 per ticket for $50 off.
- Locals as customers: history and food tours can sell to residents via local groups and posters (r/Tourguide, weak evidence).
- Google Business Profile with real photos, prices and booking link (`local-seo`).

## Mistakes

- Waiting for organic reviews on a new product while it sits unseen. Buy the first reviews with the launch mechanic.
- Loading only a few weeks of availability.
- Cancelling departures for low numbers on GetYourGuide: each counts toward the 1.0% limit. Set minimums and cutoffs in the booking system instead.
- Paying extra commission for exposure without measuring net contribution.

Tricks most owners miss: inventory depth (a year out, bookable until start) is a ranking input on GetYourGuide, not just an operations choice; Tripadvisor's "Featured" sort is paid-influenced while Popularity is review-driven, so they need different work; and the first three reviews are worth paying for.
