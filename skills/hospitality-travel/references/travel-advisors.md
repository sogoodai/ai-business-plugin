# Independent travel advisors and small agencies

The business is commission plus professional fees, sold on expertise in a narrow travel problem. Sources in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Economics first

- Hosted advisors kept 80% of commission on average; 31% kept more than 85%; host fees averaged about $500 a year, about a fifth paid none (Travel Market Report, December 2025).
- Nearly half charge a service fee (TMR 2025); 58% sometimes or always, most common average under $200 (TravelAge West 2025, about 200 advisors, self-selected); 69% of independents in 2024 (HAR, possibly outdated). Full-time experienced independents averaged $78,940 (HAR 2024, possibly outdated).
- Compare hosts on net: split x gross commission minus every fee, plus the host's supplier rates and group inventory ([economics.md](economics.md)). One advisor switched hosts for independently reserved cruise group space beyond the consortium's (r/travelagents 50).
- Luxury changes the math: a mechanic-turned-advisor's biggest check was $800 until a Viking quote showed $4,000+ commission (r/travelagents 29, 2026). An agency owner says $100K in sales is about five couples at their average ticket (r/travelagents 16).

## Niche: sell a decision advantage

Pick a narrow problem with supplier economics, repeat and referral potential, complexity and proof: multi-generational theme parks, destination weddings, expedition cruise, premium air, honeymoons, accessible travel, safari, ski groups, European family itineraries, corporate retreats (research pass 2026).

- Commodity trips lose to big sellers: Costco beat an art-history luxury advisor's Universal quote by $600 plus cash back (r/travelagents 105 thread); Costco offered 12.5% back on a suite (r/travelagents 72, 2026). The same advisor "almost always" wins inside their specialty.
- Diversify destinations: one agency's Disney sales were down about 30% year to date while total sales rose after diversifying (14 upvotes).
- Refundable deposits sell for far-out trips even at a higher price: one advisor's 2027 and 2028 clients all want them (5 upvotes).

## Stop working for free: qualify and charge before research

From the strongest thread in the data (r/travelagents, "Selling Travel is hard these days", 105, 2025):

- **First reply:** what you do, a short personal connection, a questionnaire and your fee. Prospects either return it and ask to pay, or opt out (68 upvotes; 11 agreed).
- **Quoting deposit:** usually $200, collected before quoting, credited if they book through you, kept otherwise. "Weeds out" people who take the research elsewhere (56 upvotes).
- **Consult, then fee, then proposal** (Propose to Close method): Zoom consultation, fee collected before the proposal goes out; ghosting stopped and the workload grew enough to consider an assistant (34 upvotes).
- **Proposal fee credited 100% on acceptance,** kept on ghosting or decline (4 upvotes).
- **Cap revisions in the fee:** one independent charges $100 for up to 8 quotes or changes; another caps at 2 changes after the first proposal. An advisor who agreed a fee but did not collect it before sending the proposal lost both (r/travelagents 21).
- **Release the full itinerary after hotels are booked,** with hotel options carrying perks clients cannot get alone (7 upvotes).
- **Present one package price,** showing the difference between hotel options without itemizing hotel costs; itemizing invited line-by-line comparisons with online prices (28-upvote advice, r/travelagents 21).

Fee wording (research pass 2026):

> My planning fee is $[X]. It covers destination and supplier research, itinerary design, booking management and pre-departure support. Supplier commissions, where paid, are separate and do not reduce your trip price.

> The planning fee for this itinerary is $[X], payable before research begins. It includes up to [N] itinerary revisions and booking management through departure. Changes outside that scope are billed at $[Y].

High end: a $20,000 planning fee on a $145,000 castle and island trip because commission alone did not cover the logistics (r/travelagents 32).

## Handling cheaper quotes

- Compare inclusions out loud: the $500-cheaper Caribbean quote may be Basic Economy, no bags or seats, an 8-hour layover and a worse room (28-upvote comment).
- If you cannot match and they do not need you, say so: "take the better rate, but I can't intervene if that booking goes sideways." A high producer says this builds loyalty (36 upvotes; 6 and 5 agreed).
- Check the hotel's own site and member rate before booking through a supplier portal; a TAAP rate came in $113 above the hotel's direct rate and support would not match (r/travelagents 15; 27-upvote advice: check both, book direct when lower). Use TAAP only for clients who check Expedia themselves (5 upvotes).

## Price drops after booking

Script (24 upvotes, endorsed by a sales practitioner, 18):

> hey, great news! The price has dropped significantly, and we have 2 ways to take advantage. I could get you each a bit of money back, or I could get you all upgraded to way better rooms/perks!

A group specialist (95% groups) always passes drops back and calls it their best word-of-mouth (10 upvotes). Some keep a markup equal to the original commission when repricing (4 upvotes); disclose it.

## Client acquisition

- **Friends first, with commissions explained:** "travel companies pay my commission so you don't" turned friends into first clients who referred others (9 upvotes).
- **Talking-head video:** one advisor films from the office, no resort reels, explaining who they serve and why; top 2% of their host for years with under 1,000 followers (7 upvotes, self-reported).
- **Supplier promos weekly** to Facebook and Instagram Stories from the host's marketing library (5 upvotes).
- **Launch timing:** a warm-weather niche advisor trained 90 to 120 days, then announced on the first bad-weather day in December; "busy ever since" (11 upvotes).
- **Community:** volunteer alongside people, agency email as your contact (5 upvotes); travel stories at checkout lines and hair salons (9 upvotes, untested).
- **Website for credibility, not checkout:** one advisor disabled site booking because an unknown agency's checkout competes with Expedia and Royal Caribbean trust (5 upvotes). Publish examples of trips clients should book themselves next to ones where you add value (4 upvotes).
- **One high-intent asset per niche,** for example "What a $15,000 family trip to Japan actually buys" (research pass 2026).
- **Paid leads:** test small. 50 leads for $1,575; 60% never answered; 10% dead numbers were credited; the vendor's "10% close rate" was a sales claim; prospects expected the advisor to know the Facebook ad they had seen (r/travelagents 44, 2025). Thumbtack: $300, the one serious prospect booked a local agent (r/travelagents 19).
- **Partnerships with established advisors** on a split (see [partnerships.md](partnerships.md)).

## Service that sells more

- Check in during the trip: clients book transfers, seats, bags, upgrades, extra nights and once a second cruise from the first (r/travelagents 26).
- Offer reachable support: one advisor takes collect calls in several languages 24/7 with a fully briefed backup (3 upvotes).
- Workflow that closes on the call: build a geographic itinerary live, check cheaper entry and exit airports, get hotel and room approvals one by one, send the grand total, then card authorization, then the insurance quote from the approved cart (r/travelagents 18).
- Business-class flights through the host's flight desk with a per-ticket client fee ($50) (4 upvotes); avoid economy air early.
- Do not count personal corporate hotel nights as agency revenue; corporate and discounted rates usually pay no commission (4 upvotes).
- Small products for the budget segment: premade itineraries at $5 to $10 with backup options; flat-fee research on a cash or points budget (r/travelagents 23, no sales data).

## Never

- Never do custom research before a fee, deposit or clear qualification.
- Never itemize a package for a price shopper.
- Never sign a noncompete without reading it (r/travelagents 37) and never pick a host on split alone.
- Never buy leads without the source ad and exact inquiry.

Tricks most owners miss: the fee conversation is a qualifier, not a revenue line; passing price drops back is the cheapest referral program in travel; and in-trip check-ins produce add-on bookings at almost no acquisition cost.
