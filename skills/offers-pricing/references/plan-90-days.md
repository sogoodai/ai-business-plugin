# 90-day pricing and offer plan

For an existing business. Each phase ends with an exit check; do not move on until it passes or the owner decides to skip it. Adapted from the research report's execution plan (2026) with practitioner steps. Sources: [sources.md](sources.md).

## Days 1 to 15: get the economics

- Pull 12 months of orders, jobs or invoices. Compute realized average price (after discounts), contribution margin per offer, close or conversion rate, repeat rate, churn (voluntary and involuntary apart), refunds and redos, payment method mix, add-on attach rate per base product.
- Split by acquisition channel: referral and paid-social customers often support different prices.
- Effective processor rate from statements; all marketplace and app fees per order.
- List every active discount, coupon, code, friend rate and legacy price, with who has it and since when. Expire the stale ones.
- Classify each offer: (a) underpriced or capacity-constrained, (b) priced right but poorly packaged, (c) fine core price but weak add-on revenue, (d) retention or payment problem. No discount is launched before this.

Exit check: contribution margin per offer is known; the break-even table for a 10% change is computed; the owner has seen the list of hidden discounts.

## Days 16 to 30: research

- Mystery-shop 3 to 5 competitors; record price, scope, speed, guarantee.
- Interview 5 recent buyers and 5 lost prospects: what they compared, what nearly stopped them, what they expected to pay.
- If there is enough audience: Van Westendorp for the range, then a 5-price Gabor-Granger ladder with 100+ completes per segment.
- Draft three-option quotes or tiers where real use cases differ. Add optional lines to every eligible quote template.
- Value-equation score for the main offer; pick the weakest lever.

Exit check: a candidate new price or package exists with a written hypothesis and the metric that will judge it.

## Days 31 to 45: test the core offer

- New customers only: quote or show the higher price or better package. Services use sequential cohorts; ecommerce and software randomize traffic.
- Add a specific guarantee next to the buy button or on the quote if the main fear is "will it work". Test by device.
- Track profit per lead or visitor, not conversion alone.

Exit check: 4 to 8 weeks of data or 100+ conversions per arm; decision logged.

## Days 46 to 60: expansion revenue

- One adjacent add-on before payment or in the quote; one post-purchase offer where the platform supports it.
- Bundle the most common pair with the saving shown as a line.
- Free-shipping threshold at median order plus one add-on (products).
- Track attach rate per base product weekly.

Exit check: attach rate and contribution per order measured against the 25 to 50% quote-item and 21.6% post-purchase medians.

## Days 61 to 75: recurring revenue and payments

- If there is natural recurrence and spare capacity: pilot a membership or plan with one cohort.
- Subscriptions: onboarding and skip or pause before renewal 2; save flow by stated reason; card updater, retries, dunning window.
- Deposits or card-on-file where no-shows or custom work leak money.
- High tickets: test installments or BNPL, net of the fee premium and lost post-purchase exposure.

Exit check: involuntary churn and no-show leak quantified before and after.

## Days 76 to 90: roll out and make it routine

- Roll the winning price or package to all new customers.
- Model the installed-base migration under 3 churn scenarios; send notices (60 to 90 days for recurring, 30 for others) with the scripts in [scripts.md](scripts.md).
- Put a fixed annual review date in the calendar; add an annual increase clause to new contracts.
- Keep a permanent test log: hypothesis, audience, dates, price or package shown, conversions, revenue and profit per visitor or lead, refunds, cancellations, decision.

Exit check: price change calendar set; every live discount has an owner, a reason and an end date.

## Questions for the owner along the way

1. Which customers would you be relieved to lose?
2. Who may change prices and approve discounts?
3. Which offers will you never run (deep discounts, guarantees, payment plans)?
4. How full is the calendar or how much stock can you sell at the current margin?
5. Which promises have you made publicly (lifetime prices, founding rates) that must be honored?
