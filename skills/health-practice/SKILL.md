# Health practice: answer, book, keep the plan of care

For solo clinicians to clinics of about 20 staff: talk therapists (LPC, LCSW, LMFT, psychologists), chiropractors, physical therapists and physios, general and specialty dentists, dietitians, acupuncturists, optometrists, and other cash-pay clinicians (clinical massage, naturopaths, osteopaths).

Built from 144 practice threads (r/therapists, r/Chiropractic, r/physicaltherapy, r/Dentistry) plus health threads from r/smallbusiness, r/DigitalMarketing and r/FacebookAds, a November 2026 research pass (Invoca, Planet DDS, Henry Schein One, Patient Prism, SimplePractice, Heard, APTA, WebPT, DexCare, Zencare, LocaliQ, Tebra, HHS, CMS, FTC, Google, Meta, Zocdoc, Psychology Today, ICO, AHPRA, OAIC) and the sibling skills. "Operators report" marks a practitioner anecdote with its upvotes; everything else carries its source and year. Most practice data is vendor-owned and dental-heavy: treat it as a prior, build the clinic's own baseline. Sources: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md). The owner's decisions win over this guide, except on the compliance lines in "Never".

This skill does marketing, access and operations. It never gives clinical advice, writes clinical claims the clinician has not approved, or decides a legal question. Flag legal questions to the owner's counsel or licensing board in one line and keep working.

## How private practices actually grow

- **Demand already exists; the clinic leaks it.** Only 54% of healthcare calls reached a person, 45% of qualified phone leads booked on the call, and 43% of organizations never asked the caller to book (Invoca 2026). Dental: 1.45M of 11.55M calls ended without an appointment and 890,000+ never got a callback (Patient Prism, 2025 calls).
- **Speed decides the first visit.** Inquiries with a human reply inside 5 minutes booked at more than 3x the rate of those waiting over 24 hours; only 6% got that reply (1,536 inquiries, 9 specialty practices, 2026, agency data). The average therapist took 28 hours to respond on Zencare (2025).
- **Wait time kills online booking.** About 70 bookings per 100 searches when the wait is under 7 days, 6 per 100 at 30+ days (DexCare 2026, health systems). Dental new-patient lead time averages 25 days, 4.5 at the top 10% (Henry Schein One 2026).
- **Revenue = new patients x visits per case x collected per visit.** Chiropractors call it NP x PVA x OVA (operators report, 70). Moving PVA from 4 to 9 at 30 new patients and $75 a visit is +$11,250 a month with no extra marketing. Most "I need more new patients" problems are PVA or collections problems.
- **Referrers and directories carry most practices before Google Ads.** 90% of recent PT patients were physician-referred, yet 71% of prospective patients explore PT on their own (APTA 2025). Psychology Today is $29.95 a month; Healthgrades is free for any NPI.
- **Payer mix is a marketing decision.** Therapy self-pay averages $139.75 vs $99.75 insurance (SimplePractice 2025), but cash-only therapists spend much of the gap on marketing (Heard 2026). Insurance buys demand; cash buys margin and costs acquisition.
- **Compliance is part of the funnel.** Pixels on booking pages, review replies and testimonials have produced HHS settlements ($23,000 to $30,000) and Meta now cuts health-flavored conversion signals. A clinic that copies an e-commerce playbook loses both data and money.

## First session: ask, then compute

Ask the owner:

1. Profession, license type, country and state or province; solo, group or multi-site; telehealth share.
2. Software (SimplePractice, TherapyNotes, Jane, WebPT, ChiroTouch, Dentrix, Open Dental, Denticon, Weave, NexHealth) and whether you can export 12 months of inquiries, appointments, cancellations, no-shows, visits and collections.
3. Payer mix: which plans, Medicare, Medicaid, out-of-network, cash rates, platforms (Headway, Alma, Grow, Rula, Zocdoc, BetterHelp).
4. Who answers the phone, forms, directory messages and texts, and how fast; what happens after hours.
5. Current no-show and late-cancel policy, card on file, and payer contracts that limit fees.
6. Directories and listings in use, with cost; current referral sources by name.
7. Google rating, review count, how reviews are requested, and who writes replies.
8. Every tracking tag on the site and booking flow (Meta pixel, GA4, Google Ads tag, TikTok, chat widgets), and whether any vendor signed a BAA.
9. Offers they will and will not run; which clinical claims the clinician approves; who may change prices.

Then compute ([references/economics.md](references/economics.md)):

```
answer rate = calls answered live / inbound calls (business hours and after hours separately)
speed to lead = minutes from inquiry to first two-way human reply (autoresponders do not count)
inquiry to attended first visit = attended first visits / qualified inquiries, by source
new-patient lead time = days from request to first available appropriate slot
show rate = attended / scheduled; cancellation and no-show tracked separately
recovered cancellation rate = cancelled slots refilled / cancelled slots
visits per case (PVA) = visits in period / new patients (quick) or / unique patients (true)
collected per visit (OVA) = collections / visits
plan-of-care completion = cases reaching planned discharge or goal / cases started
attended-patient CAC = channel spend / attended new patients from that channel
contribution per clinical hour = net collected - billing cost - expected denial write-off - acquisition cost per visit
```

Report the three biggest gaps in money, not percentages.

## Decision rules, in order

1. **Phones and inbox before traffic.** Answer rate under 80% in business hours, or median two-way reply over 15 minutes, gets fixed first: overflow line, missed-call text-back, one owner per inbox, after-hours self-booking. Every qualified inquiry ends in one of four states: booked, booked later plus waitlist, waiting on insurance or referral info, lost with a reason. "Will call back" is not a state.
2. **Ask for the appointment.** Two concrete times, every call ("Tuesday at 10 or Thursday at 4?"). 43% of organizations skip this (Invoca 2026).
3. **Lead time under 7 days for new patients.** Hold protected new-patient slots each week. If lead time drifts past 14 days, move money from acquisition to capacity (hours, associate, hygienist, telehealth block).
4. **Insurance answer in one message.** Publish accepted plans as text on the site, Google, directories and intake. Insurance was a barrier 11x as often as price on dental calls (Patient Prism 2025).
5. **Cancellation recovery over punishment.** Reminders with one-tap reschedule, waitlist backfill, card on file where allowed. Fees only where payer contracts and law allow: Medicare permits a missed-appointment fee applied equally to all patients; Medicaid patients generally cannot be charged.
6. **Book the plan of care, not the next visit.** Schedule the clinically expected visits before the patient leaves visit 1 or 2; pre-appoint dental hygiene at checkout. Track early dropout separately from successful early discharge.
7. **Recall and reactivation before new ads.** Segment by reason (overdue recall, unfinished treatment, cancelled and never rebooked, plan-of-care dropout). Factual reminder first, no discount. A 1,000-contact dormant dental list booked 15 appointments in 2 weeks (operators report, 131).
8. **Reviews: same neutral ask to every eligible patient, never confirm a patient in a reply.** Therapists also follow their code: APA 5.05, ACA C.3.b and NBCC bar soliciting testimonials from current clients and anyone vulnerable to undue influence.
9. **Tracking audit before any paid campaign.** No ad pixel on booking, intake, portal or condition pages without a BAA or a de-identified setup. No health-condition audience or event names anywhere.
10. **Ads last, judged on attended patients and 90-day contribution.** Google Search on high-intent service and location terms first; Meta only with real clinic video and a privacy-safe conversion. A median mental-health search lead ($141.17) can lose money if few become retained clients.

## Directory economics in one screen

- **Psychology Today:** $29.95 a month, no paid placement; sort uses relevance, completeness, preferences, engagement and rotation (Psychology Today 2026). One retained client pays for years. Open with problem, approach and availability, not biography. Measure fit inquiries and attended intakes, not views.
- **Zocdoc:** one-time fee per new-patient booking, $30 to $140 by specialty and location, charged at booking even if the patient no-shows (Zocdoc 2026). Sponsored Results bid on top. Max bid = expected contribution x acceptable marketing share - booking fee - expected no-show loss. Fill insurance, visit reasons and near-term availability before bidding.
- **Healthgrades:** free profile for any NPI; claim it, fix NPI-derived specialty and address, add photo. "Twice the appointment requests" for complete profiles is a vendor claim without method. Buy sponsorship only with booking-source tracking.
- **Insurance platforms for therapists (Headway, Alma, Grow, Rula):** credentialing and billing in exchange for a cut, a fee or a negotiated rate; some also send referrals. Compare net per session and referral volume against direct credentialing (3 to 6 months per payer).
- **Volume marketplaces (BetterHelp-style):** a therapist reported $30 to $70 an hour by volume tier and $10 for a late cancel, and referrals stopping after a year (operators report, 739). Use only as a bridge, never as the caseload.

Platform detail, profile copy and referral programs: [references/acquisition.md](references/acquisition.md).

## Insurance, cash or hybrid

- Compute contribution per clinical hour by payer and CPT code, not "insurance vs cash." A low-rate plan that fills empty hours can beat a high-rate plan with denials and slow pay.
- Heard 2026: cash-only therapists about $94,000 revenue and $58,000 profit vs $79,000 and $55,000 insurance-accepting; 74.6% accept insurance. The revenue gap largely went to marketing.
- An in-network therapist reports over six figures gross at under 20 sessions a week, billing everything herself, with Medicaid among the most consistent referrals (operators report, 733).
- Out of network: offer a superbill, never promise reimbursement. US self-pay patients booking 3+ business days ahead get a written Good Faith Estimate (No Surprises Act); disputes open at $400 over the estimate (CMS 2026).
- Cash discounts: a time-of-service discount around 5 to 15% tied to real billing savings is the defensible range; routine 40 to 50% cuts read as a dual fee schedule (trade-law commentary). Never charge insured patients more than cash patients for the same service.
- Leaving a plan: give notice per contract, tell affected patients early with their options, and model who stays at your cash or out-of-network rate. A dentist left Delta Dental after 3 to 6% fee raises on a few codes; his state's Medicaid paid better (operators report, 271).

## When practitioners and the data disagree

| Topic | Practitioners say | Data says | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phone screening therapy clients | Screen every caller before booking (1,514) | Bookable consults complete 73.7% vs 40.4% for message intake (Zencare 2025) | Self-book a 15-minute consult; screen on that call, not in a reply chain |
| Free exams and consults | Low exam fee filled a micro chiro practice (65); giving advice free taught nothing (25) | Federal beneficiary inducement limit is $15 per item, $75 a year (OIG 2016) | Low paid exam or free consult for cash patients; never a free exam advertised to Medicare or Medicaid patients |
| Care plans | 12 to 18 visit plans feel "salesie" to patients (25); scripted 4 to 6 month plans for everyone are a cult (43) | WebPT's 10 visits per case is a vendor target to read with outcomes | Plan by diagnosis and goals, re-exam on a date, report early dropout and early success separately |
| More new patients | "I need more NPs" | Same NP count, PVA 4 to 9 = +$135,000 a year (operators report, 39) | Fix PVA and OVA before buying NPs |
| Cash is better | Go cash, escape insurers | Cash-only profit only about $3,000 higher (Heard 2026) | Model by payer after CAC and vacancy |
| Ads | Agencies "start good, then fade" (23) | Lead CPL is not attended CAC | Own the account; report cost per attended patient monthly |

## Never

- Never confirm or deny that someone is a patient in a public review reply, social comment or ad. Manasa Health Center paid $30,000 (2023) and New Vision Dental $23,000 (2022) for review replies.
- Never publish a testimonial, patient photo or "success story" with identifiable health information without a signed HIPAA marketing authorization (Complete P.T. paid $25,000). A public review is not an authorization.
- Never gate or incentivize reviews, and never solicit testimonials from current therapy clients.
- Never place ad pixels or analytics that send booking, intake, portal or condition-page data to a vendor without a BAA or a validated de-identified setup; a cookie banner is not HIPAA authorization.
- Never name audiences or conversions after conditions, upload patient lists to ad platforms, or retarget by condition.
- Never advertise a free exam, waived copay or gift above $15 to Medicare or Medicaid patients, and never charge insured patients more than cash patients.
- Never charge Medicaid patients missed-appointment fees, or any fee a payer contract forbids.
- Never promise outcomes, cures or reimbursement; never use clinical testimonials in Australian advertising (AHPRA).
- Never send PHI by ordinary SMS or email marketing tools; first messages ask for plan name, never member ID or symptoms.
- Never invent a benchmark, a clinical claim, an offer the owner has not approved or a patient quote.
