# Scripts

Adapt names, times, plans and prices. Short and warm. Messages never claim a person wrote them if they didn't, never include diagnosis or symptom detail in SMS or ordinary email, never promise outcomes or reimbursement, and never use an offer the owner has not approved. "Operators report" lines carry their thread upvotes; unmarked scripts are built from the cited evidence and are not tested verbatim.

## First reply to an inquiry

**Text or email, any specialty:**
"Thanks for reaching out to [Practice]. I can help you find a time. Are you looking for [service] in person at [location] or by video? If you're using insurance, just send the plan name (no member ID or health details here). Our next new-patient openings are [day time] and [day time], or you can book here: [link]."

**Missed call text-back:**
"Sorry we missed your call at [Practice]. Reply here with a good time to call you back, or book directly: [link]."

**Voicemail greeting:**
"You've reached [Practice]. To book or check availability any time, go to [short link]. Leave your name and number and we'll call you back within [15 minutes during office hours / first thing tomorrow]. If this is an emergency, call 911." (Therapists add the 988 line in the US or local crisis line.)

**Autoresponder that is honest about timing (solo clinician):**
"Thanks for your message. I'm in session most of the day and reply to every inquiry by [time]. If you'd like to skip the wait, you can book a free 15-minute consult here: [link]."

## Intake call (front desk, chiro, PT, dental, acupuncture, optometry)

1. "Thanks for calling [Practice], this is [name]. How can I help?"
2. Listen, then reflect in one line: "So your lower back has been bothering you for a few weeks and you'd like to get it looked at."
3. "Have you been to us before?" (existing vs new)
4. Insurance in the first minute: "Are you planning to use insurance? Which plan?" Then one of:
   - In network: "Good news, we're in network with [plan]. We'll check your benefits before your visit and tell you what to expect to pay."
   - Out of network: use the superbill script below.
   - Self-pay: use the cash-pay price explanation below.
5. Ask for the appointment: "Based on what you've told me, the next new-patient opening is [time A] or [time B]. Which works better?" (43% of organizations never ask, Invoca 2026.)
6. Neither works: "I can add you to our cancellation list and also hold [later slot] so you have something confirmed. Would you like both?"
7. Confirm: "You're booked with [clinician] on [day] at [time]. I'm texting you the forms and directions now; filling them in before you arrive saves about 15 minutes. Parking is [where]."
8. Lost: record the reason from the fixed list.

PT direct access add-on: "In [state] you don't need a referral to start physical therapy. If your plan requires one, we'll tell you when we check your benefits."

## Therapy consult call

Fifteen minutes. The clinician's own risk-screen protocol comes first and is not in this script.

1. "Thanks for setting this up. I'll ask a few questions so we can both see if I'm the right fit, and then you can ask me anything. Sound OK?"
2. "What's bringing you to therapy right now?" Listen. "What would be different in your life if this went well?"
3. Logistics: "Sessions are [50] minutes, [weekly] to start, [in person / video]. I'm [in network with X / out of network, fee $Y]. Does that work for you?"
4. Fit statement, specific: "What you're describing, [paraphrase without labels], is a lot of what I work on. I use [approach] and we'd start with [first focus]."
5. Close: "I have [day time] or [day time] for a first full session. Which works?"
6. Hesitation: "Totally fine to think about it. I'll hold [slot] until [tomorrow at noon] so you don't lose it, and you can let me know by text."
7. Not a fit: "I don't think I'm the best person for this, and I'd rather you get the right help quickly. Two colleagues who [specialty] and have openings are [name, contact] and [name, contact]."

## Consult close (chiro, PT, acupuncture report of findings)

Order: goal, findings, plan, measure, money, booking. Lead with the patient's goal to avoid sounding "salesie" (operators report, 25).

"You told me your goal is [lift your daughter / run again / sleep through the night]. Here's what I found: [plain-language finding]. What I recommend is [n] visits over [weeks], then we re-check on [date] using [the same tests we did today] so you can see whether it's working. If it isn't, we'll change the plan or I'll refer you. Your cost per visit is [$], [or: your plan covers it after a $ copay]. Let's book the first [two weeks] now so you get the times you want: [options]."

If "I need to think about it": "Of course. What's the main thing you want to think through, the time, the cost, or whether this will help?" Answer that one thing. Then: "Would it help to start with just the first [3] visits and decide at the re-check?"

Never: scare language about permanent damage, a contract for months of care, or prepaid packages sold at visit 1 to federal beneficiaries.

## Cash-pay price explanation

**Plain fee, said with confidence:**
"Our first visit is [$], which includes the full evaluation and your first treatment. Follow-up visits are [$]. We give you a receipt you can submit to your HSA or FSA [and a superbill if you'd like to try out-of-network reimbursement]."

**Why cash, if asked:**
"We're out of network so we can spend [45] minutes with you one-on-one and plan care around what you need rather than what a plan authorizes. Most people finish in about [n] visits, so the total is usually around [$ range]. We'll tell you at your re-check whether you need more."

**Package or course (acupuncture, dietitian, cash PT):**
"Most people do a course of [6] sessions. Paid as you go it's [$] each; if you book the course upfront it's [$] total, and you can pause it any time with [24] hours' notice."

**Good Faith Estimate (US, self-pay booking 3+ business days out):**
"Because you're paying directly, you'll get a written Good Faith Estimate of the expected cost before your visit. If your bill ends up $400 or more above it, you have the right to dispute it."

**Price question as the first question:** answer it with the number and the next step. Never "If price is your first question we're not a fit" (see `fitness`, same failure).

## Out of network and superbill

"We're out of network with your plan. We can give you an itemized superbill after each visit that you can submit for possible out-of-network reimbursement. Your insurer, not us, decides whether it's covered and how much they pay. Before booking, you might ask your plan: Do I have out-of-network benefits for outpatient [mental health / physical therapy] with CPT code [90837 / 97110]? What's my deductible and how much is met? What's the allowed amount? How do I submit a claim?"

## Insurance answer when you are in network

"Yes, we're in network with [plan]. We'll verify your benefits before your first visit and let you know your expected copay or coinsurance in writing, so there are no surprises."

## Reminders

- **48 to 24 hours:** "Reminder: [first name], you're booked with [clinician] at [Practice] on [day] at [time]. Reply C to confirm or use [link] to change it. [Address / video link]."
- **Morning of:** "See you today at [time] at [Practice]. Parking: [where]. Running late? Reply here."
- **1 hour (dental case reports this third touch cut no-shows 30%, operators report, 131):** "See you at [time]. If you can't make it, reply and we'll offer your time to someone on our waitlist."
- **Waitlist offer:** "A [time] opening just came up with [clinician] [today/tomorrow]. Reply YES to take it. First to confirm gets it."
- **Missed visit, same day:** "We missed you today. Would [time A] or [time B] work instead? Or pick a time here: [link]."

## No-show and cancellation policy

"Please give us at least 24 hours' notice if you need to change an appointment. Late cancellations and missed appointments may be charged [$ amount] where permitted by law and your insurance agreement. To change a visit, use [link] or reply to any reminder so we can offer the time to another patient. We understand emergencies happen and review those individually."

First instance waiver: "No problem this time. Just so you know, from here on our 24-hour policy applies. Would [time] work to reschedule?"

Medicaid patients: remove the fee sentence for them.

## Recall and reactivation texts

- **Dental recall at checkout:** "You're due for your next cleaning around [month]. I can reserve [day time] now and you can move it later if needed."
- **Recall due:** "Hi [first name], it's time for your [cleaning and checkup / annual eye exam] at [Practice]. Book here: [link] or reply with a day that suits you. Reply STOP to opt out."
- **Unfinished treatment (factual):** "Our records show your recommended follow-up with [clinician] has not been scheduled yet. If you'd like to continue, you can choose a time here: [secure link]. Questions? Call [number]. Reply STOP to opt out."
- **Plan-of-care dropout (from the clinician):** "Hi [first name], it's [clinician]. I noticed we haven't seen you since [date]. How are you doing? If you'd like to pick up where we left off, I have [day time] or [day time]."
- **Cancelled, never rebooked:** "We still have you on our list for a new time with [clinician]. Want [day time] or [day time]?"
- **Contact lens run-out:** "Your contact lens supply from [month] is likely running low. Reorder here: [link], or book your annual exam: [link]."

Never name the condition or procedure in these texts.

## HIPAA-safe review request

Dental, PT, chiro, optometry, dietitian, acupuncture (not therapy; see [reviews-and-compliance.md](reviews-and-compliance.md#1-review-requests-all-countries)). Sent to every eligible patient the same way:

"Thank you for visiting [Practice]. If you'd like to share your experience, you can leave an honest review here: [Google link]. Positive, negative or neutral feedback is all welcome. Please don't include any private health details in a public review. Reply STOP to opt out."

In person, same words for everyone at discharge: "If you have a minute, we'd appreciate an honest review on Google. There's a link in the text we'll send you."

## Review replies

Same structure for every review. Never confirm the person is a patient.

- **Positive:** "Thank you for taking the time to share this. Our team works hard to make every visit [welcoming / on time / clear], and we appreciate the kind words."
- **Negative:** "Thank you for sharing feedback. We take concerns about service seriously, but privacy rules prevent us from discussing any individual's care publicly. Please contact our office at [number] and ask for [role] so we can look into this."
- **Wait time or billing complaint:** "Thank you for the feedback. We're always working on [wait times / billing clarity]. Privacy rules keep us from discussing anyone's care here, but please call [number] and ask for [role]."
- **Clearly fake or not a patient:** do not say "you were never our patient." Flag it to Google; reply with the negative template if needed.

## Referrer outreach

**Capacity sheet cover note (PT, chiro, dietitian, therapy):**
"We currently have new-patient availability for [conditions or populations], accept [plans], and can usually see appropriate referrals within [current lead time]. Referrals can be sent via [secure route]. We'll confirm receipt within one business day and, with the patient's authorization, keep you updated on progress and discharge. Direct contact: [name, phone]."

**Quarterly update:** "Quick update from [Practice]: new-patient lead time is now [n] days, we've added [Saturday hours / pelvic health / Spanish-speaking clinician], and we're now in network with [plan]. Thank you for the [n] referrals this quarter."

**Therapist to peer:** "I have two openings on [days] for [population]. If you're full and have someone who'd fit, I'd be glad to help. And if you have space for [population I don't serve], I'd love to send people your way."

## Workplace introduction

Existing patient, after a good visit (operators report, 27): "Thank you for letting me take care of you. I bet you have others at your office dealing with the same thing. Do you know who would be a good person to talk to, for me to see what I can do to help?" Target HR, morale or wellness committees.

## Networking invitations

Phone, to insurance agents, realtors, bankers and photographers (operators report, 27): "Hi, I'm Dr. [name] and I'm getting some local business owners together to learn about each other's businesses and help each other grow. Would you like to join us?" Goal 20 calls a day or until 20 no's.

Walk-in with food: "Hi, I'm [name] from [Practice] down the street. We just wanted to say hello to our neighbors. If anyone here ever has [back, neck, jaw] trouble, here's how to reach us." Twenty seconds, then leave unless they ask questions.

## Leaving an insurance plan (patient notice)

"As of [date], [Practice] will no longer be in network with [plan]. You can keep seeing [clinician] as an out-of-network patient at [$] per visit, and we'll provide a superbill for possible reimbursement. If you'd prefer an in-network provider, we're happy to help you find one and transfer your records. Your care won't be interrupted before [date]."

## Ad hooks (approve every claim with the clinician)

- Chiro, PT: "[City] desk workers: 3 stretches I give almost every patient with neck pain" (video, real clinician). "New patient exam [$], same-week appointments" (cash patients only; exclude Medicare and Medicaid).
- Dental: "Same-day dental emergencies in [town]. Call by 10 a.m." / "In network with [plans]. New patients seen this week." / Procedure-specific with the clinic's real advantage (operators report, 131: tripled conversion vs generic).
- Therapy: "Therapy for [specific population] in [state], evening appointments, [plans] accepted." No symptom questions aimed at the viewer ("Struggling with depression?") on Meta; personal-attribute assertions get rejected.
- Dietitian: "Covered by many insurance plans: meet a registered dietitian by video this week."
- Never: "cure," "pain-free," before/after without consent, testimonials about outcomes in Australia.
