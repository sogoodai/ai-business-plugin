# Retention, plan of care, recall and reactivation

The clinically defensible model is not "maximize visits." It is prescribed visits > scheduled > attended > appropriate completion or discharge. Report early self-discharge (dropout) separately from successful early discharge. Pushing visit counts past need creates poor care, payer scrutiny and bad reviews.

## Plan-of-care completion (PT, chiro, acupuncture, dietitian, therapy)

**Measure:**
```
completion rate = cases reaching planned discharge or goal / cases started (cohort by start month)
dropout by visit = share of cases whose last visit was visit n, n = 1..12
```
The dropout curve shows where the leak is. A drop after visit 1 is an exam and explanation problem; after visit 3 is usually expectations, cost or scheduling.

**Why cases fall apart (from a multi-clinic chiro owner's PVA post, operators report, 39):**
- Day zero: hard to book, unanswered phone, no confirmation, clipboard paperwork on arrival.
- Day one: dated, dusty office; nobody explains what happens; patient feels processed.
- Middle: no progress measure, so the patient decides "better enough" or "not working."
- Fix list: easy booking and confirmations, forms before arrival, a clean office, a plain explanation at visit 1, a measurable goal, a scheduled re-exam with results shown, and the next block booked at the re-exam.

**Tactics:**
- Book the first 2 to 3 weeks before the patient leaves visit 1 (PT, chiro, acupuncture).
- Re-exam on a date written in the plan; show the measure (pain scale, ROM, Oswestry or NDI, PHQ-9 or GAD-7 where the clinician uses them).
- Missed-visit trigger: same-day text offering two times, call at 2 missed visits.
- Goals must be quantifiable and current (a chiro auditor's documentation checklist, operators report, 29). The same goals drive retention messaging and payer documentation.
- Cost clarity up front: total estimate, insurance share, what happens if coverage runs out.
- Do not run scare-based "Day 2" reports, everyone-gets-the-same 4 to 6 month $2,000 to $3,000 plans, or lifetime weekly maintenance by default; operators describe these as manipulative even in 4.9-star offices (operators report, 43), and they invite payer and board risk.
- Maintenance or wellness care is a separate, opt-in offer after discharge, not part of the acute plan.

Benchmarks: WebPT recommends 10 visits per completed PT case as a target to read with outcomes (2025). UK chiro episodes average about 11 sessions with 77.8% rebooking (operators report, 15; survey unverified).

## Dental recall

- Pre-appoint the next hygiene visit at checkout: "You're due again around [month]. I can reserve a time now and you can move it later." Planet DDS: 65% hygiene reappointment (2025), up from 59%; HS1 top 10% reach 92% (2026).
- Unscheduled recall sequence: digital reminder at the due date, second at +2 weeks, call at +4 weeks, then monthly unscheduled-treatment list.
- Unscheduled treatment list: every accepted-but-not-scheduled and diagnosed-but-not-accepted item, worked weekly by a named person. Planet DDS: 56% acceptance, 46% completion.
- A patient who skips recalls for years and then breaks a tooth often blames the dentist (operators report, 245). Document recall attempts; it protects the relationship and the record.

## Optometry recall

- Annual or per-clinician interval recall, plus contact lens supply reminders timed to box run-out.
- Diabetic patients: recall plus a report to the PCP (a referral source).
- Optical capture: hand the prescription to an optician in the same visit, show the patient's vision benefit dollars, and send a 7-day "your glasses benefit is still unused" reminder. Independent capture of 50 to 70% is called healthy (Review of Optometry); definitions vary.

## Therapy caseload stability

- Standing weekly slots reduce cancellations and admin.
- Track sessions per client and the share of clients past session 4 and session 12, by referral source. A source that sends many 1 to 2 session clients is a poor source at any CPL.
- Planned termination is success; ask for nothing at termination except permission to keep them on a check-in list if they want.
- Never solicit testimonials from current clients (APA 5.05, ACA C.3.b) and do not market to former clients in ways that exploit the relationship.

## Reactivation queues

Segment by reason; never send one "We miss you" blast.

| Queue | Who | First message | Second | Stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overdue recall | Past due date, no appointment | Factual due reminder with link | +2 weeks reminder | after 3 touches or opt-out |
| Unfinished treatment | Accepted or diagnosed, not completed | "Our records show your recommended follow-up has not been scheduled" | Call from the clinical team | patient declines |
| Cancelled, never rebooked | Cancellation with no new date in 14 days | Two times or link | Call | 2 touches |
| Plan-of-care dropout | Missed 2+ planned visits | Check-in from the clinician | Re-exam offer | 2 touches |
| Former patient, new need | Discharged 6+ months | Seasonal or general check-in where consent allows | none | opt-out |

Rules: no discount on the first touch; factual and tied to the existing care relationship; consent and channel rules per jurisdiction (`email-sms-loyalty`); no condition named in SMS; honor STOP immediately.

Evidence: dental dormant leads, 1,000 contacts, 15 bookings in 2 weeks (operators report, 131). A 13-year chiro with a shrinking wellness base had never run a reactivation campaign before trying ads (operators report, 23): run it first, it costs almost nothing.

Personal reach-outs beat automation for long-time patients: a 25-year chiro keeps patients for decades by contacting them personally (operators report, 86).

## Memberships and wellness plans

- Dental in-house plans: for uninsured patients, 2 cleanings, exams, X-rays and a discount on treatment for a monthly or annual fee. Vendor retention claims (85 to 93% vs 41 to 55% uninsured) conflict and are unverified: pilot and measure your own.
- Chiro wellness memberships (monthly fee for a set number of visits) after discharge from active care. A 13-year DC considered switching from care plans to Joint-style memberships at a lower price (operators report, 23). Check state rules on prepaid care and never sell memberships to Medicare beneficiaries for covered services.
- Acupuncture and dietitian programs: a defined course (for example 6 or 8 sessions) at a package price, paid upfront or monthly, with a re-assessment at the end.
- Model contribution per clinician hour after redemptions, not just revenue.
