# By sub-model

Each section: how it grows, the numbers that matter, the levers in order, the traps. Benchmarks with sources in [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md).

## Talk therapy, solo (LPC, LCSW, LMFT, psychologist)

**Grows through:** Psychology Today and other directories, insurance panels or platforms (Headway, Alma, Grow, Rula), peer and physician referrals, a niche.

**Numbers:** self-pay $139.75 vs insurance $99.75 per session, 58.8% of sessions insurance-funded (SimplePractice 2025). $100,000+ take-home usually needs 28 to 35 clients a week, all insurances, and 10 to 15 hours a week of business development; only 20 to 40% of interviewed therapists got there (recruiter, operators report, 968). Counter-example: $80,000 net at under 20 sessions a week, credentialed with almost all plans plus Medicare and Medicaid, billing herself (733).

**Levers in order:**
1. Reply speed and a self-booked 15-minute consult (28-hour average reply on Zencare; 73.7% vs 40.4% completion).
2. Niche Psychology Today profile with problem-first opening lines and current availability.
3. Payer decision by contribution per hour: start in network with 2 to 4 plans that fill the caseload, add cash slots at a higher rate as demand allows.
4. Evening and weekend slots (availability sorts and converts).
5. Peer referral swaps: a list of colleagues with openings in specialties you don't serve.
6. Groups for a defined population (a beta gardening therapy group filled, operators report, 889).

**Traps:** soliciting testimonials from clients (APA 5.05, ACA C.3.b); BetterHelp-style platforms as the core caseload; no cancellation policy (whole days lost, 886 and 966); starting solo private practice before licensure and experience (operators report, 963).

**Messaging:** concrete method and population, not "I help people with anxiety and depression." "Psychologist teaching you the exact CBT techniques I use in session" (operators report, 225).

## Talk therapy, group practice

- The intake coordinator is the sales team: measure their speed, consult-to-intake rate and routing by clinician availability.
- Route inquiries to clinicians with open slots, not the owner's favorite.
- Each clinician gets a Psychology Today profile and a site page; the group page is the umbrella.
- Clinician retention is acquisition: associates leave for Headway, Alma or solo practice when splits feel unfair (r/therapists thread on group owners, 845). A 60/40 split with full admin support was acceptable to one clinician (963). Losing a clinician loses their caseload's referrals.
- Track fill rate per clinician weekly; market the specific empty clinician ("Dr. X has Tuesday evening openings for teens").

## Chiropractic

**Grows through:** Google profile and reviews, local networking and community events, video ads with a low-barrier exam (cash), referral partners (massage, trainers, stylists, doulas), care plans and wellness memberships.

**Numbers:** 138 patients and 7 new patients a week, $450,425 collections (Chiropractic Economics 2025, survey). Track NP, PVA, OVA monthly (operators report, 70). UK: 77.8% rebooking, 11.9% cancellation, 8.2% DNA, insurance adds only 17% revenue (survey, operators report, 15). A May surge appears in the UK data (gardening and DIY season); plan content and ads before it.

**Levers in order:**
1. OVA: verify benefits, code correctly, collect at time of service. Low OVA burns out docs (operators report, 70).
2. PVA: book the first block at visit 1, re-exam with measures, missed-visit follow-up.
3. Reactivation of past patients (a 13-year DC had never done it, 23).
4. Local networking groups, neighbor walk-ins, partner lunches (27).
5. Video of real sessions plus a low paid exam fee for cash patients (56 then 80 new patients a month in a $8,000 micro practice, 65).
6. Paid ads managed against attended CAC.

**Traps:** free exams advertised to Medicare and Medicaid patients; dual fee schedules; scripted high-pressure reports and identical long plans for everyone (43, 45); expensive equipment that collects dust (86); four agencies in a row without owning the data (23); competing as one of 50 clinics in 5 miles without a reason to choose you (23). Personal outreach to long-term patients outlasts gadgets (25-year DC, 86).

## Physical therapy

**Grows through:** physician referrals (90% of recent patients), direct access, gyms and trainers, Google, payer contracts, cash niches (pelvic health, sports, vestibular, running).

**Numbers:** PT search CPL $32.79 at 15.35% CVR, the cheapest of the health categories in LocaliQ's 2025 data. WebPT target 10 visits per case. Large operator net revenue $105.76 per visit (USPh FY2025).

**Levers in order:**
1. Referral received to attended eval: call the patient the same day the referral arrives; 28% of referred prospects never start (APTA 2025).
2. Capacity sheet and closed-loop notes to every referrer.
3. Book the plan before the patient leaves visit 1.
4. Direct access messaging for your state (all 50 states allow some form; 21 unrestricted, APTA).
5. Google Search on condition plus location.
6. Gym and trainer partnerships (fair market rent, no referral payments).

**Traps:** inaccessible location or no parking (268); percentage-of-revenue rent beyond the first 3 to 4 months (268); visit-count targets without outcomes.

**Starter economics (operators report, 268):** rent one room at about $700 a month or less in a community center or gym with parking; cap percentage rent at 3 to 4 months.

## Dental

**Grows through:** phones, hygiene recall, insurance visibility, Google profile and reviews, Search Ads, emergency availability, cosmetic and implant procedures on Meta, in-house membership plans for the uninsured.

**Numbers:** 47 new patients a month, 65% hygiene reappointment, 56% acceptance, 46% plan completion, 14.4% cancellation, 6.2% no-show (Planet DDS 2025); 25-day new-patient lead time vs 4.5 at top 10%, 80% vs 97% collection rate (HS1 2026); dental phone leads book 53% on the call (Invoca 2026); search CPL $84.77.

**Levers in order:**
1. Phones: answer rate, missed-call callbacks, scripted insurance answer, ask for the booking.
2. New-patient lead time under 7 days; daily emergency slots.
3. Pre-appoint hygiene at checkout; unscheduled-treatment list worked weekly.
4. Treatment plan phasing and financing in writing; schedule phase 1 before checkout.
5. Insurance and membership plan visibility on site and Google.
6. Search Ads for emergency, insurance and procedure terms; Meta for high-value procedures with real clinic video (operators report, 131).

**Payer moves:** some procedures lose money at insurance rates (an extraction kept $30 to $35 of a $90 reimbursement vs $115 for an exam and PA, operators report, 164); a dentist left Delta Dental after 3 to 6% raises on a few codes (271). A retired dentist ran an all-cash practice for 15 years on complex TMD and full-mouth cases, three days a week with three staff (337). Before dropping a plan, model how many patients stay at cash or out-of-network rates.

**Traps:** courtesy discounts that invite disputes (a $1,200 discount on four crowns still drew an overcharging accusation, operators report, 393); production quotas on financing applications and reviews (226); ads with a 25-day wait.

## Dietitian and nutrition

**Grows through:** insurance (in many US plans, medical nutrition therapy is covered for some diagnoses; Medicare covers MNT for diabetes and kidney disease), insurance-billed platforms (Nourish, Fay), physician and GLP-1 prescriber referrals, niche content (PCOS, IBS, sports, eating disorders with a therapist team), employer programs.

**Numbers:** thin public data. Nourish lists $145 self-pay and says 94% of in-network patients pay $0 (2026, platform claim).

**Levers:**
1. Decide insurance or not first; patients increasingly expect coverage because platforms advertise $0 sessions.
2. Physician referral pad and a one-page "who we help, how to refer, which plans."
3. A program (for example initial plus 5 follow-ups over 3 months) instead of single sessions; re-assessment at the end.
4. Telehealth across your licensed states.
5. Content that answers specific questions ("what to eat on GLP-1 to keep muscle"), with clinician review.

**Traps:** weight-loss promises; before/after photos; competing on price with $0-copay platforms without insurance.

## Acupuncture and other cash-pay clinicians

**Grows through:** Google profile and reviews, referrals from chiro, PT, OB-GYN and fertility clinics, oncology support, a course of care, sometimes insurance or workers' comp where covered.

**Numbers:** $75 to $150 per session typical in private clinics, cities higher (price guides, weak). No public rebooking benchmark: measure visits per course and rebook at checkout.

**Levers:**
1. Book a course at visit 1 with a re-assessment date.
2. Fertility and pregnancy partnerships (clear, compliant claims only).
3. Rent or share space with a chiro or PT for two-way referrals (massage therapists report chiro offices as the best referral base, 199).
4. Package pricing paid upfront or monthly ([scripts.md](scripts.md#cash-pay-price-explanation)).

Massage and spa patterns (deposits, pressure intake, boundaries): `beauty-business`.

## Optometry

**Grows through:** recall, vision plans and medical billing, optical capture, contact lens subscriptions, PCP and endocrinologist referrals (diabetic exams), pediatric and school programs, myopia management, dry eye clinics as cash services.

**Numbers:** eye-care phone leads book 51% on the call (Invoca 2026). Optical capture 50 to 70% called healthy at independents (Review of Optometry), vendor top quartile 75 to 82%.

**Levers:**
1. Recall system and online booking.
2. Same-visit hand-off to optical with benefits explained; follow-up for unused benefits.
3. Contact lens annual supply and auto-ship.
4. Medical eye care services (dry eye, myopia control) marketed as distinct cash or medical services.
5. Diabetic eye exam reports back to PCPs.

**Traps:** prescriptions walking to online retailers with no follow-up; recall only by postcard.
