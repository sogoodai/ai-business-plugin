# Courses, cohorts, launches and communities

Benchmarks: [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md). List-to-revenue math: [economics.md](economics.md#list-to-revenue-launches). Email build and deliverability: sibling skill `email-sms-loyalty`. Ads: sibling skill `paid-ads`.

## What to sell in 2026

- **A system around the information.** A course has three layers: curriculum (AI can now produce much of it), access (to a known expert), accountability (schedule, deadlines, peers, feedback). Price the access and accountability layers on purpose instead of burying them in "bonus content".
- **Scheduled beats self-paced on completion:** 53.4% vs 41.9%; lesson discussions 50.9% vs 37.3% (Ruzuku 2026, observational). Add at least one live milestone before the point where passive courses lose people (usually the first third).
- **Price on live hours and applied work, not video hours:** Maven puts live cohorts at $800 to $2,450; self-paced one-time courses have a $150 median (Ruzuku). The same topic sold as a cohort with a project can be priced 5 to 10x the recorded version.
- **Design the second offer from day one:** course to workshop, cohort, office-hours membership or 1:1 review; cohort to alumni community or advanced cohort. 53.8% of Kajabi creator earnings came from second or later purchases.

## Choosing the launch model

No public 2025 to 2026 data shows one model is more profitable for small educators. Choose by economics:

| Model | Use when | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-sale (sell before building) | Demand is uncertain; fewer than 5 paying customers so far | You cannot deliver live if the recordings are not ready |
| Beta cohort (discounted, live, feedback) | First run; you need testimonials and a tested syllabus | You plan to keep the beta price forever |
| Waitlist plus cohort | Live access, deadlines, peers and projects are the value | You have no time to run live sessions |
| Free live workshop or webinar | It qualifies buyers or produces a visible quick win | It attracts hobbyists who will never buy |
| Challenge (3 to 5 days) | The outcome has a quick first win and a community | The topic needs weeks before any result |
| Evergreen (automated webinar or email funnel) | Demand is continuous, delivery is mostly async, and the funnel already converts without you selling | You have not run at least two live cycles |
| Flash sale (48 to 72 h) | Warm lapsed list, at most every 90 days (public playbook heuristic) | Used often: buyers learn to wait |

Kajabi's data says creators who eventually sold took 67 days to a first sale (30% within 30 days). Pre-sell first, record after the first payments.

## Cohort launch runway (6 weeks)

Maven's targets: 100 course-page visits a week, 1 to 2% enrollment, about 5 visits per buyer before enrolling, up to 50% of enrollments in the last 1 to 2 weeks, about 0.5% conversion before proof and syllabus exist. Courses over 100 weekly visits were 50% more likely to fill a first cohort (Maven 2026, vendor guidance).

| Week | Do | Measure |
|---|---|---|
| -6 to -5 | Publish the specific problem and your framework; open the waitlist; offer a diagnostic or resource | Page visits/week, waitlist adds |
| -4 to -3 | Free or low-cost live lesson; collect objections; message every engaged person and offer a short 1:1 conversation (Maven tells instructors to post on LinkedIn 3 times a week in this sequence) | Attendees, conversations booked |
| -2 to -1 | Publish proof, syllabus, outcomes, who it is for and not for; open paid enrollment | Page conversion |
| Final 7 days | Promote harder, not softer | Daily enrollments |
| Final 48 h | Answer objections, show exactly what happens in week one, send "who this is and is not for"; keep enrollment open while seats remain | Close-day enrollments |

If visits run under 100 a week by week -4, add a traffic source (partner newsletter, guest podcast, workshop, small ad budget) instead of posting more of the same.

## Webinars and workshops

Measured (Livestorm, 2025 data): 49.7% of registrations in the final 7 days, 15.3% on the day, 47.7% live attendance (51.7% on Tuesdays), 26-minute average watch time on a 68-minute average webinar.

- Keep registration open until the start; never close a day early.
- Teach and make the transition within the first 30 minutes.
- Reminder stack (coreyhaines31/marketingskills, MIT): calendar add on the confirmation page (the biggest single fix), reminder the day before teasing one specific insight, 1 hour before, 5 minutes before with the join link.
- Run of show that does not feel like a pitch (same source): 0 to 5 min restate the promise and say an offer comes at the end; 5 to 35 min three teaching points each with proof; a scripted 60-second transition ("everything I showed you can be done on your own; here is what it looks like when we do it together"); 5 to 8 min one offer with a real reason to act now; Q&A with the offer restated.
- Diagnose: low registration = topic or title; attendance under 30% = reminders or calendar add; people leave early = slow opening; they stay but do not buy = transition or offer.
- After: engaged attendees get a personal message within 24 hours referring to their question; people who left early get the replay with a timestamp; no-shows get the replay with a deadline over 2 to 3 emails; replay window 72 hours to 1 week.
- Registration page (coleschaffer/dtc-copywriting-skills, MIT): headline, subhead and button visible on one phone screen; "Reserve my seat" over "Register now"; bullets one mobile line each, starting "How to", "Why", "The #1".
- Coaches use webinars to refill the pipeline and reuse the same material monthly (operators report, 6). Several coaches report most clients from "a few workshops a year" (r/lifecoaching, 74) or in-person workshops ($250K+ relationship coach, 7); a BNI-sourced workshop of 20 attendees produced 2 sign-ups.

Public webinar conversion ranges (registration 30 to 50% warm and 10 to 25% cold, 1 to 5% of attendees buying directly) are uncited directional ranges from public skills: use as a first assumption only.

## Challenges

From ominou5/funnel-architect-plugin (MIT, uncited targets): 3 days gives the highest completion, 5 the best balance. Each day: a "Day 3 of 5" badge, one 10 to 20 minute lesson, one completable action, a share or mark-complete step, a preview of tomorrow. Emails: morning unlock, evening "did you complete today's action?", offer after the final training, 24-hour reminder, close at 48 hours. Targets to replace with your own: day-1 completion over 70%, day 5 over 30%, registration to purchase over 3%. Seed the paid offer from day one; never surprise people with it on the last day.

## Launch emails (adapted from naveedharri/benai-skills, MIT)

Teaser 7 days before, pain 5 days before, reveal 3 days before or on launch day, curriculum deep dive (+1), proof (+3), objection answers (+5), close on the final day. Matching social posts: teaser without naming the course, announcement, behind the scenes, student result, last call. "Last chance" is used once, for a real deadline.

## Pricing tiers and payment plans

- Three tiers: self-paced; plus community and live Q&A; plus 1:1 calls or reviews. Most buyers take the middle tier when it is the obvious fit; the top tier anchors.
- Payment plans: price the plan above paid-in-full (public examples: $1,997 or $797 x 3, a 20% premium; $4,000 or $1,500 x 3). Paperbell saw 31% of coaching packages bought on plans. No public data shows plans raise or lower refunds or completion: track refunds and completion by payment type yourself.
- Early-bird price then regular price, with a real date. Founding-member price only if it really rises at the stated number.
- Guarantees: a conditional guarantee fits courses that need effort ("complete the six modules and the final exercise within 60 days; if you have not [outcome], full refund"; coreyhaines31/marketingskills, MIT). In the EU and UK, many online purchases carry a 14-day withdrawal right: check before writing "no refunds". Never guarantee an income or score.
- Do not fake discounts by inflating the list price.

## Curriculum and completion (adapted from naveedharri/benai-skills, MIT)

- Design backward from the end state; 3 to 8 milestones; 3 to 12 lessons per module, one objective each.
- Engineer a first win in week one; every completer becomes a case study candidate.
- Cut test for every lesson: could students still reach the outcome without it?
- Define what counts as completion and track it per cohort.
- Never wipe learner progress when restructuring: a language app's restructure that reset learners' progress drove paying users to quit publicly (r/languagelearning, 49 upvotes, corroborated).

## Course SEO and owned demand (keupera/niche-seo-content, MIT)

- Position on outcome plus audience plus time ("Python to automate your own spreadsheet reporting in 6 weeks"), not topic.
- Keep content on your own domain; use the course platform only for checkout.
- Publish honest comparison pages ("[course] vs free alternatives", "is [course] worth it"), free calculators and templates (they earn links the sales page never will), dated testimonials with numbers, and the completion rate and refund policy.
- Course schema with hasCourseInstance for cohort dates.
- SEO articles answer the question completely; lead magnets give the diagnosis and leave the system for the paid product.

## Lead magnets that sell education

Choose a magnet that diagnoses or produces an output: a 10-question placement test, an exam error audit, a "score this sample answer" rubric, a course ROI calculator, a 15-minute practice routine, a niche checklist, a benchmark spreadsheet, a live mini-workshop. The paid offer must solve the gap the magnet revealed. A five-day email mini-course that solves one specific problem and ends with a discovery-call link worked for one coach (r/Coaching, 3); a case-study magnet (challenge, diagnosis, solution, reusable resources, call link) brought fewer but better-fit leads.

## Communities

- **Small is normal:** median paid community 23 members; 79% have 100 or fewer; typical price $35/month, cheapest plan median $25; 49% offer annual (Heartbeat 2026). Thirty members at $35 is $1,050 a month: viable as a layer on top of teaching or coaching.
- **Sell people, not content:** 66% of builders say members value people (live sessions, access, accountability, peers) vs 9% content.
- **First month decides retention:** members who RSVP'd to an event in month one were 51% active at six months vs 27% (Heartbeat, observational). Book the first live event before the new member leaves checkout; ask for an intro post with one specific goal and a first milestone date.
- **Retention infrastructure, not a product on its own:** attach the community to a course, cohort or coaching tier; alumni access after a cohort; graduation into membership after a block (a training gym moves most coaching clients into regular memberships, about $1,800 lifetime value, r/smallbusiness, 32).
- **Start-up (coreyhaines31/marketingskills, MIT):** hand-recruit 20 to 50 founding members before opening; seed 5 to 10 example posts; welcome every member by name; one weekly core action rewarded publicly; watch for the same 5 people producing most activity and new members going silent after the intro.
- **Platform fees:** Skool Pro 2.9% + $0.30 (3.9% above $900 transactions), Hobby 10% + $0.30; Discord about 10% plus 6% processing, worse on iOS. Circle survey: 54% of communities monetize through paid memberships.
- **Retention mechanics that work in learning products:** a small daily allowance can keep maintainers engaged (Clozemaster's 30 sentences a day, operators report); a weekly shared quest with a family member kept one user on an app they wanted to leave (34 upvotes). Guilt messages do the opposite.
- **AI in communities:** 88% of builders use AI, mostly backstage (writing, planning, admin); only 12% let it answer members. Keep member answers human or clearly labeled.

## Piracy, platform risk and relaunches

- Pirates sell what is copyable. Add what is not: live classes, feedback, updated templates, hosted resources with access control, community.
- A one-month early-access tier (Patreon-style) for new videos is a cheap recurring product (r/Udemy, 31; untested).
- Relaunch at a natural update moment (major software release, new exam spec).
