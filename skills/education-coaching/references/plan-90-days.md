# 90-day plans

Three plans: tutor or teacher (including music and language), course creator, coach. Each assumes a new or stalled business. Order matters: instrument and fix the offer, get the first 5 to 10 paying customers through at most two channels, install retention, then test paid acquisition, then raise value per customer. Each phase ends with a short owner report: what changed, scorecard numbers, decisions needed.

Scorecard for all three (from [economics.md](economics.md)): source of every lead, response time, lead-to-paid rate, contribution per customer, month-N retention or completion, second-purchase rate, refunds, referrals.

## Plan A: tutor, test prep, music or language teacher

### Days 1 to 14: offer and economics

- Pick one buyer and one measurable outcome with a deadline ("Grade 8 algebra from C to B by the spring report", "IELTS 6 to 7 in 8 weeks", "first recital piece by June").
- Build the diagnostic and the gap map template; write one block package (sessions, mock, report, price).
- Write policies: monthly prepay or block, 24 to 48 hour cancellation, makeup blocks, notice to leave. Signed before the first paid lesson.
- Compute net per hour per platform ([economics.md](economics.md#net-per-hour-by-platform)) and set a price in the lower third of the band.
- Booking stack: scheduler with payment (Cal.com or Calendly with Stripe), intake form, "How did you hear about us?" question.
- Marketplace profile on one platform, tuned: specific title, intro video, peak-hour availability, notifications on. Note its off-platform rule.
- Owner questions: which students came from which platform? Which hours do you actually want to fill? Will you teach groups?

Exit check: the diagnostic, package and policy exist in writing; price set; one profile live.

### Days 15 to 30: first 5 to 10 paying students

- Marketplace: reply within the hour; first message from [scripts.md](scripts.md#marketplace-first-reply); on Preply keep the trial to diagnosis and a plan.
- Owned channel, one of: Google Business Profile with outcome-based copy (local), parent groups and enrichment-center flyer swaps (K-12, music), job-specific groups and a free 15-minute check (language).
- Tell your network the exact openings and dates.
- Every new student starts from a diagnostic and gets a gap map within 24 hours.

Exit check: 5+ paying students; lead-to-paid rate measured on at least 10 inquiries.

### Days 31 to 60: retention and proof

- Progress reports every 3 to 5 sessions; first end-of-block decisions booked before the block ends.
- Code every end reason; separate exam-done from churn.
- Ask for one review from every family at the first visible win (monthly email line); never incentivize.
- Add one second purchase: exam sprint, group session, summer block, recital prep, niche guide.
- Check the calendar for the next demand spike and open those blocks to current families first.

Exit check: month-2 retention known; 3+ reviews or testimonials with permission; second offer priced.

### Days 61 to 75: paid test (only if economics allow)

- Max CAC from contribution; max CPL from lead-to-paid. Google exact-intent terms for local tutoring or test prep, or Meta to a diagnostic for parents, 2 to 3 weeks before a demand spike.
- Kill rule: pause above 1.25x max CAC after enough paid conversions to judge. Depth: sibling skill `paid-ads`.

### Days 76 to 90: value per student

- If full or near full: raise for new students; waitlist with a deposit for the next term.
- Referral ask after wins; reward only on purchase.
- Platform insurance: export roster, screenshot reviews.
- Decide next quarter's budget by contribution per student, payback, 90-day retention and referral rate.

## Plan B: course or cohort creator

### Days 1 to 14: validate before building

- One audience, one capability or project the course produces; write "who it is for" and "who it is not for".
- Decide the model ([courses-and-launches.md](courses-and-launches.md#choosing-the-launch-model)): fewer than 5 paying customers so far = pre-sale or beta cohort.
- Sales page with outcome, syllabus outline, schedule, live elements, price, plan option, guarantee you control, and a waitlist.
- Lead magnet that diagnoses (placement test, error audit, rubric, calculator).
- Owner questions: list size and how engaged? Proof in hand? Hours available for live sessions? Platform fees (Maven 10%, Udemy 37 or 97%)?

Exit check: page and waitlist live; tracking of page visits per week works.

### Days 15 to 30: pre-sell

- 10 to 20 direct conversations with people who fit; offer the beta at a founding price with a real end date.
- One free live lesson or workshop; message every attendee who engaged.
- Target: first 5 to 10 paid seats before recording more than the first module.

Exit check: paid seats or a clear "no" with objections written down.

### Days 31 to 45: repeatable traffic

- Publish 6 to 10 pieces on one buyer problem, each ending in the same lead magnet or waitlist: YouTube (title, thumbnail, first seconds, no tag work), LinkedIn 3 times a week for professional topics, TikTok content gaps for younger audiences.
- Start the 6-week runway for the next cohort; target 100 page visits a week; add a partner newsletter, guest podcast or joint webinar if under target by week -4.

Exit check: page visits per week trending to 100; waitlist growing weekly.

### Days 46 to 60: deliver for completion

- Run the beta live: first win in week one, discussion on every lesson, a live milestone at the first-third mark.
- Collect results and testimonials at the end; ask completers what almost stopped them from buying; turn answers into bonuses or FAQ.
- Community layer (if any): first event booked at checkout; founding members hand-recruited.

Exit check: completion rate measured; 3+ case studies with numbers.

### Days 61 to 75: launch with proof

- Final two weeks of the runway: proof, syllabus, week-one walkthrough, fit and non-fit; push hardest in the last 7 days; keep enrollment open to the start.
- Optional paid test to the free workshop, judged on buyers, not registrants.

### Days 76 to 90: second offer and evergreen decision

- Second offer from observed demand: office hours, advanced cohort, 1:1 review, alumni community, workshop.
- Evergreen only after two live cycles that converted without heavy founder selling.
- Report: buyer rate by segment, revenue per delivered subscriber, completion, refunds by payment type, second-purchase rate.

## Plan C: coach

### Days 1 to 14: positioning and package

- One sentence: "I help [specific person] [outcome] in [time]." Test the label (life, performance, career, executive coach).
- One package of at least 3 months with a mutual continue decision; price paid in full and as a plan; a guarantee you control.
- Website or one page optional; a booking link with 3 to 5 intake questions and a price range is enough.
- Warm list from sent email and messages: 50 to 100 names that fit.
- Owner questions: who already sends you clients? Which past results can be shared? How many clients can you hold?

Exit check: positioning line, package, booking link with price range, warm list.

### Days 15 to 30: first conversations

- 10 individual check-ins in week 3 (no pitch), then 5 personal messages a day.
- One talk or mini-workshop at a local group, association or networking club.
- Practice or pilot clients if needed: a paid 30-day pilot for 3 to 5 people at a low price, documented as case studies.
- Run every discovery call with the same structure ([coaching-sales.md](coaching-sales.md#discovery-call)); send a summary and worksheet within 24 hours.

Exit check: 10+ discovery calls held; first 3 to 5 paying clients; booked-showed-closed numbers recorded.

### Days 31 to 45: one content channel and partners

- One channel matched to the buyer: LinkedIn for career and executive, niche Reddit or Facebook groups for life-stage audiences, a podcast guest slot for life and relationship coaching. Use clients' exact words.
- Two referral partner conversations a week (therapists, HR, agencies, nonprofits, pediatric offices, other coaches).

Exit check: inquiries arriving from a source other than your own outreach.

### Days 46 to 60: retention and proof

- Session recaps, shared client doc, check-in boundaries stated.
- Renewal conversations planned in month 2 of each 3-month agreement.
- Testimonials that include unexpected results; permission in writing.
- Second offer: group program, assessment product, maintenance plan, alumni group.

### Days 61 to 75: paid search test (named services only)

- Exact-intent keywords to a single-service page, daily budget set from max CAC (the reported executive-coach case ran about $150 a day), judged on booked calls, shows and clients. Remarketing optional. Depth: sibling skill `paid-ads`.

### Days 76 to 90: value and capacity

- Raise the price for new clients if the calendar is full or most qualified calls close; otherwise fix positioning first.
- Publish a "from" price on the page.
- Decide next quarter: which channel produced clients at what cost and time, renewal rate, referral share. Commit to any new channel for a full quarter before judging it.
