# Economics: compute before recommending

Pull 12 months of the roster or sales export (covers the exam year and summer). Columns needed: customer, start date, end date, sessions or purchases with dates, price paid, source (marketplace, referral, Google, social, ads), end reason. With no export, ask for the current roster and price list and label every result an estimate.

## Net per hour, by platform

```
net per hour = list price x (1 - platform fee) - payment fee - (prep + travel + admin minutes / 60) x your hourly value
```

Always add unpaid time: trial lessons, messages, prep, travel, waiting for Free Talk calls. One Palfish teacher made $12 across 4 hours plus $4 for a trial no-show against an advertised $8 per 25-minute lesson (r/OnlineESLTeaching, 78-upvote thread).

Worked examples at a $60 list price (fee pages, 2026):

| Platform and stage | Fee | Net per lesson | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wyzant, platform-found student | 25% | $45.00 | |
| Wyzant, student you referred | 0% to you (student pays 9%) | $60.00 | Send your own leads through your Wyzant referral link instead of losing them |
| Preply, trial of a new student | 100% | $0 | The trial is your acquisition cost |
| Preply, first 20 hours | 33% | $40.20 | |
| Preply, after 20 hours | 28% | $43.20 | |
| Preply, 400+ hours | 18% | $49.20 | |
| italki single lesson (secondary source) | 21% | $47.40 | |
| italki 20-pack (secondary source) | 15% | $51.00 | Push packs: the fee drops 6 points |
| Superprof US direct payment | 0% | $60 minus your card fee | Allowed in the US |
| Outschool, per learner | 30% | $42.00 | Multiply by learners |

**Preply trial cost per kept student:**

```
acquisition cost per kept student = trial price / trial-to-regular conversion rate
```

$30 trial and 40% of trials becoming regular = $75 of free teaching per kept student, before the 33% fee on everything after. Over a 20-lesson student at $30: the student pays $630 (trial + 20), you receive $402. Effective platform take 36%.

**A suggested price can be a trap.** One tutor accepted Preply's suggested $12: $0 for the trial, about $8 per paid hour after 33%, then raising the rate for an existing student failed because the student-specific price, lesson length and prepaid balance did not update together (r/OnlineESLTeaching, 72 upvotes). Set the rate you can live with before the first student.

**AmazingTalker (operators report, historical):** 30% commission falling 1 point per $100 earned in the month, plus an 8% fixed fee and transfer fees; students pay 10% above the listed rate. A tutor earning over $3,000 a month reported paying only the 8%. Non-English languages started at 15% (r/OnlineESLTeaching, 66).

## Group math

```
group net per hour = learners x price per learner x (1 - fee)
break-even learners = your target net hour / (price per learner x (1 - fee))
```

- Outschool: target $60 net at $15 per learner-hour: 60 / (15 x 0.7) = 5.7, so you need 6 learners. One enrolled learner at a typo price paid a teacher $6 for an hour (r/OnlineESLTeaching, 52): set a minimum enrollment and cancel below it.
- ESL weekly speaking group: $15 x 4 to 6 = $60 to $90 per hour from students you already teach 1:1 (operators report, 63 upvotes).
- Coaching group: a 60-minute call with 20 people is 3 minutes each; above that it becomes a lecture (public playbook heuristic). Groups of 8 to 30 and 1:1 caps of 4 to 10 clients are the common public ranges (coreyhaines31/marketingskills, MIT, uncited).
- Repeat a prepared class: after the first run, prep falls to near zero. One Outschool teacher averaged about $90 per teaching hour with almost no prep or grading (operators report).

## Course and resource platforms

**Udemy: who made the sale decides the share.**

```
your revenue = marketplace sales x 37% + your-coupon sales x 97% + subscription minutes share (pool 15% in 2026)
```

1,000 sales at $15: marketplace-driven = $5,550; driven by your own coupon = $14,550. Every buyer you send with your link is worth 2.6x a buyer Udemy found. The subscription pool dropped from 20% (2024) to 17.5% (2025) to 15% (2026); a forecast built on old payouts overstates revenue. One instructor with 21 high-rated art courses reported earning "virtually nothing" and moved them to a Patreon shop (r/Udemy, 31).

**Teachers Pay Teachers, Basic vs Exclusive (2027 terms):**

```
Basic net per sale     = price x 0.55 - 0.30
Exclusive net per sale = price x 0.775 (- 0.15 if the order is under $3)
Exclusive pays when    0.225 x annual TPT revenue + 0.30 x sales count > 59.95 + contribution lost by no longer selling those resources elsewhere
```

- With no sales elsewhere, Exclusive pays above about $270 of annual TPT revenue (59.95 / 0.225). On a $5 resource: Basic $2.45, Exclusive $3.88.
- With an Etsy or own-site store, add that store's lost contribution to the right side. A top 1% seller with another marketplace store said Basic means TPT keeps 45% instead of the old 20% (r/TeachersPayTeachers, 16).
- Own-store comparison for a $5 resource: Etsy about $4.08, Payhip free plan $4.31, Shopify or WooCommerce $4.56 (Shopify adds $39/month); only Etsy brings its own shoppers (r/TeachersPayTeachers, 3, fee comparison).
- Do the math on actual sales: one seller made about $62 on Basic against $104 on the old Premium before its fee, about $44 after it (r/TeachersPayTeachers, 8, historical terms).

**Skillshare:** below 50 followers and 400 qualifying minutes a month you earn no royalties. Model referrals instead: 60% of a referred member's first-year annual fee.

**Maven:** 90% of price minus processing. A $1,200 cohort with 15 students = $16,200 before processing.

## Acquisition math

```
contribution per customer = collected revenue - delivery cost (incl. owner time) - platform and payment fees - refunds and credits
max CAC = contribution per customer x share you accept (30 to 50% to start)
max CPL = max CAC x lead-to-paid rate
payback months = CAC / monthly contribution
```

Worked (modeled, not benchmarks): a tutoring block worth $600 contribution at 50% gives a $300 max CAC. At a measured 25% lead-to-paid rate the break-even CPL is $75. Google's education average is $90.02 per lead and Meta's $28.22 (WordStream 2025). At a 20% lead-to-paid rate those become $450 and $141 CAC; if Meta leads close at 5%, Meta becomes $564.

Normalize every channel to the same unit: cost of one paid customer with X days of contribution. A 25% Wyzant fee can be cheaper than any ad campaign if it fills hours that would sit empty. A "free" channel that takes 10 founder hours per client is expensive: value founder time.

Kill rule: pause a campaign when trailing paid CAC exceeds 1.25x max CAC after enough conversions to judge. Change one variable at a time: offer and angle, then audience or query, then landing page.

## List to revenue (launches)

No public formula exists, and old "$1 per subscriber per month" rules are folklore. Model it and then measure:

```
launch revenue = reachable subscribers x click-to-page rate x page conversion x price
buyer rate = unique buyers / unique reachable subscribers
revenue per delivered subscriber = collected launch revenue / unique recipients
```

Worked model, cohort course at $1,000, 2,000 engaged subscribers:

- Maven runway: 100 page visits a week for 6 weeks = 600 visits. At 1 to 2% = 6 to 12 students, $6,000 to $12,000. Before proof exists, 0.5% = 3 students.
- If the list can only send 300 visits, the gap of 300 must come from LinkedIn, partners, a free workshop or ads. Plan the traffic source per week, not "post more".
- Webinar path: of 400 registrants, about 191 attend live (47.7%, Livestorm 2025). Public skills cite 1 to 5% of attendees buying on a direct offer (uncited): 2 to 10 sales. Track your own and replace the assumption.

A 5,000-person list of matched buyers beats 100,000 broad subscribers. Report buyer rate by segment (customers, workshop attendees, lead-magnet only).

## Retention and lifetime

```
month-N retention = cohort members with a paid session or payment in month N / cohort size at month 0
planned completion rate = ends with goal reached / all ends
lifetime revenue = average monthly revenue per student x average months active
second-purchase rate = customers who renew or buy again / customers whose first product ended
```

- Build a cohort table by start month. Separate planned completion (exam taken, goal reached, course finished) from churn (stopped, disappeared, moved to AI or self-study). An SAT student who stops after the test is a success to ask for a referral, not a loss.
- Code every end reason, one per end: achieved goal, schedule conflict, affordability, tutor or coach mismatch, service issue, season or exam ended, moved to self-study or AI, competitor, moved away, unknown. Only 6% of music school cancellations cited cost (Opus1 2026): do not assume price is the churn reason.
- Coaching: renewal at the end of each 3-month agreement is the real conversion event. Track renewal rate per coach and per niche.

## Price change break-even

```
visits or students you can lose before contribution falls = p / (m + p)
p = price increase as a fraction, m = contribution margin as a fraction
```

A 15% raise at a 60% margin survives losing 20% of students. Owner-delivered teaching has high margin, so raises are safer than they feel. Raise for new students first and watch inquiry conversion for 4 to 6 weeks.
