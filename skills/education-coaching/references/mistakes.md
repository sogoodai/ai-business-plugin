# Expensive mistakes

Two tables: what the data and platform terms show, and what practitioners report losing.

## From the data and platform terms

| Mistake | What it costs | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Moving Wyzant, Preply or Outschool clients off-platform | Account loss and the demand source with it; direct payment is prohibited on all three | Per-platform rule; Superprof US allows direct payment; build owned demand upstream |
| Teaching the whole Preply trial | Preply keeps 100% of the trial; a $30 trial at 40% conversion is $75 of free teaching per kept student | Under 10 minutes of teaching; diagnose, plan, agree a routine (Preply's own advice) |
| Forecasting Udemy income on old payouts | Subscription pool fell from 20% (2024) to 15% (2026); marketplace sales pay 37% vs 97% on your own coupon | Drive buyers with your own link; build an owned list |
| Treating Skillshare as passive income | No royalties under 50 followers and 400 to 650 qualifying minutes a month (2026) | Use referrals (60% of a referred annual fee) or skip |
| Choosing a TPT tier from old numbers | Premium closed Oct 2026; Exclusive drops to 77.5% in 2027 and bans selling the same resources elsewhere | Run the Basic vs Exclusive break-even with real sales and other-store revenue |
| Optimizing ads to cost per lead | Google education CPL $90.02 = $450 CAC at 20% lead-to-paid; Meta $28.22 leads = $564 CAC if they close at 5% (WordStream 2025, modeled) | Report cost per paid student and 90-day contribution |
| Closing webinar registration early | 49.7% of registrations come in the final week, 15.3% on the day (Livestorm 2025) | Keep registration and promotion open to the start |
| Pitching at minute 55 | Average watch time 26 minutes of a 68-minute webinar | Teach and transition in the first 30 minutes |
| Automatic package discounts | 15% off a $1,000 package = $150 given away; larger packages kept the same hourly price (Paperbell 2026) | Sell the outcome; price single sessions higher |
| Selling a passive library as the main product | Open-access completion 41.9% vs 53.4% for cohorts; AI makes explanation abundant | Add schedule, discussion, live milestones, feedback |
| One-offer catalog | Kajabi creators with one offer: $180 typical lifetime earnings; 53.8% of earnings come from repeat purchases | Design the second offer once the first works |
| Building before selling | First sales took 67 days typically, among creators who ever sold (Kajabi) | Pre-sell, then record |
| Tags and posting-time optimization on YouTube | YouTube says tags matter little and publish time is not known to matter long term | Title, thumbnail promise, first seconds, satisfaction |
| Planning on an agency case number | A 68% assessment-to-paid case is one center, high selection bias | Use your own conversion after 20+ assessments |
| Assuming price drives churn | 6% of music-school cancellations cited cost (Opus1 2026) | Code end reasons; fix schedule, mismatch and progress first |
| Promoting a cohort for 2 weeks | Maven: buyers visit about 5 times; up to 50% enroll in the last 1 to 2 weeks of a 6-week runway | 6-week runway, 100 page visits a week |
| Comparing your price to competitors' website prices | Coaching sites showed $997; packages actually sold at $736 (Paperbell) | Use transaction data and your own conversion |

## From practitioners

| Mistake | What it cost | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Accepting a marketplace's suggested low rate | $12 on Preply: $0 trial, about $8 per paid hour, and raising it later broke bookings (72 upvotes) | Set your sustainable rate before the first student |
| Starting at about $5 for 50 minutes | Difficult students who did not respect the teacher (19) | Lower third of the band, never the bottom |
| Waiving cancellation fees repeatedly | Lost trips, repeated no-shows, then the parent quoted the flexibility back; family dropped (111) | Same rule every time; one documented exception at most |
| Teaching unpaid lessons and open payment plans | "Probably thousands over the years" (16) | Monthly in advance; pause after one reminder; no partial payments |
| Changing a minor's lesson time verbally | School refunded all the disputed lessons (6) | Write to the parent; wait for confirmation |
| Giving the trial incentive before attendance | Free guitar promo: 2 trials booked, 0 attended (23) | Reward after the lesson happens |
| Pressure tactics on a sales call | $7,400 program lost the buyer and drew a 186-upvote public warning; others added it to do-not-contact | Time to decide; book a follow-up; no threats |
| "Don't come unless you'll buy" pre-call email | Buyer cancelled the call (19) | Fit statement and price range before the call |
| Buying a full launch formula before any clients | $6,000 coaching, site, brand, VA, 3 videos a week: zero clients after 8 months (59) | Warm outreach to 10 people first |
| Relying on one platform for most income | TakeLessons shut down; a Wyzant profile was deleted without explanation; TPT sales fell from $300 to $500 a month to under $40 for one seller | Monthly review screenshots, roster export, own list and second channel |
| Asking marketplace students for 5 stars | Tutor dismissals per an Engoo employee (3) | Ask for honest feedback; teach well; report sabotage with evidence |
| Ignoring rating thresholds | Rating 4.7 to 4.6 cost about 75% of students (5); below about 4.93 bookings fell to 50 to 75% (104) | Watch rating daily; decline lesson types that reliably drag it down |
| Answering marketplace matches slowly | 20+ AmazingTalker matches missed at 1 to 3 minutes (12); a Strawberry.me client unmatched within an hour | Notifications on; reply in minutes during open hours |
| Hiring to absorb a waitlist | Nearly all waitlisted prospects refused the hired coach (5) | Test transfer with a few clients first |
| Over-questioning a tactical client | 1-star first review: "stop asking me so many questions" (3) | Blend coaching with direct advice for tactical goals |
| Narrowing so hard that buyers feel excluded | Prospects: "I was interested but then I saw you don't work with men anymore" (21) | Separate offers per audience under one brand |
| Wiping learner progress in a course update | Paying users quit publicly (49, corroborated) | Preserve progress and demonstrated levels |
| Guilt-based reactivation | "You made Duolingo sad" lost a user (32) | Neutral check-in |
| Fake discount anchoring | $15 course relisted at $150 "90% off" drew public criticism (42) | Real prices and real deadlines |
| Discounted acquisition offers for coaching | Clients who could not afford continued coaching (9) | Qualify on budget; price the first step honestly |
| Teaching a one-student group at a typo price | $6 for an hour after the 30% fee (52) | Minimum enrollment; check price before class day |
| Counting Facebook seller-group followers as buyers | 200 followers, almost all other sellers, no redemptions (4) | Measure sales by source |
