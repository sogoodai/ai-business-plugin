# How AI changed demand since 2023, and what to sell now

## What the evidence shows

- **Students use AI as the default explainer.** UK 13 to 18 year olds: 66.5% had used generative AI in 2025; weekly or more rose from 31.1% (2024) to 45.6% (2025); 60.9% of monthly users use it for homework (National Literacy Trust 2025). UK undergraduates: 92% use AI (66% in 2024), 88% for assessments (53% in 2024) (HEPI 2025, university students, not K-12).
- **Answer businesses got hit first.** Chegg's FY2025 filing describes sharp declines in academic services and names free and paid generative AI as a competitive factor. Answer delivery lost its scarcity; that is not the same as tutoring dying.
- **The low end of conversation teaching is compressing.** ESL teachers report bookings down 50% in two years with colleagues reporting the same (r/OnlineESLTeaching, 8). A 12-year veteran: "Teachers that rigidly follow books or turn up for 'a chat' will go" as AI voice apps at about $30 a month become "good enough" (21). A student who left for AI conversation practice came back after a year saying it was too robotic to give insight (19).
- **Human teaching keeps a status premium.** Top answer (124 upvotes): "Nobody brags their kid has weekly lessons with a robot." Anything cheap and available to everyone reads as lesser in status-conscious markets.
- **Resource marketplaces are flooded.** TPT buyers complain that the first activities they see are AI-made and visually overloaded for students with disabilities (r/TeachersPayTeachers, 36), and TPT signaled action against low-effort AI stores and AI covers and descriptions at its 2026 town hall (sellers' reports). Demographics also shrink some markets: one ESL commenter named a shrinking customer base as the bigger threat (9).
- **Course production got cheap; trust did not.** As anyone can generate a curriculum, proof, proprietary diagnostics, real feedback, reputation and live access become the scarce part.
- **Creators use AI backstage.** 88% of community builders use AI, mostly for writing, planning and admin; 12% let it answer members, 4% moderate with it (Heartbeat 2026).

## What AI does well vs what the human offer should sell

| AI now supplies cheaply | Sell instead |
|---|---|
| Explanations on demand | Diagnosis of which misunderstanding actually costs points |
| Unlimited practice questions | Choosing and sequencing the right practice for this student |
| Draft feedback | High-stakes judgment against the real rubric and examiner context |
| Generic study plans | Accountability and adapting the plan to actual results |
| Instant conversation practice | Pronunciation correction, cultural nuance, confidence, commitment |
| Generic business or career advice | Decisions with this person's constraints and consequences |
| Course summaries | Live application, project review, cohort deadlines, expert critique |
| Homework answers | Parent-visible progress, integrity, foundational mastery |
| Content generation | Credentialed interpretation and a trusted, named expert |

## Adapted offers by sub-model

- **K-12 tutor:** "AI-proof" mastery: timed work without tools, written gap maps, parent reports. Teach the student to use AI as a practice partner and check its answers; offer "homework integrity" plans for parents worried about AI copying.
- **Test prep:** official-format diagnostics, error logs by question type, mock exams under real conditions, a human examiner's eye on writing and speaking.
- **Writing tutor:** source verification, rubric-based revision, voice; teach how to revise AI drafts honestly where the school allows it.
- **Language teacher:** stop selling "conversation"; sell correction (live chat corrections, monthly speaking recordings, pronunciation), exam bands, job English (interview, nurses, hospitality), and accountability. Add a prompt-assisted practice routine between lessons, then correct the output in class.
- **Music teacher:** AI cannot hold a bow arm or a performance nerve. Sell technique feedback, performances, ensembles, exams.
- **Course creator:** move from library to system: cohort deadlines, live sessions, discussions, project feedback, community. Use AI to cut production time and spend the time on live elements and proof.
- **Coach:** clients prepare with AI; sessions are for decisions, accountability and a person who notices patterns. Market judgment ("the three signals I use to tell which of four problems you have"), not tips.
- **TPT seller:** no-prep, accessible, classroom-tested resources with an "original work" story and visible authorship; keep source files; avoid AI covers and descriptions on TPT given its 2026 stance; diversify off a single marketplace.
- **Udemy instructor:** subscription minutes pay less each year; add live and feedback products off Udemy, sold through your own list.

## Marketing consequences

- Do not compete with AI on volume of free explanation. Content demonstrates judgment and ends in a diagnostic.
- Make the human parts visible in the offer name and page: "with weekly written feedback", "live correction", "parent report every 4 sessions".
- Address the AI question head on in sales: "Your child can get answers from AI. What they can't get is someone who finds the one gap that's costing them marks and checks it's fixed."
- Add "moved to AI or self-study" as an end reason in the cohort table and watch it each quarter.
- Use AI internally for prep, reports, scheduling and drafting; never let it send messages to parents or clients without the owner's review, and never present AI-generated materials or voices as the owner's own work where it matters to the buyer.
