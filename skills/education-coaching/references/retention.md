# Retention: visible progress, planned decisions

The retention product is not more content. It is visible movement toward the goal the buyer named, and a decision point the buyer expects.

## What the data says (and does not)

- 47% of new music-school students were still studying after a year; only 6% of cancellations cited cost (Opus1 2026, vendor sample of operationally mature schools).
- Scheduled cohorts complete at 53.4% vs 41.9% open access; discussion lessons 50.9% vs 37.3% (Ruzuku 2026).
- Community members who RSVP to an event in month one: 51% active at six months vs 27% (Heartbeat 2026).
- No public data exists for tutoring retention by month or coaching renewal rates. Build the owner's cohort table and use it as the benchmark from then on.

## Cohort table (build it first)

Rows: start month. Columns: month 0, 1, 2, ... 12. Cell: share of the cohort with a paid session or payment that month. Add two columns at the end: planned completions (goal reached, exam done, course finished) and churn (stopped without reaching the goal). Code every end with one reason: achieved goal, schedule, affordability, mismatch, service issue, season or exam ended, moved to self-study or AI, competitor, moved away, unknown.

Read it for: the month where most churn happens (usually month 1 to 3), which source keeps students longest (referrals usually beat marketplaces and ads), which teacher or coach keeps students longest (centers), and whether "moved to AI" is growing.

## Month-by-month checkpoints

| When | Risk | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Before session 1 | No-show, wrong expectations | Written plan, policies signed, payment in, calendar invite, what to bring |
| Week 1 | No early win | Engineer one visible win in the first two sessions or the first module; tell the parent |
| Sessions 3 to 5 | "Is this working?" | First progress report: baseline, evidence, next target with date, one home action |
| Month 2 to 3 | The dip: novelty gone, results not yet | Name the dip in advance ("most students plateau around week 6; here's what we do"); small milestone; for courses a live session or office hours here |
| End of block or quarter | Silent ending | Reassessment and a recommendation: stop, continue, intensify, change focus. Book the next block in the same conversation |
| Before exams or performances | Planned ending | Book the next step before the event; ask for result and review after |
| December, summer | Seasonal drop | Sell the January block in early December; summer offers in May; keep the weekly slot reserved for a deposit |
| Month 6 to 12 | Boredom, plateau | New goal, performance, exam, group class, level test; for music, songs the student chose |
| Any time a payment fails | Involuntary churn | Retry, one friendly message, pause lessons until settled |

## Progress reporting

- **Tutoring (parent buyer):** every 3 to 5 sessions, one screen. Template in [scripts.md](scripts.md#parent-progress-report).
- **Coaching:** make the client state the success metric before buying; score it at every milestone; send a recap after each session with your reflections and next actions. Ask for unexpected results outside the original goal too; they make the best testimonials (r/lifecoaching, 53).
- **Courses and cohorts:** a progress email at each milestone with what they completed and what the next session unlocks; a public "wins" thread.
- **Language:** written corrections log (chat corrections during lessons), monthly speaking sample recording to compare with the first.
- **Music:** a recording at the start and every term; recital or video performance.

## Renewal mechanics

- Coaching agreements of at least 3 months with a mutual continue decision at the end. Renewal is the real conversion event: plan the renewal conversation in month 2, not in the last session.
- Introduce the next package before the current one ends, framed by the reassessment.
- Explain how any change on your side affects their plan: a teacher announced a master's program two months out and the student cancelled all remaining classes, and the platform waived its usual repricing of discounted lessons (r/OnlineESLTeaching, 3). Say what stays the same first.
- Graduation path for completers: maintenance plan, advanced cohort, alumni community, membership. A training gym moves most coaching clients into regular memberships (about $1,800 lifetime value, r/smallbusiness, 32).

## Win-back

- Trigger on each student's own rhythm: weekly students who miss two weeks without notice, monthly coaching clients past their normal gap. Rank by past value, not by time gone (anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins, Apache-2.0).
- First message asks and does not pitch; a complaint reply stops the sequence and gets a personal call.
- Never guilt ("You made Duolingo sad" is a public example of losing a user, r/languagelearning, 32).
- Past-exam students: reach out at the next exam or school transition, not with a discount.

## Referrals

- Ask after a visible win (score jump, performance, promotion, first fluent conversation), not at signup.
- Reward only when the referred buyer pays: one free class for a 10+ lesson package (r/OnlineESLTeaching, 4). Avoid permanent percentage discounts; they compound every renewal.
- Referral reward budget = target CAC minus other acquisition cost per customer (coreyhaines31/marketingskills, MIT).
- Make it specific: "I have two assessment slots before [date]; I'm happy to run the same baseline for them" (wording in [scripts.md](scripts.md#referral-ask)).
- Rewards do not create referrals that results did not earn: a parent shared a WeChat advert with a free-class reward and nobody commented (r/OnlineESLTeaching, 25), while the same tutor's unprompted referrals filled her schedule for years.
- Personal anniversary note: a subscription founder emailed each one-year customer offering a free box for someone they think "would love" it, and reports a large flow of new subscribers (r/ecommerce, 154). For coaching and lessons: at one year, a personal note offering a free diagnostic for someone they know.

## Reviews and testimonials

- One uniform review request to everyone at a natural milestone; never incentivize reviews or ask only happy clients. Depth: sibling skill `local-seo`.
- Testimonial request that forces specifics: what was hard before, what changed, who you would recommend this to, numbers if possible.
- Atypical results need typical results stated; material connections disclosed; get written permission before using names, photos or scores of minors.
- On marketplace rating systems, never ask for a star count.
