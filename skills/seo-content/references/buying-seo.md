# Buying SEO help: agencies, writers, tools, and the break-even math

Owners lose more money buying SEO badly than doing it badly. Use this when the owner asks "should I hire an agency", "is this quote fair", "why has my agency not delivered" or "which tools do I need". Thread URLs in [sources.md](sources.md).

## Break-even before any contract

```
required extra gross profit per month = monthly SEO cost
required extra revenue per month      = monthly SEO cost / gross margin
required extra organic customers      = required extra revenue / (average order value x purchases in 12 months)
required organic sessions             = required customers / organic conversion rate
```

Worked example: $2,600 a month retainer, 40% margin, $500 average first-year customer value, 3% organic conversion rate. Needs $6,500 extra revenue a month = 13 new customers = about 433 extra organic sessions a month that convert at 3%. Check that against the Ahrefs median: a site with 50 to 100 indexed pages gets about 400 organic clicks a month in total (June 2026). Most SEO work pays back in months 6 to 18, so the owner must fund the gap.

Real case: $60,000 spent in a year with no return; at a 20% margin it needed $300,000 of extra revenue, doubling a $300,000 a year business (r/SEO, 164). Run this math before signing.

When the total market is about 2,000 buyers worldwide, skip SEO and do direct outreach (r/SEO, 285); see the `b2b-outbound` skill.

## Questions to ask an agency before signing

1. What ships in the first 90 days: how many pages created or rewritten, which, and why those?
2. Which link types, from where, and will we see every placement URL? Any paid placements?
3. Which keywords and conversions will you report on? Non-brand clicks and organic conversions, not "keywords ranking" or "traffic value".
4. Show two clients in our niche or size with GSC screenshots over 12 months. (Check their claimed case studies in a third-party traffic history: one well-known consultant's showcase sites had fallen 17,000 to 315 estimated visits, r/juststart, 87.)
5. Who owns the CMS, GA4, GSC, content and links if we part ways? Answer must be: the owner.
6. Who writes, and do they know our industry? (Good providers decline content work when they lack industry writers or client data, r/SEO, 173.)
7. What happens during a core update: what will you change and when?

Red flags: guaranteed rankings or "page one in a month"; refusal to give admin access back; a monthly report that is a tool export; link packages without URLs; "we submit you to 500 directories"; AI content at volume on a new domain; disavow files as a routine service; long contracts without a 90-day exit.

## Agency, freelancer or in-house

- **Owner plus AI agent plus one expert writer** covers most small businesses: the owner supplies first-hand detail, the agent does research, briefs, internal links, audits and reporting, a writer or editor shapes the copy.
- **Freelance specialist for a bounded project** (migration, technical cleanup, one digital PR campaign) beats a vague retainer. Two threads agree: buy scoped work with an hourly rate and deliverables (r/blogging, 66; r/juststart, 72).
- **Agency** makes sense when there is budget for 12+ months and someone in-house can review work weekly.

One owner replaced a $2,000 a month agency with a $150 Semrush account and did more in a day than the agency did in a week (r/SEO, 164 commenter). That is the default to test first.

## Hiring writers that work

- 100 applicants yield about 5 credible, 5 trials, 1 to 2 hires; one freelancer handles about 10,000 words a month, so 30,000 words a month means sourcing 200 to 300 applicants (r/juststart, 121).
- Sources that worked: a $70 ProBlogger listing (200 to 300 applicants), a boosted Facebook Jobs post, expat Facebook groups, referrals from good hires (r/juststart, 121). Mills and generic marketplaces underdelivered in five threads.
- Screen with an instruction-following question, then a paid trial article ($10 to $50) (r/juststart, 78). Portfolio samples get faked or heavily edited.
- Brief every piece ([templates.md](templates.md)) and keep the final edit in-house (five threads).
- Compare outsourced pages with owner-written ones after 6 months before spending more.

## Tools: the minimal stack

| Need | Free first | Paid only when |
|---|---|---|
| Own performance | Google Search Console, Bing Webmaster Tools, GA4 | Never needed for your own data |
| Keyword ideas | GSC queries, Google autocomplete, People Also Ask, Keyword Planner (with any Ads account), Keywords Everywhere (cheap credits), AnswerThePublic free searches | One month of Ahrefs or Semrush for a research sprint, then export and cancel (r/bigseo, 38) |
| SERP check | Incognito search; `udm=14` for organic-only view | A rank tracker for 20 to 50 money terms (about $2 a month for 50 keywords on one device at one API vendor, r/TechSEO, 61) |
| Crawl | Screaming Frog free (500 URLs) | Paid licence (about $259 a year) for larger sites |
| Links | GSC Links report, Bing backlink tool | A paid index for competitor gap research |
| AI visibility | Manual prompt panel in a sheet | A tracker only after the manual panel shows what matters |

Cancel tools with the confirmation email and a screenshot: one owner was charged $211 after missing it (r/SEO, 151). Connecting GSC to a third-party tool can let that vendor aggregate your query data (r/bigseo, 170); weigh free audits against that.
