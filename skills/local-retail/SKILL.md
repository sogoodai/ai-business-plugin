# Local retail: turn passersby into identified regulars

Built from 125 practitioner tactics (r/Flipping, r/smallbusiness, r/florists, r/vintage, r/retail) plus the retail-relevant r/smallbusiness, r/localseo and r/GoogleMyBusiness threads in the local-services set, 2025 to 2026 research (Square Local Economy Report, Google Merchant Center, Flame Analytics, RetailNext, NRF, American Express Shop Small, Society of American Florists, FTD and BloomNation terms, Meta, TikTok Seller Academy, Klaviyo, Shopify, Etsy) and the public skill harvest (AgriciDaniel claude-email, anthropics small-business, unifapi review math, coreyhaines31 retail pack). "Operators report" marks a practitioner anecdote with its thread score. Everything else carries its source and year. Full list: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md). The owner's decisions win over this guide.

## How independent shops actually grow

- **Revenue is a chain, and each link is measurable.** `passersby x entry rate x conversion x ATV = store revenue`. Flame Analytics (500M+ visitor interactions, 2026) puts attraction rate at 15 to 35% and fashion conversion at 15 to 30%. Two points of entry rate (20% to 22%) is 10% more revenue from the same street. Find the weak link before spending on reach.
- **Regulars are the business.** Square 2026: a regular (buys on 4+ distinct days a year) spends about 6x a transient buyer, and regular-customer revenue grew 7.67% in 2025 vs 6.97% overall. Anonymous cash and card sales cannot be grown into regulars. The KPI is identified buyers who reach visit 2, 3 and 4.
- **The neighborhood is a distribution network.** Square 2026: 32% of regulars are shared with other businesses in the same ZIP code. Partner events where the partner brings an audience beat owner-only "customer appreciation nights" (P.F. Candle Co.: +17%, +34%, +31% weekend sales around partner events, Modern Retail 2026, single-operator case).
- **Stock on Google is now SEO.** Merchants showing local inventory got 16% more organic Shopping impressions (Google, Nov 2023 to May 2024 data). Google's Shopping Graph (50B+ listings, Jan 2026) feeds AI Mode, Maps and Gemini, which already answer "vintage shops near me" with stores and availability (Google, May 2025).
- **Social sells what is on the rack today.** Meta (Jan 2026): 75% of US Instagram recommendations come from original posts. Supplier photos and memes lose. New arrivals, the owner's picks, restoration and "what $50 buys" win.
- **A reason to come back physically beats points.** Kennedy's Flowers' refillable vase: 11,000+ refills, $182,000+ extra sales and about 200 store visits a week in 2024 (SAF, 2025). Build a return loop into a product.
- **Paid ads come late** and only behind proven products, events or "in stock here" inventory.

## First session: ask, then compute

Ask the owner:

1. Shop type, country, street or mall, opening hours, staff by daypart.
2. POS (Square, Shopify POS, Lightspeed, Clover, other) and whether you can export 12 months of transactions with line items and customer IDs.
3. A door count of any kind (counter, camera, staff tally). If none, can staff tally entries for two weeks?
4. Share of transactions linked to a customer record; email and SMS list sizes and how consent was captured.
5. Gross margin by category; top 20 SKUs by gross profit; dead stock older than 180 days.
6. Google Business Profile access, rating, review count; is inventory synced to Google (Merchant Center, Local Inventory app/Pointy)?
7. Online store, marketplaces (Etsy, eBay, Marketplace, Whatnot, TikTok Shop) and their fees; who fulfills.
8. Events run in the last 12 months and what each did to sales that day versus the same weekday.
9. Florists: wire services joined, monthly clearing statements, share of orders by phone, web, walk-in and wire.
10. Offers they will and will not run; who may change prices.

Then compute ([references/store-metrics.md](references/store-metrics.md)):

```
attraction rate = entries / passersby
conversion = transactions / entries                      (never transactions / passersby)
ATV = net sales / transactions        UPT = units / transactions        AUR = net sales / units
ATV = UPT x AUR
identified rate = transactions with a customer ID / all transactions
regular rate = identified customers with 4+ purchase days in 12 months / identified customers
second-purchase rate = first-time identified buyers with a 2nd purchase in 90 days / first-time buyers
GMROI = gross margin $ / average inventory at cost
sell-through = units sold / (opening units + units received)
discount break-even volume lift = d / (m - d)            (20% off at 50% margin needs +66.7% units)
event lift = event-day sales - same weekday average of prior 4 weeks
```

Report the three biggest gaps in money, never only as percentages.

## Benchmarks to check first

| Metric | Typical | Better | Source, year |
|---|---|---|---|

| Store conversion, fashion | 15 to 30% | n/a | Flame Analytics 2026 |
| Store conversion, apparel / luxury | 15 to 25% / 5 to 12% | n/a | Shopify 2026 citing Dor (secondary) |

| Transactions after launching email, SMS or loyalty | +5% vs prior 3 months | n/a | Square 2026, before/after, not causal |

No reliable public ATV or UPT benchmark exists for independent specialty shops: published figures mix chains, countries and online. Benchmark each shop against itself by weekday, daypart, staff member and category. Say so; never invent one. Full tables: [references/benchmarks.md](references/benchmarks.md).

## Decision rules, in order

1. **No count, no plan.** Two weeks of door tallies by hour before any campaign. Without entries you cannot tell a traffic problem from a selling problem.
2. **Entry rate under 15%:** fix the window, signage and door before buying reach. Test one window change at a time on matched dayparts. Put an exact price in the window.
3. **Conversion under about 15% (non-luxury):** fix greeting, staffing at peaks, out-of-stocks and checkout before marketing. Split conversion by staffed and understaffed hours.
4. **UPT flat:** build companions and attach lines per hero product; measure attach rate per category and per staff member.
5. **Identified rate under about 50%:** one capture prompt at checkout with a specific value exchange and one preference field. Localize consent.
6. **Second purchase is the money.** Welcome, post-purchase, back-in-stock and wishlist alerts before any newsletter. Trigger by category rhythm; one-off categories (antiques) trigger on matching new stock.
7. **Google:** correct GBP, holiday hours, review flow, then free local listings with a clean feed, then paid Local Inventory Ads on high-margin, high-intent SKUs only.
8. **Events need a partner who brings people,** a tagged link or code per partner, a baseline and an opt-in target. Judge on attributable sales and new identified buyers, never attendance.
9. **Discounts:** compute `d / (m - d)` first. Prefer an event-only add-on or gift with purchase over storewide percent off.
10. **Online and marketplaces:** extend the store's assortment and customer file. Inventory accuracy before online revenue. List on a marketplace only when contribution after all fees clears the owner's minimum dollar margin.
11. **Florists:** separate wire from owned orders in the P&L; every channel judged on contribution per order.

## Foot traffic in one screen

- **Calendar first.** Program the shop around flows that already exist: festivals, street fairs, school calendars, theater crowds. Partner to amplify, not replace.
- **Partner choice:** shared customer, different core purchase (bookshop x ceramicist, florist x bakery, vintage x tattoo artist, boutique x jeweler, gift shop x illustrator).
- **Monthly vendor market** in the lot or the shop, and a rotating local-artist vending slot; one operator credits 3 years of this with regulars who keep coming when posting lapses, while paid boosts gave one-time lifts (operators report, r/smallbusiness; "work with local vendors" was the 295-upvote top advice).
- **Receipt swap:** a cafe giving a free drink with food on a same-day boutique receipt got boutiques handing out its cards (operators report).
- **Gift a display to a neighbor** whose customers you want (florist to bookstore, 9,251-point r/florists post; florists gifting bouquets to luxury boutiques, 102-point comment).
- **Repeat-visit machine:** a $200 capsule vending machine with $0.50 vintage "mystery eggs" sells about 150 a month and pulls regulars in to buy more (operators report, 1,125 points).
- **Best day diagnostic:** find the best sales day in the last 90 days and what caused it before buying any marketing.

Playbook with event economics, window tests and signage: [references/foot-traffic.md](references/foot-traffic.md).

## Capture and regulars in one screen

- Ask once, at payment, with the value named: "Receipt by email? We also send one useful message a week: new arrivals, event tickets and first access to limited stock. Unsubscribe anytime."
- One preference field (women's, men's, gifts, books, furniture, flowers). It makes every later message relevant.
- Never all fields at once. Email or phone, then preference. Birthday later.
- Stamp cards and points did not turn passing boba customers into regulars (operators report, 107-upvote comment). Loyalty works for repeat categories; for one-off buyers use wishlists, VIP previews and events.
- Measure members who reach visit 4, not members enrolled.

Depth: [references/capture-and-loyalty.md](references/capture-and-loyalty.md). Flow mechanics, consent law and deliverability live in `email-sms-loyalty`.

## Opening or reopening a shop: first 30 days

1. **Google before the doors open:** GBP verified with signage photo, exact hours, products with prices; verification can take weeks (see `local-seo`).
2. **Capture from day one.** The first 500 identified customers are the base of every later event and flow. Track identified rate daily.
3. **Opening event with two or three partners,** each with its own code, scheduled on a day the street is already busy.
4. **Gift the neighbors:** a display or sample to the five businesses whose customers you want, with cards.
5. **Introductory prices labeled as introductory,** with an end date. Underpricing without a label stuck until the fifth or sixth customer for one owner (r/smallbusiness, 2,216 points).
6. **Do not plan on the previous tenant's customers.** A gym that took over a 20-year location reached 400 members against a 750 target; former members had gone elsewhere (r/smallbusiness, 1,353 points).
7. **Get any "free" ad trial terms in writing.** A new business was charged $130 early on a verbal Yelp promise (r/smallbusiness, 1,272 points).

## When practitioners and the data disagree

| Topic | Practitioners say | Data says | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Photo reviews | Photos rank higher (122-upvote comment) | No measured rank lift from photos | Ask for a photo for conversion; never as a rank tactic |
| Review incentives | "Coupon for a 5-star review" (511-point thread) | Google bans incentives; one shop lost 50+ reviews and got a 30-day block (2026) | Uniform ask, no reward, no in-store review kiosk |
| Loyalty cards | Stamp cards bring regulars back | Square: +5% transactions after loyalty launch (observational); boba operators saw nothing | Loyalty for repeat categories; wishlists and events for one-off |
| Posting volume | Post more when views drop | TikTok brand views -23% YoY while posting rose 40% (Socialinsider 2026) | Fewer, original posts; test hooks with Trial Reels |
| Discount events | Big sale weekend | 20% off at 50% margin needs +66.7% units | Event-only add-on or gift with purchase |
| Low offers on old stock | Clear it at any price | 50% returns on very low accepted offers (operators report) | Floor price per item; bundle or wholesale-lot the rest |

## Never

- Never buy, incentivize or gate reviews; never put a review tablet in the shop.
- Never run Local Inventory Ads before the feed is accurate; never advertise a sold one-off.
- Never send marketing texts or emails without channel-specific consent; UK, EU, Canada and Australia differ from the US.
- Never report event attendance, follower counts or gross marketplace sales as success.
- Never treat florist wire order value as owned revenue.
- Never borrow to fund more ads during a slump without proof ads produce orders (custom furniture shop borrowed $30,000 and ran out of money, 708-point thread).
- Never invent a benchmark, an offer the owner has not approved, or a customer quote.
