# Foot traffic: events, windows, signage, partnerships

Foot traffic has three separate problems: not enough people pass, people pass but do not enter, people enter but do not buy. This file covers the first two. Selling inside is in [in-store-selling.md](in-store-selling.md). Measure before choosing ([store-metrics.md](store-metrics.md)).

## Diagnose first

| Symptom | Likely problem | First move |
|---|---|---|
| Few passersby, good entry rate | Location or calendar | Partner events, borrowed audiences, Google inventory, destination product |
| Many passersby, entry under 15% | Window, sign, door, hours | Window and signage tests below |
| Entries fine, conversion low | Inside the store | [in-store-selling.md](in-store-selling.md) |
| Event days great, normal days flat | Regulars not captured | POS capture at events, follow-up flows |

If exterior traffic rises while entry rate falls, the storefront failed, not the marketing. If entries rise and transactions do not, the problem moved inside (research synthesis 2026).

## Events that pay

**Evidence.** P.F. Candle Co. reported weekend sales lifts of 17% (Echo Park), 34% (New York) and 31% (San Francisco) around partner pop-ups; the San Francisco store paired activations with larger neighborhood festivals (Modern Retail 2026, single-operator case). Square 2026: 32% of regulars are shared between businesses in the same ZIP code. Events work by borrowing an audience.

**Event types that bring new people**

| Type | Who brings the audience | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Maker or brand pop-up inside the shop | The maker's followers and list | Boutique, gift, vintage |
| Author talk, signing, book club launch | Author, publisher, club members | Bookshop |
| Workshop (bouquet bar, wreath, candle, styling, repair) | Paid tickets; instructor's followers | Florist, gift, vintage, boutique |
| Monthly vendor market in lot or shop | 6 to 15 vendors each promoting | Any shop with space; a retail owner runs one at least monthly (operators report, r/smallbusiness) |
| Rotating local-artist vending slot | The artist's audience every month | Gift, cafe-shop, bookshop |
| Festival or street-fair tie-in | The festival | All; plan around the calendar |
| Fan or community event with purchase gate | Fan group (boba shops run K-pop "cupsleeve" events: merch only with a drink purchase) | Gift, books, collectibles |
| Late opening on a theater, gallery-walk or concert night | The venue's crowd | Bookshop, gift |

**Owner-only events** ("customer appreciation night", "sip and shop" to the existing list) mostly reach people who would have come anyway. Run them for VIPs and launches, not for acquisition.

**Economics checklist (set before the event)**

1. Baseline: same weekday and hours over the prior 4 comparable weeks.
2. Target: new identified customers (email or phone with consent), e.g. 25 for a 2-hour pop-up.
3. Codes: one per partner (link with UTM, QR, spoken code). One POS discount or tag code for event sales.
4. Offer: an event-only add-on or gift with purchase above a threshold, not storewide percent off. A cheap high-margin item at checkout beats discounting the store (research synthesis 2026).
5. Capture: a signup with a reason to give details on the night (raffle of a store item for opted-in attendees where local law allows, early access list, tickets to the next event).
6. Cost: partner fee if any, food, staff overtime, print.
7. After: event lift, event margin, new identified, partner ranking, 90-day value. Repeat the top partner; drop events with no measurable path to a sale.

**Calendar rule.** Schedule when natural traffic is already rising (festival weekends, first warm Saturday, payday weekend, school holidays for family shops). Use the partner to amplify that flow.

**Event cadence for a small shop.** One acquisition event a month with a partner, one VIP moment a quarter, one big seasonal moment (holiday, Independent Bookstore Day, Mother's Day). More than this usually burns the owner without new customers.

## Windows

**Measure attraction, not beauty.** Flame Analytics 2026: attraction rate 15 to 35%. RetailNext's Balsam Brands case: merchandising tests raised entry rates about 8 to 10% year over year (vendor case).

**Test procedure**

1. Hold the promotion constant. Change one visual proposition.
2. Count passersby and entries on matched dayparts for at least 5 to 7 comparable trading days per version.
3. Keep the version with the higher entry rate. Log it with a photo.
4. Rotate a new test every 2 to 4 weeks; seasonal changes count as tests.

**Window rules that raise entry**

- **One hero idea, readable in 3 seconds from across the street.** A wall of product reads as "a shop", not a reason.
- **An exact price in the window.** Florists: show what a ready-to-buy price gets you ("$45 today"). Gift shops: "Gifts under $30".
- **A specific curation, not a category.** Bookshop: "Five novels for people who hated their book club's last pick". Vintage: era, provenance or scarcity ("1970s Danish teak, one of each").
- **Movement or light after dark.** Evening passersby see a lit window; a dark shop window reads as closed.
- **Show the inside.** Keep the sightline to the counter and people inside clear. An empty-looking store loses entries.

## Signage

- **A-frame on the pavement** with one line that changes daily (new arrivals, today's event, weather hook). Check local permit rules.
- **"Open" visible from both directions of the street.** Hours on the door, including holiday hours, matching Google.
- **Projecting or blade sign** where the frontage is not visible from the walking line.
- **Exterior QR** to the shop's Google product listings or "what's new this week" page for after-hours passersby.
- **Car and van branding** for shops that deliver (florists, furniture, antiques): an agency owner's $100 car stickers brought a barter client who referred others (operators report, r/smallbusiness, 442 points).

## Partnerships

**Pick partners by shared customer, different core purchase** (research synthesis 2026):

| Shop | Good partners |
|---|---|
| Bookshop | Cafe, ceramicist, stationery maker, theater, school, book clubs, local authors |
| Florist | Bakery, wine shop, wedding venues, funeral homes, realtors, restaurants, med spas, upscale boutiques |
| Boutique | Jeweler, salon, nail studio, cafe, alterations, photographer |
| Gift shop | Illustrator, candle maker, card designer, cafe, hotel concierge |
| Vintage and antiques | Tattoo artist, record shop, interior designer, home stager, realtor, film and photo shoots |

**Mechanisms, smallest first**

1. **Receipt swap.** A cafe gave a free drink with food on a same-day boutique receipt and supplied cards for boutiques to hand out; boutiques now promote it and send customers and staff, especially Fridays (operators report, r/smallbusiness, 258 points). Reverse it for a shop: a same-day cafe receipt gets a small gift with a $40 purchase.
2. **Gifted display.** A florist made a Dr. Seuss arrangement for a local bookstore to get seen (r/florists, 9,251 points; no sales reported). A higher-end florist gifted bouquets to businesses its luxury customers already use (102-upvote comment). Leave a card and a QR.
3. **Co-event** with split promotion and shared results.
4. **Product collaboration** (a bookshop tote with a local illustrator, a florist and bakery Mother's Day box).
5. **Resident packets.** Apartment complexes distribute annual resident packets and flyers for a small buy-in (operators report, r/smallbusiness). Property managers can email residents an offer; rank buildings by distance and units (gym operator thread, 1,353 points, 117-upvote comment).
6. **Overflow referrals with a non-competing specialist.** A repair shop got its biggest sale ($3,000, paid within an hour) from a specialist it had befriended months earlier (r/smallbusiness, 1,047 points).

Ask each partner to repost your original post rather than upload a copy; Instagram keeps attribution and can show the reposted original to the reposter's followers (Meta, Aug 2025).

## Shop-local campaigns

- **Small Business Saturday** (US, Canada: Saturday after US Thanksgiving; UK: first Saturday of December). Calendar in [holiday-calendar.md](holiday-calendar.md).
- **American Express Shop Small** runs a merchant resource hub and recommended 6M+ businesses to 19.5M card members in 2025, crediting $14M of "new spend" by its own 90/30-day definition (Amex 2026). Check eligibility on the hub. Amex says its historical Small Business Saturday spending figures are survey projections, not receipts (Amex 2026). Use the materials; do not quote the totals as measured.
- **Chamber, BID and council campaigns** (passport stamps, trails, late nights). Join the ones with a mechanism (stamp passport, shared map, shared ad) and ask for last year's footfall numbers.
- **What works is referral infrastructure:** 75% of consumers say community social recommendations influence which small business they visit; 81% of Millennials and Gen Z found an independent business through social media in the past year (American Express 2025, commissioned survey).

## Requests for donations and sponsorships

- **Route donation requests to a written process.** One owner's web form (one year's notice, tax ID, amount, contact, a 500-word essay on how it relates to the business) took requests to zero (r/smallbusiness, 1,092 points). Softer version: a quarterly donation budget with a short form.
- **Test sponsorship small.** Offer $25 instead of a $500 "Bronze" package and ask for a short video filmed in the shop for the school's or club's social accounts, which often have a few thousand local followers (same thread, proposed, not measured).

## Tricks most owners miss

- **Different code per partner,** or you will never know whose audience came.
- **The refillable vase is a traffic contract:** 200 visits a week from one product (SAF 2025). Every niche can build one: book club pickup night, vintage "first look" Thursdays, alteration pickup, refill station, plant clinic.
- **Mystery capsule machine** for vintage, antiques and gift: about $200 setup, $0.50 per egg, about 150 eggs a month; regulars stop in "for an egg" and buy more. Free egg over $100. Add an "EGG RETURN" drawer and organza bags (r/Flipping, 1,125 points).
- **Closing-time crowds:** when a theater lets out 15 minutes before close, station staff at the door with "If you know what you want, Barb will get it for you" so buyers buy and browsers do not wreck the tidied floor (r/retail, 1,439 points).
- **Do not chase tourists without a reason:** a coffee shop owner chose the highest-rent spot because 3 million tourists a year pass and pay with less price resistance (operators report, r/smallbusiness, 868 points). Rent per expected entry, not rent per square foot, is the comparison.
