# Florists: wire services, pricing, subscriptions and events

Florists need their own P&L view: gross order value hides channel economics that can destroy margin once flowers, labor, delivery and remakes are counted.

## Wire-service and network economics

| Program | Who keeps what | Source, year |
|---|---|---|
| FTD transmitted order | Filling florist about 71 to 73% of order value; sending florist 20%; FTD clearinghouse 7 to 9%; other standard fees may apply | FTD Member Standards 2025 |
| FTD Local Order Program | Florist keeps 80% and pays $3.99 per order; driver expects pickup within 60 minutes | FTD, current page |
| BloomNation Florist-to-Florist | Free to send; fulfilling florist keeps 90%; no hidden fees under that program | BloomNation Help Center, updated Oct 2026 |
| Teleflora | Current full member economics are not verifiable publicly; indexed numbers are pre-2024 | Use the shop's own agreement and monthly clearing statement |

FTD's two programs differ. Never mix them in one model.

### Channel P&L (compute for every channel, monthly)

```
net payout to florist           (order value x share kept, minus per-order fees)
- floral COGS                   (stems at actual wholesale, incl. shrink)
- container and add-ons
- design labor                  (minutes x loaded hourly cost)
- delivery labor and mileage
- remake and refund allowance
- payment and platform fees
= contribution per order
```

**Worked example (illustrative arithmetic).** A $75 incoming FTD order, filler keeps 72% = $54. Flowers at the SAF 33% ceiling on the $75 retail value = $24.75, vase $6, 15 minutes design at $24/hour = $6, delivery $9, 3% remake allowance = $1.62. Contribution = $54 - $47.37 = $6.63, about 9% of the consumer price. The same arrangement sold direct at $75 with 3% card fees contributes about $25. Same flowers, nearly four times the money.

The trap this creates: a filler who shrinks the recipe to protect contribution hurts the recipient's experience, and the recipient is a future local customer.

### What to do with wire

1. Separate wire-in, wire-out and owned orders in the books.
2. Compute contribution per order for each. Keep wire-in only where it fills idle design and delivery capacity (weekdays, off-season) and covers labor.
3. Raise owned demand where wire-in is losing: Google profile and local inventory for standard arrangements, a fixed-price website menu, phone staffed at peaks.
4. Turn wire recipients into owned customers only where service agreements and platform rules allow: a discreet "designed locally by [shop]" card where permitted, and a care card with the shop's details.
5. Compare alternatives: BloomNation F2F (90% kept), FTD Local Order (80% minus $3.99), and direct florist-to-florist relationships in other cities.

## Pricing arrangements

- **SAF profitability targets** (May 2025, from Paul Goodman's work with hundreds of shops): arrangement COGS at most 33% of retail; payroll including owner pay, taxes and benefits at most 30% of sales; facilities about 10% or less. Management targets, not averages.
- **3x wholesale flowers, plus vase, plus hourly labor.** A Dallas-Fort Worth florist priced a birthday arrangement at $300: about $80 wholesale flowers, $25 vase, labor at $35 an hour (r/florists, 2,754 points).
- **Price stem by stem before quoting premium mixes.** A pictured bouquet had $134 in listed stems (3 stargazer lilies $36, 3 hydrangeas $24, 7 roses $42, 3 snapdragons $12, 5 hypericum $20) before vase, greens and labor; florists valued it at $175 to $200 and said $100 loses money (r/florists, 6,610 points).
- **Fixed-price designer's choice menu** with a minimum order set by the minimum viable flower spend: one florist starts at $100, shows a visible menu, and reports customers keep returning (r/florists, 886 points).
- **Stretch with greens** to hit a price point: a $35 flower budget can become 2 to 3 arrangements that each look worth $35 (r/florists, 1,763 points, proposed).
- **Cut what buyers do not value.** A $678 150-rose heart had a $100+ handmade stand; prospects ghosted after quotes. Commenters advised a cheaper base since the roses carry the value (r/florists, 886 points).
- **Respect the request in the design.** Lilies open and bury lower hydrangeas; place mini hydrangeas higher so requested flowers stay visible (r/florists, 1,628 points).
- **Budget objection:** offer a smaller scope at the same rate, never a lower rate ([scripts.md](scripts.md#budget-objection)).

## Weddings and events

- Sell the look and color palette, not exact stems; keep substitution rights in the contract (SAF wedding-margin guidance, May 2025).
- Wholesale costs move between contract and date. One wedding florist running a 10% margin raised prices on new weddings ahead of tariffs but expected to lose money on contracts already signed (r/smallbusiness, 1,148 points). Add a cost-escalation clause or a flower-cost review date to new contracts.
- Source from local farms where possible to reduce exposure (same thread).

## Repeat revenue: subscriptions and standing programs

- **Refillable vase program:** Kennedy's Flowers & Gifts logged 11,000+ refills and $182,000+ in extra sales in 2024, with about 200 customers a week coming in; SAF's 2025 Marketer of the Year. The product is a traffic contract with a cross-sell on every visit.
- **Subscriptions:** UrbanStems reports subscriber LTV about $500 vs $250 (Shopify case, single company).
- **Local B2B standing orders:** weekly office or lobby flowers, restaurant bud vases, hotel reception, realtor closing gifts, funeral home relationships, med spa and salon counters. Group accounts on the same delivery day and route.
- Pitch: [scripts.md](scripts.md#florist-standing-order-pitch). Start with four weeks, cancel anytime, three budget options.

## Getting seen locally

- **Gift arrangements to businesses whose customers you want** (luxury boutiques, med spas, bookstores). A Seuss-themed piece for a bookstore drew 9,251 points on r/florists; a higher-end florist credits gifting plus small events and frequent posting for getting known (102-upvote comment). Leave cards.
- **Mother's Day data:** 38% of Americans bought flowers or plants in 2025, local florists got 23% of purchases (up from 15% in 2022), average spend $71 (SAF/Ipsos 2025). Orders came 39% by phone, 37% web, 23% walk-in. Staff the phone.
- **Windows with exact prices** ("$45 today, ready to go") turn passersby into walk-ins.
- **Social hook that works:** "What $50, $75 and $100 actually buys from a local florist this week."
- **Distinctive sympathy work** (armatures, ornamental cabbage, $125 "unique with pink and white") stands out for families asking for something different (r/florists, 903 points).

## Tricks most owners miss

- Put wire-out commissions (the 20% sender share) in the P&L as income: website orders for other cities can be sent out profitably.
- Track remakes by channel; wire-in remakes are often the hidden loss.
- Build occasion reminders from every order ("Remind me next year?"), the florist's best second-purchase trigger.
- Spray-painting greens to match a strict palette widened options for a budget order; it shortens vase life, so tell the customer (r/florists, 24,808 points).
