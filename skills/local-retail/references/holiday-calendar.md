Build the year around real traffic peaks and the shop's own best weeks. Pull the last two years of POS sales by week first; that is the true calendar. Then add the dates below.

## What the national numbers say

- 202.9M US shoppers between Thanksgiving and Cyber Monday 2025; 129.5M shopped in stores (+3%). Small Business Saturday drew 62.7M in-store shoppers (61.1M in 2024). NRF had forecast 67M: never report a forecast as the result (NRF, Dec 2025).
- 92% of holiday shoppers planned store visits and 52% planned BOPIS (ICSC 2025).
- US holiday retail spend +4.2%, ecommerce +7.8% and 27% of holiday retail; UK +3.6% and +5.8% (Visa 2025).
- More than half of physical small-business owners expected Small Business Saturday to bring up to 15% of annual revenue (American Express 2025): an owner estimate, not receipts.
- Mother's Day 2025: 38% of Americans bought flowers or plants; local florists' share rose to 23%; average spend $71 (SAF/Ipsos).

## Holiday plan, week by week

**8 to 6 weeks before the peak**

- Pick the 20 to 40 SKUs most likely to be bought as gifts (operating choice, not a benchmark). Order depth and substitutes.
- Build price-point collections (under $25, $50, $100) in store and online.
- Make sure every hero has Google local inventory and accurate titles.
- Photograph actual stock. Write staff scripts and the substitute matrix.
- Lock partner events and the Small Business Saturday plan with neighbors.
- Florists: wire-service holiday minimums, delivery zones, cut-off times, staffing.

**4 weeks before**

- Turn on BOPIS or reservations if available; "order online, collect today" on reliable high-intent pages.
- Pre-write back-in-stock, last-local-pickup and last-delivery emails and texts.
- VIP preview hour invitation goes out.
- Gift cards placed inside product displays, not only at the till.

**Thanksgiving week and Small Business Saturday (UK, EU: your own peak weekend)**

- Paid spend only behind proven products and events.
- Experienced staff on expected conversion peaks. Second till or mobile POS.
- Capture at checkout every time: new holiday buyers are next year's regulars.
- Shop-local tie-in with neighbors: shared map, stamp passport, cross-promoted posts.
- An event-only gift with purchase above a threshold instead of a storewide discount. Run the break-even first: 20% off at a 50% margin needs 66.7% more units ([store-metrics.md](store-metrics.md)).

**Last ten days**

- Switch the message from discount to certainty: "In stock here today", "Pickup in two hours", "Gift wrapped", "We can recommend this in five minutes". Start the "last-minute local" campaign when parcel cut-offs pass.
- Extended hours, set as special hours on Google.
- Gift receipts by default.

**After the holiday**

- Do not train new customers to wait for markdowns. Send an exchange-friendly message and a category-specific second-purchase prompt.
- Gift-card redemption visits are acquisition: greet, capture, show new arrivals.
- Review the season: sales by week vs last year, new identified customers, second purchases by end of January, best and worst partner events.

## Tricks most owners miss

- **"In stock here" is the holiday ad hook** for Google and social once delivery dates slip.
- **Holiday hours on Google** weeks ahead; mismatches cost "open now" visits.
- **Independent Bookstore Day exclusives** are ordered months ahead: for 2027, ABA's catalog went live 24 Sept 2026 and ordering closes 5 Nov 2026. Put next year's order date in the calendar now.
- **Mothering Sunday and US Mother's Day are two months apart.** Florists with UK and US customers run two campaigns.
- **Florists: phone still matters.** 39% of Mother's Day 2025 orders came by phone vs 37% web (SAF). Staff the phone at peaks.
