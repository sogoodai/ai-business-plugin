# Scripts

Replace brackets. Keep the owner's voice: read it aloud; if the owner would not say it, rewrite it. Where wording comes from a source, the source is named. Unlabeled templates were written for this skill from the mechanisms in the other references. Every marketing text needs consent for that channel and the opt-out line your country requires.

## POS capture

At payment (research synthesis 2026):

> "Would you like your receipt by email? We also send one useful message a week: new arrivals, event tickets and first access to limited stock. You can unsubscribe anytime."

SMS (research synthesis 2026):

> "Want first text access when [high-demand category] arrives? It's usually one or two texts a month. Reply STOP anytime."

Preference follow-up (one tap or one word):

> "Great. Should we send you mostly [women's / men's / gifts / books / furniture / flowers]?"

Wishlist slip at the counter:

> "If it's not here today, tell me what you're hunting for and I'll text you the day one comes in. [Era / size / author / color]?"

Event signup card:

> "Leave your email for early access to the next [maker] drop and tickets to our [month] event. One email a week, no spam."

## Review request

Counter card or receipt QR, and the day 3 to 5 email (research synthesis 2026, within Google's rules):

> "Thanks for shopping with us. If you found what you needed, would you mind sharing what you bought and how your visit went on Google? It helps nearby shoppers know what to expect. Here's the direct link: [link]"

Add a photo prompt:

> "A photo of what you picked up helps people see what we carry."

Never add a discount, gift, entry or points. Never send the ask only to happy customers.

## Neighbor partnership pitch

In person or by email, owner to owner (research synthesis 2026):

> "We share customers but don't sell the same thing. I'd like to run a two-hour [theme] event on [date]. We provide the space and checkout; you bring [demo / product / audience]. We each promote it twice to our own list and social audience. No booth fee. We'll exchange post-event results (attendance, new opt-ins and attributed sales) so we know whether to repeat it."

Receipt swap:

> "Your customers and mine overlap. Want to try a simple swap for a month? Anyone who shows a same-day receipt from you gets [small gift] with a [$40] purchase here, and I'll do the same for yours. I'll print the cards. We compare redemptions at the end of the month."

Gifted display drop-off (florist, gift maker):

> "We made this for your counter, no strings. If customers ask, the card has our details. If you'd ever like a standing arrangement for the shop, I'd be glad to quote one."

Artist or maker vending slot:

> "We give one local artist the [front table / Saturday market spot] each month at no fee. You bring your work and tell your followers; we handle checkout and keep [X%]. Interested in [month]?"

> [Name], your mum's birthday was around this week last year. Want me to put together something like last time? [Price range] works?

## Staff greeting and add-on lines

Greeting (within 30 seconds, not "can I help you?"):

> "Hi, welcome in. Everything on the front table came in this week."

> "Hi, take your time. Are you shopping for yourself or for a gift today?"

Gift path:

> "Who's it for, and roughly what would you like to spend? I can show you three things in two minutes."

Add-on lines (research synthesis 2026):

> Bookshop: "Do you want the edition most people buy with this as a gift? It's $14."

> Florist: "Would you like the vase-ready version for $18 more, or wrapped at the current price?"

> Boutique: "Most people wearing this trouser need a shorter top proportion. Want to see the two that work best?"

> Gift shop: "This is one of the items people usually pair with the card and gift wrap. Want me to show you?"

Out of stock:

> "We're out of that size here, but I can order it now and have it [shipped to you / ready Thursday]. Takes a minute."

Closing time with a late crowd (r/retail, 1,439 points):

> "We're about to close. If you know what you want, Barb will be happy to get it for you."

## Budget objection

Custom work (r/smallbusiness, 319 points):

> "I understand. What budget range are you working with? Sometimes I can adjust the scope to fit."

> "I can definitely work within that budget. At $[X], I'd recommend we focus on [specific piece]. You'd get [outcome] without the [removed elements]. Would that work?"

Cheaper competing quote (same thread):

> "Does their quote include [specific thing]? What's their revision policy?" then "I'm confident in my pricing because [one sentence on value]. But if they're a better fit for your budget, I completely understand."

## Price hold

Claimed cheaper elsewhere (r/smallbusiness, 1,175 points, repair shop):

> "The lowest I've done is $[X], and that was for several items at once."

Short cash at pickup (r/Flipping, 1,293 points):

> "Cash only. If you need cash there is an ATM down the street."

Introductory pricing that does not stick (r/smallbusiness, 2,216 points):

> "This is our introductory pricing." / "Right now I'm not too busy as we're just starting out, so I can get to this right away."

## Donation and sponsorship requests

Redirect (r/smallbusiness, 1,092 points):

> "Oh wow, that sounds so cool! So the process for donations is this: [link to form]."

Small test sponsorship:

> "We can do $25 this year. In return, could your students film a short video in the shop for your Instagram? If it brings people in, we'll do more next year."

## Florist standing-order pitch

To offices, restaurants, salons, hotels (research synthesis 2026):

> "We already deliver in your area on [days]. I'd like to quote a simple standing flower program: one fixed budget, one invoice, seasonal designs, and automatic replacement on [day]. We can start with four weeks and stop anytime. Would you prefer a $75, $125 or $200 arrangement?"

Wire recipient card (where the network allows):

> "Designed and delivered by [shop], your local florist on [street]. Care tips inside. Come by for a free refresh of the water and stems this week."

## Back in stock and last-minute holiday

> [Shop]: The [item] is back. We're holding [3] for list members until [time] tomorrow. Reply HOLD. Reply STOP to opt out

> Too late to ship? We have [category] in stock today, gift wrapped while you wait. Open till [time]. [Address]

## Complaints

> "I'm sorry, how can we fix this for you?" (r/smallbusiness, 485 points; refund at once if they ask for money or stay unpleasant)

Missing item on an order (r/Flipping, 1,418 points):

> "I'm sorry, I can't find the [item] you ordered. I have [comparable item] in the same size and condition. Would you like that instead, or a full refund?"
