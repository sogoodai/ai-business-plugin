# Store page, trailer and demo

The page converts traffic from every other channel. In one Next Fest, 84% of wishlist additions came from people who never played the demo, and operators say 68 to 88% is normal (r/gamemarketing 19, 2026). That 84% decided on capsule, trailer and page.

## Audit order (fix in this order)

1. Capsule at thumbnail size: genre and fantasy readable by a stranger in 3 seconds.
2. First five tags: specific subgenres, not "Indie", "Singleplayer", "Casual" (r/gamemarketing 37 comment).
3. First line of the short description.
4. First 5 seconds of the first trailer.
5. First four screenshots.
6. Social links (Discord, X, YouTube, TikTok) on the page: 42% of unreleased pages link none; median followers 7 with none vs 56 with five or more (correlation, r/gamemarketing 339). Two minutes of work.
7. Long description with 60 fps WebM clips.

Village Merchant had neither trailer nor demo at first; after adding both, daily wishlists rose about 10x (r/gamemarketing 15). A developer's priority list after a successful first game: capsule, trailer, screenshots, description (r/gamedev 1,941).

## Capsule

- Approval test: five cold viewers from the genre audience name the genre or fantasy at thumbnail size. Aesthetic preference does not count.
- Use capsule art to amplify a plain-looking game: Cultist Simulator's capsule keeps the game's style but is far more detailed than any in-game card (r/gamemarketing 39).
- Keep the focus off the exact center and the title clear of the demo banner area; the 2026 carousels show neighbors' edges and crop sides.
- Test off-platform: run the same ad copy and audience with two images, judge CTR first, then Steam UTM visit-to-wishlist yield. Steam has no native capsule A/B test, and sequential swaps are confounded ([Steam Page Analyzer, 2026](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/steam-page-ab-testing), vendor method).
- One older case: a key-art change preceded +119.9% CTR, +145% visitors and +150% daily wishlists (HTMAG, 2021, single game, outdated).
- Promo art: a $150 commission briefed with a rough sketch and notes; ask for sketch-to-final progress shots to rule out undisclosed AI (r/IndieDev 8,052; 346-upvote comment on sketches cutting revisions).

## Tags

- Build the list from 5 to 10 comparable successful games' tags plus player interviews. One team ran two rounds of player interviews, then chose Survival, Adventure, Zombies as primary and Post-apocalyptic, Single player, Crafting, Multiple endings as secondary (r/gamemarketing 22).
- The Personal Calendar and More Like This match on tags and similar players. A wrong top tag now sends the game to the wrong players.

## Short and long description

- Short description template: three verb-plus-evocative-noun commands that sell the fantasy. Cultist Simulator: "Seize forbidden treasures. Summon alien gods. Feed on your disciples." (r/gamemarketing 39; about 160,000 wishlists).
- Lead the long description with a divisive design constraint if the game has one ("a game with zero handholding") to qualify buyers and cut refunds.
- Replace GIFs with 60 fps WebM clips (operators report a 12-second limit per clip; export VP9 WebM, e.g. from Shotcut). 12 fps GIFs made viewers think the game lagged, the opposite of its "hundreds of enemies" hook (r/gamemarketing 38).
- Say plainly what is playable now vs planned. Fantasy World Manager's 19.8% refunds partly came from players expecting the automated mode scheduled for later (r/gamedev 823).
- Answer the purchase objections up front: Mac or Steam Deck, controller, co-op mode, length, language list.

## Screenshots

- Before features exist, stage screenshots by hand: place objects and names in the editor to show the intended loop (Mortal Glory, r/gamedev 1,941). Show the real game when it is real.
- Editor screenshots and concept-art capsules were enough for 900 wishlists on day one when a clip had already created demand (r/gamedev 2,332).
- The 2026 checklist needs at least five gameplay-tagged screenshots.
- Lead with the game's most distinctive moment. A viewer reported dismissing a tower defense from screenshots, then wanting it after seeing 10,000 orcs move (r/gamedev 2,332).
- Make every image prove the pitch. "Mining Hell" drew a visit, but the page showed space, ships and turrets; the visitor saw no hell (r/IndieDev 8,205).

## Trailer

Structure (operating template, not a Valve rule):

| Time | Show |
|---|---|
| 0 to 3 s | The strongest playable fantasy, no logos |
| 3 to 10 s | The core interaction or loop |
| 10 to 25 s | Escalation and variety |
| 25 to 45 s | Systems, co-op, social proof |
| End card | Title, platforms, "Wishlist on Steam", date or window |

Rules from the data:
- No publisher logos, black screens with text, or personal story first. Dawnfolk's trailer spent 22 seconds on the developer's story; a viewer with a friend "both cringed" (r/godot 3,130, 143-upvote comment). Removing text-heavy openings and speeding up got one game into the showcase that produced 8,500 wishlists after earlier rejections (r/gamemarketing 350).
- Show the real activity mix early. A one-minute preview spent 20 seconds in a boat; viewers took it for a sailing game. Cut to 7 to 10 seconds and show on-foot play (r/Unity3D 1,139).
- One loop, one fantasy. SKINATOR's "Slay demons and WEAR their flesh to grow in power", shown as fight, steal limb, equip limb, become stronger, fixed "looks cool but I have no idea what I'm looking at" and beat the previous game's 1.5-year wishlist total in four months (r/gamemarketing 29).
- A familiar comparison plus a twist explains instantly: "Marble Madness clone" with standout art (r/gamedev 936), "Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes with blind, deaf and mute monkeys" (r/gamemarketing 145).
- Never use a non-playable camera as the hook. A three-quarter trailer camera beat top-down so strongly that 95% of comments asked for it, which forced a choice between redoing marketing and rebuilding controls (r/IndieDev 3,247).
- Hold back footage. A team used nearly everything in the announcement trailer and had nothing new to post afterwards (r/gamemarketing 72).
- A staged live-action "fail" cut into gameplay worked for Totally Reliable Delivery Service's launch (r/indiegames 1,400). Only if it ends on real gameplay.
- A professional reveal trailer paid off for Outbound: about 400,000 YouTube views and 100,000 wishlists in two weeks (r/gamemarketing 19).

Trailer test: put $5 to $10 of YouTube ads behind each cut and compare audience-retention curves under Engagement. One action-first cut dropped at 5 to 6 seconds while a loop-first cut held. Stop near 1,000 views; more spend bought little extra data (r/gamemarketing 83). Then check which cut drives Steam UTM wishlists.

Make the first 5 to 10 seconds work alone: the 2026 Steam home page autoplays micro-trailers in discovery modules.

## Demo

- Purpose: convert exposure into wishlists and feed creators. Daily wishlists after demo releases in the data: 5,000+ on day two, then 1,000 to 2,000 a day (r/gamedev 2,332); 10x after adding trailer and demo (r/gamemarketing 15).
- Put the distinctive mechanic in the first minutes. A team that entered Next Fest with 8,000 wishlists said the familiar opening hid the interesting mechanics and planned a vertical slice next time (r/gamemarketing 19).
- Time it from a first-timer's recording, not from testers who know the controls. A 40-minute YouTube session showed the demo ending just as the player understood the mechanics (r/gamedev 5,983).
- End screen: show the full game's locked options instead of a thank-you screen (ΔV: Rings of Saturn), then the wishlist button.
- Wishlist button on the main menu and on completion, including in itch.io web demos (at least 15 wishlists in two days from one, r/gamemarketing 15).
- Instrument: demo_start, tutorial_complete, minute_5, first_core_loop_complete, demo_end, wishlist_click. Median playtime around 10 minutes with 50+ testers signals an early wall (r/gamemarketing 111). Web players leave sooner than Steam players; read them separately.
- Use creator traffic before Fest to find friction. After patches, one team's average playtime rose about 10 minutes, co-op median reached about 17 minutes and 18%+ relaunched the demo (r/gamemarketing 27).
- Turn the demo into recurring news: add content, announce each update everywhere (r/gamemarketing 23); one team released a demo update, a new trailer and a devlog on the same day, then emailed 100+ creators and 50+ outlets (r/gamemarketing 30).
- Do not crunch a rushed demo for a festival deadline: a rushed horror-fest demo brought 305 wishlists in two weeks and little festival traffic (r/gamemarketing 30).
- A demo after an Early Access launch can still open a new channel: a Douyin streamer found one released a year after EA, and daily sales broke records the following week (r/gamedev 1,317).
- Skipping a demo is fine when the trailer shows the whole proposition of a small game (Marble's Marbles, r/gamedev 936).
- Itch-first: release the demo on itch.io, spend a month on QA from comments and ratings, then put it on Steam (r/gamemarketing 34).

## Playtests

- An open Steam playtest found by creators without outreach: 200 concurrent players day one, then 100k-view videos and about 1,000 wishlists a day (r/gamedev 2,332).
- Closed playtests with two keys per participant to share brought 1,500 players and 200 surveys (r/gamemarketing 23).
