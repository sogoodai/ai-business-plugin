# Pricing, launch timing and after launch

For price psychology in general, see `offers-pricing`. This file is about premium PC and console games.

## Setting the price

- Price against 5 to 10 comparable games in the same subgenre, scope and art quality, then sanity-check hours of content. Cheap Steam games are short: of 52 games under AUD 5 played in 2025, about 30% rolled credits within 2 hours, 80% within 8, none past 10 (r/gamedev 815).
- Small, tight games at low prices did well: Bunny Park, 5 hours at $5, about $30k in year one (r/gamedev 2,282).
- Price can block conversion: WarriOrb at $17.99 (17% off to get under $15) converted about 1.1% of wishlisters by day three, and its developer would launch under $10 next time (r/gamedev 3,385, hypothesis). Commenters on a 300-wishlist launch that sold 4 copies suggested $3 to $5 for a simple loop, while another cited Chris Zukowski's advice to raise prices (r/gamemarketing 59).
- Rule: do not cut price to fix a positioning or audience problem. Check page conversion and refund comments first. Lower price only when comparables at the same scope are clearly cheaper.
- Use Valve's suggested regional pricing as the base; review China, Turkey, Argentina and other low-price regions against refund rate and key resale.
- Early Access price: set it below planned 1.0 price and say so; Early Access transparency does not excuse a bad first session (Fantasy World Manager, 19.8% refunds at $15 with 10% off, r/gamedev 823).

## Launch discount

- Valve suggests 10 to 15%; allowed 10 to 40% for 7 to 14 days. No evidence that deeper launch discounts buy algorithmic reach (GameDiscoverCo).
- Keep the 20% wishlist-email threshold for a later beat: a seasonal sale or a major update.
- After launch, successful games commonly start discounting early: first discount after a median 48 days, usually 20% (selected sample of 519 hits, SteamSage 2026).

## Release date

- Set a specific date at least 60 days out (Personal Calendar), confirm at least 3 weeks out for front-page eligibility, and never slip more than 24 hours inside the final two weeks.
- Avoid: Steam seasonal sales (a shop sim launched in the Spring Sale for Popular Upcoming and sold about 300 on day one then fell off, r/gamemarketing 15; Marble's Marbles moved its launch out of the winter sale, r/gamedev 936), Next Fest week (r/gamemarketing 59), big-name launches in the same genre, and the weeks of major showcases.
- Reveals can ride the week after a showcase: TOWERPUNK revealed the week after gamescom, got a US Labor Day weekend tail and passed 3,000 wishlists in a week vs a 100 to 300 expectation (r/gamemarketing 56). Check competing news before scheduling trailers and dates (Eastshade, r/gamedev 2,013).
- Console: confirm certification before announcing a hard date (Xbox recommends planning six months out, two months for test and resubmission).

## Launch week checklist

1. Build approved, checklist done (5+ minutes of Steam playtime, 5+ gameplay screenshots).
2. Launch discount set (10 to 15%), override art scheduled.
3. Creators keyed two weeks earlier, embargo or "go live at launch" agreed.
4. Trailer premiere offered to IGN/GameTrailers two to three weeks earlier.
5. Discord, mailing list, Steam announcement, socials scheduled for the same hour.
6. Five real wishlisters confirm the launch email; support ticket if not.
7. Day 1 to 3: answer every review and bug report, hotfix fast, ask fixed players for reviews.
8. Day 3: read refund reasons and country split; fix the expectation gap in the page or trailer the same week.

## Early Access

- One Update Visibility Round pool (five) is shared with 1.0. Spend none on patches.
- EA buyers judge the current build, not the roadmap. Put the roadmap on the page, but make the first hour complete.
- A demo released a year after EA launch still found a new audience (r/gamedev 1,317).

## After launch: the long game

- A launch that misses is not over. Treat the game as a product you grow: updates, discounts, ports, creators, bundles (r/gamemarketing 113).
- Use each substantial update as a new press and creator pitch (r/Unity3D 2,441 comment), with a visibility round, a 20%+ discount and an artwork override.
- Small post-launch discoveries with achievements give players a reason to return (Find My Frogs, r/indiegames 1,822). Free bonus DLC (printable coloring pages of the game's scenes) earned goodwill.
- Free games advertise paid ones: 85% of $100k lifetime Steam revenue came from the paid title promoted inside the developer's free games (r/IndieDev 3,877).
- A short free first game teaches the release process and builds a following: The Children of Clay passed 17,000 players and 1,000+ reviews in a month (r/IndieDev 7,008).
- Live events: a sponsor-backed tournament with streamed, commentated matches and finals timed with the end of the Autumn Sale (r/gamedev 2,210).
- Shared community goals keep players coming back: a 1,000,000-level target with teased milestones (r/gamedev 3,325).

## Price objections in public

When someone says the price is too high, do not argue. Thank them, suggest wishlisting for later, and if the store allows coupons (itch.io, your own store, a book), drop a public code. A Godot shader book author's reply "<3 No worries, feel free to save it for later! Here's a coupon code in case it helps" led to two confirmed purchases (r/godot 3,773). Steam has no public coupon codes; point to the next discount instead.
