# Pricing, bundles and licenses

Digital products cost nothing to copy, so price carries the whole margin and the whole quality signal. Pricing psychology, guarantees and price-increase rollouts are in `offers-pricing`; this file covers digital-specific ladders, licenses and checkout mechanics. Fee math: [economics.md](economics.md).

## Set the first price

1. **Start from the job's value, not creation time.** Operators recommend $27 to $97 for problem-solving products sold to an audience and say they priced too low at first (r/passive_income, 866 pts); £9 to £27 for plug-and-play kits usable in 10 minutes (r/Entrepreneur, 251 pts). A €50 to €100 client-acquisition guide is framed against one extra client worth hundreds or thousands (r/sidehustle, 1,109 pts).
2. **Check the fee floor.** Under about $9, fixed fees take 14 to 29% on Etsy and Gumroad ([benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md)). A $5 printable on Etsy needs 245 orders for $1,000 a month; a $15 one needs 76.
3. **Read the marketplace band.** On Etsy, sort competitors for the exact query and note the price of listings with many reviews; price at or slightly above the middle if your preview is stronger. On Creative Market, the minimums set the floor.
4. **Test the middle.** €5, then €12, settled at €9 (r/Entrepreneur, 1,530 pts). Change one price at a time and hold it 3 to 4 weeks or 100+ checkout visits.
5. **Cut only with evidence of price resistance.** A $10 cut three weeks after launch, on a video priced at nearly twice the companion ebook, unlocked hesitant buyers (r/Entrepreneur, 1,532 pts). Strong traffic with no sales is more often a preview or fit problem than price.

Stan's 2025 means ($51 downloads, $96 courses, $191 calls) skew upward by large sellers; they show the ladder, not a target for a $12 printable shop.

## The ladder

| Rung | Job | Typical form | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free or $1 entry | Capture email, prove quality | One tab, one week, 5 templates, a chapter | $1 bundle: 21 buyers in 4 days, then $197 and $3,750 upsells (r/passive_income, 312 pts) |
| Core product | Solve one narrow job | $9 to $29 template, printable, guide | Most operator wins sit here |
| Bundle | Solve the whole workflow | 2 to 5x the core price, 30 to 50% below the sum (our starting default, no benchmark exists) | Gumroad bundles and replacement upsells; Payhip cross-sells |
| Bump at checkout | Remove the next obstacle | $5 to $15 companion (calculator, extra pack, video walkthrough) | Gumroad, Stan Pro, Payhip, Lemon Squeezy support it |
| License tier | Same files, more rights | Commercial, extended, team | Creative Market: $9 / $14 / $36 template minimums |
| High rung | Implementation | Course, done-with-you setup, consulting | PDF bundles to course to consulting ladder (r/passive_income, 1,956 pts comment) |

Rules:
- **Keep one narrow hero product as the landing page;** the bundle is the upgrade, not the first thing a stranger sees (report, 2026).
- **A tier must change value or rights,** not just say "Pro".
- **Test one rung at a time** and judge net revenue per checkout, not attach rate.

## Bundles

- **Bundle what is bought together,** not everything you made. Look at orders with 2+ items and at what buyers ask for next.
- **Upgrade without double paying:** Gumroad's "replace cart" upsell moves a buyer from the core product to the bundle at checkout; say "you won't pay for it twice".
- **Back-catalog bundles:** series, themes and buy-one-get-one bundles multiplied one ebook author's titles (about 300 bundles on 700 stories) (r/passive_income, 304 pts).
- **Gift multiples:** a printed study guide sells "Buy 2 Get 1 Free" at $69.98 with a free app, a mystery gift and a 365-day plan as bonuses (r/Entrepreneur, 249 pts; physical, estimate only). For printables: a family or classroom pack.
- **Content packs at a low price** only when you already own the assets (one seller packed 30,000 clips at $5; unverified claim, 4 pts).

## Checkout bumps and upsells

Make the dependency explicit; "customers also bought" is weak:

- Bump: "You're buying the [core template]. Add the [companion calculator] for $X so you can work out the numbers before you fill it in."
- Bundle upgrade: "Upgrade to the complete system for $X more and get [A + B + C]. You already have A in your cart, so you won't pay for it twice."
- Post-purchase: "Your [product] is ready. Want the [next-step product] at [price] today only? One click, no new checkout." (Use only where the platform supports one-click and the deadline is real.)

Benchmarks for bump attach rate on digital goods are not public. Track attach rate and net revenue per checkout per bump; drop any bump that lowers core conversion.

## Licenses

- **Personal:** the buyer uses it for themselves; no resale, no sharing, no client work.
- **Commercial:** use in the buyer's own business or for clients' end products, up to a stated volume (Creative Market's commercial license: up to 5,000 end products).
- **Extended:** use in products for resale (merchandise, templates for client deliverables, physical products sold in volume), above the commercial cap.
- **Team or seat:** for Notion, spreadsheets and courses used inside a company. A tool seller turned free trial licenses for one manager into a 25-license, $18,000 annual contract by personally fixing problems during the trial (r/Entrepreneur, 1,713 pts).

Rules: put the license summary in an image and the full text in the file; state what is not allowed in plain words ("You can't resell or share these templates, even edited"); a buyer who took 1,200+ designs had agreed to personal-use terms (r/Etsy, 668 pts), so terms deter honest buyers only. Store license provenance for every font, photo, mockup and Canva element you used ([delivery-refunds-ip.md](delivery-refunds-ip.md)).

## Pay what you want

- **Evidence (2024):** a five-experiment study found higher suggested prices raised payments; a lab and field study found payments stayed below the anchor, and "what others paid" framing pulled payments closer than seller instructions ([Wang et al.](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/mar.21993); [2024 study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10696679.2024.2398698)).
- **Use it for:** entry products, free tools with a tip jar, community goodwill. Not for proven core products.
- **Copy:** "Pay $0 or more. Suggested price: $9." Use "Most customers who support updates choose $9" only if your transaction data shows it.
- **Gumroad mechanics:** minimum and suggested price are separate fields; with a $0 minimum anyone can take it free; any positive payment must be at least $0.99.

## Subscriptions vs one-time

- **One-time for finite jobs.** A wedding tool nearly launched subscriptions, switched to one-time event passes at $69 to $149 (customers need it about 6 months) and conversions jumped (r/Entrepreneur, 557 pts). An AI website builder on monthly pricing made $20 MRR from 15,000 visitors because buyers compared it with Framer and Webflow; back on one-time it sold about $7,000 in 3 months (r/Entrepreneur, 2,500 pts).
- **Subscriptions for a stream:** a library that keeps growing (template club with new releases, a case-study database: ~2,500 members, ~$30,000 a month, 38% of revenue, r/Entrepreneur 1,259 pts), ongoing alerts, or a community. Gate unlimited access, give a few free items a month.
- **Free tier just below the real need:** a wedding tool's free tier caps at 100 guests while most weddings have 120 to 200+; the founder calls it the best conversion driver (r/Entrepreneur, 557 pts). For templates: the free version handles one month, one project or one client.
- **Freemium through a native marketplace:** a WordPress theme given away free in the official repository (instead of charging $59) sold premium extensions, templates and support: $15,000 to $20,000 a month by year two, $1.5M in three years, no ads (r/passive_income, 1,667 pts; identity questioned by one commenter). The template equivalent is a free Notion Marketplace or Canva template with a paid full system.

## Discounts

- **No permanent sale.** Buyers read forever-50%-off as fake pricing (`ecommerce-brand`).
- **Founding price with a real cutoff** for launches (for example "$97 for the first 200 buyers, then $147", coreyhaines31 offers example, MIT).
- **Community perk** instead of a public discount: 25% for joining the Discord (r/passive_income, 354 pts).
- **Seasonal sales** on the buying calendar (New Year planning, back-to-school, wedding season, Black Friday for design assets), announced to the list first.
- **Audit Etsy coupons quarterly:** a flat coupon made when everything cost $30+ later let a $6 item sell for $1 (`ecommerce-brand`).

## Customization pricing

- **Price by the variable that drives effort:** custom portraits at $49 or $100 by the number of people (r/sidehustle, 166 pts).
- **Limit custom changes** to template edits under 5 to 10 minutes; anything more is a separate, higher-priced listing.
- **Preset combinations beat free choice:** 12 primary x 12 secondary colors made 144 combinations and a flood of angry reviews when buyers picked unreadable pairs; offer photographed presets and custom by message (r/Etsy, 662 pts).
