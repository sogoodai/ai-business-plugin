# Email, launches and affiliates

The list is the only traffic you own. Every channel in this skill should end in an email address or a sale that adds one. Flow mechanics, deliverability and consent law live in `email-sms-loyalty`; this file covers the digital-product versions. Verbatim copy: [scripts.md](scripts.md).

## Why flows first

Automated flows beat broadcasts by an order of magnitude on intent: 5.58% click and 2.11% placed-order rate vs 1.69% and 0.16% for campaigns; flows made about 41% of email revenue from 5.3% of sends, and about 48% of flow revenue came from new buyers (Klaviyo, 183,000+ customers, 2026, vendor data). Omnisend's 2025 data: $3.41 per automated email vs $0.155 per campaign email. Neither is digital-only, but the gap is large enough to act on. Build flows before buying traffic.

## The lead magnet: a partial solution

- **Give one piece of the paid product,** not a generic ebook: one calculator tab, one week of the planner, 5 of the 50 Canva templates, one preset, one chapter, one checklist from the system (report synthesis, 2026). The buyer should feel the next missing piece.
- **Consumable in under 10 minutes,** one format, mobile-friendly, one specific problem in the title ("27 free tools" beats "the best free tools") (coreyhaines31/marketingskills lead-magnets, MIT).
- **Benchmarks are soft.** Landing-page opt-in by format: checklist 30 to 50%, template 25 to 45%, ebook 20 to 35% (MIT skill, uncited); creator landing pages averaged about 4% in Kit's 2024 data across all traffic (possibly outdated). Trust your own number after 200+ visits.
- **Gate the useful version, not the preview.** Readers could right-click and save an infographic for free, yet an emailed PDF version helped one site collect 1,000+ signups a month with a popup and scroll bar (r/juststart, 94 pts).
- **Placement matters more than design.** Moving the opt-in above the 50% scroll mark tripled signups (r/juststart, 131 pts).
- **$1 instead of free** when you want buyers, not freebie seekers: a $1 bundle sold to 21 people in 4 days and produced a $197 upsell and a $3,750 advisory request (r/passive_income, 312 pts). Check the fixed fee: on Gumroad direct a $1 sale keeps about 7 cents; use Payhip, Lemon Squeezy or a card-only checkout for $1 products.

**Capture paths by channel**

| Channel | Path |
|---|---|
| Pinterest | Pin to a blog post or free-resource page with the opt-in above half-scroll |
| Instagram, TikTok | "Comment GUIDE" to auto-DM with the signup link ([scripts.md](scripts.md#comment-to-dm)) |
| Etsy | Buyers are not marketing consent. Put a free bonus with opt-in inside the delivered file and check Etsy's current fee-avoidance rules before linking to your store |
| Gumroad Discover, Creative Market | Same: a bonus or update list inside the product |
| YouTube, blog | Content upgrade specific to the video or post |
| Free tools and templates on GitHub or Notion | Donation and newsletter links at the top and the bottom of the README (r/Notion, 1,328 pts) |

## Minimum viable flow set

1. **Delivery (immediate):** the asset, one action, "it takes about 10 minutes."
2. **Quick win (day 1):** how to use the one feature that gets a result; reply-to invitation.
3. **Next problem (day 2 to 3):** "Most people fix A and then hit B."
4. **Bridge offer (day 3 to 5):** what the paid product adds, who it is for, who should skip it.
5. **Branch:** clicked the offer but did not buy: FAQ and objections email within 24 hours. Bought: onboarding and cross-sell.
6. **Post-purchase (immediate, day 2, day 7):** where the file is and how to start; one tip; a review request only after a real success moment.
7. **Browse-based drips:** a visit to a product URL adds the subscriber to that product's sequence ("info, info, info, info, buy it", r/juststart, 75 pts). Needs identified subscribers and a tool that tracks visits.

Weekly broadcasts sit on top: new releases with subscriber-first access (a planner seller built a 1,200-person list on first access to new planners, r/passive_income 362 pts), useful tips, one product mention.

**Hygiene that saves lists:** answer every email-provider notice. MailerLite removed one seller's 458-person list after a missed notice; it took four weeks to reach 25 subscribers again (r/juststart, 18 pts). Export the list monthly. A two-email onboarding (expectations immediately, a sectioned "best of" directory the next day) drove recurring visits for a content site (r/juststart, 94 pts).

## Launches

**Launch size does not scale with followers.** Two launches at about 12,000 and 24,000 followers each made about $40,000 in the first two weeks; the second product's later momentum, at a higher price, was the real difference (r/Entrepreneur, 1,532 pts). Judge a launch at week 3 and week 8, not day 2.

**Pre-launch (2 to 4 weeks)**
- Waitlist page with the outcome, a preview and a founding price or bonus.
- Show the build in public: "I did X and learned Y" posts (r/Entrepreneur, 2,500 pts).
- Optional presale: a $49 early-access Carrd page with Gumroad checkout made $1,000 in a couple of days (r/Entrepreneur, 1,373 pts). Paid beta on Patreon at $3 or $5 (name on the site) raised $1,200 in 3 months for a tool (r/juststart, 166 pts).
- A $50 Facebook ad of a static homepage screenshot promising a 20% launch coupon reportedly gathered 1,500+ waitlist signups for a microSaaS (r/juststart, 134 pts; unusually cheap, verify with your own test).

**Launch week**
- Email the list on day 1 with the founding offer and a real deadline; reminder at 48 hours and on the last day.
- Post in every community where you have contributed, using the rules for self-promotion.
- Product Hunt for tools and templates aimed at makers: a free version with a donation button drew 20,000 visitors on day one; a paid version built that night sold within 5 minutes; €15,000 in month one, ~€6,000 a month after (r/Entrepreneur, 1,530 pts).
- Affiliates and partners all post on the same day (concentrated launch dates outperform drip; coreyhaines31 affiliate skill, MIT, anecdotal).

**Relaunch levers for a flat product:** a price change plus a fresh ad push gave a second sales boost to a PDF released 3 months earlier (r/Entrepreneur, 1,532 pts). Seasonal relaunch at the buying month (back-to-school in August, tent season, wedding season, January planning).

## Affiliates

**When:** only on direct checkout (Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Payhip and similar tools have affiliate features; check the current fee: Lemon Squeezy adds 3% on affiliate orders). Etsy has no seller affiliate program.

**Terms (coreyhaines31/marketingskills affiliate references, MIT, uncited):** one-time commission 10 to 30% for ecommerce, recurring 10 to 25% for subscriptions; tiers ("20% for 1 to 10 sales, 25% for 11 to 25, 30% for 26+"); cookie 7 to 14 days for ecommerce, 30 days for software; hold commissions through the refund window; a few super-affiliates drive most sales, so give them custom assets and early access.

**Digital-product adjustment (our math):** with near-zero marginal cost you can pay more. Ceiling = (net per order minus your target contribution) / price; on a $49 bundle via Lemon Squeezy with $20 kept, that is about 50% ([economics.md](economics.md#8-affiliate-commission-ceiling)). Start at 30%, go to 40 to 50% for proven partners.

**Who to recruit, in order:**
1. Your own buyers who post about the product (they already did the work).
2. Newsletter writers and YouTubers in the buyer's niche with small, matched audiences.
3. Template directories and "best tools for X" bloggers.
4. Complementary sellers (planner shop and sticker shop, Notion template and Notion course).
5. Coaches and consultants whose clients need the tool.

**Process:** find 30 candidates, send a personal note with a free copy and no ask attached first, follow up after they used it with the offer and assets (swipe copy, images, a discount code for their audience), track by link and code, pay monthly after refunds. Scripts: [scripts.md](scripts.md#affiliate-and-creator-outreach).

**Sponsorships vs affiliates:** a niche blogger made about $800 a month from one e-guide (about one copy a day) while single sponsorships were worth thousands (r/Entrepreneur, 1,132 pts); a 56,000-subscriber YouTuber takes about one $3,000 to $5,000 sponsorship a month (r/sidehustle, 167 pts). If your audience is larger than your catalog, sponsorships may out-earn products; if you are the buyer of attention, a small matched creator beats a big general one.

## Creators and UGC

- Brief creators on the problem the product fixes and let them show their own use; the product should enter naturally.
- Disclose paid posts (#ad); undisclosed paid endorsements and staged "customer stories" are deceptive. One founder staged a couple's "met on our app" story; do not copy it.
- A creator's own buyers make the best affiliates: send the product free, ask for honest use, offer the affiliate link after.

## Metrics to report weekly

Subscribers added by source, opt-in rate per page, flow click and placed-order rate vs the Klaviyo benchmark (investigate flows materially below 5.58% click or 2.11% order rate), revenue per subscriber over 90 days, unsubscribes in the first 3 emails (low lead quality), launch revenue at week 1, 3 and 8.
