# Above the fold, headlines, CTAs, proof and price display

What the first screen must do, the wording patterns that keep showing up in wins, and where proof and prices go. Real data from the owner fills every bracket; never invent ratings, counts, credentials, response times or guarantees. Sources in [sources.md](sources.md).

## The first screen answers five questions

A visitor should answer these in about 5 seconds without scrolling (research synthesis 2026; coreyhaines31 cro skill, MIT):

1. **What is it?** (service, product, tool)
2. **Who is it for?** (customer type, location, situation)
3. **What result do I get?** (outcome, ideally with a number or time)
4. **Why trust you?** (one proof line)
5. **What happens if I click?** (the CTA says the next step)

Run the five-second test from [testing.md](testing.md) on 5 people. If 2 cannot answer, rewrite before any other work.

**First-screen layout that holds up:** audience or location line, headline, subhead, primary CTA, proof line directly under the CTA, and one real image of the product, the work or the people. On mobile the CTA must be visible without scrolling; most mobile visitors to one store never scrolled, and moving the buy button up "a few pixels" raised conversion (r/ecommerce, 282 upvotes).

## Headline patterns

Templates (fill only with real facts):

> **[Desired outcome] for [specific customer or place], without [main objection].**
> "Same-week bathroom refits in Leeds, without the builder's mess."

> **[Outcome] in [time].** "Your seating chart in 5 minutes instead of 5 hours." (r/Entrepreneur, 559)

> **[Verb] [concrete result] from [mechanism].** "Get $20k back from the IRS in 20 minutes." Subhead: "We help SaaS startups claim tax credits that most accountants miss." (r/marketing copywriter, 969)

> **[Category] for [specific group]:** "A creative hub for 150,000 authors" beat "A creative hub for anyone and everyone" (r/Entrepreneur copywriting tips, 1,195).

> **Statement, not service description:** "It's how you make a podcast" over "We help you make a podcast"; "Slack replaces email inside your company" over "An email alternative inside your company" (same thread).

> **Priced local offer:** "Make your home look like new for under $399*" with "*depending on size and scope of job" (r/pressurewashing, 581). Make sure the asterisk and the base price agree.

**Evidence that outcome wording beats technology wording (4 threads agree):**
- "Publish on 13 platforms from one chat." got 40 signups in 2 weeks; a tech-focused rewrite got 0 for a week on unchanged traffic; "Your social media. Done in 30 seconds." got 8 signups that night (r/indiehackers, 113).
- A roofing estimating tool replaced AI and automation copy with "stop leaving money on the table with bad estimates": signup 0.3% to 1.4% (r/EntrepreneurRideAlong, 47).
- "Powered by AI" became "Save 300 hours per month thanks to our customizable AI" (r/SaaS, 288). One small-business AI startup bans "AI" and "machine learning" from all copy (r/juststart, 100).
- Translate specs: "A 5GB MP3 player" vs "1,000 songs in your pocket" (r/shopify, 110); "Whisper Large V3 Turbo" vs "Turn a meeting into text without uploading the recording" (r/AppBusiness, 353).

**Write for a 5th to 7th grade reading level.** Professional-services pages at that level converted 12.9% vs 6.6% at 8th to 9th grade (Unbounce 2024, correlation). Short words, short sentences, no jargon.

**Audience line above the headline.** One line naming who the page is for ("For dog owners in Austin", "For Shopify stores doing $20k+ a month"); one operator claims it can halve hard bounces on paid traffic (r/shopify, 131, no data). Cheap to add, easy to test.

**Mine the words, do not invent them.** Take headline language from reviews, sales-call recordings, support emails and competitors' Amazon reviews. One copywriter across 20+ brands says the winning headline came from voice-of-customer research "every time" and the founder's favorite angle never won (r/copywriting, 64). Process: rank 6 to 10 themes by frequency and emotional intensity; theme 1 becomes the headline, themes 2 and 3 the mechanism and proof, the main skepticism becomes the objection block and guarantee, outcome moments become testimonials.

**The page must show what the ad or post promised.** A "mining Hell" pitch that landed on a page showing space ships lost the reader (r/IndieDev, 8,205). Map every ad headline to the page claim it echoes; no match means fix the page or kill the ad (aaron-he-zhu harvest, Apache-2.0, via `paid-ads`).

## CTA patterns

**Button names the outcome or the next step, never the form action:**

| Weak | Strong | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Submit, Send | Get My Free Quote; Check Availability; See Times | coreyhaines31 (MIT), research synthesis 2026 |
| Sign up now | Create your website | r/Entrepreneur, 1,195 |
| Get started | Hire top designers | same |
| Request a demo | See Drift on your site | same |
| Shop now | Shop our bestsellers | r/ecommerce, 56 |
| Learn more | See our difference | same |
| Subscribe | Get Weekly Tips; Send Me the Guide | coreyhaines31 popups (MIT) |
| Book now (service) | Get a fixed quote; Check availability this week | research synthesis 2026 |

**Repeat the headline's keyword in the button.** A 2024 cleaning-site study (Kutzner et al., cited on r/digital_marketing, 97) found exact repetition ("Dreaming instead of cleaning" + "At last, time for dreaming") got 58 to 59% click-through vs 48 to 51% for synonyms and 42 to 45% for an unrelated message.

**First person often reads better in buttons:** "Get My Discount" vs "Get Your Discount" (coreyhaines31, MIT; test it).

**Microcopy under the button answers the fear at that exact step** (DTC landing page directory of 300+ pages, r/shopify, 66):
- "Takes 30 seconds. No obligation."
- "Free cancellation up to 24 hours before."
- "You won't be charged yet." (Airbnb at the commit step, r/startups, 261)
- "No credit card required." (only if true)
- "We reply within 2 business hours, Mon to Fri." (only if true)
- "Yes, it really is this easy!" beside the booking picker, added because customers booked online and then phoned to ask what else to do (r/sweatystartup, 578).

**One primary CTA per page.** Secondary actions (call, chat) styled as secondary. On paid landing pages, remove the main navigation and social links: removing Instagram, TikTok and WhatsApp links took one page from 0 to 5 sales a month to about 150 a week; the client's preferred version with the links got 0 sales in 3 days (r/googleads, 74).

**Click every CTA before launch.** $50k+ of ad spend went to a page whose main buttons pointed to an external site (r/marketing, 443); a doubled URL sent affiliate buyers to a 404 for months, a fix worth $812 a month (r/juststart, 76).

## Proof: put it next to the objection it answers

Review quantity is losing power: 42% of US consumers trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations, down from 79% in 2020 (BrightLocal 2025). Specific, recent, verifiable proof beats a big star widget.

| Objection | Proof that answers it | Where |
|---|---|---|
| "Will this work for someone like me?" | Testimonial from the same customer type with a specific result and name; case snippet with a number | After each benefit section |
| "Are they any good?" | Rating and count from a platform the visitor can check (Google, Trustpilot, Etsy); job photos; before/after | Proof line under the hero CTA |
| "Who will be in my home / handle my data / treat me?" | Named people, real photos, licenses, insurance, years trading | About section, near the CTA |
| "Is the card form safe?" | Encased card fields, padlock, recognized seal | Inside the payment box only |
| "What if it goes wrong?" | Guarantee with condition, remedy and claim process | Under the CTA and in FAQ |
| "Is this a real business?" | Address or service area, phone, real email domain, company number where customary | Footer and contact page |

**Practitioner proof patterns:**
- **Testimonial after every benefit section,** each with name and title (r/shopify landing page template, 110). Reject "Great service!": a testimonial needs a specific result and context.
- **People over products.** "5 or 6 people that are super happy" instead of photos of mops and lawnmowers; about 30-second video testimonials (local services owner at $2M a year, r/Entrepreneur, 1,468).
- **Stars directly under the product title,** above the price (r/shopify, 171).
- **Off-site verification.** Shoppers suspect on-site reviews are fake; an external review widget (Trustpilot, Google) counters it (r/dropship, 66-upvote comment).
- **Making-of proof for handmade:** a 10-second process clip in the listing; the shop sign in frame survives copying (r/Etsy, 944; r/EtsySellers, 648).
- **About section is a purchase gate** for some buyers: an Etsy shopper spending $200 to $1,000 a month checks it every time and never buys from empty ones (r/Etsy, 944).
- **Third-party certification for high-value goods:** a GIA report (about $150) before selling a 5-carat stone; without it, buyers price the worst case (r/jewelers, 1,274).
- **Map of completed jobs nearby** after the reviews block for local services (r/googleads, via `paid-ads`).
- **Solo founder: "I", not "we",** plus a real photo, and testimonials just before pricing and again at checkout (r/microsaas, 65).
- **Fake proof backfires.** "23,000+ happy customers" on a store with zero add to carts read as a scam (r/dropship); leftover template text "[yoursitename]" and a dead privacy link made a store look suspicious (r/dropship, 186).

**Press logos: test, do not assume.** One local-services owner says put them on the homepage (1,468); a content marketer replaced "Featured in Forbes" with real customer comments (67). Neither has data. Use press logos only when the buyer would recognize them.

**Guarantees work only when specific.** State the condition, the remedy, the exclusions and how to claim. "Pay $0 if we don't save you any money" in the hero subhead (r/marketing, 969) is the model. Cap results guarantees: an agency with an uncapped $2,600 money-back promise paid 20 refunds ($52,000) and folded (r/startups, 270). Honor every guarantee you publish; one lash tech who refused a promised free touch-up faced a chargeback and a public review.

## Pricing display on the page

- **Show price, a range, or how price is calculated.** Hidden costs are the top checkout abandonment reason (40% of actionable abandoners, Baymard 2026). For quote-based services:
  > "Most [service] projects fall between $[low] and $[high]. Your price depends mainly on [driver], [driver] and [driver]. Get a fixed quote →" (research synthesis 2026)
- **"From $X" only if many buyers actually pay close to X.** A "starting at" monthly price lifted lead conversion 15% over about 22,000 visits in one older, undated test; a misleading floor creates distrust at the quote.
- **Publishing prices brings warmer leads.** Freelancers report leads arriving "warm or hot" at the expensive end of the market (r/freelanceWriters, 2 threads); a failing custom furniture shop was told to list standard builds with prices and a buy button, since "custom" reads as unaffordable (r/smallbusiness, 711).
- **Prices unlock cheap price-intent searches.** "car detailing prices" and "mobile detailing cost" cost about $1.58 a click; worth bidding only if the page shows prices (r/EntrepreneurRideAlong, 77).
- **Shipping cost on the product page, before any popup.** The top reason one shopper leaves small sites (r/smallbusiness, 1,077); a 305-upvote reply asked for shipping estimates before any signup request. A ZIP-only "check shipping" button, ZIP remembered across pages.
- **Show both % and money saved** on sales (r/shopify, 66), and the strikethrough only if it was a real recent price (see [accessibility-and-legal.md](accessibility-and-legal.md)).
- **Units that remove doubt:** "$4.99 for 5 styles" read as $1 an image; it was 8 images per dollar (r/webdev, 1,984). State what the price buys in the buyer's unit.
- **Subscription pages:** recurring price, interval, trial end date and how to cancel beside the button ("You won't be charged until 14 Dec. Cancel anytime in Settings.").
- **Pricing page structure** (coreyhaines31 experiments, MIT): 2 to 3 tiers, a recommended tier, "which plan is right for me?" answered in one line per tier, FAQ on billing and cancellation, value testimonial near the price. Pricing psychology and tier design live in sibling `offers-pricing`.
- **For AI answers, keep one plain price paragraph.** A competitor's single paragraph with a broad range and bullets won the AI Overview citation over complex tables (r/DigitalMarketing, 64).

## Page length follows price and awareness

| Visitor | Page | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Product-aware, warm (email, community post, retargeting) | Short: headline, mockup, one form or buy button | Prelaunch signup 9% to 27% to 52% as the page was cut to the signup box (r/startups, 249) |
| Searching with intent ("emergency plumber Leeds") | Service + city page, phone and form above the fold, proof, FAQ | Roofing pages at about 21% (r/DigitalMarketing, 114) |
| Cold social traffic, impulse product | Visual page: video, images, short copy, sticky add to cart | Shoppable visual pages beat PDPs for one store (r/shopify, 97) |
| Cold traffic, considered or $3,000+ purchase | Long page: story, mechanism, proof blocks, FAQ, guarantee, booking at the end | High-ticket service sales letters of 2,500 to 5,000 words (r/LeadGeneration, 15) |

Paid landing pages typically carry about 70% less content than SEO pages with one CTA (r/FacebookAds, 145, via `paid-ads`).

## Tricks most owners miss

- **Hero image overlay on one side only.** A diagonal dark gradient behind the text keeps the photo bright (r/web_design, 54); a full black overlay makes landscaping or food look dreary.
- **Lead image must be literal.** Clear product shot first, artistic shots later (mirrored candle photos made bowls look like hourglasses, r/candlemaking, 232).
- **Show every customer segment you serve in examples.** A woman assumed an app was men-only because every example was male (r/webdev, 1,984, 90-upvote comment).
- **Disclose the effort required before the click.** A homepage implying one photo hid a requirement of 8 to 20 photos; a visitor called it clickbait (r/webdev, 1,984).
- **Put contact within one thumb scroll on mobile.** Five blog previews pushed the contact footer 2 to 3 swipes down; cutting to one fixed it (r/SideProject, 1,492).
- **Real stock and real cutoffs only.** "4 left" from inventory and "Order in the next 3 h for same-day dispatch" are honest; resetting timers read as fake (r/shopify, 197).
