# Page templates by business type

Section-by-section blueprints, top to bottom, with wording to adapt. Everything in brackets comes from the owner's real data. Segment depth (pricing, offers, retention) lives in the segment skills named under each template. Sources in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Service business: one page per money service

For trades, cleaning, landscaping, repair, legal, accounting, agencies, consultants. Segment depth: `home-services`, `professional-services`, `agency-freelance`.

| Section | Content | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Top line | "Serving [towns] since [year]" or "For [customer type]" | Audience and area before the headline |
| Headline | "[Service] in [City], [outcome], without [objection]" e.g. "Gutter cleaning in Leeds, done in one visit, no mess left behind" | Search pages: echo the query; dynamic "[term] service in [location]" ran about 21% for a roofer (r/DigitalMarketing, 114) |
| Subhead | Mechanism or scope + "Fixed price before we start." | "Fixed price, so you know the cost before we start." (r/AppBusiness, 376) |
| CTA pair | Primary: "Get a fixed quote" or "Check availability"; secondary: tap-to-call with number as text | Emergencies: call first |
| Proof line | "[4.9] from [212] Google reviews · Licensed and insured · [guarantee]" | Real numbers only |
| Price block | "Most [service] jobs cost $[low] to $[high]. Price depends on [driver], [driver], [driver]." | Unlocks price-intent searches |
| How it works | 3 steps: "Tell us about the job (60 seconds) > Get a fixed price today > We turn up in a 2-hour window" | Each step removes a fear |
| Real work | Before/after photos with town names; annotated photo of what will be done | Annotated photo quotes won tree jobs (r/sweatystartup, 117) |
| Reviews | 3 to 6 reviews from this service and area, with names and job type; map of completed jobs nearby | |
| Who you are | Owner photo, van, team, licenses, insurance, years | Replacing stock photos with the owner and van was part of a rebuild after which "the phone won't stop ringing" (r/EntrepreneurRideAlong, 101) |
| FAQ | Price, timing, mess, parking, payment, guarantee, area | Objections from real calls |
| Form | Name, phone or email, postcode, job type (+ optional photo upload); "We reply within [2 business hours]" | Then calendar if the job type is standard |
| Sticky mobile bar | Call · Quote | |

Variants: **instant quote** by address or size for standard jobs (lawn, cleaning, house washing; operators quote from maps and square footage, r/sweatystartup, 477 and 1,082); **emergency page** separate from the standard service page, urgent CTA, real 24/7 hours.

## 2. Local storefront: restaurant, café, salon, studio, clinic, shop

Segment depth: `restaurant`, `beauty-business`, `fitness`, `health-practice`, `local-retail`; Google profile in `local-seo`.

| Section | Content |
|---|---|
| First screen | What + where + the one action: "Book a table", "Book online", "Order pickup", with hours today ("Open now until 10 pm") and address |
| Photos | The space people will sit in, the work, the team. A café's site needed seating-area photos for dates and groups, not only latte art (r/web_design, 2,426) |
| Menu or services | Real prices as HTML text, not a PDF or image (AI answers and phones read text) |
| Booking | Embedded booking or ordering with card capture where no-shows hurt; voicemail and DMs point to the same link |
| Proof | Google rating and count linking to the profile; 3 recent reviews |
| Practical | Parking, accessibility, dietary or allergy notes, kids, groups, private events (bookable minimums) |
| Ordering | Persistent basket, saved details, reorder; one site's timeouts sent a regular back to walking in (r/smallbusiness, 247). If "order direct and save", the final total must actually be lower than the delivery apps |

## 3. Ecommerce: product page and paid landing page

Product page depth, store setup, fraud and AOV offers: `ecommerce-brand` (and `fashion-apparel`, `jewelry-accessories`, `consumer-products`, `art-handmade`). Checkout: [checkout.md](checkout.md).

**Product page, top to bottom** (pattern from `ecommerce-brand` plus operators):
1. Title that describes the item in search words ("eco-friendly glass lunchbox with lid", not "lunchbox 2.0", r/shopify, 138), stars and count directly under it.
2. Gallery: 3+ images (almost every buyer viewed 3+, barely anyone read past paragraph 2, r/shopify, 278); clear literal shot first; image 2 = scale in a hand; variant selection switches the image; arrows plus thumbnails.
3. Price, real compare-at if on sale (% and money saved), installments for AOV $100+.
4. Add to cart full width, express wallets, delivery date and shipping cost, returns line, real stock count.
5. 3 to 5 benefit bullets written as "why": "We decided to use seamless hems on our shorts to avoid chafing on your legs while running." (r/shopify, 170).
6. Answers in images for marketplaces and mobile (icons for "not for cars, rain, sun" on stickers, r/EtsySellers, 499).
7. Customer photos and reviews; FAQ mined from competitor reviews and Amazon Q&A.
8. Recommendations block; cross-sell under 50% of the item price.

**Paid landing page for cold traffic** (operators report dedicated pages beat the PDP for cold ads "9 times out of 10", r/shopify, 127, untested claim):
1. One line naming who it is for.
2. Promise from the ad + hero product + offer above the fold; ad-promised discount visible on arrival.
3. Video or GIF of the product working; context-rich photo reviews showing situations the buyer recognizes (backpack brand $18k to $260k a month, r/Entrepreneur, 2,733, many changes at once).
4. Problem, mechanism, proof blocks; "us vs them" comparison table.
5. Specific guarantee; FAQ at the bottom; sticky add to cart.
6. No navigation.

**Single-product store:** one "Buy Now", price visible, big images, short copy (r/Entrepreneur, 2,481). Do not build a full store for one product if a payment link and a page will do (r/web_design, 689).

## 4. SaaS and apps: homepage, ad page, pricing, signup

Segment depth: `saas-apps`.

| Page | Blueprint |
|---|---|
| Homepage | Outcome headline with a time or number ("Your social media. Done in 30 seconds."), subhead naming the user, primary CTA "Start free" or "Try it with your data", product visual showing the result, 3 jobs it does, proof (logos the buyer knows, numbers), pricing link, secondary "Book a demo" for larger teams |
| Ad landing page | One segment, one offer, one price, one CTA; no navigation, no plan grid, no newsletter (r/SaaS, 288) |
| Pricing | 2 to 3 tiers, recommended tier, annual plan above the fold (an app with annual below the fold sold "almost zero" annual, r/AppBusiness, 107), billing FAQ, value testimonial near the price, "You won't be charged until [date]" |
| Signup | Google sign-in prominent (operators report 80%+ prefer it), email + password only, password rules shown before typing, optional fields labeled; try before signup where possible (guest mode: "would not pay without seeing what I get", r/Entrepreneur, 2,500) |
| Paywall in product | After the user has committed to a specific output; show the locked value (count of critical issues found, blurred real result): one security scanner's conversion tripled with "show the count, lock the findings" (r/AppBusiness, 163) |

Wording: plain labels over invented ones ("Projects" not "Workspaces": activation 30% to 54% in a week, r/indiehackers, 70). No "AI-powered" without the outcome.

## 5. Creator and digital products: newsletter, course, coaching, templates

Segment depth: `creator`, `digital-products`, `education-coaching`.

| Page | Blueprint |
|---|---|
| Newsletter signup | No-scroll page: headline that says what readers get and when ("We interview NBA athletes to show you their mental preparation before games"; "One email every Friday at 7 am"), email field, button, subscriber count or one testimonial, sample issue link. Remove archive and login links (r/Newsletters, 2 threads). Identity button: "Yes, I hate instant coffee" |
| Digital product | Lead with the part buyers praised (a PC guide that sold nothing led with its troubleshooting table after all feedback praised it, r/indiehackers, 45); preview pages or a sample; what's inside with page or lesson count; who it is not for; refund terms; price with "instant download" |
| Course or cohort | Outcome range grounded in past students ("in eight to twelve sessions most clients can..."), curriculum, cohort date and seat limit (real), instructor proof, FAQ on time per week, payment plan |
| Coaching or high-ticket | Published price or range (avoids prospects expecting $1,000 when the fee is $10,000, r/lifecoaching, 15), 3 qualifying questions before the calendar (r/personaltraining, 53), long page for cold traffic with booking at the end |
| Link-in-bio | Own landing page, not a generic link tree; one primary offer first |

## 6. B2B, manufacturing and wholesale

Segment depth: `b2b-manufacturing`, `b2b-outbound`.

- **One page per service or capability,** case studies split by service so each page has its own proof (r/b2bmarketing, 39).
- **Quote form with qualification:** company, need, quantity or volume, timeline, file upload (drawings); "We reply with a quote within [2 business days]".
- **Wholesale page:** unit price, suggested retail, MOQ and lead time per item; trade application form.
- **"[Competitor] vs [You]" pages** for competitor-review searches; open generously ("First we'd like to give a nod to the team at [Competitor]. They've built a great product.", r/EntrepreneurRideAlong, 148).
- **Quiz instead of contact form:** 2.5% to 9.3% (r/LeadGeneration, 16).
- **Inbound reply = direct booking link,** never the homepage (r/LeadGeneration, 32).

## Shared rules for every template

- One primary conversion per page; secondary actions styled as secondary.
- Proof next to the objection it answers.
- Price, range or mechanism visible before the ask.
- Mobile: CTA visible on load, sticky action bar, nothing covering it.
- Real people and real work in images.
- A thank-you page that states the next step and time ("We'll text you within 2 hours") and offers the calendar or a useful next action.
