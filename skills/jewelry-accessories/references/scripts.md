# Scripts and templates

Exact wording for recurring conversations. Fill the brackets from the owner's data; never invent prices, lead times or claims. Lines marked "verbatim" are quoted from the practitioner who used them; the rest are templates built from the research and practitioner threads in [sources.md](sources.md). The agent drafts; the owner sends.

## 1. Boutique wholesale pitch (first email)

Subject: [Collection name] for [Store name]: six pieces, $[opening minimum] to start

> Hi [Buyer first name],
>
> I think [collection] fits [Store name] because [one concrete reason tied to their store: "you carry [adjacent brand] and nothing in recycled sterling under $100" / "your window this month is all warm gold tones"].
>
> Our best seller, [SKU name], retails at $[MSRP] and wholesales at $[wholesale]. It comes in [metals or colors]. Opening order is $[minimum], reorders $[reorder minimum], and current lead time is [X] days.
>
> I've attached a one-page line sheet with the six strongest sellers rather than the full catalog. Every opening order comes with a counter card, care cards and on-body photos you can post.
>
> May I send two samples, or set up a 10-minute preview this week?
>
> [Name], [Brand], [phone]

Source: report template (Faire 2026 structure), adapted. If the boutique already uses Faire, add: "You can also order through our Faire link: [link]" (0% commission to the brand for retailers it brings, Faire 2026).

Follow-up, day 5 (one only):

> Hi [Buyer], quick follow-up on [collection]. Our [SKU] sold [X] units in [month] at [another stockist or our studio]. Happy to drop samples by on [day] if that's easier. [Name]

In-person opener when the store discounts a lot (verbatim, jewelry-care supplier, r/Entrepreneur):

> "If I had a way for you to give out less discounts would you be interested in hearing me out?"

## 2. Gallery or museum shop pitch

Subject: Submission: [Collection name], [material], [price range]

> Dear [Curator or buyer name],
>
> I make [material and technique, e.g. hand-forged sterling and enamel] pieces in [city]. My new collection, [name], is [one sentence on the idea and why it fits their program or current exhibition].
>
> Retail runs $[low] to $[high]. I work on wholesale at 50% of retail with a $[minimum] opening order, or on consignment at [60/40] in my favor with a written agreement.
>
> Attached: six images (each piece on body and alone), a price list with dimensions and metals, and my CV with stockists. I'd welcome a studio visit or can bring the collection to you.
>
> With thanks, [Name], [site], [phone]

## 3. Consignment agreement terms (term sheet to adapt; have it reviewed locally)

> **Consignment agreement between [Maker] ("Consignor") and [Shop] ("Consignee")**
>
> 1. **Goods and title.** The items listed on the attached inventory sheet, with photos, remain the property of the Consignor until the Consignee has paid for them in full.
> 2. **Retail price and split.** The Consignee sells at the retail prices on the inventory sheet. The Consignor receives [60]% of each retail sale price; the Consignee keeps [40]%.
> 3. **No markdowns** without the Consignor's written consent for each item.
> 4. **Payment.** By the [10th] of each month for the previous month's sales, with a sales statement listing item code, sale date and price.
> 5. **Display.** All consigned items are on display in [location] during opening hours. Items kept in storage count as returned.
> 6. **Loss and damage.** The Consignee is responsible for theft, loss and damage while items are on its premises, at the Consignor's share of the retail price, and confirms its insurance covers consigned goods.
> 7. **Reconciliation.** Inventory is counted together [monthly / at each restock] and signed by both parties.
> 8. **Term and return.** The term is [90] days, renewable. Either party may end it with [14] days' written notice. Unsold items are returned clean and undamaged within [7] days, at [the Consignee's / the Consignor's] shipping cost.
> 9. **Care.** Items are kept in anti-tarnish storage or display and are not cleaned with abrasive products.
>
> Signed and dated by both parties. Inventory sheet attached.

Rules behind it: 60/40 is the common split, refuse 50/50 (ask for wholesale instead) (Jewelry Making Journal); "never ship consignment inventory on a handshake" (research report); check the stock is actually on display (r/Entrepreneur, 1,115 pts).

## 4. Custom order intake

Form fields (web form or DM checklist):

1. Name, email, phone, country.
2. What is it for and who will wear it? Occasion and date needed.
3. Piece type and size (ring size or wrist and neck measurement; how they measured).
4. Metal preference (sterling, 14k yellow, white or rose, 18k, platinum) or "advise me".
5. Stones: natural, laboratory-grown, either, or customer-supplied (photos and any certificate).
6. Budget range: under $[minimum] / $[A] to $[B] / $[B] to $[C] / above $[C].
7. Inspiration images and what they like in each.
8. Engraving: exact characters, font preference.
9. How did you hear about us?

Reply once the form is in (send within one business day):

> Hi [Name], thank you for the details on [piece]. Here's how custom work runs with us:
>
> 1. **Design:** I'll send [a sketch / a CAD render] within [X] days. The design fee is $[fee], credited to your piece.
> 2. **Quote:** with the design you get a written quote naming metal weight, stones and their grade or origin, and a total valid for [14] days (gold prices move, so after that the metal line updates to the day's price).
> 3. **Deposit:** to start production I take $[amount], which covers the metal and stones I buy for you. It is non-refundable once materials are ordered.
> 4. **Balance:** due before shipping or at pickup. Shipping is fully insured and needs a signature.
> 5. **Timing:** about [X] weeks from your design approval.
>
> For the budget you mentioned, I'd suggest [option A] or [option B]. Which direction feels right?

Budget mismatch (adapted from a store owner who redirects early, r/jewelers):

> "Thanks for being upfront. For a [platinum ring] my work starts around $[X], because [metal weight and hand-setting]. If that's above where you want to be, Etsy makers and [silver option] can be a great fit, and I'm happy to point you to a few. If you'd like to stay with me, I could do [smaller scope] at $[Y]."

Scope instead of discount (verbatim, r/smallbusiness):

> "I understand. What budget range are you working with? Sometimes I can adjust the scope to fit."
>
> "I can definitely work within that budget. At $X, I'd recommend we focus on [specific piece]. You'd get [outcome] without the [removed elements]. Would that work?"

Cheaper competitor quote (verbatim, r/smallbusiness):

> "Does their quote include [specific thing]? What's their revision policy?" ... "I'm confident in my pricing because [one sentence on value]. But if they're a better fit for your budget, I completely understand."

## 5. Permanent jewelry event booking

Host pitch (from the research report, wording recommended, not a legal template):

> "For your event I'll provide a two-hour permanent-jewelry bar and handle setup, welding and aftercare. I need a minimum of [X] confirmed appointments 48 hours before the event or a $[minimum] sales guarantee. You'll receive a booking link and three ready-to-post story assets. If the minimum is not reached, we can reschedule once rather than run an under-attended event."

Walk-in pitch to a boutique owner (Tuesday or Wednesday morning, five minutes):

> "Hi, I'm [Name] from [Brand]. I run permanent jewelry pop-ups: a 15-minute experience that brings your customers' friends into the store. I bring everything; you give me a 4 to 6 foot table and an outlet. You'd get [15]% of the event's gross and a booking link to share. Could I send you two dates for next month?"

Then confirm in writing within 24 hours: date, hours, table and outlet, host share or flat fee, minimum and reschedule rule, promotion each side will do.

Client booking confirmation:

> "You're booked for permanent jewelry at [venue], [date] at [time]. What to expect: we'll pick your chain and fit it to your wrist, then weld the clasp closed. It takes about 15 minutes. Prices: [gold-filled from $X, sterling from $Y, solid 14k from $Z]; charms $[A] to $[B].
>
> Please tell us before your slot if you have a metal allergy or an upcoming MRI or surgery. Your bracelet can be removed with scissors anytime and we re-weld [free within 90 days / for $X].
>
> Running late or need to move? Reply here and we'll find you another time. [Deposit or no-show terms if any.]"

Private party offer:

> "Bring [8] friends and the birthday person's (or bride's) bracelet is free. Package: [8] bracelets for $[580]. A $[75] deposit holds the date; the rest is paid on the day by each guest or by the host."

## 6. Creator outreach

Gifting, no obligation (report wording):

> "Hi [Name], your [specific post] is a strong fit for how we want [product] shown. I'd like to send you [piece] as a gift with **no posting obligation**. If you genuinely like it, may we follow up about a paid or affiliate collaboration? You can choose from [2 to 3 options], and we cover shipping."

Paid deliverable (report wording):

> "We'd like to commission one [TikTok/Reel] showing [specific use case], with the first three seconds demonstrating [product fact]. Fee: $[X]. The agreement includes 30 days organic usage and separately prices paid-ad usage. You keep your voice; we only require accurate materials claims and the appropriate ad disclosure."

## 7. Bridal

Lead magnet (report):

> "Tell us three things: target budget, preferred center-stone shape, and whether natural/lab-grown/both are in consideration. We'll prepare six options for your appointment, including one below budget and one alternative you may not have considered."

Abandoned bridal inquiry (report):

> "I saved the options you viewed. The biggest decision usually isn't the setting, it's balancing center-stone size, quality and total budget. Reply with your ceiling and I'll narrow this to three combinations with exact totals. No appointment required."

## 8. Price objections in person

Care kit instead of a discount (verbatim proposal, r/Entrepreneur):

> "I'm sorry sir we are quite firm on the price, however what can provide you is this free jewelry cleaning kit so you can keep your investment pristine."

Give the same kit as a thank-you to customers who did not ask for a discount.

Handmade silver price question (from a 59-pt reply in r/streetwearstartup): do not blame the cost of silver. Say what the price buys:

> "Each piece is designed and finished by hand here in [city]. That's [X] hours of work plus the tools and casting behind it."

## 9. Review request (product-specific)

> "Would you review the exact piece you bought? The most helpful details for another shopper are: how the scale/fit felt, how the finish/material looked in person, and what occasion you bought it for. If you're comfortable, an on-hand/on-ear/on-neck photo is especially useful for judging size. [review link]"

No incentive conditioned on a positive review; disclose any incentive. Never ask a buyer to change a rating; you may clarify a misunderstanding (see 10).

## 10. Reviews, copies and disputes

Public reply when buyers think you resell the copies (verbatim, r/EtsySellers, 204 pts; report the review to Etsy first, because a reply locks it):

> "I'm glad you liked them! Unfortunately, the reason you are seeing my products on other websites is because my photos have been stolen by scammers. Rest assured that you have purchased the original product and that these are my photos."

Add: "Every piece is handmade in my studio in [city]."

Private message to clarify misuse (pattern from r/EtsySellers, 282 pts, a silicone necklace that broke when chewed): explain the intended use, thank them, and ask whether the review text could reflect that. The buyer rewrote the text but kept 3 stars: expect text changes, not rating changes.

Non-delivery claim when the buyer posted the piece (verbatim, r/EtsySellers, 720 pts):

> "upon further investigation I can confidently say that you definitely have my one-of-a-kind, handmade item in your possession. I want to thank you for supporting my shop and my passion. I was honoured to see it make an appearance on your Instagram account. If you could please tag my shop in the future I would be so grateful!"

Unverifiable complaint: prepaid return label and full refund on receipt (verbatim, r/EtsySellers, 30 pts; the approach drew 830 pts):

> "Thank you so much for bringing this to our attention, we take complaints very seriously. ... I'm sending you a prepaid return label and as soon as we receive the item, will issue a full refund. Due to the nature of the complaint, we cannot issue a partial refund. I am deeply sorry that you received an unsuitable product, and thank you for your patience."

Requests to teach your process (verbatim, r/EtsySellers, 939 pts):

> "It's cool you're interested in [craft]! I've been perfecting my skills for [x] years. I'm not much of a teacher though - here's a link to help you get started."

## 11. Policy lines

Personalized items:

> "Because this item is made to your specifications, change-of-mind returns are not accepted where local law permits. This does not limit any statutory rights you have if the item is faulty, damaged or not as described."

Engraving confirmation:

> "Please confirm your engraving exactly as it will appear: '[text]'. We copy capitalization and punctuation as entered."

Gemstone beliefs at a booth or in a listing (r/jewelrymaking, 516 pts):

> "Some people believe that this stone is good for [X]." / "It's been said that in the past this stone was used for [Y]."

Lab-grown title pattern:

> "1.0 ct Laboratory-Grown Diamond Solitaire Ring, 14k Gold"
