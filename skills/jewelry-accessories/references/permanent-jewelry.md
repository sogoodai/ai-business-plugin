# Permanent jewelry

Welded chain bracelets, anklets, necklaces and rings, sold as a 10 to 20 minute experience at a studio, a host venue or a private party. Almost all published economics come from equipment and chain vendors (Sunstone, The Maker's Chest, Stones and Findings) and the Reddit corpus has no permanent-jewelry threads with numbers. Treat every figure here as a planning prior and replace it with the owner's own event log after three events.

## How the business actually makes money

- **Material is trivial, time is everything.** Gold-filled chain costs about $0.15 to $0.25 per inch wholesale, so a 7-inch bracelet holds $1 to $2 of chain, sold for $65 to $95 (vendor blogs, 2026; Sunstone panel 2024: $50 to $150 for sterling or gold-filled, $115 to $300+ solid gold). The binding constraints are appointments per hour, show rate and host terms.
- **Throughput:** 2 to 3 appointments per hour for a new artist, 3 to 4 for an experienced artist with pre-screened clients; a weld takes 10 to 12 minutes pre-screened vs about 20 without (The Maker's Chest, 2026).
- **Attach rates carry margin:** charms at $25 to $35 retail on $3 to $5 materials attach on 25% to 40% of clients; a second bracelet on 10% to 20% (same source).
- **The event is acquisition.** Every booking is a CRM record for re-welds, charms, birthdays and the next party. Measure contacts captured and repeat purchases per event, not just gross.
- **Equipment:** welders from major brands start at $999; Sunstone estimates payback after 50 to 70 bracelets (2024). Portable power station $400 to $800 for venues without outlets (The Maker's Chest).

## Pricing

- **Flat tiers for gold-filled and sterling** ($65, $75, $85 round numbers), **per-inch or tiered for solid gold** where chain cost is real. Sunstone says most artists charge by the inch; other guides say by piece. Pick one structure and show it on a menu card.
- **Group packages** for private parties: "Eight bracelets for $580 (normally $75 each)"; bride's bracelet free with 8+ (about $3 of material) (The Maker's Chest).
- **Minimum spend for private bookings:** one provider sets $350 per hour (6 bracelets) plus a $75 non-refundable deposit for groups of 10 or fewer (consumer booking page, 2026).
- **Never sell "permanent" as a literal promise.** Define in writing how removal, resizing, breakage and re-welds work, and whether a re-weld is free or paid.

## Accepting an event

Model it before saying yes ([economics-and-pricing.md](economics-and-pricing.md) section 7):

```
expected gross = booked x show rate x ticket + expected walk-ins x ticket + charm attach x charm price
contribution/hour = (gross - chain - charms - card fees - host share - travel) / total hours incl. setup and travel
accept if contribution/hour >= owner's floor AND contacts captured >= target
```

Host terms seen in 2026 guides: 10% to 20% of gross with no upfront cost, or a $50 to $150 flat fee (The Maker's Chest). Trunk-show style shop commissions run 20% to 25% (Jewelry Making Journal forum). A host that profits far more from the traffic than the artist should waive rent (Stones and Findings).

Require, in writing:

1. A minimum: X confirmed appointments 48 hours before, or a sales guarantee, or one free reschedule instead of running an empty event.
2. Host promotion: booking link in their email and Stories, pinned post, in-store sign two weeks out. Supply three ready-to-post story assets.
3. Table 4 to 6 feet and a standard outlet (or the artist brings power).
4. Timed slots for 60% to 80% of expected capacity, the rest for walk-ins.

Booking-pitch and confirmation scripts: [scripts.md](scripts.md).

## Running the event

- **Pre-screen while clients queue:** metal, style and wrist length decided before they sit down cuts weld time roughly in half.
- **Stock:** at least 20 bracelets' worth of chain per style for a 4 to 6 hour event with 8 to 12 appointments; common pre-cut lengths 6.5, 7 and 7.5 inches; spare electrodes, lenses, flush cutters and chain-nose pliers (The Maker's Chest).
- **Capture phone and email at booking, not after welding.** Consent for marketing is a separate checkbox.
- **Safety policy in the confirmation:** who should not get a weld (for example, upcoming MRI or surgery, metal allergies), removal instructions, aftercare. Run safety specifics past the equipment vendor's training; do not improvise medical advice.
- **Photograph every finished stack** with permission for the gallery and the host's post.
- **Budget for variance:** plan for 3-appointment slow days and 12-appointment good days (The Maker's Chest).

## Formats ranked by typical contribution per hour (inference from the math above; verify with the owner's log)

1. Private parties (bachelorette, birthday, team events) with a minimum spend and deposit.
2. Studio appointments booked online with a deposit.
3. Boutique pop-ups with host promotion and a minimum.
4. Corporate or school events with a flat fee plus per-bracelet pricing.
5. Public markets and festivals without bookings (high variance, high setup cost).

## Growth loops

- **Friends book friends:** at checkout offer a group-party card ("Bring 6 friends, your bracelet is free").
- **Host rotation:** after a good event, book the next date before leaving. Rank hosts by contribution per hour and contacts captured.
- **Charm and stacking return visits:** a re-visit offer for adding a second chain or a charm within 60 days.
- **Google Business Profile** if there is a studio or a fixed weekly location (`local-seo` for depth).
- **Two-format pilot first:** run one partner-venue event and one private group, then compare contribution per labor hour and repeat rate before booking a season.

## What to measure per event

Booked slots, show rate, walk-ins, average ticket, charm attach, second-piece attach, gross per hour, contribution per hour, contacts captured with consent, re-weld requests within 90 days, repeat purchases within 180 days.
