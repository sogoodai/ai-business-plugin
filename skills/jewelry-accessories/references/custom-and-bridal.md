# Custom, personalized and bridal work

Custom work is where small jewelers earn the most per piece and lose the most per mistake. The agent's job: qualify budget early, get paid before metal is cut, put every decision in writing, and ship expensive pieces so a lost parcel cannot wipe out the margin.

## Qualify before you design

- **Budget first.** A jewelry store owner quotes up front (for example $2,000 for a platinum ring); when the reply is "oh absolutely not", they point the shopper to Etsy and online sources and say shoppers leave happy and the shop avoids bad-fit clients and bad reviews (r/jewelers, 14 pts on a 619-pt thread). Shoppers looking for the lowest price are usually already on AliExpress, not in a local store (same thread).
- **Custom minimum.** One jeweler stopped taking customs under $250 after a run of loss-making discounted jobs; larger customs ($1,500 to $3,000) attract clients who sometimes pay more than quoted (r/jewelers, 4 pts on a 180-pt thread). Discount requests at intake predicted difficult orders in the same thread (13 pts).
- **Scope down instead of discounting.** "I can definitely work within that budget. At $X, I'd recommend we focus on [specific piece]..." (r/smallbusiness, 324 pts; any custom business).
- **Bring-your-own stones and metal:** decide the policy before the first request. A client handed custom stones to a jeweler at a fine jewelry show after a loose $500 to $600 quote and then felt ghosted (r/jewelrymaking, 640 pts). Written intake with dates prevents both sides' anxiety.

## Get paid before metal is cut

```
deposit >= metal at today's price + stones + design or CAD hours already committed
```

- Explain the safeguards before the price conversation. A jeweler whose client asked for a discount "because the business is small" was advised to present a written quote naming the stones, written deposit terms, and a step-by-step "what happens when" before price (r/smallbusiness, 276-pt thread); the jeweler already recorded jewelry and stone serial numbers on invoices.
- Sequence: design fee or deposit, sketch or CAD approval in writing, materials payment before casting, balance before shipping or at pickup.
- Price gold customs with the metal surcharge formula and a quote validity date ([economics-and-pricing.md](economics-and-pricing.md) section 3).
- Itemize materials for friends and family; never "materials only" (the $200 knotted necklace that collapsed after 7 hours of work, r/jewelers, 44 pts). Components from a failed commission can become simpler pieces for several buyers: the same maker got a $600 upfront offer for pendants made from the beads (13 pts).

## Personalization policy

In the EU (14-day withdrawal right) and UK, goods made to the consumer's specifications or clearly personalized are an exception to change-of-mind returns, but defect and misdescription rights remain (Your Europe; GOV.UK, 2026). Use:

> "Because this item is made to your specifications, change-of-mind returns are not accepted where local law permits. This does not limit any statutory rights you have if the item is faulty, damaged or not as described."

Never write a blanket "no returns". Etsy can still refund a buyer from seller funds on a "not as described" case even for custom items: one seller shipped a custom piece in a clean reused Amazon box and the buyer used the box to claim the item was "Used"; Etsy refunded and two weeks of appeals failed (r/EtsySellers, 380 pts). Ship customs in new branded packaging.

Engraving copy: show the exact characters back to the buyer before production ("Please confirm: 'Always, M.' with a comma and capital M").

## Shipping high-value pieces

- **Insurance is in the price, not optional.** A buyer declined insurance to save on UPS for a custom gold tennis bracelet; tracking stalled at customs for over a month and Etsy refunded the buyer from the seller's funds (r/jewelrymaking, 1,233 pts).
- **Check that the policy covers buyer fraud.** A seller lost a $1,400 Cartier watch and the payment after signature delivery, driver confirmation and GPS evidence, because insurance excluded "delivered" fraud; later resolved through the BBB (r/EtsySellers, 653 pts). Another seller was reimbursed for a $900 diamond ring stolen in transit after filing police and postal reports (15 pts). A 66-pt commenter warned about exposure above the then-current $250 seller-protection threshold (verify the current figure).
- **Intercept when a refund lands before delivery:** one seller paid $172 through Pirate Ship to return a $500 order in transit after Etsy refunded a late-shipment claim (r/EtsySellers, 31 pts).
- **Evidence for non-delivery claims:** sellers have found the "missing" piece on the buyer's TikTok or Instagram and submitted it with tracking; one chargeback was resolved for the seller (r/EtsySellers, 8 pts; r/EtsySellers, 720 pts with a polite script in [scripts.md](scripts.md)).
- **Do not remake on a missing scan.** A maker remade 13 orders that had not scanned; the originals arrived later; one buyer ended up with four items, the maker lost $350 and still got two negative reviews (r/EtsySellers, 366 pts). Rule: replacement only after the carrier's claim protocol.
- **When a refunded order finally arrives and the buyer offers to pay,** send a recreated custom listing: one buyer paid within 5 minutes (r/Etsy, 809 pts).

## Screening

- Check the buyer's review history before committing production time on rush or threat-laden orders. Sellers cancel orders with impossible deadlines or review threats (r/EtsySellers, 14 and 29 pts). Historic review data can be stale; read before judging.
- If you cancel a buyer, watch for a second order from the same address under another name (r/EtsySellers, 682 pts, seller with 9,000+ sales).
- Keep refund-for-review threats as screenshots for Etsy, but plan for the review to stay: removal was refused in one case (r/EtsySellers, 261 and 10 pts).

## Bridal and engagement

- **The buying cycle is long and shared.** 57% of proposers start ring research 6+ months before the purchase and 79% of recipients are involved in the choice; 61% of center stones were lab-grown (The Knot 2026, 10,474 US couples married in 2025).
- **Lead magnet = decision support, not a discount:** budget, shape, natural vs lab-grown, then six curated options for an appointment (script in [scripts.md](scripts.md)).
- **Follow-up after an abandoned inquiry:** narrow to three combinations with exact totals; "No appointment required."
- **SEO:** engagement-ring subcategories were the lowest-difficulty entry for one jewelry store's SEO program (r/DigitalMarketing, 154 pts).
- **Exclusivity claims must be true.** A buyer quoted about $6,500 for a band and $6,500 for a diamond questioned a "one-of-a-kind" claim after a custom jeweler spotted a vendor tag and similar catalog styles, and moved to a jeweler they trusted more (r/jewelers, 18 and 16 pts). If a design comes from a vendor catalog, say "from our partner atelier" rather than "one of a kind".
- **Durability talk sells the right design.** Explain hardness vs fragility, prongs and channel settings for daily wear; a pave-heavy design that sheds stones becomes a review problem.
- **Lab-grown naming:** "1.0 ct Laboratory-Grown Diamond Solitaire Ring, 14k Gold" in the title, never "Diamond Solitaire Ring*" with origin in the description ([compliance-and-trust.md](compliance-and-trust.md)).

## Estate, vintage and resale pieces

- **Grade big stones before pricing.** A trade commenter quoted about $150 plus shipping and insurance to GIA-grade a roughly 5 ct stone and estimated $20k+ more in proceeds versus buyers assuming worst-case grades (r/jewelers, 17 pts; certification backed at 101, 95 and 38 pts). Unbranded mountings: consider selling the stone loose.
- **Appraisal is not market value.** Insurance appraisals price replacement at top retail (r/jewelers, 95 and 8 pts).
- **Disclose treatment in the title, not only the description.** A seller disclosed a yellow diamond's color treatment in the listing, the certificate photo and directly to the buyer, but not the title; the ring came back after a month and had to be refunded (r/jewelers, 41 pts).
- **Test purity before listing.** Several pieces hallmarked 14K tested at about 13.8K through eBay authentication, which also delays payment (r/jewelers, 76 pts).
- **Auction houses take 30% to 40%;** a $20,000 antique piece sold at Sotheby's in under 24 hours at a 30% commission; commission on high estimates is negotiable if you ask (r/jewelers, 5 to 8 pts). Dealer consignment can net more but can take years.
