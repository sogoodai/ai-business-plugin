# Offers and post-click: landing pages, forms, speed to lead, calls, messages

Most "the ads stopped working" cases are post-click problems. Diagnose in this order and stop at the first real cause: spend, traffic, conversions, creative, landing page, tracking (coreyhaines31 operating check). Then fix the largest bottleneck only.

| Symptom | Bottleneck | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Weak CTR, healthy post-click CVR | Creative or hook | New concepts ([creative.md](creative.md)) |
| Healthy CTR, poor page CVR vs same-vertical benchmark | Page | Message match, speed, proof, form friction |
| Link clicks far above landing page views (40 to 50% gap) | Accidental or bot clicks, slow page | Placements, speed, bot filtering |
| Many leads, poor qualification | Offer, form, event | One discriminating question; feed `QualifiedLead` back |
| Qualified leads, few sales | Speed to lead, sales process | Answer rate, response time, follow-up |
| Traffic and page fine, conversion weak across several concepts | Offer | Change the offer, not the ad |

## Offer toolkit

**Value equation** (Hormozi via coreyhaines31, MIT): value = (dream outcome x perceived likelihood) / (time delay x effort and sacrifice). Score each lever 1 to 10; the lowest is the constraint; fix one per iteration. Most requests to "lower the price" are really requests to raise the numerator or shrink the denominator. Stuck offers usually need proof, not features.

**Offer anatomy:** core deliverable pitched as an outcome with explicit "includes / does not include", bonus stack (one bonus per real objection, 3 to 5 bonuses, total bonus value under 2x the core price), guarantee, honest scarcity, a name, price and payment structure. Name test: can a buyer text a friend "I just signed up for the [name], it's $X and you get [outcome]"? A payment plan often beats a price cut ($6k today vs $2k x 3).

**Offer test order for services** (synthesis used across the sibling skills): proof with no discount, then a creditable consult or deposit, then an included add-on, then cash off. Discounting first trains buyers to wait.

**What operators report works in ads:**

- Free product beats percentage discounts: "free gift with your first order" beat discounts, bundles and BOGO for a clothing brand (r/FacebookAds 163); B1G1, B2G1 or "buy 3, get a popular product free" instead of 20% off (r/FacebookAds 307).
- BUY 2 GET 1 FREE built from two high-repurchase bestsellers plus a product you want them to try raised AOV enough to afford a higher CAC (2 threads, 329 and 172 upvotes).
- Threshold offers beat percentages for restaurants and retail ("Spend $75, save $5"); around 15% off produced 2.05x gross-profit growth vs 1.75x near 40% off in BFCM 2025 data (Lebesgue, vendor).
- Results-contingent guarantee in the hero subheadline: "Pay $0 if we don't save you any money" (r/marketing 969).
- The strongest Meta funnels used unusually strong offers: full refund if the problem is not solved, or more product for less, breaking even on order one only when repeat purchases recover it (r/FacebookAds).
- Lead magnet math: a free Excel template plus $200 of Google Ads gave about 50 downloads ($4 each) and 10 accounting clients (about $20 of ads per client) (r/DigitalMarketing).
- Education and coaching: sell a diagnostic, assessment or mini-event to cold traffic, never the package. Local services: neighbor offers tied to a crew already on the street; financing on $500+ jobs.
- Test incentives on a small group first: a free six-pack for a EUR 10 deposit got exactly one taker (r/marketing 443).
- Test 3 to 4 slightly different offers, each with its own landing page, judged on sign-ups not engagement. Small positioning changes sometimes beat ad changes.

**Honest scarcity only:** capacity ("we take 4 new clients a month, next opening 15 March"), cohort dates, founding price for the first N (that actually rises at N), real inventory, seasonal deadlines, bonus expiry. Never resetting countdowns, static "only 3 left" or fake viewer counts. Skip scarcity for cancel-anytime subscriptions and $5 to 30 impulse buys.

**Price in the ad:** on Meta, hiding the price can stop pre-rejection before the page (84 upvotes) while others skip ads without one; on Google, showing the price pre-qualifies clicks and usually improved ROI (r/dropship 387). Rule: show the price on Search; test it on social.

## Landing page levers with numbers

- **Message match first:** the ad promise, then a matching headline, then evidence, then one primary action. Build a message-match map: every ad headline and description lists the page claim it echoes; no match means cut the ad or fix the page (aaron-he-zhu, Apache-2.0).
- **Benchmarks:** median landing page CVR 6.6%, industry medians 3.8 to 12.3% (Unbounce, 2023 to 2024 data). Simpler pages converted better; 5th to 7th grade copy 11.1% vs 5.3%.
- **Remove navigation and side exits:** removing navigation doubled CVR for one advertiser (r/FacebookAds 145); paid pages should carry about 70% less content than SEO pages with one CTA. Removing social links and a WhatsApp button took a page from 0 to 5 sales a month to 100 to 200 a week; the client's preferred page then got 0 conversions in 3 days (r/googleads 74). Click every CTA before launch: $50k+ of spend went to a page whose main buttons pointed to an external site (r/marketing).
- **Speed:** -1 second of mobile load about +3% conversion, pages over 4 seconds 22% lower (Yottaa 2025, vendor). Slow vs fast pages in Google Ads audits: 1.28% vs 3.65% (operators). Moving a page off a slow main site restored conversions with the same ads. Target under 3 seconds on 4G.
- **Dynamic text for Search:** "[search term] service in [location]" headlines at about $60 CPC converted around 21% for a roofer (r/DigitalMarketing 114).
- **Local page:** dedicated service or service + city page, never the homepage; city visible; click-to-call above the fold; short form (name, phone, service); reviews and licenses; separate emergency page with an urgent CTA. A map of nearby completed jobs after the reviews is a strong proof block.
- **Ecommerce:** promise (offer, price, hero product) above the fold on the product page; ratings, press logos, sticky add to cart. Funnel checks: add to cart to checkout 3:1 or better, checkout to purchase 2:1 or better, fix the site before scaling if CVR is under 1% (operators, r/FacebookAds 180). For cold Google traffic: comparison pages, "us vs them" pages, advertorials and quizzes. Store CVR medians by category: food 4.58%, beauty 5.32%, fashion 2.77%, home 1.29%, jewelry 0.63% (2026).
- **Match the page to awareness:** an unaware TikTok viewer and a Google searcher need different pages; a CPM spike of 80% came with a doubled page CVR in one account, so judge net CAC, not each number.
- **Test in the in-app browser:** open the real ad preview on a phone and complete the flow inside Instagram's browser before spending.
- **Compact ad landing page structure:** hero (promise matching the ad + CTA), proof bar, 3 benefits, testimonial with a specific result, 3 steps, CTA with guarantee. Testimonials need a specific result, before/after context and a name; reject "Great service!".

## Forms

- **Field cost:** 1 field 18.2%, 2 13.0%, 3 11.5%, 4 9.9%, 5 8.1% (NP Digital, 404 pages, 2024). Only 51.7% of form starters finish (Zuko). Do not delete qualifying fields automatically: compare qualified leads per visitor and CAC.
- **Multistep quiz beats a long form:** an insurance client replaced a 15-field form with a progress-bar quiz framed "find out if you qualify" / "see your options in 60 seconds": CPL EUR 19 to EUR 4, same campaigns, audiences and budget (r/PPC 87). Show the total number of steps up front.
- **Checklist:** single column, visible labels, easy fields first and sensitive last, phone optional with "why we ask" unless calls close the sale, inline validation, button = action + what they get ("Get My Free Quote"), reassurance under the button ("Takes 30 seconds"), never clear inputs on error, 44px tap targets.
- **Spam:** honeypot question with a default answer routed to a non-conversion thank-you page; validate mobile numbers on native traffic.
- **Fire conversions on confirmed success,** not the submit click (which fires on validation errors). Submit your own form and confirm the email actually arrives: the success message can show with nothing delivered.
- **Booking tools** (Calendly, Acuity, Cal.com, Typeform, Jotform): detect the real success signal per tool; coreyhaines31's conversion-tracking skill ships snippets and GTM recipes for each.

## Speed to lead

- 57% of consumers expect a form reply within an hour, 41% get one, and 79% chose the fastest business (cited in the local services research, 2026). Only 20% of home service businesses reply within an hour (Jobber 2026). The classic "5-minute rule" rests on 2007 research: directionally right, numerically dated.
- **Minimum system for every ad lead:** instant SMS and email to the lead, instant alert to staff, a bridge call that rings staff and connects the lead, a call within 5 minutes, a double dial if unanswered. A dental clinic reported 30 to 40% more bookings from this; reminders 24 hours before, the morning of and 1 hour before cut no-shows 30% (r/DigitalMarketing 131).
- **Five-touch sequence** on Meta form leads (SMS + email mix): 58 of 166 leads booked, about 35% (same clinic).
- **First reply that names the job and asks one question** got a 60.5% vs 50.3% 24-hour reply rate (LeadTruffle, cited in local services research). Example: "Hi Sam, thanks for asking about a bathroom refit in Leeds. Is this for this month, or later in the year?"
- **Reactivate old leads before buying new traffic:** 1,000 dormant leads texted and emailed over 2 weeks booked 15 appointments (same clinic).
- **Do not judge Meta forms if nobody follows up within the hour** (r/FacebookAds). If a channel produces leads faster than the team answers them, more spend lowers booked economics: cut spend until answering catches up.
- **Audit sales follow-up:** sales took 2 to 4x the agreed time to contact leads in one company (r/marketing 267).
- **Autoresponder speed varies by email tool:** in one small test AWeber and MailerLite fired instantly, ActiveCampaign sometimes took over 2 minutes or failed (r/Emailmarketing 44). Test yours.

## Calls

- **Call-handling math** (r/PPC 37): at $150 per call, $1,000 average job and 80% close, raising answer rate from 65% to 95% and booked rate from 70% to 80% takes ROAS from about 2.4x to 4x with no change in ad cost. Targets before adding spend: 95% answered, 80% booked.
- Only 52% of inbound home-service calls reach a person; 48% of answered paid-search calls convert (Invoca 2026).
- Unanswered calls show up later as "bad leads": answer every call and call form leads back within 5 minutes (r/DigitalMarketing 114).
- Listen to recorded calls weekly; score answered, qualified, booked and lost reason. Use Google Ads call details to fact-check staff.
- For phone-driven businesses: Call-focused ads for urgent terms, phone number above the fold, call tracking that keeps the canonical GBP number.

## Messaging funnels (WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram DM)

- Use when buyers need one or two questions answered before committing. Measure message started, qualified conversation, booked, paid.
- Reply within seconds with an automated greeting and 2 to 3 quick-reply buttons (service, timing, price), then a human within business-hours SLA. Every conversation gets a CRM record with the ad ID.
- WhatsApp free entry point window and per-message pricing: [meta.md](meta.md#messaging-ads-whatsapp-messenger-instagram-dm).
- Cross-check platform-reported conversation counts against the real inbox weekly.

## Forms vs calls vs messages: choose by expected profit per visitor

A one-field form gets more leads and fewer booked jobs; a call CTA gets fewer, better contacts; messaging suits buyers with one question. Run the comparison on `(leads x qualified rate x close rate x job value) / visitors`, not on raw conversion rate.
