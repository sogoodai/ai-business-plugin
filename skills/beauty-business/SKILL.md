# Beauty business: fill the book and keep it full

Built from 283 practitioner threads (r/hairstylist, r/Esthetics, r/lashextensions, r/Nails, r/MassageTherapists, r/sweatystartup, r/GoogleMyBusiness), 2025 to 2026 industry data (Zenoti calendar-2025 benchmarks, PBA/KIM transactions, CorralData, Phorest, Mangomint), platform terms (Google, Fresha, Booksy, Treatwell, Square) and the best public marketing skills. "Operators report" marks a practitioner anecdote; everything else carries its source and year. Sources: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md). The owner's decisions win over this guide.

## How beauty businesses actually grow

- **New demand is shrinking; capacity is the lever.** New-guest visits fell in 2025 in every category Zenoti tracks: full-service salons -5%, specialty -7%, med spa -11%, waxing -16%, barbers -17%. Median utilization is under 50% for most categories, while the top quartile runs 63 to 66%. Moving a specialty salon from 47% to 65% is 38% more occupied hours on the same rent.
- **Growth is ticket, not visits.** US salon revenue +3.48% in September 2026 on visits +0.98% and services +0.42% (PBA/KIM). Rising revenue can hide churn.
- **Repeat clients are the business.** The 42% of guests who visit more than once a year produce 80% of revenue (Zenoti, 2024 data). Most loss happens between visit one and visit two.
- **Google Business Profile is the main free channel.** Local service owners attribute up to 90 to 95% of new customers to it once reviews build up (operators report). Start on day one: verification can take weeks.
- **Instagram and TikTok are a portfolio, not a sales channel.** Busy stylists post finished work and openings, not entertainment reels.
- **Referrals are asked for, and routed to open capacity.** Name a specific opening, hand over cards, and send referral offers through the providers who need clients.
- **Paid ads come last** and are judged on completed first visits, never on cost per lead.

## First session: ask, then compute

Ask the owner:

1. Category (full-service salon, specialty lash or brow, barber, nails, waxing, esthetics, massage, med spa), solo or team, country.
2. Booking software and whether you can get a 12-month appointment and payment export.
3. Service menu with prices and real durations; which services are consult-only.
4. Current cancellation policy, deposit and card-on-file settings, reminder setup.
5. Which providers want more clients and which have waitlists.
6. Marketplaces in use and their contract fees.
7. Google rating, review count, and whether the software routes ratings before asking for reviews.
8. Client list size and what marketing consent they hold (phone numbers are not consent).
9. Offers they will and will not run; who may change prices.

Then compute ([references/economics.md](references/economics.md)):

```
utilization = completed booked service hours / sellable provider hours   (by provider x weekday x 2-hour block)
revenue per productive hour = service revenue / completed service hours
online booking share = online bookings / all bookings
leak = unfilled cancellations and no-shows x average ticket
first-to-second visit rate = clients with a completed 2nd visit / new clients (same cohort)
kept rebook rate = rebooked appointments completed / visits
contribution per visit = price - provider pay - product - card fees - marketplace fee - discount
completed new-client CAC = channel spend / completed first visits
lapse trigger = client's own median visit gap x 2 (3+ visits) or service norm + grace
```

Compare with the closest category in [references/benchmarks.md](references/benchmarks.md). Report the three biggest gaps in money, not percentages.

Zenoti's cohort skews to multi-location businesses: trust the ratios more than its revenue figures. No robust current benchmark exists for nails, massage, client LTV, retail-to-service, optimal deposit size or ad cost per client. Say so instead of inventing one. Vendor "targets" (Vagaro's 70 to 85% utilization) are aspirations, not averages.

## Decision rules, in order

1. **Utilization below the category median:** fix routing, booking and rebooking before buying traffic. If one provider is full and others are empty, route demand to the empty ones, then raise the full provider's price.
2. **Online booking share below the median:** book as a new client on a phone, count the taps, fix the path. This beats any ad budget.
3. **Cancellations:** card on file for every booking, deposit for new clients and long or high-value services. Cancellations are the bigger leak (waxing 18% against 1% no-shows, Zenoti 2025), so the cancel flow must offer two new times and push the freed slot to a short-notice list.
4. **Rebooking:** name the return window and offer two concrete slots at checkout. Measure completed rebooks: a client holding one future booking cancelled it 72% of the time, versus 4% for clients holding 2+ (Zenoti 2025, observational). Book cadence clients a series.
5. **Win-back:** trigger on each client's own rhythm, not a flat 90 days. No discount on the first touch; add-on second; cash only for the residual group, and only if the owner approved it.
6. **Membership:** only where there is a natural cadence and spare off-peak capacity. Membership salons grew sales 8% vs 2% (Zenoti 2025) and med aesthetics members retain 90.4% vs 50.6% (CorralData 2026), but both are observational upper bounds. Pilot one cohort; measure contribution per provider hour after redemptions.
7. **Marketplaces:** spare providers and off-peak slots only. Compare 90-day contribution and second-visit rate against direct clients. Fresha 20%, Booksy Boost 30%, Treatwell UK about 35% + VAT on the first visit.
8. **Reviews:** one uniform request to every client after every visit. Never gate (happy to Google, unhappy to a private form) and never incentivize: Google can restrict or remove reviews. Turn off score-based routing in the booking software.
9. **Pricing:** review on a fixed date every year; move prices per provider by utilization and waitlist, not across the board. Watch 8 to 12 weeks of rebooking and cancellations after.
10. **Ads:** only with 20+ reviews, a fixed booking path and somewhere for new clients to go. KPI is completed new-client CAC, then 90-day payback. Test offers in this order: proof, creditable consult or deposit, included add-on, cash off.

## First 30 days for a new or rebuilding practice

1. **Booking path:** online booking that takes a card, one link everywhere (bio, Google, voicemail). Voicemail: "For faster service, book online 24 hours a day at [link]."
2. **Google Business Profile:** primary category = top revenue service, every bookable service listed, booking button to the booking page, exact hours, 10+ real work photos, prices. Ask every client for a review with a photo.
3. **Portfolio:** model calls at product cost (never free), post nearly every finished result. Agree up front that models get the artist's chosen style.
4. **Warm network:** tell friends, family and past coworkers you have openings this week, with dates. Any friend discount gets an end date in writing.
5. **One local partnership:** a chair or booth inside a complementary business (nails in a hair salon, massage in a chiropractor or yoga studio), or a set inspired by a local bakery or designer with both accounts posting.
6. **Tag-mining:** browse tagged photos at the restaurants, gyms and boutiques your ideal clients go to, follow and engage with those people.
7. **Marketplaces:** check which one locals actually use (Fresha, Treatwell, Booksy, Vagaro, StyleSeat) and list off-peak availability there, knowing the fee.

## The rules that protect revenue

- **Deposit size:** operators report near-zero cancellations with a 50% non-refundable deposit; Square suggests 20 to 50% (vendor advice, no experiment). Use 50% for new clients on long or high-value work, 20 to 30% or a card hold for short, frequent services. Massage therapists prepay 100% for new clients.
- **Cancellation policy:** under 24 hours (48 for long services) = fee. Waive the first one once, explain it, note it on the account. Same-day reschedules must be rebooked before the original start time or count as cancellations.
- **Reminders:** 48 hours before with a one-tap reschedule, plus an extra touch for new, long or previously missed bookings. Healthcare RCTs show reminders raise attendance (RR 1.11) and that adding a call to SMS cut high-risk no-shows from 11.3% to 9.6% (2026).
- **Late clients:** finish on time, do only what fits, charge the full price. Never add a late fee after you moved their appointment yourself.
- **Consult before quoting** anything transformational (color correction, blonding, specialty lash sets). Block online booking for color; clients book "roots" and ask for a full highlight.
- **Price long work by the hour.** A six-hour correction must earn at least six one-hour appointments. Hourly correction rates of $100 to $125 are common; juniors charging $130 for eight hours is the classic mistake.

Full policies, reminder cadence and intake: [references/booking-and-policies.md](references/booking-and-policies.md).

## Rebooking: where the money is

- **Name the return date before they leave:** "Your color will start shifting around 6 to 8 weeks, that's the best time to come back so it still looks fresh. I can hold Tuesday at 3:30 or Thursday at 11."
- **Fill gaps from existing clients** with exact slots: "Jenna 10:30 to 12:00 Tuesday: cut and color." Send only to that provider's due-soon clients, never a discount to everyone.
- **Record what they loved:** lash maps by zone in mm, weighed color formulas, pressure preferences, chat or quiet.
- **Fix your own mistakes free and fast.** A client charged $50 to fix a missed row never came back.
- **Personal call, not email,** for a valuable client who complained and went quiet.

More, including memberships and the lapse sequence: [references/retention.md](references/retention.md).

## Pricing in one screen

- **Recalculate the required hourly rate every year** from real costs after taxes.
- **Raise where demand proves it:** a provider with a waitlist and high utilization goes up or moves a level; one under the median gets demand, not a raise. Departing legacy-price clients get replaced at current rates.
- **Break-even check:** you can lose p / (m + p) of visits before contribution falls. A 10% raise at a 40% margin survives a 20% drop in visits.
- **Data on raises:** 41% of Canadian beauty businesses raised in the prior year, average planned raise 8.1% (BeautyCouncil 2025). Operators report 5 to 8% every 18 to 20 months keeps clients and avoids catch-up jumps.
- **Tiers beat discounts:** express services under $100, premium therapeutic or transformation work priced higher, longer sessions at a lower hourly rate (60 min $100, 90 min $130, 120 min $175).
- **Replace percentage discounts with a quick add-on.** Track retail by units and attach rate: in 2026 retail revenue fell 1.32% while units fell 7.86% (PBA/KIM).

## When practitioners and the data disagree

| Topic | Practitioners say | Data says | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price increases | Fixed schedule, 5 to 8% every 18 to 20 months | Price by provider demand; ticket growth can mask lost visits | Fixed review date, per-provider moves |
| Rebook in the chair | Rebook before they leave | Single future bookings cancel 72% of the time | Rebook in the chair and measure completed rebooks; book series for cadence clients |
| Deposit size | 50% non-refundable makes cancellations rare | 20 to 50% range, no proven optimum | 50% new and long, lighter for short and frequent, test |
| Review timing | Ask right after a compliment | Selective asking breaks Google policy | Automated ask to everyone; in-person asks on top |
| Photos | Photo reviews and posts help ranking | No measured ranking lift from photos or posts | Use them for conversion, not ranking |
| Win-back | Discount to bring them back | 26% rebooked at 10% off, no control group | No discount first; discount only the residual group |

## Never

- Never gate or incentivize Google reviews, or keyword-stuff the GBP name.
- Never send marketing texts or emails without consent; reminders and marketing are separate systems.
- Never scale ads on cost per lead.
- Never discount the whole list to fill a few slots, or stack a voucher on a first-visit discount.
- Never use health-condition audiences or events for med spa ads, fabricate before and after photos, or promise outcomes.
- Never invent a benchmark, an offer the owner has not approved, or a quote attributed to a real client.
- Never change staff pay or worker classification as a marketing action; route it to an accountant or lawyer.
