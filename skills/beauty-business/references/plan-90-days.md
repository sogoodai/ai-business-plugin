# 90-day plan

For an existing or stalled business with some history. A brand-new practice uses the "First 30 days" list in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md), then joins this plan at day 31. Order matters: measure, then convert and protect, then retain, and only then buy acquisition. The research behind the order: new-guest demand fell in every category in 2025 while median utilization stayed under 50% (Zenoti).

Each phase ends with a short owner report: what changed, the scorecard numbers, what you need them to decide.

## Days 1 to 30: measure and fix conversion

**Week 1: scorecard.** Import 12 months of appointments and payments. Compute, per provider where possible ([economics.md](economics.md)):

- utilization by provider x weekday x 2-hour block
- average ticket and revenue per productive hour
- cancellation and no-show rate, in money
- online booking share
- first-to-second completed visit rate, days to next visit by service
- kept rebook rate (completed, not scheduled)
- booking source mix and 90-day contribution by source
- retail attach rate and units, add-on attach rate
- Google rating, review count, reviews in the last 90 days, nearest 3 competitors' same numbers

Compare each with the closest category row in [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md). Write down the three biggest gaps in money.

**Week 2: Google Business Profile** (beauty specifics in [getting-clients.md](getting-clients.md), depth in `local-seo`):

- primary category = top revenue service; only true secondary categories
- every bookable service in GBP Services, worded like the menu
- booking button to the location's booking page; Reserve with Google if supported; UTM on the website link
- exact regular, special and holiday hours
- recent real work photos per service
- uniform post-visit review request to every client; review gating and incentives switched off in the booking software
- reply to every review from the last 6 months that has none

**Week 3: booking path.** Book as a new client on a phone. Fix every step that makes you think. If online booking share is under the category median (28% full-service, 26% specialty, 36% barber, 33% waxing, 13% med spa), this is the project, not ads. Block consult-only services from online booking and add a paid, creditable consult.

**Week 4: protect the calendar** ([booking-and-policies.md](booking-and-policies.md)):

- card on file for all, deposit for new clients and long or high-value services, policy shown before payment
- reminders: confirmation, 48-hour with reschedule link, extra touch for flagged risk
- cancel flow offers two new times and pushes the freed slot to a short-notice list
- written policy sent to all existing clients so nobody feels singled out

**Exit check:** scorecard live, GBP fixed, booking path fixed, deposit and reminder rules on. Do not launch paid acquisition yet.

## Days 31 to 60: retention and capacity fill

**Cadence rules.** Every service gets an expected rebook window; every client gets a target next-visit date. At checkout the provider names the window and offers two slots; cadence clients book the series.

**Lifecycle automation** ([retention.md](retention.md)):

- due soon: two real slots with their usual provider, no offer
- overdue (over 2x their own normal gap): personal note, no offer
- second touch: low-cost add-on by a date
- residual only: cash offer the owner approved
- stop on any reply; complaint goes to the owner; owner calls the top 3 lapsed clients by value
- keep a holdout group if the list allows

**Gap fill.** Weekly: list next week's empty blocks per provider, message only clients of that provider who are due and have booked that daypart before.

**Referrals into capacity.** Referral cards and offers go through providers and dayparts under target utilization, not through full ones.

**Membership pilot** if a service has a natural cadence and spare off-peak capacity: one offer, one cohort, peak redemptions capped. Measure contribution per provider hour after redemptions.

**Pricing review.** Providers with a waitlist and high utilization: raise or move up a level, with the dated message and a lower-tier option. Providers under the median: no raise, route demand to them.

**Exit check:** kept rebook rate and first-to-second visit rate trending up; cancellations being backfilled; reviews arriving weekly.

## Days 61 to 90: acquisition and scaling

**Paid, only now.** Prerequisites: 20+ reviews, booking path fixed, utilization data showing where new clients go. One campaign per money service or provider, Google Search on high-intent service + city terms first. Report weekly:

```
spend > leads > booked > completed first visits > completed second visits > 90-day contribution
```

Pause anything that only looks good at the lead stage.

**Offer tests** in this order: proof with no discount, creditable consult or deposit, included add-on, cash off.

**Marketplaces** as controlled inventory: list spare providers and dayparts, label every marketplace client, compare 90-day contribution and second-visit rate with direct clients. Expand only if they convert direct and return.

**Content.** Portfolio posts of finished work per service, openings when you have them, one location-tagged transformation per week on TikTok or Reels; track bookings, not views.

**Day 90 allocation.** Fund each channel by incremental 90-day contribution, repeat rate and capacity fit. A channel with too few completed visits to judge keeps a test budget only.

## What to ask the owner at each phase

- Day 1: booking software and export access; current policy and deposit settings; which providers want more clients; what offers they will and won't run; consent status of their client list.
- Day 30: approve the deposit size and policy wording; approve review-request wording; who may change prices.
- Day 60: approve win-back offers and the membership pilot; price changes per provider.
- Day 90: monthly ad budget ceiling and the CAC they can live with, from the payback math.
