# Expensive mistakes

Two tables: what the data shows (measured or arithmetic on measured numbers) and what practitioners report losing.

## From the data

| Mistake | What it costs | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Buying leads while capacity is badly spread | A specialty salon at the 47% median sits 18 points under the 65% top quartile: 38% more occupied hours left on the table (Zenoti 2025) | Route demand to empty providers and dayparts first; then buy traffic |
| Optimizing ads to cost per lead | Med spa case: $12.18 per lead, $251 per booked appointment, 20x off (MDConsultingNY 2026) | Report completed first visits and 90-day contribution per channel |
| Counting "rebooked" as retained | 72% of single future bookings cancelled vs 4% for clients holding 2+ (Zenoti 2025, observational) | Report completed rebooks and second-visit completion; pay bonuses on kept visits |
| Leaving cancellations as an abstract % | 100 unfilled cancellations at a $77 ticket = $7,700 of booked revenue; waxing runs 18% cancellations | Cancel flow offers two new times and pushes the slot to a gap-fill list |
| Paying marketplace commission on clients who would have booked direct | $30 to $52.50 on a $150 first visit (Fresha 20%, Booksy 30%, Treatwell 35% + VAT) | Direct link in bio, GBP and receipts; marketplace on spare capacity only; compare cohort contribution |
| Discounting the whole list to fill a few slots | 10% off to 100 intending clients = 10 appointments given away | Target due-soon clients of that provider; add-on before cash |
| Raising every provider by the same % | Wastes the load-balancing lever while one stylist is full and another empty | Price per provider by utilization and waitlist |
| Reading revenue growth as health | 2026 salon revenue +3.48% with visits +0.98%; retail revenue -1.32% hid units -7.86% (PBA/KIM) | Track visits, unique clients, units and attach rate next to revenue |
| Review gating because the software offers it | Google can restrict new reviews, remove reviews or show a warning on the profile | Uniform ask to every client; turn off score-based routing |
| Incentives for Google reviews | Same penalties | Ask everyone, no reward |
| Thin "salon in every suburb" pages and citation-volume work | Low priority in 2026; thin city pages are a spam pattern | One strong page per money service, accurate GBP categories and services |
| Keyword-stuffed GBP name | Suspension and re-verification risk | Real signage name only; report competitors who stuff |
| Marketing texts without consent | TCPA, PECR, CASL and Spam Act exposure | Separate transactional reminders from marketing; keep a consent ledger |
| Health-revealing ad audiences or events for med spa | Meta 2025 restrictions; account risk | Service and appearance framing, no condition-based audiences |
| Membership measured on billing alone | Full-price peak clients move to discounted dues; revenue per hour falls | Track member contribution per provider hour and cap peak redemptions |

## From practitioners

| Mistake | What it cost | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Starting color without inspiration photos after a rambling consult | 5 hours, about $600, client rejected the result | Inspiration photos are mandatory before any color |
| Proceeding toward level 10 blonde on suspected box dye | 6 hours unpaid, client hated it | Strand test or decline; trust the hair, not the denial |
| Accepting an anime lash set you had not done before | Client out $200 plus removal, 5 hours instead of 2.5 | Show your own past sets first; offer classics |
| Using 12 mm when the client capped length at 11 mm | Set removed immediately, client lost | Confirm max length and shape in mm before starting |
| Charging $50 to fix your own missed row | Client never returned, stayed 3 years with the next tech | Fix your own mistakes free and fast |
| "That's it?" at a $5 tip | Lost the client and a planned extra $10 | Turn away during tipping, never comment |
| Undated 50% friend discount | 18 months of 2 to 3 hour appointments, no tips | End date in writing, or quiet slots only |
| Private discounted booking link left active | Former client booked the old rate months later | Expire supporter links |
| Stacking a first-visit discount on a voucher | A $100 lash set sold at $57 | One offer per visit, never stacked by default |
| Moving salons 20 to 25 minutes away | Kept 20 of 65 clients | Budget for a third; tell clients before moving |
| Free root redo for regrowth after one week | Unnecessary free service | Check for new growth before agreeing to a correction |
| Agreeing to color client-supplied extensions | Half a day, complaint, client paid another salon | Decline when you can't promise a match |
| Recurring dematting absorbed into a $35 blowout | Lost the next hour every visit | Price dematting as its own service |
| Juniors quoting flat prices on corrections | $130 for 8 hours of work | Hourly correction rate; manager signs off on transformation quotes |
| Accommodating near-empty fills for a year | Client upset when finally charged a full set | Flag full-set fills before the visit from day one |
| Rushed waxing durations (legs, Brazilian, face in 60 minutes) | Incomplete waxes, client left for a competitor | Book real times (full face 30 minutes alone) |
| 9-minute grace period on a 15-minute wax | Schedule overruns all day | Grace periods proportional to service length |
| Waxing without gloves | Client never returned | Gloves for every intimate service |
| Offering wipes as optional before intimate waxing | Clients pocket the wipes | Instruct cleanup with privacy; add 15 minutes |
| $15 late fee after the tech moved the appointment | Client left despite liking the result | Never charge late fees after you changed the slot |
| Letting a session that isn't working run to the end | Nearly a full appointment, then comped | End early yourself, no charge |
| Introducing tools after a pressure complaint | One-star review | Agree on tools and pressure before booking |
| Promising deep pressure you can't sustain | Days of soreness, then two one-star reviews when the limit was stated in session | Call deep-pressure requesters before the visit |
| Implying a regular's usual choice is wrong ("if you actually want to feel better") | Membership cancelled same day | Suggest alternatives without judging their preference |
| Not telling clients the therapist is male before pickup | 3 unpaid refusals in one week | Name and gender visible at booking and check-in |
| Telling a client your hands-on hours as your whole week | Client computed your "income" and never returned | "As many as I can get" |
| Broadcasting a graphic hygiene newsletter to everyone | Other clients put off | Handle one client privately |
| Filming the whole appointment for content | Several clients left | One before and one after, with permission |
| Posting nail art with visible cuticle cuts | Prospects won't book | Check every portfolio photo for flaws |
| Selling microneedling without the peel plan | $600 spent, client quit before the peels | Sell the full plan and healing expectations upfront |
| $25,000 equipment purchase at launch | Owner unpaid six months in | Launch with low-capex services first |
| Product line with an opening minimum | $1,000+ tied up at launch | Pick lines without minimums |
| Expensive business courses and certifications for marketing | $500 to $7,000 with no client gain | Require proof of client gain; learn from a practitioner directly |
| Waiting 6 years for a first price increase | Large catch-up jump | Review prices on a fixed date every year |
| Second job overlapping client hours | Lost two regulars | Protect your existing clients' usual slots |
| Not honoring a 48-hour touch-up guarantee | Bank dispute and a bad review threatened | Make guarantees you can schedule, or don't offer them |
