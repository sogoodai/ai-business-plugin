# Booking flow and policies

## Booking flow

- **One online booking link that captures a card.** Send every DM, text and caller to it. Practitioners with $600 appointments lost them to no-shows booked over DM.
- **Online booking share is a conversion metric.** 2025 medians: full-service salons 28%, specialty 26%, barbers 36%, waxing 33%, med spas 13%; top-decile salons reach 54 to 61% (Zenoti). Below the median, book a new-client appointment on a phone yourself and count the taps before buying any traffic.
- **Phone audit checklist:** reachable in two taps from Instagram bio and GBP; services named the way clients search; prices or "from" prices shown; new-client services clearly marked; card step explained ("held, not charged"); confirmation includes prep and policy.
- **Voicemail redirect:** "For faster service, please book online at [link], 24 hours a day." When someone complains you are hard to reach, point to it.
- **Reassure next to the button** if people still call to check: "Yes, it really is this easy."
- **Block online booking for consult-only services:** color, corrections, specialty lash sets, anything over 2 to 3 hours. Clients pick "new growth" and ask for a full highlight in the chair. Med spa online booking is low (13%) partly by design: consult-led is normal there.
- **Paid consultation** as a menu item, credited toward the service. No study gives the right fee; test it against consult show rate and treatment conversion.
- **In-person consult before exact color quotes.** One stylist adopted it after prospects pushed for prices over text; it filtered out difficult prospects (82 upvotes).
- **Booking cutoff for new clients:** 24 to 36 hours ahead. Exceptions only for referrals from existing clients, who also get first access to last-minute openings by email.
- **Set real durations.** Waxing studios stacking full legs, Brazilian and full face in 60 minutes lose clients to rushed, incomplete work. A 9-minute grace period on a 15-minute service leaves 6 minutes of work. Lash: up to 2 hours for a new set, 1.5+ hours for a fill.
- **Surface add-ons in the booking flow and the reminder,** not only at checkout. A salon group that showed add-ons during booking, in pre-visit messages, at POS and in the provider app reports attachment of 25 to 30%, about double ([Zenoti Willo case](https://www.zenoti.com/blog/how-willo-salons-turned-technology-into-growth-engine-with-zenoti), vendor case).
- **Prep instructions before the first visit:** clean hair, freshly showered skin, no oil-based conditioner near lashes. Waxers send a staged guide (two weeks before, the night before, the day of) and ask clients to confirm they read it. Sending a policy to everyone means nobody feels singled out; one esthetician had every client sign and follow it.
- **Male therapists:** make the assigned therapist's name and gender visible in online booking and check-in. One therapist lost 3 unpaid sessions in a week to refusals at pickup before the spa changed this. Assign "any therapist" bookings to the underbooked therapist.

## Deposits and prepayment

- **Card on file for every booking** is the baseline. Square, Fresha and most tools support card holds, deposits, flat or percentage fees and per-service rules ([Square](https://squareup.com/help/gb/en/article/8096-deposits-on-square-appointments)).
- **How big:** Square's 2026 guidance suggests 20 to 50% (vendor advice; no salon experiment shows an optimum). Operators report cancellations become "very rare" with a 50% non-refundable deposit. Rule: 50% for new clients on long or high-value services (transformations, full sets, 90+ minute massage); 20 to 30% or a card hold for short, frequent services; none for proven regulars on card.
- **Testing default** from the research: 30% deposit, free changes until 48 hours before, deposit forfeited after that. Stricter for long, high-opportunity-cost appointments, lighter for cheap, frequent ones. Wording in [scripts.md](scripts.md).
- **100% prepayment for new massage clients**; for phone or text inquiries, send an invoice and confirm only after payment. Suspicious inquiries stop replying.
- **Repeat offenders:** deposit required before rebooking, applied to the service if they show, forfeited if more than 15 minutes late. Get written agreement (a form or reply "I agree").
- **Show the policy before purchase,** not after. A fee the client never saw is a chargeback and a review.

## Reminders

- **At least one reminder for everyone, escalate for risk.** Healthcare RCTs show reminders raise attendance (pooled RR 1.11 across 10 trials) and that adding an automated call to SMS cut no-shows from 11.3% to 9.6% in high-risk patients (2026). Beauty vendors report day-before reminders bringing no-shows under about 2% (Phorest case). Use the mechanism, not the effect size.
- **Risk flags that earn an extra touch:** new client, long or high-value booking, booked more than 3 weeks out, any past no-show or late cancel, booked through a marketplace.
- **Cadence that works for most:** confirmation at booking with prep and policy; 48 hours before (inside the free-change window, with a one-tap reschedule); day-before or same-morning "reply C to confirm" for flagged clients. Put the reschedule link in every reminder: prevention beats the fee.
- **Prep reminder:** "Please arrive with prewashed hair, or we can add a clarifying service, whichever is easier" with its price and time.
- **Keep reminders separate from marketing consent.** Transactional reminders do not need marketing opt-in in most places; promotional texts do. Possession of a phone number is never marketing consent (US TCPA, UK PECR soft opt-in, Canada CASL, Australia Spam Act; summaries in the `email-sms-loyalty` skill).

## Cancellations and no-shows

- **Under 24 hours (or 48 for long services) = fee.** Waive the first one, explain the policy out loud, note it on the account.
- **Cancellations are the bigger leak:** waxing 18% cancellations against 1% no-shows, med spa 14% against 5% (Zenoti, 2025). The cancel flow should immediately offer two new times and push the freed slot to the waitlist or a gap-fill list.
- **Same-day reschedule rule:** must be confirmed and rebooked before the original start time, or it counts as a same-day cancellation.
- **Unpaid balance blocks the next booking.**
- **Chronic reschedulers lose prebooking privileges.** They call close to the date and get what is open. One stylist lost about $700 in a month to cancellations right at the 24-hour mark before doing this.
- **Charge consistently.** One stylist billed a client $1,000 in cancellation fees before a single completed cut; the client then started cancelling 48 hours ahead and sometimes showed.
- **Standing slot that keeps failing:** "It doesn't seem like Fridays work for you, would you like to cancel all Friday appointments going forward?" If they decline, missed ones are charged.
- **Repeatedly moved appointment:** cancel it during the confirmation call and let them book when they know they can come.
- **No-show follow-up:** same day, a warm check-in that assumes the best and offers two slots; never a "courtesy discount", which rewards the no-show (harvest note on UitKit, ideas only).

## Lateness and boundaries

- **End on time, always.** On greeting: "Due to my schedule, we'll be ending the session at the regular time." If they choose to leave, the no-show fee applies.
- **Shorten, don't skip billing:** do the fill, drop the add-on, charge the add-on's cancellation fee.
- **Never charge a late fee after you moved their appointment yourself.** A $15 fee after the tech moved the slot cost a client who liked the result.
- **Grace periods proportional to service length.**
- **Early arrivers:** keep the room closed until the booked time. One therapist did this once for a client who arrived 40 minutes early; he never came early again and kept booking.
- **Hard stop you can point to:** a real cleaner arrival time or the next booking makes "we can't run over" non-negotiable.
- **Second service requested after the first is finished** (mullet, then mohawk at 9:30 pm): book it as another paid appointment.

## Screening (massage and intimate services)

- Real name and photo on the site, no stock images. Avoid "full body", "sensual", "stress relieving"; use "clinical", "therapeutic", "orthopedic".
- Booking page states you are licensed and what you do not offer; booking requires a card, phone and email plus a click-through agreement on harassment. Some display the Respect Massage logo.
- Phone screening with medical intake questions; no same-day appointments for new male clients if that is your rule.
- Mobile services: screen for home preferences ("any preference on shoes in your home?") and keep oil bottles off client upholstery; one service paid to clean or replace a leather sofa.

## Intake that prevents refunds

- **Massage:** 10-minute intake. Ask "What are our goals today, relax or work on an issue?" Then "Any areas to avoid?" and list them: scalp, hands, feet, pecs, glutes. About 25% name one they had not mentioned. For 50-minute sessions, offer full body or upper or lower focus so the client owns the trade-off.
- **Very deep pressure requests:** call before the visit: "Fair warning, I go firm and deep but not extreme." Deep tissue as a paid upgrade (two workplaces report "legit nobody books it" once it costs extra). Agree on tools such as gua sha before booking, not as a workaround mid-session.
- **Color:** inspiration photos are mandatory. Show swatches and the level scale; explain cool tones look darker than warm. Classify anything beyond single process or lift-and-tone as a transformation and list the steps before quoting.
- **Lash:** confirm maximum length and shape in mm before starting, record the map. Specialty sets only after showing your own past work.
- **Multi-step treatments:** sell the full plan upfront (microneedling plus peels) and personalize healing expectations, so clients don't judge after session one.
- **Kids:** coach parents to say "trim", cut the parent's priority area first, give a distressed child 5 to 10 minutes to settle, then stop and suggest a specialist or a watch-a-parent visit first.
