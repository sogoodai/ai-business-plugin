# By niche

Benchmarks are Zenoti calendar-2025 North American medians and top quartiles unless stated; full table in [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md).

## Hair and color (full-service salon)

- **Numbers:** ticket $114 (p75 $139), utilization 49% (63%), online booking 28% (45%). Color revenue +3.75% on color services +1.64% (PBA, Sep 2026): growth is ticket.
- **KPIs:** provider utilization, consult-to-book rate, second-visit completion, color maintenance cadence, treatment add-on attach, provider level migration. Judge paid ads for color on consult, booked, completed, not leads.
- Consultation-only for color and corrections; inspiration photos mandatory; show swatches and the level scale; a creditable consult fee or deposit for long transformations.
- Corrections by the hour ($100 to $125 an hour is common); never a flat low quote.
- Express menu under $100 (root retouch around $95) plus a premium blonding or transformation tier.
- Lower-maintenance color (natural color as lowlight, partial highlights) builds loyal clients who return on schedule.
- Return window line at checkout ("6 to 8 weeks") with two concrete slots.
- Record surcharges (long hair, clarifying, dematting) in the profile; weigh color.
- Overflow goes to a newer colleague with the formula, so the client stays in the building.

## Barbers

- **Numbers:** ticket $34 (p75 $39), utilization 56% (66%), online booking 36% (50%), new-guest visits -17%, membership sales +20%, cancellations and no-shows about 4% each (2025).
- **Play:** frequency, not big first-visit discounts. A 30% marketplace commission eats most of the first-visit margin on a $34 cut.
- Easy rebooking on each client's own cadence; book the series. Monthly membership (unlimited or 2 cuts) only if contribution per barber hour holds after redemptions and peak Saturday is protected.
- Online booking share below 36% is the first fix; walk-in-only shops lose the "open now" and booking-button searches.
- Location and hours matter more than content: open early, late and Sunday if the street supports it.

## Lash and brow (specialty salon)

- **Numbers:** ticket $77 (p75 $90), utilization 47% (65%), online booking 26% (44%), new-guest visits -7%.
- **Highest-value automation:** fill-cycle timing. Store each client's interval and trigger the rebook before they cross into full-set territory.
- Real appointment lengths: up to 2 hours for a new set, 1.5+ hours for a fill. Coverage beats oversized fans for retention; one artist reports clients returning every three weeks for four years.
- Price fills by elapsed time (2 or 3 weeks), not by lashes left; flag near-empty fills as full sets before the visit.
- Fill club or membership can work if expiry, rollover and peak-hour use are modeled.
- Record each client's map in mm by zone; post lengths in captions.
- Aftercare on every receipt; free cleanser with full sets; free fixes for your own errors within a week.
- Specialty sets (anime, manga) only if you can show your own past work; price as full sets every time.
- Retention complaints: audit the client's routine (oil-based conditioner, face sleeping, wet hair, rubbing, makeup residue) before changing products. Your other clients' 2 to 3 week wear is the benchmark.
- Positioning: "quiet sessions only" studios attract clients who want to rest.

## Nails

- **Numbers:** no clean 2025 benchmark. Zenoti's 2024 data showed high cancellations (16%) and 1% no-shows; possibly outdated, do not substitute.
- Protect long appointment blocks with deposits; track fill and rebalance cycles separately from one-off event clients; analyze utilization per tech.
- Portfolio photos must have clean cuticles.
- Hidden art under an event-appropriate set (Halloween under a wedding set, $115) creates shareable posts.
- Collab sets with local bakeries, designers and venues; booth inside a hair salon.
- Engage followers of local luxury boutiques and spas to reach higher budgets.
- Before lowering prices after a complaint, check what comparable sets cost locally.
- Fast loyalty gestures (pre-vacation chip fix before opening) earn bigger tips.

## Waxing and esthetics

- **Waxing numbers:** ticket $34 (p75 $39), utilization 38% (top decile 60%), online booking 33% (top decile 46%), cancellations 18%, no-shows 1%, new-guest visits -16% (2025).
- **Play:** cancellation recovery beats no-show punishment. The cancel flow offers two new times and pushes the freed slot to an opted-in gap-fill list immediately.
- Regrowth gives waxing a natural cadence that suits memberships and series bookings.
- Staged prep guide for new waxing clients; required cleanup with wipes and privacy; extra 15 minutes for intimate waxing prep.
- Gloves always. Realistic durations (full face 30 minutes alone).
- Sell multi-step plans upfront (microneedling plus peels) and personalize healing expectations. Separate prepaid package liabilities from earned revenue.
- Positioning wins: trans-friendly, body-hair positive (one waxer averaged $175 an hour), quiet sessions.
- Avoid big equipment at launch; product lines without opening minimums.

## Massage

- **Numbers:** no robust current benchmark for retention, membership or utilization in independent massage. Do not invent one.
- **Membership fit is strong** (repeatable, capacity-bound). The KPI is member contribution per therapist hour after redemption, not subscription revenue.
- Clinical wording and settings attract therapeutic clients and repel the wrong inquiries. Or own the opposite: one therapist built a loyal book on hot-stone relaxation and refers deep-pressure requests out.
- Prepay new clients; screening and boundary statements before booking.
- Goal question and avoid-list at intake; pressure checks twice plus "how are we doing".
- Deep tissue as a paid upgrade; premium therapeutic tier; longer sessions at a lower hourly rate.
- Couples and duo sessions where competitors can't host two at once.
- Male therapists: introduce by name, show gender at booking, auto-assign "any therapist" bookings, market to men or clinical niches.
- Referral seeding: free sessions for hair stylists and yoga teachers; rent space in a chiropractor's office; insurance billing where available.
- Your body is the capacity: pricing that requires unsustainable pressure loses money later.

## Small med spas

- **Numbers:** revenue $1.86M (p75 $2.34M), ticket $216 ($346), utilization 38% (56%), online booking 13% (18%), new-guest visits -11%, membership sales +13%, cancellations 14% (down from 16%), no-shows 5% (Zenoti 2025). Med aesthetics: 90-day rebooking 71% (76.5%), membership penetration 26.6% (40.7%), member retention 90.4% vs 50.6% non-members (CorralData 2026).
- **Funnel:** qualified inquiry, consult scheduled, consult attended, treatment booked, treatment completed, 90-day repeat or add-on. Low online booking is normal for consult-led services.
- **Ads:** a low CPL means nothing (103 leads, 5 bookings in one 2026 case). No health-condition audiences or events on Meta. Service and appearance framing.
- **Seasonality:** laser hair removal before summer, injectables before holidays and events (unifapi treatment radar, MIT).
- **Content:** log verbatim patient questions (cost, pain, downtime, candidacy, before and after, versus alternatives) as page and video topics.
- **Compliance guardrails:** clinical claims reviewed by the licensed provider; no guaranteed outcomes; real before and after photos only, with written consent, matched lighting and angle and time since treatment stated; if a testimonial shows atypical results, state typical results; disclose paid or gifted endorsements in the first lines. Check state medical board advertising rules and the brand rules for injectable names.
- **Memberships:** strongest data of any niche, but self-selection makes the retention gap an upper bound. Pilot, measure, then scale.
