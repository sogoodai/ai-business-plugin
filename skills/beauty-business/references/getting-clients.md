# Getting clients

Ordered from cheapest and most proven to most expensive. Before adding any channel, check utilization by provider and daypart ([economics.md](economics.md)). New-guest visits fell in every category in 2025 (barbers -17%, waxing -16%, med spa -11%, full-service salons -5%; Zenoti), so new demand is scarce: route it to open capacity and protect it with a good first visit.

## Google Business Profile and reviews

Depth on GBP, citations, geogrids and AI visibility is in the `local-seo` skill. What matters specifically for beauty:

- **Start immediately.** Verification can take weeks and results a few more months. Owners report 5 to 10 profile interactions a day once it matures, and local service owners attribute up to 90 to 95% of new customers to it once reviews build up (operators report).
- **Primary category = the service that makes the most money,** the most specific one that is true: "Hair salon", "Barber shop", "Nail salon", "Eyelash salon", "Waxing hair removal service", "Massage therapist", "Medical spa". Category is the top controllable Maps factor in Whitespark's 2026 expert survey and Google confirms categories affect ranking ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7249669)). Add only secondary categories the business genuinely is (up to 9), not one per service.
- **Fill GBP Services with every bookable service,** worded like the website and the menu. Sterling Sky's 2025 to 2026 tests saw ranking movement 24 to 72 hours after adding predefined services, reversing its own 2019 finding ([Sterling Sky](https://www.sterlingsky.ca/services-in-google-business-profile-impact-ranking/)). Google publishes a 2026 Services playbook ([PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/helpcenter/1_services_gbp_best_practices_playbook_2026.pdf)).
- **Booking button to the location's booking page,** not the homepage. Turn on Reserve with Google if the booking provider supports it ([Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6218037)); check whether the provider charges for those bookings. UTM-tag the website link so GBP bookings show up in analytics.
- **Hours are a ranking input for "open now"** searches ("salons open now" is Google's own example). Keep special and holiday hours exact; late-evening and Sunday hours win searches competitors miss.
- **Never stuff keywords into the business name** unless it is the real signage name. It works mechanically and gets profiles suspended. Report competitors who do it instead.
- **Photos and posts convert; they are not proven ranking levers** (Sterling Sky tests found no lift, pre-2024, possibly outdated). Use them as a portfolio: recent real work per service, prices visible. Travelers and new residents decide from the listing alone (operators report).
- **AI answers pull from GBP and your website.** BrightLocal's 2026 study of 1.9M AI citations found GBP was 28.5% of local citations and business websites 42%; a business appeared in Maps 66% of the time but in ChatGPT only 33% ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-ai-visibility-study/)). There is no special AI markup ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)): one clear page per money service, consistent facts everywhere.
- **Mobile lash, nail, massage or hair:** a service-area business hides its address and lists up to 20 areas, but proximity is still measured from the verified address. Rent a room or chair with signage if you need to rank in a specific town.

### Reviews

- **Ask every client after every completed visit, the same way.** Google bans incentives and review gating, meaning sending only happy clients to Google and unhappy ones to a private form ([Google policy](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7400114)). Penalties: review restrictions, removals, warning banners.
- **Audit the booking software.** Boulevard's rating requests can route 4 to 5 star scores to Google and keep the rest private ([Boulevard](https://support.boulevard.io/en/articles/10207593-rating-requests)); Phorest has promoted similar mechanics. Turn the routing off. Uniform automated ask, then in-person asks on top are fine.
- **No discounts, points or giveaway entries for Google reviews.** "If you loved it, leave us five stars" is selective solicitation.
- **Targets:** get past about 10 reviews fast (Sterling Sky saw the biggest ranking change there), then 20+ before paid ads (47% of consumers call fewer than 20 insufficient, BrightLocal 2026), then steady weekly flow. One profile slipped after about 18 days without a new review (field observation, not a rule).
- **Ask for a photo and the service name** in the review ("tell people which service you had"). Naming the service and city happens naturally when asked what they had; never script the words.
- **Best moment in person:** right after an unsolicited thank-you text or compliment, reply with the direct link (operators report).
- **No tips? Redirect.** Tip-included owners say "only hugs, referrals or reviews accepted" and report reviews paying off.
- **Reply to every review,** specific, within a week (81% of consumers expect that; 50% are put off by generic replies, BrightLocal 2026).
- **Bad review:** text the client privately first. If no answer, reply publicly and kindly with a concrete invitation (see [scripts.md](scripts.md)). Several operators report the client deleting the review afterward.
- **Rating math** (needed five-stars, catch-up target, velocity) is in [economics.md](economics.md).
- **Venue trick, adapted:** photographers leave photo reviews on every venue they shoot and attribute 30+ bookings to it. Beauty version: review and tag the bridal venues, hotels and photographers you work with for weddings and events.

## Referrals

- **Ask with a specific opening:** "I have a couple of openings next week if you have friends who need [service]. Here are a few cards, most of my best clients come from referrals."
- **Tell your favorite clients only** when you have room. Stylists with full books say this is all the marketing they do.
- **Route referral offers to open capacity.** One salon gave $50-off-$250+ referral certificates only through stylists under 70% productivity and reported 10% more new clients ([Salon Today](https://www.salontoday.com/articles/2026-salon-today-200-technology-and-client-retention-and-referral), 2025, single case). A blanket referral discount on a full stylist just leaks margin.
- **Reward with a quick add-on**, not cash: scalp massage, brow wax, polish change, or one free service hour. Size the total reward (both sides) under your target CAC minus other acquisition cost (harvest framework, coreyhaines31, MIT).
- **Reward size framing:** under about $100, lead with dollars ("$20 off") rather than percent; double-sided beats one-sided (same source, uncited).
- **Thank referrers by hand.** A short handwritten note costs 10 minutes and a stamp, and keeps referrals coming. Time these when you see a lull coming.
- **Seed referral partners:** massage therapists report spending about eight free hours on hair stylists and yoga teachers to find a couple of advocates who send a steady stream.
- **Overflow inside the salon:** when fully booked, refer to a newer colleague and hand over the formula and notes, so the client stays in the building.
- **Urgent caller, booked-out stylist:** offer the cancellation list or a named newer stylist today. A new stylist filled a same-day lowlight this way (525 upvotes).

## Partnerships

- **Booth or station inside a complementary business:** nails inside a hair salon, massage inside a chiropractor, physio or multidisciplinary clinic. Spend the first weeks there in person meeting clients, then switch to online booking.
- **Collab sets:** pitch a high-end bakery, fashion designer, calligrapher or wedding venue a nail set inspired by their work, posted by both accounts.
- **Clinical settings fill therapeutic massage.** Therapists moving from spas to clinics with chiropractors and physios report more clients and higher pay with almost no marketing. Some practices fill through insurance billing where it is available.
- **Duo bookings for nervous first-timers:** mother-daughter, siblings, best friends in the same room. Couples massage is the main money maker where few spas can host two at once.
- **Cross-vouchers with a boutique or gym** (15% off a first service) and a small in-salon evening event (short talk by a stylist, retail offer, RSVP deadline) are the cheap local versions (Brazil salon pack, MIT, uncited).

## Social media that books

- **Portfolio first:** finished-work photos and carousels so visitors compare your style fast. Visitors leave profiles that only show behind-the-scenes reels.
- **Post openings** when you have them, as posts or stories with the exact time.
- **Low-effort content works:** a messy color tray with empty bowls and foils, caption "every masterpiece begins with a beautiful mess", got 10,000+ views.
- **Location-tag every transformation** on TikTok; stylists report occasional viral hits there and never on Instagram, but track whether views turn into bookings. TikTok's own salon guide favors native transformations, tutorials and behind-the-scenes over polished spots ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/guides/salon-advertising-marketing-guide)).
- **Put the specs in the caption:** lash lengths and curl for each set, so clients can request a reproducible look.
- **Never film the whole appointment.** Clients tolerate a before and after; constant filming made several leave.
- **Never post work with visible flaws** (cuticle cuts on nails, patchy lashes). One bad detail stops bookings even when the design is great (14,423-upvote thread).
- **Shareable twist:** hidden seasonal art under an event-appropriate set (Halloween under a wedding set, $115) hit 20,310 upvotes.
- **Barter content help:** one stylist traded hair services for a college-student client running her social media, on a two-month trial.
- **Label AI visuals.** Never fabricate before/after photos, results or reviews; real client photos need written permission.

## Local communities

- **Facebook mom groups and Nextdoor:** comment helpfully on 20+ relevant posts over 4 to 6 weeks before ever mentioning your service. Answer "recommendation needed" posts daily.
- **One Nextdoor post** has produced first regulars for local services, and those regulars referred more.
- **Tag-mining:** browse tagged photos at the restaurants, gyms and boutiques your ideal clients go to, and follow and engage with those people. Same with followers of local luxury stores and spas, to reach bigger budgets.
- **Positioning wins clients big spas lose:** openly trans-friendly, quiet sessions only, kids specialist, body-hair positive waxing, clinical massage. Solo practitioners report large clienteles from a single clear stance.
- **Misleading post about you in a local group:** answer publicly at once, calmly, then explain privately when contacted. One stylist got the warning removed.

## Booking marketplaces

- **Check which marketplace locals actually use** (Fresha, Treatwell, Booksy, Vagaro, StyleSeat, Watalook in the Baltics, ClassPass for wellness). Customers report finding their nail tech and hairdresser there.
- **Know the fee before listing:** Fresha 20% once (min $6), Booksy Boost 30% ($10 min, $100 max), Treatwell UK about 35% + VAT, StyleSeat optional 30% capped at $50, Vagaro UK 20% from July 2026. Full table in [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md).
- **Expose spare capacity only:** underbooked providers, Tuesday mornings, late openings. Keep Friday and Saturday peaks for direct clients. ClassPass explicitly supports dynamic pricing on perishable slots.
- **Label every marketplace client** and compare their 90-day contribution and second-visit rate with direct clients ([economics.md](economics.md)). If they convert direct and come back, expand; if they stay one-off bargain hunters, throttle.
- **Stop paying for your own clients:** put the direct link in the bio, GBP and every receipt so returning clients do not rebook through the marketplace. Booksy charges Boost on no-shows unless you mark them.

## Paid ads, last

- **Prerequisites:** booking link that takes a card, 20+ reviews, real work photos per advertised service, a concrete offer, and utilization data showing where the new clients will go.
- **KPI is completed new-client CAC, then 90-day payback.** Agency cases show why: $2.10 to $3.50 per lead on Meta (Adsfox), but a med spa paid $12.18 per lead and $251 per booked appointment (MDConsultingNY, 2026); a hair salon's Google Ads ran about $79 per booked appointment (SalonBoss). None is an industry benchmark.
- **Google Search first** for high-intent service + city terms ("balayage [city]", "brazilian wax near me"); searchers are ready to book. Meta and Instagram leads need more persuasion and a concrete intro offer.
- **One campaign per money service or provider,** tight radius, real photos, price or "from" price. Pass booked or completed appointments back to the ad platform where you legally can, instead of training it on cheap forms.
- **Hook structure for transformation services:** result in the first frame, then the specific problem, then real availability. Template in [scripts.md](scripts.md).
- **Test offers in this order:** proof with no discount, then a creditable consult or deposit, then an included add-on, then cash off (synthesis, not a benchmark).
- **Respond fast:** service owners who reply within about 5 minutes win around 70% of leads; after 30 minutes most have booked elsewhere (operators report, cross-industry). Auto-text every form lead immediately.
- **Med spa and health-adjacent services:** Meta's 2025 restrictions on health data mean no audience names or conversion events that reveal a condition or treatment ([Meta](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/graph-api/reference/ad-campaign)). Talk about the service, never imply you know the viewer's condition.
- **Brazil premium launch pattern** (MIT, uncited): mystery-shop 3 premium competitors, launch above their low end with a treatment included, teaser stories with a poll, VIP pre-launch to long-tenure clients (first 15 get an add-on), then a small daily Meta budget for 15 days with a CAC ceiling that triggers a creative change.

## Low-cost offline

- Business card or QR sign where clients pick up their phone after the service (tray, coat area, next to their water).
- Text a digital business card instead of handing a paper one, then follow up.
- Expiring first-visit cards with a QR code work for home services (3 to 5 bookings per 500 cards); expect weaker results for beauty than Google and referrals.
