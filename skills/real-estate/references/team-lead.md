# Team lead: lead routing, leverage and split math

Teams did 31 to 32 sides and $17.5M median volume in 2025, against about 9 sides for an individual REALTOR (NAR 2026 Member Profile). A team is a lead conversion machine: the lead runs acquisition and routing, agents run appointments, leverage runs everything else.

## Split math before anything else

```
GCI = price x rate
after portal fee = GCI x (1 - portal referral fee%)          (Flex 40% seller side, Redfin and Opcity per contract)
agent share = after portal fee x agent split% - per-deal admin fee
team share = after portal fee - agent share
brokerage cut = per brokerage contract (before or after team split?)
team cost per closing (source) = spend for source / closings + share of ISA, CRM, TC cost
team contribution per closing = team share - brokerage cut - team cost per closing
```

Run it per source. A lead source can be profitable for the team and unlivable for the agent: one agent reported a 40% Zillow fee, 50% team split and $250 admin fee leaving him "5%" and switched teams (r/realtors 288, 2024; the claim does not reconcile, but the churn did happen). Agent churn is a cost of the lead program.

Common structure reported by one team-brokerage: 80/20 on agent-sourced (sphere) deals, 60/40 on team-supplied leads, $20,000 annual cap, with free CRM, transaction coordinator, office and leads; it grew from 0 to 36 agents in 2.5 years (r/realtors 251, 2021).

## Routing rules

- **Capacity first.** Do not give an agent more connections than they can work; Zillow's Flex playbook says the same. Measure each agent's response time, two-way contact rate and appointment rate per lead, and route more to whoever converts.
- **Live transfers go to whoever is free now,** web leads to a round robin with a 5-minute accept window, then to the backup.
- **Portal standards are team standards.** Redfin partners must keep 5-minute average connection, 55% acceptance and 80% customer list compliance, or face pause and removal (Redfin 2025). Update dispositions daily so the portal sees a working team.
- **Lower-value leads to newer agents or a showing agent.** One team gave 30% split Redfin leads to its showing agent in idle periods (r/realtors 1200, 2021).
- **Agreement on every handoff, in writing.** An agent who handed off a rental client after showing 10+ homes, without a written split, got nothing when the other agent closed the lease (r/realtors 546, 2025). The broker's suggested split was 50/50.

## Leverage

| Role | What it frees | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| ISA (inside sales) | first calls, nurture calls, appointment setting | the Facebook-ads agent outsourced first calls and kept 22 deals a year (r/realtors 1025, 2020) |
| Offshore VA | data entry, CRM hygiene, listing alerts, dialing prep | about $3 an hour in the Philippines, part of a $25M team (r/realtors 184, 2022) |
| Transaction coordinator | contract to close | part of the recruiting package above |
| Showing agent | tours for lower-intent buyers | paid per showing or split |
| AI tools | listing screening, underwriting drafts, call summaries | 15 to 20 matched deals emailed each morning from 200+ weekly listings; 3 deals found that would have been missed (r/CommercialRealEstate 135, 2025) |

Rule: leverage the tasks, keep the relationship. Replies to nurture messages, listing appointments and negotiations stay with licensed people.

## Daily and weekly team scorecard

- Daily: new leads by source, median first-response time, orphan leads, appointments set, appointments held.
- Weekly: two-way contact rate by agent and source, consultation to agreement rate, listing appointments, agreements signed, pendings, cancellations.
- Monthly: cost per closing and team contribution per closing by source; agent take-home by source; portal standing (Redfin metrics, Zillow performance); review count and velocity.
- Five daily conversations per agent is a common floor: top producers in one office each had 5 to 10 real estate conversations a day by phone (observation, r/realtors 437, 2025). Count conversations, not dials or posts.

## Recruiting and retention

- **What agents value is support, not swag.** One independent office pays for Zillow and Realtor.com leads for everyone, charges no desk fees, pays MLS dues, raises splits 5 points per $1M sold (reset yearly) and distributes leftover profit at year end after keeping a year of expenses; it reports 100% agent retention over 10 years, while admitting middle-of-the-pack production (r/realtors 388, 2025).
- **The opposite lesson:** a producing broker estimated losing $300,000 of his own productivity training and feeding agents who did little, then moved to 100% commission with a flat fee for tech, compliance, E&O and admin, and reports making more (same thread).
- **Luxury splits:** a 50/50 split on a $2M buyer beats 100% of a $500k client at the same rate (r/realtors 279, 2024). Partnering with an established luxury agent is a legitimate entry.
- **Commercial origination splits:** some brokers negotiate origination-only splits and cold-call in bursts while others manage relationships (secondhand, r/CommercialRealEstate 1188, 2023).

## The team lead's own time

- Dollar-productive activities first: lead generation, appointments, negotiations, recruiting. A new agent who spent six months on branding made nothing (r/realtors 184, 2022).
- Burnout is real at $100k: one agent at $127k was spending $3,000 a month on marketing that barely broke even, working 6 am to 10 pm and every weekend (r/realtors 358, 2024). Before adding a source, cut the one with the worst cost per closing and add leverage.
