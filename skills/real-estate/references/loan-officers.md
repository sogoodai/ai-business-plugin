# Mortgage loan officers: partners, database and compliant marketing

The purchase business runs through agents. 62% of mortgage buyers used an agent or broker referral while shopping and 20% first found the lender they used that way; first-time buyers value the lender referral more (28% rank it a top-3 agent service vs 21% of repeat buyers) (Zillow 2026). Agent referral networks are concentrated: 79% of agents send more than 40% of their clients to just four loan officers (NBER working paper, 91,988 agents and 123,749 LOs in 40 states, 2026). The game is to become one of an agent's four, not to be known by 400 agents.

Margins are thin: independent mortgage banks made $785 pre-tax production profit per loan in 2025, up from $443 in 2024 (MBA, April 2026). Optimize to funded, profitable loans, never to applications.

## Unit economics

```
LO revenue per funded loan = loan amount x comp bps (from the comp plan)
partner yield = funded loans from a partner / buyer sides the partner closed (ask them)
pull-through = funded / applications (by source)
cost per funded loan (source) = spend / funded loans from that source
partner value per year = funded loans from partner x revenue per loan
```

A rough production baseline: about 21 units a year for an average LO, about 53 for the top 10% (National Mortgage Professional, date and method unclear; treat as rough). One extra active agent sending 6 loans a year is a 25% raise for that average LO.

## Agent partner program

**What agents need from a lender (observable service, not gifts):**
- Preapproval turnaround with a real credit and income review, written down (for example same business day).
- A call to the listing agent on every offer to vouch for the buyer and the timeline.
- File status updates to the agent and buyer at fixed milestones (application, appraisal ordered, appraisal in, conditional approval, clear to close).
- Weekend and evening availability during offer season.
- A plan B for hard files: second opinions, non-QM, DSCR, bank statement, commercial for property-income borrowers.
- Market knowledge that helps agents win listings: which condo buildings are warrantable, assumable loans in the farm, how buydowns compare with price cuts.

**Partner scoring (monthly):** opportunities sent, funded loans, pull-through, time to clear to close, agent satisfaction, mutual client quality. Rank partners by actual flow, not by lunches or co-marketing activity (research synthesis, 2026).

**Prospecting agents:**
- Start with agents whose buyers already closed with you (ask both for a debrief), then agents on the other side of your deals, then high-volume buyer agents in your price bands from MLS data.
- Ask for one file, not the database: "Give me one buyer who needs a second opinion" ([scripts.md](scripts.md)).
- Show up where agents work: open houses (pay only your own costs), broker opens, office meetings with a short useful talk (assumables, condo financing, buydown math). Depth on cold outreach mechanics: `b2b-outbound`.
- Builders: preferred-lender packages with a concrete financing offer win buyers (one buyer took $9,000 toward closing costs, 5% down and a 15-day close through a builder's lender in 2021; historical terms, r/RealEstate 865, 2021).

## RESPA guardrails for partner marketing (US)

- No fee, kickback or thing of value for referrals of settlement service business, under an agreement or understanding (RESPA Section 8, Regulation X 1024.14) ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/compliance/compliance-resources/mortgage-resources/real-estate-settlement-procedures-act/real-estate-settlement-procedures-act-faqs/)).
- Co-marketing: each party pays its pro rata share based on actual use and space; you do not pay an agent's share of an ad, mailer, event or lead platform. Payments for services actually performed must be at fair market value and must not include the value of referrals.
- Paid digital comparison or lead platforms can violate RESPA when placement steers consumers non-neutrally and payment is tied to referrals (CFPB advisory opinion on digital mortgage comparison shopping platforms).
- Marketing services agreements are high risk; get compliance sign-off with written deliverables and an FMV analysis.
- Gifts and meals: keep small, occasional and not tied to referrals; follow the company policy and state law.
- Wrong: "I'll pay for your client event if you send me three buyers." Right: each buys its own defined advertising, priced on its own, with nothing conditioned on referrals (research synthesis, 2026). Not legal advice.

## Database and refinance triggers

- **Your own past clients are the second engine.** Watch for: rate drop large enough to clear costs, equity milestones (PMI removal, cash-out need), ARM resets, life events they tell you about, the 12-year average move cycle (NAR 2026).
- **Do the honest math in dollars.** One LO quoted an $800,000 refinance at the same rate as a large lender with $16,000 less in costs (r/Mortgages 352, 2025). Present fee comparisons at the same rate.
- **Show whether to roll costs in.** Break down how financing closing costs changes the balance and savings, and let the borrower choose; at 6% it deserves more scrutiny than at 2 to 3% (r/Mortgages 148, 2025).
- **Explain cash flow honestly:** $15,000 at closing ($3,000 loan costs, $12,000 prepaids), an $8,000 old escrow refund about 30 days later, no payment the next month ($3,500), so about $3,500 net out of pocket. Call it a "gap payment", never a skipped payment (r/Mortgages 148, 2025).
- **Low-rate holders who want cash:** a fixed-rate second mortgage (10 to 20 years) instead of a cash-out refinance of a 2% first, where you have the product (r/Mortgages 352, 2025).
- **Rate buydowns:** paying points for a "super low rate" is often a poor deal on a refinance; refi rates run higher than advertised purchase rates (r/Mortgages 352, 2025). Recommend by break-even months.

## Product and content angles that create referrals

- **Assumable FHA and VA loans:** teach agents to ask the listing agent at every showing; a VA loan at 2.875% with about $280,000 left was found this way; assumptions add 45 to 90 days (r/Mortgages 210, 2026).
- **State-specific content where rules differ:** one lender keeps separate Florida condo marketing because Florida condo financing has different rental and building-risk constraints (r/RealEstate 1259, 2024, 387 upvotes).
- **Do not advertise rare exceptions.** A lender's marketing team refused to promote a one-off VA condo exception because it would set expectations other borrowers could not meet (same thread, 143 upvotes).
- **Asset-rich, income-light borrowers:** "Sir, I am sorry, you do not qualify for a $700k home loan, even if you are putting 50% down. However, could I interest you in a substantially larger apartment building loan that also carries a lower interest rate to boot?" Commercial underwriting looks at property income; also ask entrepreneurs who rent their workspace about buying premises (r/RealEstate 2283, 2025).

## Advertising rules for LOs (US)

- **Regulation Z trigger terms** (a payment amount, number of payments, finance charge, down payment) require the APR and the other key terms in the same ad (12 CFR 1026.24; [Federal Reserve Consumer Compliance Outlook](https://www.consumercomplianceoutlook.org/articles/2021/first-issue-2021/understanding-regulation-zs-advertising-requirements)). A rate in an ad needs the APR.
- **Housing and financial special ad categories** on Meta and Google: no age, gender or ZIP targeting ([Google](https://support.google.com/adspolicy/answer/143465)); see `paid-ads`.
- **Licensing:** state rules commonly require the NMLS ID and company license information on ads, social profiles and business cards (general practice; confirm with your compliance team).
- **Equal housing:** use the equal housing lender statement or logo per your company policy.
- **Consent:** rate-alert and refinance texts are marketing; they need prior express written consent if automated ([compliance.md](compliance.md)).
