# Listings and sellers

A listing has two customers: the seller choosing an agent and the buyer choosing a home. The asset that wins the appointment is not always the one buyers use (research synthesis, 2026). Sellers buy pricing judgment: 42% rank pricing and market knowledge as the most valuable agent service and 67% put it in their top two (Zillow 2026). Buyers decide whether to visit from the floor plan (33%), photos (26%) and 3D tour (20%), not video (4%) (Zillow 2025).

## The listing presentation as a system

Show the sequence, not a logo wall of platforms:

`pricing evidence > launch audience > listing assets > first-week engagement > showing feedback > offer strategy > price-adjustment rule`

1. **Pricing evidence.** Closed comps last 60 to 90 days, actives they compete with, pendings, and what offers are actually winning. One agent reviewed about 60 offers in the prior month to see what buyers accept; sellers who followed his price averaged about 6 days on market (r/realtors 601, 2026). For condos, compare sold units with similar HOA fees in the last 60 days (r/RealEstate 1389, 2025).
2. **Search brackets.** Price at or just under a common filter ceiling: $499,000 rather than $510,000 to appear in "up to $500k" searches (r/RealEstate 2953, 2025); $420,069 drops out of $420,000 filters to gain $69 (r/RealEstate 3022, 2023).
3. **The cost of overpricing, with their numbers.** A seller rejected a strong first-weekend offer hoping for $50,000 more and sold 60 days and several reductions later for $47,000 less than that first offer (r/realtors 1019, 2026; 228 upvotes for using it in presentations). Another priced at $445,000 against advice of $430,000; three competing homes at $420,000 to $430,000 went under contract in week one; six months later the seller took $408,000 (same thread). Overpricing by even 5% can mean a month of chasing the market (r/realtors 601, 2026).
4. **Appraisal exposure.** A home listed 10% above the closest sale, cash offer at asking: the seller countered, the buyer walked; three months later a financed offer $10,000 under asking appraised $20,000 lower still (r/realtors 1019, 2026).
5. **Launch assets in buyer priority order:** floor plan, professional photos, 3D tour, accurate description, then video for reach. Garage interior and exterior photos before boxes pile up (r/RealEstate 894, 2021). Condo listings: floor, elevator, hurricane windows, trash chute, pets, rental rules, HOA inclusions, inspection and reserve documents uploaded to the MLS (r/RealEstate 1389, 2025).
6. **Price-adjustment rule agreed in writing at signing.** "If it doesn't sell within 2 weeks, we will adjust the price" and "any offer within 5% of asking price is a good offer... a great starting point to negotiate" (r/realtors 426, 2025). Showing trigger: fewer than 2 showings a week in a county of about 1 million people means review price (r/realtors 304, 2026). Size cuts by payment: aim for at least $200 a month less for the buyer; a $5,000 to $10,000 cut barely moves affordability (r/realtors 304, 2026).

Payment math for that rule: at a 6.5% 30-year rate, each $10,000 of loan is about $63 a month. $200 a month is about $32,000 of loan, or about $40,000 of price with 20% down. Recompute at the current rate.

## Launch hygiene that loses listings when missed

- Verify the listing is in the right local MLS on day one: one relist sat in a metro MLS two hours away for weeks, costing the spring market (r/RealEstate 915, 2025).
- Dual-listed homes: enter the open house in both MLSs and check Zillow shows it the day before (r/realtors 237, 2023).
- New construction: check portals for wrong "buildable plan" labels and stale timelines (r/RealEstate 865, 2021).
- Check the showing-request log yourself. Twelve requests went to two sellers' spam folders over 3.5 weeks before a $13,000 cut (r/RealEstate 799, 2025).
- Keep an auditable marketing spend log per listing; one top-4% agent can produce a cancellation cost report in about 2 hours (r/RealEstate 2819, 2025).
- A seller's house binder (manuals, paint codes, filter sizes, receipts, garden care) was mentioned in several offers as proof of a diligent owner (r/RealEstate 2392, 2026). Suggest it at listing.
- Prep sequencing: paint before new floors; replace worn switch and outlet covers (r/RealEstate 945, 2026).

## Private listings and portal rules (US, 2025 to 2026)

- NAR's Multiple Listing Options for Sellers (2025) lets a seller choose delayed marketing with a signed disclosure that they waive immediate public marketing; MLSs implemented by 30 September 2025 ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/03/25/home-sale-private-listing-rule), [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/06/30/its-reckoning-day-for-private-listings-what-you-should-know-about-zillows-changes/)).
- Zillow's Listing Access Standards: a listing publicly marketed but not on the MLS within 24 hours is banned from Zillow and Trulia for its life; third noncompliant listing bans the agent's listings; office exclusives and coming soon that follow NAR guidance are allowed ([Zillow](https://www.zillow.com/premier-agent/listing-access-standards)). Compass sued; check status before advising.
- Rule for the agent: never post "pocket listing, call me" publicly. Offer delayed marketing only when the seller signs the disclosure and understands they lose portal exposure.

## Seller conversations during the listing

- **Net sheets side by side** for offer and counteroffer, so the seller compares what they walk away with (r/realtors 426, 2025).
- **Ask the number.** When sellers resist an offer $5,000 over asking, ask what they expected; one revealed $20,000 over (same thread).
- **Let them talk.** One agent listened for half an hour without interrupting, called again the next morning, and the seller countered at full price with 2% to closing costs (same thread).
- **Closing cost credits:** ask the buyer's agent whether a credit is needed to close; consider $410,000 with a $10,000 credit instead of $400,000 net, checking appraisal support (r/realtors 426, 2025, 130 upvotes).
- **Inspection concessions:** compare the concession with the cost of losing the buyer. A seller refused $40,000 for a roof and moisture issue and later sold for $30,000 less than that net (r/RealEstate 2953, 2025).
- **Do not promise a bidding war** to talk a seller out of an offer. One agent did, the buyers came back lower with conditions, and the agent lost two neighborhood listings when the story spread (r/realtors 1019, 2026).
- **Keep weekend open houses** before accepting a sight-unseen offer that demands their cancellation; one seller held them and took cash $29,000 over asking days later (r/RealEstate 340, 2026). Advise per market.

## Cancellation and cost recovery

- Agree up front: a flat cancellation fee (one agent uses $1,000 for photos, marketing, open houses and time) credited back at closing if the seller relists within 12 months (r/realtors 271, 2025).
- Alternative: cancel anytime, absorb standard marketing, charge only for seller-requested extras like drone work. One agent had a single cancellation under that policy, and the client sent $5,000 anyway (same thread).
- Collect by email per the contract, never at the doorstep. A seller who paid a disclosed photo fee by email came back 1.5 years later to sell and buy with the same agent (same thread).
- In 2026 markets, sellers increasingly ask for "cancel anytime"; one agent lost a listing by refusing (r/realtors 88, 2026). Decide the policy before the appointment.

## When to walk away

- Overpriced and the seller bans open houses, social posts and flyers: one agent took a listing $70,000 over market under those terms and was fired after a month (r/realtors 400, 2025).
- Seller will not maintain showing condition after two frank talks and a family task plan (r/realtors 92, 2026).
- CRE: no listing until the owner agrees on value; a $6M expectation on a $4M property can waste years (r/CommercialRealEstate 133, 2026).

## Outside the US

- **UK:** sell evidence of price and speed; 47% of vendors would pay a higher fee for each (Rightmove 2025). Average fee about 1.3% including VAT (Rightmove, 2025 data). "Number one" or "market leader" needs substantiation; board counts and portal stock are not enough ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/property-sales-and-board-counting.html)).
- **Australia:** price representations are policed (ACCC), with state underquoting rules; REA products now report appraisal requests and lead-to-listing outcomes, so agents are measured on them ([ACCC](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/specific-products-and-activities/real-estate)).
- **Price on application:** one 17-year agent suggests "Price on Application" where allowed, to force buyer calls (r/realtors 304, 2026). Not legal in every market (underquoting and price-display rules); check first.
