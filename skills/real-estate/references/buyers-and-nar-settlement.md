# Buyers after the NAR settlement

## What the rules require (US, NAR and MLS participants, from 17 August 2024)

- **Written agreement before touring** a home in person or by live virtual tour, once you are "working with" the buyer (identifying homes, arranging tours). It may be signed earlier; state law may require earlier ([NAR facts](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/what-the-nar-settlement-means-for-home-buyers-and-sellers), [Florida Realtors FAQ](https://floridarealtors.org/law-ethics/nar-settlement-faqs)).
- **Compensation must be objectively ascertainable and not open-ended** ("2.5%" or "$12,000", not "whatever the seller offers"), and negotiable. The buyer broker cannot receive more than the agreement states.
- **No offers of compensation on the MLS.** Sellers may still offer buyer-agent compensation off-MLS, or buyers may ask for it in their offer.
- **Not required:** a buyer visiting an open house on their own, or asking an agent about services. The listing agent hosting an open house needs no agreement with visitors ([NAR consumer guide](https://www.nar.realtor/the-facts/consumer-guide-to-open-houses-and-written-agreements)).
- **An agreement is not necessarily agency.** NAR says the written agreement need not be an exclusive agency agreement; brokers choose forms within state law.
- Outside the US these rules do not apply as law.

## What happened to pay

Average buyer agent commission was 2.43% in Q2 2025, up from 2.38% a year earlier and back to Q1 2024 levels; 2.52% under $500,000 and 2.17% on $1M+ homes (Redfin, 2025). Redfin agents report most sellers still pay, some cap at 2%, and more wait to see what the buyer asks for in the offer. Plan for compensation to be negotiated per deal, and tell the buyer what happens if the seller pays less.

## The buyer funnel now

`inquiry > two-way contact > buyer consultation (agreement signed) > preapproval > search > tours > offer > contract > close`

The consultation is the product. Move the compensation conversation into an explicit value event instead of a form at the car door (research synthesis, 2026). The 20-minute format below is an operating choice, not a benchmark.

**Consultation agenda (20 to 30 minutes, video or in person):**

1. Their plan: timeline, must-haves, payment comfort (not just preapproval amount), current home or lease.
2. The closed-sale reality check (below).
3. How offers are winning right now: list-to-sale ratio, days on market, concessions, inspection and appraisal patterns.
4. Lender: preapproval status; introduce two or three lenders without anything of value exchanged (RESPA), and ask about assumable FHA or VA loans on target listings.
5. Exactly how you are paid, what the agreement covers, its term, and how to end it.
6. Sign, set up the saved search together, book the first tour.

## Qualify before you drive

- **Closed-sale reality check.** Buyer names home type, area, features and price range. Search MLS closings from the last 3 months with those criteria; the buyer must find at least 3 homes they would have bought. If not, ask which criterion they will change, or whether they will wait and save. Repeat until there are 3. Features, not price, were the most adjusted criterion; searches got dramatically shorter (San Francisco team, r/RealEstate 915, 2021).
- **Preapproval or proof of funds before showings,** explained by phone: the right home can go under contract before an unprepared buyer is approved, and touring $100,000 over budget ruins the homes they can afford (r/realtors 351, 2024). Some agents push back that refusing to meet unprepared buyers loses business (same thread). Middle path: one complimentary showing after the agreement, then preapproval before more (r/realtors 134, 2026).
- **Distant showings:** ask cash or financing, preapproval, and whether they must sell first, before a 3.5-hour round trip (r/realtors 475, 2026).
- **Represented already?** Ask on the first call (r/realtors 305, 2024).
- **Know when to stop.** A buyer who fired the agent twice, signed only property-specific agreements and wanted 16 lowball offers cost a year, 250 showings across 3 counties and $1,500 in gas with no purchase (r/realtors 318, 2023).

## Buyer fee and service models seen in practice

| Model | How it works | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Flat fee with rebate | $6,000 flat, anything the seller pays above that is rebated to the buyer | secondhand, high-cost market, r/RealEstate 2386, 2024 |
| Contract-only | $750 for writing the contract, excess compensation rebated | secondhand, r/RealEstate 2386, 2024 |
| Retainer credited at closing | retainer goes to closing costs if they buy, to the agent if the agreement ends without a purchase | r/realtors 351, 2024 |
| Per-tour fee credited | $50 a home toured, refunded from commission at purchase; for buyers who will not sign a broader agreement | proposal, r/realtors 227, 2024 |
| Paid showing for another agent's buyer | "I will show you the home for him for $100" | rejected by the buyer, r/realtors 305, 2024 |

Rebates and limited-service models are illegal or restricted in some states; check state law and the broker before offering.

## Buyer-side value most agents skip

- **Assumable loans.** Ask the listing agent at every showing whether the seller has an assumable FHA or VA loan; one agent found a VA loan at 2.875% with about $280,000 left. The buyer needs cash or a second loan for the gap and 45 to 90 extra days (r/Mortgages 210, 2026).
- **Property history research** before tours: one agent cut an 18-home shortlist to 10, sometimes visiting city hall (r/realtors 1748, 2025).
- **Negotiation as the value you sell.** "Leave it to me, you hired me to negotiate for you so please trust me on this." The buyers, ready to pay asking, got a lower price and their dates, then posted a Google review right after acceptance (same thread).
- **Deadlines are reputation.** A mortgage contingency extension requested two weeks late nearly cost a client $10,000 (r/realtors 437, 2025).

## Buyer lead sources that fit the new rules

- Portal and website leads still dominate first contact (28% website or app, Zillow 2026) but need the consultation step before tours.
- Open houses: 6% of buyers met their agent there (Zillow 2026). Book the consultation at the open house; do not tour other homes with them before the agreement.
- Meta: price-capped home lists ("Homes under $X in [area]") with qualifying questions (r/realtors 1025, 2020), under the housing special ad category.
- Relocation content (moving guides, neighborhood comparisons) for out-of-area buyers ([local-and-content.md](local-and-content.md)).
