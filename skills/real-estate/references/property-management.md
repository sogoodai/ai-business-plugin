# Property management: owner acquisition and retention

Residential property management grows on two numbers: doors signed and doors kept. Owners are a high-intent search market (Google Search for property management converts 10.24% of clicks to leads at $76.71 CPL, vs 1.29% and $157.59 for residential agents; LocaliQ 2026) and a dissatisfied one (only 23% rate their manager's value excellent; Buildium 2026, vendor survey). That combination means switchers are the cheapest growth, and churn is the biggest leak.

## Unit economics

```
revenue per door per year = monthly rent x management fee% x 12
                          + leasing fee x turnovers per year + renewal fees + other fees (inspection, onboarding)
owner value = revenue per door x doors per owner x expected years retained
cost per signed door = acquisition spend / doors signed
payback months = cost per signed door / monthly revenue per door
churn = doors lost in 12 months / doors at start (split: sold the property vs switched manager vs self-manage)
```

Example: $2,000 rent x 10% = $200 a month, $2,400 a year; plus a leasing fee of 50 to 100% of one month's rent on each turnover (secondary fee guides, 2026). A typical fee is 8 to 12% of collected rent, 10% the most cited; the often-quoted 8.49% average traces to a 2022 study (secondary sources, see [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md)). Outliers exist: an agent quoted a 30% fee for a long-term rental in Southern California, and investors called it absurd (r/realestateinvesting 296, 2023).

Separate "lost because the owner sold" from "lost to a competitor". The first is a sales opportunity (refer or list the property); the second is a service failure.

## What owners buy

Owners choose on tenant management 57%, maintenance coordination 43%, customer service 34%, responsiveness 31%; maintenance is the top stressor for 38%; poor communication is the top switching reason at 57% (Buildium 2026, vendor surveys). Sell those, with proof, not "full-service management".

**Positioning that answers the outsourcing questions:** expected rent and vacancy, maintenance coordination and approval limits, tenant screening, owner reporting, fees in full, inspection schedule, arrears process, after-hours handling (research synthesis, 2026).

**Transparency is the switching hook.**
- One owner pays 10% with actual vendor invoices, no repair markup, and approval required above $250 except emergencies (with notice); local competitors charged 8% plus repair markups (r/PropertyManagement 132, 2026).
- A Detroit manager pays vendors only with before and after photos, full receipts and complete work-order notes, and shows maintenance limits to vendors in AppFolio; overrides need management approval. He reports regularly taking clients from managers whose owners thought unexplained fees were normal (same thread).
- Publish a one-page fee sheet with every fee named. Owners comparing 8% plus markups with 10% all-in need the math done for them.

## Owner acquisition channels

| Channel | Why | How |
|---|---|---|
| Google Search and Maps | 88.4% of owner leads in one agency's 31-company dataset (2026, selection bias); PM search converts 10.24% (LocaliQ 2026) | Separate campaigns for "property management [city]", "rental management", "manage my rental"; owner-focused service pages; reviews from owners |
| Switchers | 23% excellent-value rating leaves most owners open | Ads and pages on "switch property managers [city]", a free owner statement review |
| Agents | Listings that do not sell become rentals; buyers of investment property need a manager | Referral relationship with listing agents (state rules on referral fees for PM vary; no fee where prohibited) |
| Accidental landlords | Owners who cannot sell in a slow market | Expired and withdrawn listings by mail (not cold calls to registered numbers), agent referrals |
| Vacant neglected homes | An owner far away with a visibly unkept house | County records for owner and mailing address, offer of local care (proposed tactic, r/realtors 346, 2025) |
| Investor groups and local REIA | Buy-and-hold investors add doors | Talks with real numbers: vacancy days, maintenance per door, renewal rate |
| Relocation and corporate housing | Furnished mid-term rentals | Airbnb and Vrbo with a 30-day minimum and relocation company listings brought corporate and insurance placements for one operator; adjusters wanted large homes with fenced yards for displaced families (r/realestateinvesting 1101, 2023) |

**Lead offers that beat "free rental analysis":**
- "We'll show you what the property should rent for, the maintenance issues most likely to become expensive, and exactly how owner communication works before you sign anything" (research template, 2026).
- Meta or search lead form: "Rental owner in [market]? Get a one-page rent, vacancy and maintenance-risk review, plus the three issues most likely to create an expensive call this year" (research template, 2026).

## Screening owners (bad doors cost more than no doors)

Decline owners who demand rent well above market (15% over in one case), refuse showings while occupied, want move-in the day after they move out, and cannot absorb a couple hundred dollars a month or a $200 faucet repair. One manager accepted these "reluctant landlords" before and says it was terrible business every time; he now turns them down despite several inquiries a week (r/PropertyManagement 59, 2026).

Ask before signing: target rent and their minimum, reserves for repairs, mortgage cash flow, timeline (rent then sell?), who decides, prior manager and why they left.

## Leasing and vacancy (what owners see first)

- **A manager producing zero showings is a churn event.** An owner lost two units for four months, zero applications, about $10,000 of rent, then fired the manager; he later filled both by flying in, putting a "for rent" sign on the building and showing himself, about a week each (r/realestateinvesting 1101, 2023).
- **Clean and stage before cutting rent** when the asking rent is fair (r/realestateinvesting 282, 2025). If comparable units sit 100+ days, price under them: a duplex near Fort Hood leased after dropping from $1,450 to $1,250 against $1,395 comps sitting empty (r/realestateinvesting 100, 2026).
- **Price slightly under market to choose tenants.** One manager of 30 units lists 10% under the going rate and selects for credit (r/realestateinvesting 282, 2025); others argue higher rents attract better tenants (33 and 31 upvotes). Make it the owner's decision with the vacancy math shown.
- **Start the next leasing cycle early** in seasonal markets (student rentals filled late at a one-year discount, r/realestateinvesting 402, 2019).
- **Housing vouchers:** a converted hotel had units inspected with the housing authority and stayed full, about 12 inquiries a week, when 9 in 10 local voucher holders could not find a participating landlord; a charity bridged rent while paperwork cleared (r/realestateinvesting 1409, 2022). Source-of-income protections exist in many states and cities; refusing vouchers can be illegal there.

## Retention: the monthly owner update

Lead with outcomes: "This month: rent collected, outstanding actions, maintenance spend versus plan, lease and renewal risks, and the one decision we need from you" (research template, 2026). Send it on a fixed date. Add a churn alert when an owner goes quiet, disputes an invoice, asks about selling, or mentions another manager.

**Tenant-side retention protects owner retention:**
- Approve modest urgent repairs immediately: $300 for a long-term tenant's July AC failure rather than two weeks of quotes to save $50 (r/PropertyManagement 150, 2025).
- Space increases for clean, reliable tenants; turnover is expensive (several commenters, 26 to 80 upvotes); others prefer annual increases kept under market (43 upvotes) (r/realestateinvesting 282, 2025). Show the owner turnover cost vs the increase.
- Renewal options: one park ties the one-year increase to the Social Security COLA, the two-year lock to COLA plus 1.5%, and month-to-month at market (r/realestateinvesting 55, 2026).
- Late rent process, same every time: day 1 short message, day 5 call and the fee in writing (never waived on request: the one waiver led to three more late months), day 10 formal step (240 doors, Memphis, r/PropertyManagement 195, 2026). Split-rent services (half on the 1st, half two weeks later, about $25 fee, estimate) and deposit alternatives (about 20% of the deposit as a non-refundable premium) help residents whose paychecks or move costs do not line up (r/CommercialRealEstate 49, 2025).
- Move-out photos of everything, close enough to read, dated: two deposit disputes ended in one email each (r/PropertyManagement 131, 2026).

## Reviews and referrals at value events

Ask owners for a review or referral right after a visible win: a hard maintenance problem solved, a strong tenant placed fast, a vacancy avoided, a renewal at a good rent (research synthesis, 2026). Ask tenants separately; mixed owner and tenant reviews show both sides of the service.

## AI in the PM workflow

AI adoption in real estate management reached 58% in 2026 from 21% in 2023 (AppFolio/IREM, 3,662 professionals). AppFolio's "31% vs 12% expected growth" for AI adopters is expectations and correlation; do not forecast from it. Useful jobs: triage inquiries, summarize owner conversations, flag open maintenance, draft rent-review explanations, detect churn language, surface owners who may sell. Keep human approval on tenant selection (fair housing) and on legal or financial messages.

## UK note

From 1 May 2026 in England, letting ads must state a specific rent and must not imply discrimination against tenants with children or on benefits; do not copy these rules to Scotland, Wales or Northern Ireland ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/property-letting-agents.html)).
