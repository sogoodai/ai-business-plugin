# ClassPass and other aggregators

Treat aggregators as airline-style yield management. An empty reformer, bike or mat at class start is perishable inventory worth zero. An aggregator booking that replaces a full-price member, or teaches a member to buy cheaper through the app, has a negative value. Every number from ClassPass is vendor data with a strong interest in the answer.

## How ClassPass pays (2026)

- Partners negotiate a **confidential minimum floor rate**; SmartRate adjusts the credit price a customer pays around demand but never pays out below the floor; payouts follow the partnership agreement. Credits have no fixed cash value (ClassPass, updated March 6, 2026).
- **SmartSpot** uses historical attendance and current bookings to expose spots it predicts will not sell directly.
- ClassPass runs a **trial program** where qualifying first visits can be unpaid; a 21-year yoga studio owner reports receiving nothing for those visits and lower payouts afterwards than direct bookings (r/YogaTeachers, 205). Read the contract for trial visit terms before signing.

## What the vendor says

- Average studio fills under 36% of listed capacity through its own channels (Jul 2025 to Jun 2026, studio offerings with capacity of 80 or less).
- 94% of ClassPass users are new to the venue they book (2020 to January 2025, partly outdated).
- More than 99% of businesses using both Mindbody and ClassPass had positive incremental revenue (Dec 2024 to Dec 2025). Shared ownership lets them see both sides, but it is still their own claim.
- SmartRate users in 2024 averaged about 20% higher payouts, 2x+ new visitors and 14% higher fill than non-users (possibly outdated, self-selected).

## What operators say

- **"Most costly business mistake" in 21 years:** unpaid trial visits, lower payouts, and students buying the same classes cheaper through ClassPass instead of memberships (r/YogaTeachers, 205).
- **A former instructor and ClassPass customer's playbook** (r/YogaTeachers, 174): 1 to 2 ClassPass spots in popular classes, more inventory for hard-to-fill noon and 3 pm classes; a direct price close to what the customer pays ClassPass (if the customer pays about $15 and the studio receives $10, a $15 direct booking beats a $30 to $40 drop-in); a long-validity 20-class flash pack at $15 to $20 a class; members get a 2 to 4 hour cancellation window vs 12 hours on ClassPass. Proposals from one buyer, not tested results; the purchase behavior they describe is real.

## Rules for the agent

1. **List only class-hours that historically leave sellable spots empty.** Default: off-peak and underfilled. Peak classes that fill from owned demand get 0 to 2 spots.
2. **Measure incremental, not gross:**

```
incremental revenue per class-hour = aggregator payout
                                     - direct revenue displaced (members who downgraded, drop-ins who would have paid direct)
                                     - variable cost per added attendee (towels, heat, consumables, instructor per-head pay)
```

"ClassPass made us $4,000 this month" means nothing if most of it came from classes that would have sold out. ClassPass's own partner FAQ defines incrementality net of displaced direct revenue.

3. **Watch the downgrade signal monthly:** members who cancelled and then appear as aggregator users, and direct drop-in volume in classes where aggregator spots opened. If either rises, cut inventory in those classes.
4. **Track aggregator visitors as a funnel:** first visit, second visit, lifetime aggregator revenue and any direct conversion the agreement and consent rules allow. An aggregator visitor is not a lead you own; do not market to them without consent and contract permission.
5. **Make direct the better deal for regulars,** not the cheaper one only: shorter cancellation window, priority booking for popular classes, rollover, member-only events. Keep the drop-in price as an anchor for the intro offer.
6. **Do not list a class you might cancel.** Last-minute cancellations hit members first (r/pilates, 442).
7. **Exit test after 90 days:** if incremental revenue per listed class-hour is low and direct membership sales in the same dayparts fell, reduce to off-peak only or leave.

## Other aggregators and lead marketplaces

- **Gympass/Wellhub, Urban Sports Club, Hussle and similar:** same yield logic; corporate wellness platforms bring employees who would not have paid you directly, but get the payout per visit and any monthly cap in writing. No robust public payout benchmark was found.
- **Thumbtack (pay per lead, PTs):** one trainer built a profile with reviews from free and cheap early sessions and made it the main channel, reaching $103,000 in year five at $89 an hour (r/personaltraining, 148). Another spent $200+ over a year and 9 of 10 leads never replied (6 upvotes). Rule: reply to every lead within minutes, track lead reply rate and spend per paid client monthly, and stop when spend per paid client passes the max CAC from [economics.md](economics.md#the-lead-funnel-and-cac).
- **Matched-client coaching platforms:** a coach lost a matched client by replying an hour late while teaching (r/lifecoaching, 48). Only list when you can reply fast.
