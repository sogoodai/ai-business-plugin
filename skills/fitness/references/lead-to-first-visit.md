# Lead to first visit to member

This is the clearest leak in the data. In North America 57% of membership inquiries got no reply; replies took 4 h by email, 17.5 h on Facebook and 37 h on Instagram; 52% of gym searching happens outside opening hours; 55% of operators sent no reminder before a booked tour; only 3 to 6 of 100 paid leads became members (Keepme 2026, vendor research). Fix this before any ad or content work. Exact wording: [scripts.md](scripts.md).

## Stage taxonomy (set it up on day 1)

`new lead > contacted > qualified > booked > showed > won or lost > active > at risk > churned`

Every lead carries source, campaign, channel, timestamp of first reply, and a lost reason from a fixed list: no response, schedule, price, location, partner approval, injury or medical, wrong service, not ready, competitor, other. Without the lost reason, an agent cannot tell a weak ad from bad hours or a mismatched offer.

## Response SLA

- **One inbox.** Website forms, Meta lead ads (use the Leads webhook so leads arrive in real time, not on a daily poll), Instagram and Facebook DMs, Google Business Profile calls, email, phone. Every channel has a named owner.
- **At submission:** acknowledge at once, answer the question if you can, offer two bookable times, capture source.
- **In staffed hours:** a human picks it up. Trainers who convert call web leads within 2 to 3 minutes and get alerts on both a work and personal phone; "waiting over an hour effectively loses the lead" (r/personaltraining AMA, 364).
- **After hours:** a self-book link and an automated reply that does not pretend to be a person; context queued for staff in the morning.
- **Binary question beats open question.** "Tuesday at 6 or Thursday at 7?" instead of "How can I help?" (suggested copy in the research, not a tested script).
- **Question-first works only if you keep the answer.** If you ask about goals and past attempts, store it and use it at the visit. Asking the same story three times is friction.
- **Do not harass.** The point is no dead channel and no dead period, not more messages.

## Qualification: fit, not gatekeeping

- **PT and online coaching:** ask about schedule and budget before a long sales conversation. One trainer lists rates on the site and asks direct inquiries "When would you like these sessions and what is your budget?"; emails from the site mean the prospect has already seen prices (r/personaltraining, 156, agreed at 9).
- **Online coaches:** hold the calendar link until three answers come back: can you invest in help now, are you ready to do the work, what failed before and why (r/personaltraining, 53). Application structure and disqualify rules: `education-coaching`.
- **Screen for repeated disputes:** ask why previous trainer relationships ended; repeated "every trainer was bad", especially over billing, is a red flag (r/personaltraining, 97).
- **Qualify for duration, not just a start.** Clients who can only afford a short run create churn; one operator's average client stays four years (r/personaltraining, 82).
- **Never answer a price question with a brush-off.** Four CrossFit boxes replied "If your first question is about the fee, then we're not a good fit for you" and lost the same prospect (r/crossfit, 162). Give the range, then invite a visit.
- **Walk-up price question in a gym:** give a goal-based range ("75 to 160 depending on what you want to achieve"), then hear goals before quoting (r/personaltraining, 156; operator later sold at $140 to $160 an hour).

## Booking and show

- **Send the next step the minute the call ends:** time slots and the waiver. Trainers lose prospects who loved the call when this waits (r/personaltraining, 200).
- **Paperwork goes online before the visit.** Moving 15 to 20 minutes of floor paperwork into a pre-session form (Typeform, Dashform, ScoreApp, Google Forms, Tally) made first sessions feel less like a clinic (r/personaltraining, 139). Online coaches: collect equipment, schedule, medical history, goals and experience first, or discover at week 3 that the client only has dumbbells (r/personaltraining, 50).
- **Reminders:** confirmation at booking, a reminder the day before with a one-tap reschedule, a short one on the day with parking and what to wear. The number is not proven; having none is the common failure (55% of operators, Keepme). Reply to reminder replies fast.
- **A missed visit is a reschedule, not a dead lead.** Same day: "No worries, want Thursday at 6 instead?" Track show rate apart from close rate.
- **Prepay private sessions.** A yoga teacher saw about 50% of private bookings cancel the day before; commenters require full prepayment, 24-hour reschedule notice and a new payment after a late cancel, agreed in writing (r/YogaTeachers, 40).

## The no-sell intro session

"No-sell" means diagnose before prescribing, not "never ask". Structure that works for gyms, boxes, PT and studios with a consult:

1. **Rapport about them, not fitness.** A trainer's first real client came from 20 minutes about football before training came up (r/personaltraining, 221).
2. **Diagnose.** What changed recently that made this worth solving now; what they tried; what gets in the way; days a week they can realistically commit. For high-ticket PT, the "why" ladder: "Why?", "Why now?", "Why does that matter?", "Walk me through that.", "What happens if this doesn't change?" (r/personaltraining, 92).
3. **Coach a real workout, not a test.** Replace a 20 to 40 minute, 6 to 7 pattern screen with: injury and pain check, short dynamic warm-up, 2 to 3 movements coached deeply (squat, hinge, push or pull) with regressions, then a finisher (sled, carries or bike intervals). Clients leave feeling capable, not failed (r/personaltraining, 139). A big-box trainer who replaced the gym's template session with his own squat, bench, press and deadlift progressions beat quota for 6 to 7 months ($4,000 lowest month, about $8,000 average; r/personaltraining, 122).
4. **Talk about the future as "we".** Describe what training together looks like in weeks and months; most clients raised buying themselves, largest sale $2,400 paid in full (r/personaltraining, 88).
5. **Ask permission, agree frequency and duration, then price.** "We have a variety of options for personal training, would you like to hear about them?" then "Based on what we've discussed for your goal of X, it would be best for you to work with us for 6 months at X frequency, does that seem fair?" Ask again before the price (r/personaltraining, 156, two commenters use the same framework).
6. **Explain the terms out loud before the e-signature** (5 minutes): billing date and amount, cancellation, no-show policy, communication hours. "Just so we're clear, here's how the billing works..." and "I want to make sure there are no surprises for you down the road." Operator reports higher retention and less stress (r/personaltraining, 97).
7. **Low-pressure close if it fits the room:** "So, would you like to try out a few sessions?" (a new big-box trainer who became #1 in PT sales in month one, r/personaltraining, 73).

Gym-floor trainers: let the member's own words open the door. When a member says "I hope I see results this time" or "I sometimes struggle with correct execution", explain what paid coaching adds (time and precision), and let them ask the price (r/personaltraining, 91).

## Objections

- **Spouse:** "Thank you for sharing that. Didn't you say your wife was supportive earlier?" Then offer a short call or visit with both.
- **Money moving:** "What numbers would you need to move around?"
- **Sticker shock:** say nothing; let the silence run (r/personaltraining, 156 thread, reply at 3).
- **Discount request:** "I don't offer discounts, but I also don't charge you upfront" (23-year trainer, 9 upvotes). One brief negotiation is fine; continued pressure after a clear answer is a fit signal to decline.
- **Budget mismatch is real sometimes.** Commenters push back that selling value cannot fix a $50 budget against a $75 rate (14 and 9 upvotes). Offer a lower tier (semi-private, group, online) or let them go kindly.
- **Affluent buyers:** present large packages without projecting your own price sensitivity. One trainer closes many packages above $20,000 (r/personaltraining, 89).

## After the visit

- **Same day:** a short message from the coach referencing one thing they did well, plus the recommended start date.
- **Not bought:** one follow-up within 24 hours offering the two best start times; then a nurture message at day 7 and day 21 with one useful thing tied to their stated goal; then the 90-day list. This cadence is a testing default, not a measured optimum; stop on any reply asking to stop.
- **Bought:** the onboarding sequence in [retention.md](retention.md#first-30-days) starts at once.

## Feed the outcome back to the ads

- Platforms optimize to the event you send. A form fill teaches them to find form fillers.
- **Google:** upload qualified, booked, attended and member events as offline or enhanced conversions; new implementations use the Data Manager API since June 15, 2026.
- **Meta:** use the Leads webhook into the CRM, send CRM stage events back, and test the `QUALITY_LEAD` optimization goal in the Marketing API.
- Upload membership value, not just a count. Mechanics: `paid-ads`.
