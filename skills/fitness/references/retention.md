# Retention

Retention is a risk-detection system. A cancellation form is a lagging signal; attendance falls first. Every number here is observational; use the business's own retained-member pattern as soon as it has 6 months of attendance data. Flow building, consent and dunning mechanics: `email-sms-loyalty`. Wording: [scripts.md](scripts.md).

## Measure it by segment

Report retention by coach, class time, acquisition source, intro offer type and first-month attendance band. A business-wide churn rate hides that one campaign brings fragile members or that one 6 am class builds unusually strong relationships.

## First 30 days

- **Activation target.** Studios: customers reaching 5 visits in their first month retain at 90%+ (ABC / Mariana Tek 2026, observational). Use the business's own threshold once measured. Gyms and PT: the agreed weekly frequency x 4.
- **Checkpoints at days 7, 14, 21 and 30.** Behind pace at day 7: automated nudge with their usual class and a held spot. Behind at day 14: coach message. Behind at day 21: coach call to adjust schedule or format.
- **Easy first weeks.** Tell new clients the first one to two weeks will feel easy and start below their self-reported ability; trusting self-reports once left clients sick and dizzy (r/personaltraining, 81). "You will never lose a client for too easy of a workout but you will lose them for too hard of a workout" (779).
- **Text after the first workout:** check how they feel, handle soreness before the next session (r/personaltraining, 80, one of the operator's three highest-return texts).
- **Text the day after workouts in general,** even if they do not always answer (14-year trainer, 127).
- **Make beginners feel safe.** An instructor's line "Don't hurt yourself, it's just Pilates" is why one member "never missed a class" (r/pilates, 183).
- **Online coaching starts small:** 2 to 4 workouts a week of 25 to 45 minutes, not a 5 to 6 day advanced plan that makes them quit (r/personaltraining, 182).
- **Injury-history intake for clients with limits:** one pilates instructor coordinated with the physical therapist, reviewed the MRI, started with 10 private sessions, then moved to 2 and later 4 classes a week; three years later the client attends 4 to 6 times a week (r/pilates, 562).

## PT and coaching: the 90-day arc

From an operator who copied a colleague with a permanently full roster (r/personaltraining, 337):

- **Days 1 to 30:** celebrate small milestones nearly every session (first week done, first full squat, first week hitting protein).
- **Days 31 to 60:** one mini challenge, a benchmark workout they can beat.
- **Days 61 to 90:** more technical teaching, tied to beating that benchmark.
- Do not prove expertise by front-loading knowledge and credentials.

Reported result: clients easier to teach, wanted more than 3 days a week, faster results, more referrals, more renewals. Pair it with the online coaches' rule: ask for 90 days before judging, without a lock-in contract ([scripts.md](scripts.md#onboarding-and-check-ins)).

Program design that keeps long-term clients (14-year trainer, 46):

- Keep workouts the same for 3 to 4 weeks and progress load or reps inside the block; changing exercises every other session prevents competence. Trainers get bored before clients do.
- Create variety through rep ranges: weeks 1 to 3 at 8 to 12, 4 to 6 at 6 to 8, 7 to 9 at 8 to 10, 10 to 12 at 5 to 7, then cycle.
- Ask at signup which exercises they hate and love; send a short feedback form before every new phase.
- Diet advice: 90% of clients ignore broad advice; ask "What one food item do you think is hurting you the most? How can you modify it?" (r/personaltraining, 779, operator's wording is coarser).
- One notebook per client with every session recorded, used to show progress and handed to them when they finish (81).

## Online coaching check-ins

- **Weekly check-in is the retention engine.** Spend 10 to 15 minutes per client linking the training log to what they told you last week: a squat stalled two weeks, they mentioned work stress, so ask about sleep and adjust. Never reply "looks good keep it up" (r/personaltraining, 184).
- **Monday and Thursday touchpoints** by audio message or occasional Loom video, about adherence and how they feel (six years fully online, 182).
- **30-second extras between check-ins:** travel workout tips before a trip they mentioned; acknowledge a personal record the same day (184).
- **Plan around known disruptions:** kids' activity days, conferences (short office workouts), seasons when they usually fall off (91).
- **Set messaging hours at the start:** Monday to Thursday 9 to 6 and Friday morning, no replies Friday 2 pm to Sunday; clients stop expecting weekend replies (182).
- Benchmark the curve: 94% at month 1, 82% at month 3, 65% at month 6, 45% at month 12 (Coachway Nordic, 2026).

## Attendance triggers (studios, gyms, boxes)

| Trigger | Default timing | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| New member behind activation pace | day 7, 14, 21 | Held spot in their usual class; coach message; coach call | automation, then coach |
| Absence | about 5 days (ABC framework) | Light message offering their usual class and time | automation |
| Absence | about 10 days | Personal message referencing the goal they gave | coach |
| Absence | about 14 days | Call; ask what changed; adjust schedule, format or freeze | coach or owner |
| Frequency drop | visits in last 14 days under half their own 8-week average | Same as 10-day step | coach |
| Milestone | 10th, 25th, 50th, 100th visit; month anniversaries; first benchmark beaten | Same-day recognition; a referral ask follows good moments | coach |
| Pack use above membership breakeven | rolling 30 days | Membership conversation | front desk |
| Card expiring | 30 days before | Update link | automation |

Only the 5, 10 and 14-day absence steps come from a published framework (ABC 2026); the other thresholds are testing defaults. Adapt the day counts to the member's normal frequency: a 5x-a-week member gone 5 days is a bigger signal than a 1x-a-week member. The second message only works if the CRM captured the goal and the member's consent to be contacted.

## Community you can count

- **Make connection observable:** coach contact per member per week, introductions between members, small groups, events, peer recognition. 67% of consumers name community as a motivation driver (ABC 2026); "post about community" is not a tactic.
- **No person left behind (CrossFit):** members who finish the WOD early record their time, catch their breath, then keep going at reduced reps or load until the last athlete finishes; a member ran the last 400 m with a slower athlete (r/crossfit, 567).
- **Outdoor WODs in neighborhoods you do not usually reach,** recurring, families invited, with teen and kids rates (r/crossfit thread, 1,846).
- **Consent for touch:** reversible cards by the mat ("Yes, please" / "No, thanks"), or a private opt-out during child's pose or the first down dog; one teacher remembers answers under 20 students and uses a chip by the mat above 20 (r/YogaTeachers, 103). A prospective teacher-training student left over unwanted adjustments (1,043).
- **Filming policy that is enforced:** members left or threatened to leave studios where influencers filmed classes and the studio reposted footage without consent (r/pilates, 320 and 102); an intro customer caught in a member's video left after her second visit and was refunded (r/YogaTeachers, 42). Enforce the ban even when member posts bring exposure.
- **Do not change a format under regulars.** Replacing a Hot 26+2 class with hot pilates lost a student with no nearby alternative (r/YogaTeachers, 62). Doubling savasana into 20 minutes of yoga nidra without warning took a class from 25 to about 10 for that segment and drew a complaint (89). Add, test, then move.
- **Own your mistakes visibly.** A teacher who missed a 6:30 am class brought "Apology Coffee" for everyone next time (r/YogaTeachers, 67).

## Schedule reliability

- **Teach the one-person class.** Members book late; one member booked 20 minutes before a class with one enrollee and found it full on arrival (r/pilates, 442, comment 22; instructors at 181 and 18 teach classes of one).
- **If you must cancel,** do it the night before and compensate unlimited members with 1 to 2 extra days (barre owner, 39; 26-upvote reply says just teach it).
- **Fix weak slots at the next schedule change,** after asking members which times and instructors they want (442 thread, 62 and 9 upvotes).
- **New yoga teachers:** sub a lot while your own slots grow from 0 to 4 students; it builds a following (r/YogaTeachers, 60).

## Freeze and cancel

Law first, then saves:

- **US:** the FTC "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated by the Eighth Circuit on July 8, 2025; state auto-renewal laws and ROSCA still apply. California's amended Automatic Renewal Law (AB 2863, in force July 1, 2025) requires separate consent to auto-renewal, an annual reminder, online cancellation where the member signed up online, prompt handling of phone and voicemail cancellations, and limits on how save offers are presented during cancellation. Many states also have health club statutes (cooling-off periods, term limits, cancellation on moving or medical grounds). Check the member's state before writing any flow.
- **UK:** the DMCC Act subscription regime is not yet in force; government now expects spring 2027. The Act's unfair-practices rules have applied since April 2025.
- **EU:** many online sign-ups carry a 14-day withdrawal right.
- **Everywhere:** cancelling must not be harder than joining. One save offer, clearly optional, then process the cancellation.

Save sequence (in person or by phone, wording in [scripts.md](scripts.md#freeze-and-cancel-save)):

1. **Ask why, and listen.** Log the reason: moving, injury or medical, money, schedule, not getting results, didn't like coach or class, life event.
2. **Match the offer to the reason**, never a discount by default: moving gets a referral to a partner gym or online option; injury gets a freeze plus a return plan; schedule gets a format or time change; money gets a lower tier or fewer sessions; results get a goal reset session with the coach.
3. **Freeze is a real option for temporary causes,** with a dated restart and a reminder a week before. Track freezes that never restart as churn.
4. **If they still want to leave, cancel cleanly and kindly.** Hand over their training record (the notebook trick). A good exit drives the return.

## Involuntary churn

About 23% of one gym's discounted annual members stopped paying within four months on cards that failed (r/smallbusiness, 1,353). Card updater, smart retries, expiry warnings 30 days ahead, and a no-login update link from day one. Mechanics: `email-sms-loyalty`.

## Win-back and former clients

- **Personal texts to 15+ former clients a month,** around holidays and remembered details: "Hey Sam just wanted to say Merry Christmas and I hope you and your family are having a wonderful day. Hope your daughter Maddie is still crushing her soccer." Operator reports repeated returns and referrals (r/personaltraining, 80). Calendar reminders make it routine.
- **Birthdays and events** from the intake form go in the calendar, including former clients; a good-luck text the morning of a client's charity 5K takes 30 seconds (127).
- **Start the conversation when you see them.** "How have you been?" at an outdoor event brought back a former client who wanted to return but was afraid to ask; she became a success story (59).
- **Trigger on rhythm:** a member's own normal gap x 2 is quiet, x 3 is gone (win-back by rhythm in `email-sms-loyalty` and `beauty-business`). No discount on the first touch.
- **Graduation instead of exit:** small-group or PT clients move into an access membership when coaching ends (r/smallbusiness, 535).
