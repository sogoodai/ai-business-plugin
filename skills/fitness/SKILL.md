# Fitness: answer the lead, activate the first month, keep them training

For personal trainers (in a gym, independent, in-home, studio owners), boutique studios (yoga, pilates and reformer, cycling, HIIT and bootcamp, barre, martial arts, dance), small gyms and CrossFit or functional boxes, and online fitness coaches.

Built from 170 practitioner tactics in 311 threads (r/personaltraining, r/YogaTeachers, r/pilates, r/crossfit, plus fitness threads from r/smallbusiness, r/Coaching and r/InstagramMarketing), a November 2026 research pass (ABC Fitness, Health & Fitness Association, Keepme, Two-Brain Business, Mindbody, ClassPass, WordStream/LocaliQ, Institute of Personal Trainers, Coachway, Trainerize, NASM, Google and Meta documentation) and the best public marketing skills. "Operators report" marks a practitioner anecdote with its upvotes; everything else carries its source and year. Most fitness data is vendor-owned and observational: treat lifts as priors for a test, not as causes. Sources: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md). The owner's decisions win over this guide.

## How fitness businesses actually grow

- **New joins are shrinking; frequency and retention are growing.** ABC Fitness H1 2026 (30,000 businesses, 40M members): gym joins -9% YoY, studio check-ins +27%, studio cancellations -6%. Growth now comes from conversion, attendance and retention, not from more top-of-funnel.
- **The biggest leak is the unanswered lead.** 57% of membership inquiries got no reply at all; replies took 4 h by email, 17.5 h on Facebook, 37 h on Instagram; 52% of gym searches happen outside opening hours (Keepme 2026, 45 brands, 1.6M conversations, vendor). Only 3 to 6 of 100 paid leads became members.
- **The first 30 days decide the member.** Studio customers with 5+ visits in month one retain at 90%+; first-time visitors return 57% of the time on average, 76% at a top performer (ABC 2026, vendor, observational).
- **One point of monthly retention is worth more than a new ad.** Microgyms retain 92.7% a month (7.3% churn); at 100 members x $179, moving to 93.7% is modeled at about $26,000 a year (Two-Brain, Nov 2026, scenario math).
- **Referrals and Google carry the solo trainer.** Independent and in-home trainers with 10 to 15 years in business report most clients from word of mouth and a Google profile with reviews, no social media (r/personaltraining, 200 upvotes, agreed at 9 and 3). Referred prospects close around 95% (operator, 89).
- **Studios sell a schedule and a room; PTs sell a relationship; online coaches sell accountability.** Price, pitch and retain each differently (route below).
- **Paid ads come after the follow-up works.** A $27.11 Meta lead (WordStream 2026) costs $452 to $904 per member at a 3 to 6% lead-to-member rate, and about $174 at the 15.6% a tight follow-up reaches (33.3% set x 75% show x 62.5% close). Same ad, 5x different CAC.

## Route by sub-model first

| Sub-model | Revenue engine | Main KPI | First lever | Classic trap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PT inside a commercial gym | Floor relationships, gym referrals, inductions | Consults booked per week, close rate | Work the floor in the hours you want filled | Selling the gym's template session instead of your coaching |
| Independent or in-home PT | Google profile, referrals, PT/chiro network | Roster size at target rate, client tenure | Monthly autopay by weekly frequency | Session packs that expire, hoard and stall renewals |
| PT studio owner | 1:1 plus semi-private, own Google demand | Contribution per coach-hour | Semi-private tier, owner hours priced | Margins that only work because the owner trains 50% of sessions |
| Boutique studio | Intro offer to membership, schedule, ClassPass yield | Intro-to-member rate, first-month visits, class fill | Paid multi-visit intro, attendance triggers | Free or cheap trials, aggregator on peak classes, last-minute class cancellations |
| Small gym or box | Consult-first sales, coaching tiers | Set, show, close rates; monthly churn | Answer every lead fast, no-sell consult | Discounted annual plans, dismissive price replies |
| Online coach | Content or community to DM to consult | Paid clients, month-3 and month-6 retention | Weekly human check-in, 3 to 5 first clients from warm network | Cheap info product, unbounded 24/7 access |

Sub-model detail, including modality notes (yoga, pilates, cycling, HIIT, barre, martial arts, dance): [references/by-submodel.md](references/by-submodel.md).

## First session: ask, then compute

Ask the owner:

1. Sub-model, modality, country and city; room capacity or coach-hours per week; who coaches what.
2. Software (Mindbody, ABC Glofox, Mariana Tek, PushPress, Wodify, Zen Planner, Trainerize, Stripe) and whether you can export 12 months of leads, bookings, attendance, sales, cancellations and failed payments.
3. Price list: intro offer, drop-in, packs, memberships, PT sessions or monthly plans, semi-private, online tiers; contract terms, freeze and cancellation policy.
4. Lead sources and who answers each channel (forms, Meta lead ads, Instagram DMs, phone, email) and how fast, including after hours.
5. Aggregators in use (ClassPass, Gympass/Wellhub, Urban Sports Club, Thumbtack) and their contract floor rate.
6. Google rating, review count and how reviews are requested.
7. Member list size and what marketing consent they hold.
8. Offers they will and will not run; who may change prices; any approved discount.

Then compute ([references/economics.md](references/economics.md)):

```
monthly churn = cancellations in month / active members at start of month   (split voluntary vs failed payment)
early-life retention = share of a join-month cohort still paying at day 30, 60, 90
mature churn = churn among members past month 3
cohort LTV = sum over months of (survival x monthly price) x contribution margin
lead funnel = lead -> contacted -> booked -> showed -> bought, by source; response time by channel
member CAC = channel spend / new paying members from that channel
intro conversion = intro buyers who start a recurring plan within 14 days of expiry / intro buyers
first-month activation = share of new members with >= target visits in their first 30 days
class fill = attendees / capacity, by class-hour; contribution per class-hour
coach-hour contribution = revenue per coach-hour - coach pay - facility cost per hour
```

Report the three biggest gaps in money, not percentages.

## Decision rules, in order

1. **Lead response before ad spend.** If any channel has no owner, or median first reply in opening hours is over 5 minutes (converting trainers call within 2 to 3; operators say an hour loses the lead), fix routing first. Every lead gets an immediate reply with two bookable times and an after-hours self-book link.
2. **Show and close before volume.** Under 33% set or under 60% close after an attended visit: fix reminders, the intro session and the close before buying traffic.
3. **First-month activation.** Count visits in days 1 to 30 for every new member. Studios: 5+ visits is the retained pattern in ABC's data. Gyms and PT: use the business's own retained-member pattern; until measured, the agreed weekly frequency x 4. Anyone below it gets a coach call, not an email.
4. **Absence triggers on attendance, not billing.** Light automated touch at about 5 days absent, personal at 10, coach call at 14 (ABC 2026 framework); adjust to each member's normal frequency.
5. **Paid multi-visit intro, short expiry.** Studios: 14 to 30 days of access at about half the monthly unlimited (Mindbody's $79 vs $159). Gyms and PT: consult plus a coached session. No endless free week.
6. **Monthly autopay over packs.** PT and coaching: flat monthly price by agreed weekly frequency, auto-renewing until cancelled. Packs only for genuinely irregular customers; flag any pack cheaper per visit than the membership at realistic use.
7. **Retention one point before acquisition.** Model the dollar value of 1 point of monthly retention before approving new ad budget.
8. **ClassPass on unsold inventory only.** Off-peak, underfilled class-hours first; 1 to 2 spots in popular classes at most; judge incremental revenue net of displaced direct sales.
9. **Raise prices from capacity, not costs.** Near full or waitlisted: raise for new clients first, then existing with a month's notice. Never justify a raise with your personal expenses.
10. **Ads last, judged on members.** Only with fast follow-up and offline conversions fed back (Google Data Manager API since June 15, 2026; Meta QUALITY_LEAD). Kill on cost per attended visit and cost per retained member, never CPL.

## The lead-to-member funnel

- **One inbox for every channel** (form, Meta lead ads via webhook, Instagram, Facebook, phone, email); 55% of operators sent no reminder before a booked tour (Keepme 2026).
- **First reply asks a binary booking question**, not "how can I help": "I can get you in Tuesday at 6 or Thursday at 7, which works better?"
- **Trainers call web leads within 2 to 3 minutes**; alerts go to two phones; "waiting over an hour effectively loses the lead" (operator, 364).
- **After a good call, send times and the waiver immediately.** Trainers lose clients who loved the call when the next step waits (operator, 200).
- **Run a no-sell intro:** diagnose, coach a real workout, then prescribe one option with frequency and duration agreed before the price. Script: [references/scripts.md](references/scripts.md#no-sell-intro-session).
- **Log a lost reason** from a fixed list: no response, schedule, price, location, partner approval, injury or medical, wrong service, not ready, competitor, other.

Full playbook: [references/lead-to-first-visit.md](references/lead-to-first-visit.md).

## Retention: where the money is

- **Days 1 to 30 small wins nearly every session, days 31 to 60 one benchmark challenge, days 61 to 90 technique tied to beating it** (operator, 337: clients trained more, referred and renewed).
- **Start new clients easy.** "You will never lose a client for too easy of a workout but you will lose them for too hard of a workout" (operator, 779).
- **Run classes with one booked person** and fix weak slots at the next schedule change; members leave studios that cancel within the hour (442-upvote thread, top comments 925 and 614).
- **Enforce filming and consent rules.** Studios lost intro customers and regulars to influencers filming class (102, 320 and 42-upvote threads).
- **Failed cards are churn.** About 23% of a gym's discounted annual members stopped paying within four months on cards that failed (r/smallbusiness, 1,353). Dunning from day one, owned by `email-sms-loyalty`.

Triggers, freeze and cancel save, community, milestones, win-back: [references/retention.md](references/retention.md).

## ClassPass in one screen

- An empty bike or reformer at class start is worth zero; a ClassPass booking that replaces a member is worth less than zero.
- Studios fill under 36% of listed capacity through direct channels and 94% of ClassPass users are new to the venue (ClassPass data, strong vendor bias, the 94% partly pre-2024).
- Payouts sit on a negotiated confidential floor; SmartRate moves credit prices above it (ClassPass, March 6, 2026).
- A 21-year studio owner called ClassPass their most costly mistake: unpaid trial visits, lower payouts, members downgrading to the cheaper channel (r/YogaTeachers, 205).
- Rule: expose off-peak inventory, cap peak classes at 1 to 2 spots, give direct members a shorter cancellation window (2 to 4 hours vs 12), and measure incremental revenue per class-hour. Math: [references/classpass-and-aggregators.md](references/classpass-and-aggregators.md).

## Pricing in one screen

- **PT anchors:** US single session $75 median, middle half $50 to $110; UK £45, London £57.50; US monthly 1:1 plan $460, small group $189.50 (iPT Oct 2026; 70% of priced sites last edited before 2023, so anchors only).
- **Operators at $140 to $165 an hour** in affluent areas report $240,000 to $300,000 a year without social media (r/personaltraining, 132 and 89); location carries much of it.
- **Semi-private wins when** price per person x occupied seats - extra delivery cost > the 1:1 hour it displaces. One trainer: 4 spots, break-even at 1 (r/smallbusiness, 535).
- **Online coaching:** monthly, not per session; $150 to $300 starting range (operators); median US advertised $199.50 (iPT 2026). Two tiers by accountability: async weekly check-in vs added weekly call.
- **Discounting:** gyms that do not discount earn about $2,900 more a month at the median (Two-Brain 2026, likely confounded). Use a dated one-week sale (20 to 35% off) for a dry spell, never a permanent cut.

Depth: [references/intro-offers-and-pricing.md](references/intro-offers-and-pricing.md); generic price-raise mechanics in `offers-pricing`.

## When practitioners and the data disagree

| Topic | Practitioners say | Data says | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Free first session | Free sessions or even a free month build trust (81) | Paid intro buyers retain 54% vs 35% (Mindbody, association); free trials underperform consult-first in Two-Brain's network | Paid, short, multi-visit intro; free only as a coached consult or a referral gift |
| Attrition budget | Plan for 10% a month (129) | Microgym median 7.3% | Budget 10%, investigate above 7.3%, celebrate under 5% |
| Pricing on the site | Publish rates to qualify (156) vs defer price to the call (200) | 61% of 492 PT sites show a price; effect untested (iPT 2026) | Show "from" pricing or a range; never answer a price question with "not a good fit" |
| ClassPass | "Most costly mistake" (205) | 99% of joint Mindbody/ClassPass users positive incremental revenue (vendor) | Off-peak only, measured per class-hour, exit if direct members downgrade |
| Content volume | Post 2 Reels a day | No 2025 to 2026 evidence that frequency drives coaching sales | Measure content to DM to consult to client; drop channels that send the wrong people |

## Never

- Never answer a price question with "If your first question is about the fee, we're not a good fit" (four CrossFit boxes lost the same prospect that way, r/crossfit, 162).
- Never scale ads on CPL or optimize to form fills when offline outcomes can be sent back.
- Never gate or incentivize Google reviews; no free class for a review.
- Never promise weight loss or show before/after photos without written consent and typical-result context; no health-condition ad targeting.
- Never make cancelling harder than joining; follow the auto-renewal law where the member lives ([references/retention.md](references/retention.md#freeze-and-cancel)).
- Never touch, film or repost a client without consent.
- Never invent a benchmark, an offer the owner has not approved or a client quote.
