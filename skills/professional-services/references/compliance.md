# Compliance: advertising, solicitation, testimonials, referrals

This is a control design for the agent, not legal advice. Every rule below is enforced by a state, provincial or national regulator, and the controlling text is the one adopted where the professional is licensed. Before publishing, confirm the current rule for the licensing jurisdiction; several changed in 2025 and 2026. When in doubt, mark the asset amber and route it.

## The publication gate

| State | What | Who approves |
|---|---|---|
| Green: auto-publish from approved templates | Hours, closures, office news, staff introductions without credentials claims, neutral review requests, reminders | Agent, logged |
| Amber: named approver signs | Any claim, comparison, statistic, case result, testimonial, review feature, performance, rate, fee promotion, ad, landing page, AI-generated article, tax or legal or financial information, outreach templates | Responsible lawyer, CCO or principal, licensed agent, CPA partner |
| Red: never from the agent | Individualized advice; guarantees; "specialist", "expert", "certified", "best" without basis; projections of investment performance; live solicitation; outreach outside licensed states; paid referral arrangements | Licensed owner decides, often with counsel |

Operating rules (research report 2026):
- Keep an approved-claims library tagged by jurisdiction, product and expiry date, and a prohibited-words list.
- Archive every ad and post as actually served (screenshot, date, platform, approver).
- Auto-pause acquisition when compliance review backs up, not just when capacity does.
- AI does not change the rules. FINRA's February 2026 rulemaking materials restate that firms using third-party tools remain responsible; SEC, FCA, ASIC, SRA and CIRO rules apply to AI-produced content exactly as to human content. AI use in US independent agencies outpaces written policy (Big "I" Sept 2026; Liberty Mutual Aug 2026). Lawyers: ABA Formal Opinion 512 (July 2024) covers supervision and confidentiality when using generative AI.

## Lawyers (United States)

The ABA Model Rules are models; each state's adopted version controls (ABA). Core rules:

- **Rule 7.1:** no false or misleading communication about the lawyer's services, including true statements that omit facts needed to avoid misleading. Past results and client testimonials can mislead if they create unjustified expectations; disclaimers ("prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome") are the usual cure where allowed.
- **Rule 7.2(b): no giving anything of value for recommending the lawyer,** except reasonable advertising costs; the usual charges of a legal service plan or a nonprofit or qualified lawyer referral service; paying for a law practice purchase; non-exclusive reciprocal referral agreements with another lawyer or nonlawyer professional where the client is told about the agreement; and nominal gifts that are neither intended nor reasonably expected to be compensation for a recommendation. Comment: reciprocal agreements should not be indefinite and should be reviewed periodically.
- **Rule 7.2(c):** no stating or implying certification as a specialist unless certified by an approved organization, identified in the communication.
- **Rule 7.2(d):** every communication names at least one responsible lawyer or firm with contact information.
- **Rule 7.3:** live person-to-person solicitation for pecuniary gain is prohibited unless the person is a lawyer, has a family, close personal or prior business or professional relationship with the lawyer, or routinely uses the type of legal services for business. Any solicitation is barred if the person has asked not to be solicited or it involves coercion, duress or harassment. Written, recorded and electronic solicitation is generally allowed, subject to state labels and waiting periods.
- **Rule 5.4:** no fee sharing with nonlawyers in most states. Arizona Alternative Business Structures and the Utah sandbox allow nonlawyer ownership (coreyhaines31/marketingskills legal pack, MIT).
- **Pay-per-lead and lead generators:** permitted in many states if the vendor does not "recommend" a lawyer and the fee is not a share of legal fees; state ethics opinions differ. Check before buying leads or LSA-like products from third parties.
- **Online reviews:** asking is fine; paying or gifting for a review can be "value for a recommendation" under 7.2(b) as well as a Google and FTC violation. Responding to a negative review may not reveal confidential information (ABA Formal Opinion 496, 2021). Safe reply: "We take every client's experience seriously. Our professional obligations prevent us from discussing any matter. Please contact [name] at [phone]."

### State variations to check first

- **New York (amended, effective 1 June 2026, per NYSBA, Sept 2026):** the "Attorney Advertising" label requirement and the 30-day (or 15-day) blackout on PI and wrongful death solicitation were eliminated; live person-to-person solicitation for gain stays banned; testimonials need the person's permission to use name, image and likeness; communications must still include a lawyer or firm name and a phone, address or web address; Rule 7.4 was folded into new 7.1(c), so "certified specialist" still requires accredited certification while "focuses on" or "practice limited to" is the safer wording. Agency templates written before June 2026 for New York are out of date.
- **Florida:** the US Supreme Court upheld Florida's 30-day ban on direct-mail solicitation of accident victims and their families (Florida Bar v. Went For It, 1995). Florida also runs an ad review and filing program. Confirm current rule text and fees before any campaign.
- **Texas:** many advertisements and solicitation communications must be filed with the State Bar's Advertising Review Committee. Confirm filing timing and exemptions.
- **Federal:** unsolicited communications to families of air-carrier accident victims are barred for 45 days (49 U.S.C. 1136(g)(2)).
- **Everywhere:** some states still require labels, ad filing, fees, retention of copies for set periods, or specific disclaimers on results and dramatizations. Arrest-list and accident-report mailers (r/LawFirm 548) are lawful written solicitation in many states but often carry labels, waiting periods or envelope rules.

### England and Wales

The SRA Code of Conduct (updated 11 April 2025) requires publicity to be accurate and not misleading and bars unsolicited approaches to members of the public, except current and former clients, to advertise legal services. Broad untargeted advertising is distinguished from targeted approaches (older SRA guidance, check current version). The SRA Transparency Rules (in force since December 2018, check for updates) require published price and service information for listed services such as conveyancing, probate, motoring offences, employment tribunal claims, debt recovery, immigration and licensing.

## Financial advisers and broker-dealers

### SEC-registered investment advisers: Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1)

Effective 4 May 2021, compliance from 4 November 2022 (SEC small-entity guide). The SEC issued a Risk Alert on Marketing Rule deficiencies on 16 December 2025 and updated its FAQ on 15 January 2026; read both before any campaign.

- **Seven general prohibitions:** no untrue or misleading material facts; no material claims you cannot substantiate on demand; no misleading implications; benefits only with fair and balanced risks; specific advice only if fair and balanced; performance and time periods presented fairly; nothing otherwise materially misleading.
- **Testimonials and endorsements are allowed** with clear and prominent disclosure of whether the person is a client, whether they are compensated, and material conflicts. Compensated promoters need a written agreement unless they are affiliates or receive $1,000 or less in the preceding 12 months. Certain "bad actors" cannot be compensated promoters. The adviser must oversee compliance.
- **Third-party ratings** (awards, rankings) only with required disclosures and criteria on how the rating was prepared.
- **Performance:** gross only alongside net; prescribed time periods in most cases; hypothetical performance only with policies ensuring it fits the audience's financial situation; no implying SEC approval.
- **Reviews:** a review on a third-party site that the adviser features, shares, comments on, edits or curates becomes part of the adviser's advertisement and needs the testimonial disclosures. Have the CCO approve the review-request text and the reply policy.
- **Records:** keep copies of advertisements and substantiation under the books-and-records rule.
- **Referral arrangements** with CPAs or attorneys that pay anything are promoter arrangements; reciprocal referrals with no payment are simpler but still need conflict review.
- Smaller advisers registered with states follow state securities rules; confirm with the state regulator.

### FINRA member firms and registered reps: Rule 2210

- **Retail communication** = written or electronic communication to more than 25 retail investors within 30 calendar days; **correspondence** = 25 or fewer.
- Retail communications need approval by a qualified registered principal **before the earlier of use or filing**, with narrow exceptions (for example, posts on interactive forums supervised like correspondence).
- **New member firms** file retail communications in public media at least 10 business days before first use for their first year.
- **No predictions or projections of performance**, no implying past performance will recur, no exaggerated or promissory claims (narrow exceptions with written policies).
- **Testimonials about investment advice or performance** must disclose that it may not represent other customers, is no guarantee of future success, and, if more than $100 was paid, that it was paid.
- Records retained under SEA Rule 17a-4 with first and last use dates and the approving principal.
- Practical: reps cannot run their own LinkedIn automation, ads or Google reviews programs without the firm's approval. Automated LinkedIn messages were flagged by one firm's compliance for tone (r/financialadvisors 27).

### Other countries

- **Canada:** CIRO's 2025 compliance report requires social-media policies and books-and-records capture; CSA and CIRO issued finfluencer guidance on 11 December 2025: paid or affiliated promoters can trigger securities obligations.
- **UK:** financial promotions must be fair, clear and not misleading; FG24/1 (26 March 2024) covers social media and warns that unauthorized finfluencers communicating promotions can commit a criminal offence.
- **EU:** MiFID II Article 24 requires fair, clear, not misleading and identifiable marketing; ESMA (27 May 2024) stresses approval procedures, records and balanced risk presentation on social media.
- **Australia:** ASIC's new RG 234 (9 June 2026) covers advertising of financial products, advice and credit. ASIC Report 845 (9 November 2026) reviewed 15 licensees using lead generators in personal advice: no paid lead generation, "free health check" funnel or social promotion for advice without AFS licensee approval.
- **CFP marks:** follow CFP Board usage guidelines (marks as adjectives, with the registered symbol; firm names cannot imply the firm is certified).

## Insurance

- **State law governs** licensing and advertising; NAIC models are templates, binding only where a state adopts them (NAIC). Determine the producing state and product line before approving any claim.
- **Unfair trade practices:** misrepresentation, false advertising, misleading comparisons and rebating are prohibited under state versions of the NAIC Unfair Trade Practices Act. Rebating and inducement rules decide whether you can give gift cards, review incentives, client gifts or referral rewards; many states now allow limited value-added services and small gifts, with caps that vary. Check before any "free gift" offer.
- **Life and annuity ads:** state versions of the NAIC advertising model regulation require clear identification of the product, insurer and agent, and ban implying an insurance product is a deposit or investment without disclosure. Senior "annuity myth" events draw suitability and senior-marketing scrutiny (r/CFP 51).
- **Referral fees** to unlicensed people are limited or banned in many states, often allowed only if small and not contingent on a sale.
- **Titles and claims:** do not use "medical underwriter", "director of underwriting" or "fully vetted by the FBI" style scripts some lead organizations teach (r/InsuranceAgent 25). Use the licensed name and license numbers where states require.
- **Medicare Advantage and Part D:** CMS marketing rules apply on top of state law (TPMO disclaimer, scope of appointment, limits on unsolicited contact and gifts). The CY2027 final rule (2026) removed the 48-hour wait between scope of appointment and the appointment and changed TPMO disclaimer timing; industry sources report different effective dates (June or October 2026). Confirm with the carrier or FMO compliance team before changing scripts.
- **ACA:** lead vendors have used agents' NPNs and identities on healthcare.gov without consent; one recorded call showed a vendor posing as a licensed agent and asking for the consumer's one-time passcode (r/InsuranceAgent 30). Audit vendor call flows; your license carries the consequence.
- **Canada:** provincial regulators; CCIR fair-treatment guidance (2026) expects accurate, clear, non-misleading promotion; Ontario's FSRA took 2025 actions on false solicitation and unlicensed activity. **EU:** the Insurance Distribution Directive requires fair, clear, identifiable marketing. **UK:** FCA ICOBS.

### Calls, texts and bought leads (all professions, mostly insurance)

- TCPA private suits run $500 to $1,500 per call or text (detail in `real-estate` compliance.md). One purchased live transfer produced a $25,000 settlement demand (r/InsuranceAgent 160).
- The FCC's one-to-one consent rule for comparison-shopping leads was vacated by the 11th Circuit in January 2025; consent must still be real and provable for the seller.
- Require US-based vendors with express written consent records, opt-in date, resale count, and contract terms covering claims from their calls (r/InsuranceAgent 160, 26). Scrub against the DNC, known TCPA litigator lists and reassigned-number data (FCC Reassigned Numbers Database) before dialing.
- Keep searchable outbound call logs and recordings: one lender answered a demand by showing no outbound calls in the alleged period (r/InsuranceAgent 160).
- Honor stop requests immediately; revisit timing objections in one to two weeks only when consent remains (r/InsuranceAgent 40).
- Cold email law (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, PECR, CASL): `b2b-outbound`.

## Accountants, bookkeepers and tax preparers

- **AICPA Code (adopted or adapted by state boards):** 1.600 bars false, misleading or deceptive advertising and solicitation. 1.510 bars contingent fees for attest clients and for preparing original or amended tax returns or refund claims, with narrow exceptions. 1.520 bars commissions and referral fees tied to attest clients and requires disclosure of permitted ones. 1.700 protects confidential client information. Section numbers confirmed; read the current text through the AICPA or your state board.
- **State boards differ.** California, for example, bars accepting any fee solely for referring a client to a third party, requires signed, clear written disclosure of permitted commissions (amount or computation, source, relationship), and bars contingent fees for attest clients and for original or amended returns (California Board of Accountancy). Oregon permits commissions and referral fees under its own rules with reporting.
- **Independence:** if the firm performs audits, reviews or certain compilations, no commissions or contingent fees from those clients, and no marketing that makes the firm a promoter of an attest client's products. Route co-marketing with attest clients to the independence partner.
- **"CPA" and firm names** are restricted to licensees and registered firms under state accountancy laws. In the UK "accountant" is unprotected while "chartered accountant" is protected.
- **IRS Circular 230:** practitioners may not use false, fraudulent or misleading statements or solicitation. Enrolled agents may describe themselves as "enrolled to represent taxpayers before the Internal Revenue Service", "enrolled to practice before the Internal Revenue Service" or "admitted to practice before the Internal Revenue Service", never as "certified" or as IRS employees (31 CFR 10.30; read the current text, some provisions predate 2024). Published fee information must be honored for a set period. Contingent-fee rules for returns are in flux: Treasury proposed Circular 230 changes in December 2024 and a 2025 House bill provision tried to block contingent-fee regulation; check status, and remember AICPA and many state boards still bar contingent fees on original returns.
- **IRC 7216:** using or disclosing tax return information for anything other than preparing the return needs the taxpayer's prior written consent in the IRS-prescribed format (Rev. Proc. 2013-14). Soliciting additional tax-return preparation is generally allowed; soliciting financial planning, insurance or investment services from tax data is not, without consent. This governs every "tax client to advisory" campaign and every client list shared with a partner. Criminal and civil penalties apply.
- **Australia:** the Tax Practitioners Board Code Determination (2024) added obligations around false or misleading statements; advertising tax or BAS agent services while unregistered can attract sanctions.
- **Reviews:** asking is fine; replies must not confirm engagement details or reveal tax information (AICPA 1.700 and IRC 7216).

## Reviews and testimonials, all professions

| Question | Lawyer | CPA / tax | Adviser (RIA) | FINRA rep | Insurance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ask every client for a Google review? | Yes, neutrally | Yes | With CCO-approved text | Only under firm policy | Yes |
| Offer anything for a review? | No (Google, FTC, 7.2(b)) | No | No | No | No (also rebating) |
| Feature reviews on site or ads? | With state disclaimers, permission | Yes, accurate | Testimonial disclosures required | Principal approval and (d)(6) disclosures | Check state ad rules |
| Reply to a negative review with facts? | No confidential info (ABA Op. 496) | No return info | No account info | Firm policy | No policy details |

FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (effective 21 October 2024) bans fake reviews, reviews bought on condition of sentiment and review suppression, with civil penalties; the FTC sent warning letters in 2025. Google bans any incentive and any gating, and can restrict a profile's reviews. Review mechanics: `local-seo`.
