# Retention and expansion

Existing clients are underused because service data and marketing data live in different systems. Give every client record a `next_best_service` field and a trigger list. Exclude clients with open service issues from any upsell. Track expansion revenue separately from new-client revenue.

## Why it pays

- Top-20% RIAs got 4.2x more assets from existing relationships and 2.8x more from new clients than other firms; revenue growth 21.3% vs 11.3% (Schwab 2026, observational).
- CAS practices with significant CFO-level work had 30%+ higher monthly recurring revenue; median CAS growth 17% (CPA.com/AICPA 2024, 2023 data).
- Agency clients with 5+ policies retained at 84.7% vs 77.1% for one policy (MarshBerry via Relay 2025). Association only: rounding may not cause retention, but it is the best available signal.
- Legal CMOs rate structured client feedback (60%) and cross-selling (55%) as very effective (LMA 2025, larger firms).

## Trigger matrix

| Firm | Trigger in the data | Next service |
|---|---|---|
| Bookkeeping | Monthly close shows payroll growth, new state, cash swings | Payroll, sales tax, cash-flow forecast, CFO |
| Bookkeeping | Owner plans a sale or line of credit | Sale-ready cleanup (one serial entrepreneur hired the same bookkeeper to prep three businesses for sale, r/Bookkeeping 109) |
| Bookkeeping | Client hires an in-house accountant | Paid training engagement at 3x rate (r/Bookkeeping 66) |
| Tax | Revenue over a threshold, S-corp candidate, multi-state hiring, K-1s added, property sale | Entity planning, projections, quarterly estimates (charge for estimates separately, r/taxpros 81), audit defense |
| Tax | High-net-worth returns | Paid audit defense on carefully reconciled returns; "clients never question the fees" (r/taxpros 197) |
| Tax | Clients discuss investments and retirement | Advisory referral or in-house planning (see "tax to advisory" below) |
| CPA | Client new to a licensing board that requires reviewed statements | Compilations and reviews (contractors, about 10% of one firm's revenue, r/taxpros 75) |
| Law, estate | Death, sale, marriage, new grandchild | Probate, trust updates, beneficiary reviews |
| Law, business | New contract, hire, dispute risk, funding round | Specialist colleague introduced with context, not a generic practice-area email |
| Law, PI | Settlement complete | Referral relationship for life (annual card or mailing, [lead-sources.md](lead-sources.md)) |
| Insurance | Home purchase, new driver, new business, renewal, rate increase | Bundle, umbrella, commercial; win commercial first, then cross-sell personal lines (r/InsuranceAgent 29) |
| Insurance, Medicare/ACA | Enrollment done | ID cards, network checks, what happens next; one agency keeps churn about 3% (r/InsuranceAgent 65) |
| Advisor | Inheritance, liquidity event, stock vesting, NQDC elections, RMD age, divorce | Planning project, concentrated-stock strategy, family meeting |
| Advisor | Client's adult children or parents | Family-level fee breakpoint, junior advisor paired with the next generation |

Message structure for a trigger (research report 2026): start from the observed change, name the usual consequence, propose a short scoped review.

## Tax to advisory

- An advisor who also runs a tax practice asks every tax prospect: "Joe and Jane, one of the biggest ways you can control your tax situation is how you handle your retirement and your investments. Can you tell me about what you have going on in that world?" Over 60% then ask whether he can help with that too (r/CFP 78). Works for mass affluent, less for HNW.
- A 25-year firm owner partnered with an investment advisor and got a Series 65; recurring income from existing tax clients at a much higher rate per hour than 1040s (r/taxpros 294).
- A tax business still preparing about 150 returns fed a solo advisor's $160M book (r/CFP 185).
- Compliance: using tax return information to solicit non-tax services needs written IRC 7216 consent; dual registration triggers SEC or state adviser rules; AICPA commission rules apply. See [compliance.md](compliance.md).

## Retention rhythms

- **Advisors:** semiannual reviews, but most clients prefer annual; batch reviews into "surge" weeks (Matt Jarvis method) to free capacity (r/CFP 163). Young sudden-wealth clients: review every 6 months or less; they churn toward TikTok advice and rental-property pitches (r/CFP 101).
- **Downturn days:** call clients, worriers first, open with a personal check-in and let them raise markets. 21 calls, 18 raised the market, 17 were not worried but grateful (r/CFP 63). Framing: "we are long term investors not short term traders" (r/CFP 97).
- **Tax:** structure return reviews around the two-year comparison, big swings and major calculations (r/taxpros 90). Check client-visible details (name, address, occupation): a carried-forward old occupation made a retiree scrutinize a correct return (r/taxpros 90).
- **Tax season health:** Sundays off and a closed April 16 led one preparer to 559 returns vs peers' 401 and 245 and fewer errors; errors lose clients faster than delays (r/taxpros 145).
- **Insurance:** same-day callbacks and a direct number (r/InsuranceAgent 65). Never spam the book with unsolicited life quotes: one agency's clients stopped answering even service calls (r/InsuranceAgent 52).
- **Business owners notice attention, not just contact.** A CPA lost a 4-year client despite monthly contact with its accountant and an annual steak dinner: "we just don't feel like we are getting any personal attention anymore" (r/taxpros 90). Owners get a direct touch at least quarterly.
- **Personal notes:** birthday calls, handwritten notes, gifts tied to interests (wine from their winery, a dog toy) for prospects over years (r/CFP 392); scratch-off cards in birthday cards, excluding anyone with a gambling problem (r/CFP 185). Insurance and law: check gift limits first.
- **Fix your own errors fast and free;** a free targeted amendment when a small retroactive benefit appears (about $300 average savings for under 5% of clients) builds goodwill (r/taxpros 114).

## Competitive saves

- When a client shows you a competitor's "no fee" product, walk through what it costs in caps and lost dividends. One client relayed the explanation to the neighbor who pitched it; the neighbor cancelled in the free-look period and moved the whole relationship (r/CFP 286).
- A former advisor calling one day after a surrender period ends, after years of silence, had already lost the client (r/CFP 134). Silence is the churn signal.
- Position as the backup: "If you got a number 1 that's cool, I'm here to be number 2 when things go sideways or number 1 retires." One advisor's biggest client called back after the first advisor's mistake (r/CFP 98).

## Firing and pruning

- Firing poor-fit clients raises income: one preparer lost about 20% of clients and gained about 30% revenue after enforcing weekend, deadline and interruption boundaries (r/taxpros 102); an advisor fired half the book and called it great (r/CFP 55).
- Pass small clients to a partner or junior when capacity binds; a $3M-revenue partner's habit was exactly this (r/CFP 55). Standardize service below a threshold (plan + two meetings under $1.5M; reserve complex planning for $5M+, r/CFP 55).
- Let simple, price-shopping relatives use cheaper providers rather than matching $150 quotes (r/taxpros 89).
- Before selling a firm, do not tell clients informally (r/taxpros 203).

## Succession and inherited books

- Pair senior advisors with junior associates who build relationships with clients' children; one practice reports 93% asset retention across generations (r/CFP 392).
- Emergency succession: meet most clients personally during the transition, call top clients individually; one 25-year-old successor reported only positive feedback and nearly $5M net new assets (r/CFP 87).
- Inherited bank books: first call with no pitch, follow-up email with contact details, second round prioritizes clients likely to hold assets elsewhere (r/CFP 52).
