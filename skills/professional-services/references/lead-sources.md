# Lead sources and their economics

Order of build for almost every firm: referrals and centers of influence (COIs), Google profile, directories, then content, then paid. Exceptions: consumer law (PI, family, criminal, immigration) and Medicare/ACA/final-expense insurance buy demand early, but only after intake is ready.

## Referrals: engineer them

- **Source at person level.** `referral/client/Jane-Smith`, `referral/CPA/Firm-X`, `referral/attorney/John-Doe`. Then compute signed rate, first-year revenue and 12-month expansion per referrer. Top RIAs track inquiry source 85% of the time vs 71% (Schwab 2026).
- **The specific ask beats "keep us in mind."** "We're trying to help more [specific profile] dealing with [specific problem]. If someone comes to mind, would you be comfortable introducing us by email? I can send you two sentences to forward." (research report, 2026).
- **Give each referral profession its own trigger list** (operators report, r/CFP 71): realtors spot high-income 40+ movers from out of state with old 401(k)s and retirees downsizing; mortgage brokers spot high-income first-time buyers; CPAs and attorneys spot business owners and HNW clients. Use anonymized client examples.
- **Small clients refer big ones.** A PI firm kept small cases because those clients referred some of its largest matters (r/LawFirm 177). Underpricing in exchange for "referrals to come" fails though: the promised referrals rarely arrive (r/taxpros 128, 17 upvotes agreeing).
- **Former-client mailings work for law.** A PI lawyer mailed every former client a letter plus a $5 (later $10) co-branded coffee card each year, about $15,000 a year; one former client later referred a matter that paid a $434,000 fee (r/LawFirm 177). Check state rules on gifts and Rule 7.2(b) before copying; a thank-you to a former client is not payment for a referral, but tie nothing to referrals.
- **Handwritten thank-you, birthday and holiday cards to every client**: a 7-attorney PI firm gets over 50% of new clients from client referrals (r/LawFirm 284).
- **Same-day callbacks and a direct line** drive insurance referrals; one ACA agency reports churn about 3% with post-enrollment help on ID cards and network checks (r/InsuranceAgent 65).

## Centers of influence (COIs)

Kitces 2026: COI CAC $7,371, more than twice client referrals. They work when the list is finite and the exchange is useful.

| Your firm | Best COIs | What you give them |
|---|---|---|
| Bookkeeper | Tax CPAs and EAs (meet outside tax season), payroll providers, business bankers | QuickBooks setup and clean books for their clients; send them tax work (r/Bookkeeping 260) |
| Tax / CPA | Bookkeepers, financial advisors, realtors (capital gains), bankers, larger CPA firms whose minimums exclude small clients | Overflow capacity below their minimums (r/taxpros 61); quick answers for their clients |
| CPA niche | The niche's professional society, CE providers, brokers, lenders, attorneys serving the niche | CE classes: one practitioner reports about 300 clients in two years by teaching through target professionals' regulatory CE (r/taxpros 61) |
| Law, consumer | Other lawyers (conflicts, overflow, out-of-area), medical providers (PI), mediators, bar referral service | Referral-back records; take matters they decline. A mediator winding down litigation referred 3 of a new family firm's first 10 clients (r/LawFirm 89) |
| Law, business / estate | Financial advisors, CPAs, bankers, funeral homes (probate), realtors | Office next to two referring advisors (r/LawFirm 117); probate book in funeral-home lobbies (r/LawFirm 103) |
| Insurance | Realtors, mortgage brokers, title, lenders, CPAs, door-to-door B2B operators | Fast quotes that close their deals; Friday visits to 5 to 6 offices with cookies and a handwritten note (r/InsuranceAgent 24) |
| Advisor | CPAs (start with your clients' CPAs), estate attorneys, P&C brokers, CDFAs, mortgage brokers | Show the work: identify a tax or estate issue, send implementation to the CPA or attorney, follow up so they see how you helped the shared client (r/CFP 55, 92 upvotes) |

Tricks:
- **Ask clients which CPA they use and get the warm intro.** One advisor got 3 referrals from a client's CPA within months; another cold-called young CPAs who own their firms, offering HNW business owners looking for a CPA, and got four $1M+ referrals in a year (r/CFP 59, 44 upvotes).
- **Lunch after tax season, collaborate on mutual clients first**, ask for referrals later (r/CFP 392).
- **Check what the partner wants.** A tax pro prepared an R&D credit presentation for bank managers expecting referrals; they wanted to learn to spot and pitch credits themselves (r/taxpros 133). Ask "what would make an introduction easy for you?" first.
- **State your minimums to referrers.** A CPA telling advisors "send me clients willing to pay $1,000 for a 1040" got few referrals: advisors expect $500k to $1M investable (r/CFP 59). Align on the client both sides want.
- **Daily referral-source calls.** A real estate and corporate firm owner doing $1.2M to $1.5M revenue makes them personally every day while staff process files (r/LawFirm 236).
- **Never leak a working referral source** to someone you mentor; one PI lawyer's mentee went to the referring firm to take its cases (r/LawFirm 74).
- **Referral fees:** lawyers, Rule 7.2(b) and 5.4; CPAs, state board (California bars fees solely for referral; commissions need signed disclosure); advisers, SEC promoter rules; insurance, state law on paying unlicensed people. A paid "finder's fee" that is fine for a bookkeeper ($100 per paying referral, r/Bookkeeping 198) can be a violation for a lawyer. See [compliance.md](compliance.md).

## Google Business Profile and reviews

Depth in `local-seo`. Profession angles:
- Primary category = the most valuable practice area that is accurate ("Estate planning attorney", "Tax preparation service", "Bookkeeping service", "Insurance agency", "Financial planner").
- Practitioner listings for each lawyer, CPA or advisor are allowed for independent practitioners with their own phone or extension; they can outrank the firm page (`local-seo`).
- Shared-office or virtual addresses risk suspension; one firm accelerated a real office after warnings and a commenter was asked for an office video (r/LawFirm 89).
- A small satellite office opened as an SEO experiment now brings "nonstop calls" and six figures for a transactional solo (r/LawFirm 103). Only with real staffed hours.
- Suburb-focused site + 11 reviews + online consult booking and payment ranked first or second for key searches in a 50,000-resident suburb (r/LawFirm 89).
- Legal review velocity is lower (3 to 5 a month is normal, practitioner estimate in `local-seo`). Ask everyone, neutrally, after a defined milestone.
- Underserved practice areas rank fast: an employee-side employment firm an hour from a metro went from unranked to page one in about three months (r/LawFirm 89).

## Google Ads and Local Services Ads (lawyers mostly)

Mechanics in `paid-ads` (Google) and `home-services` (LSA). Legal specifics:
- **Separate P&Ls.** LSA was 40% of spend and 49% of signed cases; Search 60% of spend, 76% of leads and 51% of signed (Pareto Legal 2026). Report by practice area.
- **LSA:** pay per valid lead, Google Verified badge (replaced Google Screened in October 2025, which covered lawyers and financial planners), license and background checks. Missed business-hours calls over 20 seconds can be charged. Messaging leads may cost less. Dispute junk weekly, answer 90%+ of calls, pause hours you cannot staff.
- **Search hygiene:** a family law account at about $17,000/month was bidding on "employment lawyer", "24 hour divorce court", competitor names and "real estate lawyers" after successive Google reps accepted suggestions; most spend was waste (r/LawFirm 91). Audit search terms weekly; reject rep-proposed campaigns unless tied to signed-case data.
- **Branded misspellings:** a PI firm put most of its Google budget for years on misspellings of its lawyers' names to avoid $100+ clicks (r/LawFirm 177).
- **Case results on the landing page** lifted click-to-call from about 5% to 25% for a criminal defense solo (r/LawFirm 548). Results need state disclaimers (see compliance).
- **PMax in legal** can spend on informational queries; an $8,000 loss was attributed to missing conversion tracking and negatives (r/googleads 74).
- **Budget floor:** PI Search under $10,000 to $15,000 a month struggled to sign consistently (r/PPC 118). Estate planning grew on $500 to $2,000/month Search + LSA plus a $500 management fee to $290,330 gross in 2025 (r/LawFirm 117). Small budgets suit lower-CPC practice areas.
- **Freelancer setups:** a $125 Fiverr build produced "Federal comp lawyer. federal comp lawyer. Boston." (r/LawFirm 106). Review keywords and copy before paying.
- **AI Max and auto-generated assets:** default to human approval; generated text is advertising under the bar rules.
- **Ownership:** agencies such as national legal marketing firms are reported to own the website and sometimes the domain (r/localseo 29). Contract for ownership of site, domain, GBP, ad accounts and call recordings.

Accountants and advisors: Search works for narrow, high-intent services (IRS resolution, S-corp election, R&D credits, fee-only planner + city). A CPA built a medical-practice niche with professional-society ads plus targeted Google Ads, then referrals took over after two years (r/taxpros 119).

## Directories and marketplaces

Judge by mechanics, not prestige. Claim and complete free profiles first, collect reviews, measure signed revenue, buy sponsorship only where organic profile views already show demand.

## LinkedIn

Use where the buyer has a job title: business accountants, outsourced CFO, commercial insurance and benefits, corporate and employment law, advisors to executives and founders.
- Offer a diagnostic, not "our services": "2026 cash-flow and tax exposure review for US architecture firms with 10 to 50 employees", "Workers' comp mod audit for manufacturers paying over $100k premium", "Founder liquidity-event checklist: 12 decisions before signing an LOI" (research report, 2026).
- Lead Gen Forms prefill and allow up to 12 standard fields plus 3 custom questions; ask only what changes qualification.
- Thought Leader Ads (boosting an expert's post) beat single-image ads on CTR and CPL in LinkedIn's own data; vendor numbers, test only.
- An advisor with $300M AUM spends 2 hours a week prospecting on LinkedIn and recently landed a $1.5M household (r/CFP 50). Leading DMs with tax prep, not planning, worked for a tax-plus-advisory firm (r/CFP 50).
- Validate assets and decision rights before a big occupational niche: Sales Navigator on hospital nurses failed on low assets, spouses handling finances and 403(b)s (r/CFP 50).
- Automated LinkedIn messages got flagged by a broker-dealer's compliance for tone (r/financialadvisors 27). Advisors: every template goes through the CCO.
- Cold email infrastructure and LinkedIn mechanics: `b2b-outbound`.

## Seminars, classes and events

- **Paid classes beat free dinners.** Retirement classes at a local university, recruited by a mail provider (FMT): 10,000 mailers bring 10 to 30 households at $50 each; the fee filters. 80% of the advisor's sourced assets, $22M from 17 households in a year (r/CFP 111).
- **Free dinners leak money:** one advisor paid $500 per dinner for "several thousand dollars in pasta" and one small annuity prospect (r/CFP 50). Kitces 2026: client appreciation events $44,191 CAC.
- **Curriculum:** tax, estate and protection planning get attention more than investments (r/CFP 111).
- **Booth events outside finance:** farmers markets, 5Ks ($100 a booth), home shows, tech expos; "RSU Diversification Strategies" signage; a prize drawing with topic checkboxes; 5 to 50 leads per weekend; call Monday, retry Wednesday and Friday (r/CFP 169). Coordinator ask: "I realize I am not in tech but I'd love to set up a booth and teach people about financial wellness. How much do you typically charge?"
- **Libraries and community venues** give rooms at low or no cost; topics "saving taxes in retirement", "maximizing Social Security" (r/CFP 71).
- **Workplace planning as a benefit:** $250 per participant, minimum 5, two meetings each, then $1,200/year ongoing; batch on one day; at least one AUM client from every group (r/CFP 50).
- **One talk can pay for years.** An accountant traced about $30,000 of business to one free one-hour talk to a small-business group; serving as treasurer of another group produced referrals for years (r/EntrepreneurRideAlong 281).
- Senior-facility "annuity trivia" events exist and draw scrutiny as predatory (r/CFP 51); check suitability and state senior-marketing rules first.

## Networking groups and local presence

- BNI, tennis and golf groups took a Boise tax accountant from about $100,000 to $300,000 billings over 8 years (r/taxpros 124). One BNI referral produced a $700,000 PI fee (r/LawFirm 177). A side-practice CPA built 125 clients and $100,000 with BNI plus answering "recommend a CPA" posts in city Facebook groups (r/taxpros 61).
- An independent P&C agent joined 3 groups and attended one meeting a day; replaced a corporate brokerage income within a year (r/InsuranceAgent 37).
- Meetups beat networking events for advisors: two social Meetups a month led to a UHNW client (r/CFP 71). Nonprofit boards, Porsche club, golf and watches open $1M to $5M relationships, slowly (r/CFP 55).
- Youth sports sponsorship kept producing accounting clients five years after it ended (r/Bookkeeping 46).
- Nextdoor: one post, about 3,000 impressions, "a bunch of" tax leads (r/taxpros 102).

## Outbound and prospecting

- New tax firm: cold calls to bookkeepers, advisors and small businesses plus card drops; $80,000 in year one on 20 hours a week in season (r/taxpros 175).
- Bookkeepers: apply to part-time bookkeeping jobs on Indeed; read Upwork posts for company names and contact them directly (r/Bookkeeping 260); scraped review-site emails won a first client (r/Bookkeeping 105). Volunteering for nonprofits produced first paying clients for two practitioners (r/Bookkeeping 260, 22 and 16 upvotes).
- Lawyers: direct mail to recent arrestees, addresses from the jail book when not online, $1,000 over 8 months, beat Google Ads early (r/LawFirm 548). Check state solicitation rules and waiting periods first.
- Advisors: track SBA loan announcements, commercial lease listings and new chamber members, then "do you have a retirement plan set up for your staff yet?" (r/CFP 50). Cold calling built an 80-client wirehouse book (r/CFP 59); a solo RIA had zero success with cold calls and bought leads and bought a book instead (r/CFP 131).
- Planning-commission minutes show rezoning; affected businesses need attorneys and engineers (r/Entrepreneur 654).

## Bought leads and live transfers (insurance, some advisors)

- Test in tiny batches; scale only vendors with real contactability and fast, written refund rules (r/InsuranceAgent 65). One agency estimates a restart would cost $10,000 to $20,000 vs the $100,000 it spent learning.
- Score vendors weekly: reached / total, kept appointments, submits, time to first touch, refund rate and turnaround, CPA trend; one ACA operation reports about $34 CPA (r/InsuranceAgent 65).
- Ask for the opt-in date and resale count, not "exclusive": batches can be half old data (r/InsuranceAgent 26); 700 duplicates across EverQuote and SmartFinancial in two months (r/InsuranceAgent 42); prospects saying they submitted 2 to 7 days earlier and already bought (r/InsuranceAgent 26).
- Aged leads (90 days to 12 months) at a fraction of the price beat $30 to $60 "high intent" leads for several agents (r/InsuranceAgent 35, 40). Delay higher-cost leads ($70 live transfers) until 30 to 50 policies sold (r/InsuranceAgent 35).
- 30 final expense leads at $23: 9 appointments, 3 kept, 0 sales (r/InsuranceAgent 40). Verified phone numbers are not intent.
- Lead strategy, cadence and caller ID: see [intake-and-conversion.md](intake-and-conversion.md). Consent and TCPA: [compliance.md](compliance.md).

## Buying a book or a practice

Often the fastest route to revenue for advisors and tax firms. Book at 75 cents per revenue dollar over 4 years (r/taxpros 99); 2x revenue seller-financed over 5 years (r/CFP 52); $15M AUM at 2x revenue in year two (r/CFP 392). Have the seller attend every client meeting before transfer, set handoff duties in writing and negotiate first-year clawbacks (r/CFP 41). Check the seller's historical fees: a retiring accountant's referrals were paying $300 when the buyer quoted $750, and all were shocked (r/taxpros 266). Do not announce a sale informally: two clients told early left for cheaper unlicensed preparers and hurt the sale price (r/taxpros 203).

## Bridge income for new solos

Contract work for established firms (10 cold emails, 3 offers, $70/hour estate planning and $1,350/week PI work) funded a biglaw-to-solo launch (r/LawFirm 171). Converting an in-house role into an outside-counsel contract paid $231,000 in year one with no ads (r/LawFirm 117). A bookkeeper's employer can become the first outsourced client (r/Bookkeeping 235). A tax preparer took a tax firm's bookkeeping overflow and kept non-firm inquiries (r/Bookkeeping 42).
