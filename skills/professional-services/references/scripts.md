# Scripts

Exact wording. Quotes marked with a thread are practitioner-tested wording; unmarked templates are drafted for this skill from the research and must pass the firm's approver (amber in [compliance.md](compliance.md)) before use. Replace brackets; keep sentences short; never add guarantees.

## 1. Inquiry acknowledgment (instant, automated)

> Thanks for contacting [Firm] about [matter/service]. [Name] will call you by [time today]. If this is urgent, call [number]. Please don't send confidential details yet; we first need to confirm we can help you.

Lawyers: the last sentence protects conflicts checks. Advisors: add the firm's required disclosure link if policy requires.

## 2. First human call

> Thanks for reaching out. I want to understand enough to see whether we're the right fit and what the next step should be. What prompted you to contact us today?

Then capture: event, deadline, jurisdiction, entity or household type, other parties (conflicts), budget or asset band, decision-makers, next step. Close every call with a booked step or a recorded state.

Shared-lead callback (advisors, r/CFP 55):
> Hey, I'm [name] with [firm]. We're one of the local firms that was matched up with you through [platform]. I waited a while to call you because I'm sure you got bombarded with calls and texts and emails, but I just wanted to see what questions you had and how I can help.

Hard-to-reach solicited lead (insurance, r/InsuranceAgent 29):
> Hi Jim, you're a hard man to get through to! I bet this isn't even an ideal time either. If I was really excited to get to know you and your family, what time would be best to call you?

## 3. Time-boxing a free call (r/taxpros 123)

> Okay, let's schedule a time to talk about this more in depth. I have a meeting coming up so I can't talk any longer.

Paid consult offer:
> The best next step is a [45]-minute consultation. The fee is $[250], and it's credited to your first invoice if we work together. I have [day/time] or [day/time].

## 4. Consultation close

Advisor discovery and commitment (r/CFP 58):
> What are your plans for retirement? ... What could throw a monkey wrench in those plans? ... Give me an example of a scenario that would damage those plans. ... How do you feel your plan would hold up if history repeats itself?
> Do you need help? Do you want help? Do you want our help? What do you see our role being in this?

Scope-and-price close (all professions, drafted):
> Here's what I'd recommend: [scope in one sentence]. That's a [flat fee of $X / monthly fee of $X], and it includes [three deliverables]. It doesn't include [main exclusion]; if that comes up, I'll quote it before doing anything. We can start [date] once the engagement letter is signed and the [deposit/retainer] is paid. Does that work for you?

Decision follow-up (r/CFP 41):
> Please don't decide today. Take a few days, and call my cell with a yes or a no. If I haven't heard from you, I'll call you at this same time next week. Fair?

Two-choice close (life insurance, r/InsuranceAgent 41): show two plans and ask "which one would you prefer?" rather than "do you want to go ahead?"

Age objection (r/CFP 190):
> Obviously I'm a younger advisor. I believe that's a strength, because it tells my clients I'll be around for a long time.

Backup advisor positioning (r/CFP 98):
> If you've got a number one, that's cool. I'm here to be number two when things go sideways or number one retires.

Paperwork embarrassment (r/CFP 83):
> Bring it in and I'll sort and make sense of it.

## 5. Quoting, minimums and declines

Range before review (bookkeeping, r/Bookkeeping 105):
> For a business like yours, monthly bookkeeping usually runs $[800] to $[1,200]. I'll give you an exact price once I've looked at the books. Can you add me as an accountant user?

Minimum disclosure (inherited or price-shopping prospects, r/taxpros 103):
> Before we go further: our minimum for a personal return is $[X]. If that works, I'll send the document list today.

Poor value decline (r/taxpros 97, paraphrase):
> Honestly, with a return this simple our minimum fee wouldn't be good value for you. [Software/a volunteer site] would do this well. If your situation gets more complex, call us.

Price-only switchers (r/taxpros 97):
> If you're leaving your current preparer because of price, we won't be cheaper. If it's something else, I'd like to hear it.

Separate billing for out-of-scope work (r/taxpros 88):
> I'm happy to continue assisting with this kind of review, but any time spent on it will need to be billed separately from our standard tax engagement.

Requote on new facts (drafted from r/taxpros 111):
> The documents you sent include [12 additional K-1s / foreign accounts requiring FBAR and Form 8938]. That's substantially more work than last year, so the fee for this return is $[X]. I'll start as soon as you confirm.

Reduction announcement (r/taxpros 87):
> Good news: I was able to reduce this for you.

Three-way trade-off (r/taxpros 266, 37 upvotes):
> Affordable, fast, and high quality. You can have two, but you can't have three.

Income-based fee objection (r/taxpros 142, proposed): ask the client to subtract two pairs of numbers on a calculator and note it took the same time: "Different numbers, same amount of time. That's what preparing a tax return is."

Payment gate (insurance, r/InsuranceAgent 41):
> Sorry, the carrier doesn't allow us to bind coverage without confirmation of payment.

Extension retainer (r/taxpros 114):
> It's a $100 retainer for an extension.

Cleanup prepayment (r/Bookkeeping 198):
> My rate is $75 an hour, and cleanup requires 8 hours paid up front.

Exposure-for-free requests (r/Bookkeeping 198):
> I'll need full price for this work, but I'll pay you a $100 finder's fee for every paying client you refer. (Bookkeepers only. Lawyers, CPAs, advisers and insurance agents: check referral-fee rules first.)

Excluded service, offered alternative (r/Bookkeeping 105, drafted):
> We don't pay bills for clients; we're a small team focused on accurate books. Bill.com handles it well, and I'll set it up for you.

## 6. Engagement scope (paste into the letter, owner approves)

> **Services included:** [monthly bookkeeping for 1 entity, up to 150 transactions a month, 2 bank and 2 card accounts; monthly close by the 15th; quarterly review call; federal and state income tax returns for the entity and owners].
> **Your responsibilities:** documents and answers by the 5th of each month; one business account kept separate from personal spending; receipts for transactions over $[75].
> **Not included:** accounts payable, payroll processing, sales tax filings, cleanup of periods before [date], audits, notices, amended returns, advisory projects. We quote these before starting.
> **Changes in scope:** if volume, entities or accounts increase, or personal and business transactions are mixed, the fee is reviewed at the next quarter.
> **Fees:** $[X] a month, billed on the 1st by automatic payment. Returns are released and filed after payment in full.
> **Price review:** fees are reviewed every [December] and new fees apply from [January].
> **Communication:** we reply within [1 business day]. Calls are by appointment.
> **Ending the engagement:** either side with [30] days' written notice.

Unlimited support always needs listed exclusions (r/taxpros 119).

## 7. Fee increases

One-line notice, ongoing clients (r/taxpros 111):
> By the way, my rates for [2027] are increasing to $[380] per hour.

One-line notice, annual return clients, at the bottom of the document request (r/taxpros 111):
> By the way, my fee for [2027] has increased to $[1,200] for the return.

First contact of the year (r/taxpros 155):
> My rate for this year is increasing to $[x]/hour.

No explanation unless asked. If asked, one sentence about scope or cost of service.

Formal letter, recurring fee (research report 2026):
> Effective [date], your recurring fee will change from $[X] to $[Y]. The scope continues to include [three concrete deliverables]. The change reflects [specific scope or cost change]. If your needs have changed, we can review the service level before the renewal date.

Avoid "we hate to do this": it frames the increase as arbitrary.

Surprise objection (r/taxpros 266):
> Sorry that the fee increase surprised you. I'm fine with you paying what you paid last year, but please realize our fee will be this going forward, with a price increase each year.

Legacy client, two-year reset (drafted from r/taxpros 157):
> We've kept your fee well below our current rate for years. To bring it in line, this year's fee is $[550], and next year it will be $[800], which is our standard fee for a return like yours. Nothing about the service changes.

## 8. Referral partner asks

Specific client ask (research report 2026):
> We're trying to help more [specific profile] who are dealing with [specific problem]. If someone comes to mind, would you be comfortable introducing us by email? I can send you two sentences to forward.

Two sentences to forward (drafted):
> [Name] at [Firm] works with [profile] on [problem]. They're good to talk to if you're [trigger], and the first call is [free/$X].

COI first meeting (drafted, follows r/CFP 55 and r/Bookkeeping 260):
> I'd like to understand which of your clients you wish you had someone to send to. We work with [profile], and we send out a lot of [tax/estate/insurance] work. Who's a good fit for you, and who isn't?

Cold call to a young CPA firm owner (drafted from r/CFP 59):
> I work with business owners worth [$1M+], and several of them are asking me for a CPA who's responsive. Would you be open to coffee to see whether your practice is a fit?

Contract work for a new solo lawyer (r/LawFirm 171, structure): email about 10 firms in the target practice area asking for steady contract work at a stated rate; 6 or 7 replied and 3 offered work.

Leaving a firm on good terms (r/LawFirm 91):
> My plan is to leave in three months. If you want me to leave sooner I can do that. If you want me to stay longer, we can work out an independent contractor relationship for the time after three months.

Booth coordinator (r/CFP 169):
> Hey, I realize I'm not in tech, but I'd love to set up a booth and teach people about financial wellness. How much do you typically charge?

Tax client to advisory (r/CFP 78; needs IRC 7216 consent where tax data is used):
> Joe and Jane, one of the biggest ways you can control your tax situation is how you handle your retirement and your investments. Can you tell me about what you have going on in that world?

## 9. Review requests within the rules

Neutral request, all professions (research report 2026):
> Would you be willing to share an honest review of your experience? Here is the direct Google link: [link]. Please describe whatever was most useful, or anything we could improve.

Send to every client at the same milestone (matter closed, return filed, policy issued, plan delivered). No gifts, no discounts, no "if you're happy" filters. Advisers: CCO approves the text and the firm does not feature or reply with substance. FINRA reps: only under firm policy. Lawyers in New York: permission needed before featuring a testimonial with name or likeness (2026 rules).

Negative review reply, lawyers and CPAs (drafted, ABA Op. 496 and AICPA 1.700 safe):
> We take every client's experience seriously. Our professional obligations prevent us from discussing any matter publicly. Please contact [name] at [phone] so we can talk.

## 10. Cross-sell triggers

Advisory from bookkeeping data (research report 2026):
> Your monthly close is now showing [payroll up 40% since January]. At your size, we usually see that create [cash or tax] problems before year-end. I'd like to use 30 minutes to model two scenarios and decide whether you need anything beyond your current scope.

Business-owner retirement plan opener (r/CFP 50):
> Congratulations on the new location. Do you have a retirement plan set up for your staff yet?

## 11. Insurance objection handling

"I saw $40 a month" (r/InsuranceAgent 101):
> Yeah, I wish my rates were that low too. I haven't seen quotes that low in years! Those ads are mainly there to get you shopping. Let's go over your policy and make sure everything is up to date.
Then: "Is coverage important to you, or are you just looking for the cheapest thing possible?"

"Insurance is a scam" (r/InsuranceAgent 95):
> I honestly understand how you feel. However, it is required, and my job is to get you the best protection for the lowest price. If you feel it's a necessary evil, let's work on figuring out the best way to win on price and coverages so you at least feel you fought a good fight. Fair enough? There are some coverages required by the state and the finance company. Let's start there.
Concrete pivot: "Would you like to keep your collision deductible at $500 or increase it to $1,000?" With a CLUE claim on file: "If you didn't have insurance that day, would you have had $50,000 to pay out of pocket?" (r/InsuranceAgent 41)

Rate-increase anger (r/InsuranceAgent 49):
> I totally agree. They don't even give me a discount, and I thought that would be one of the perks of this job.
Then options. If they escalate: "I'm going to look into this as soon as we get off the phone. The sooner, the better!" Let them vent until they say "like I said before", then respond, then stop talking (r/InsuranceAgent 48).

"I need to think about it" (life, r/InsuranceAgent 41, aggressive, use with care):
> Totally understand. Usually when people need to think about it, it's because they feel they don't have enough information. What do you feel you're still missing?

Home coverage gap (r/InsuranceAgent 66): show dollars, "$150,000 of dwelling coverage against a $244,000 rebuild cost means you'd fund $94,000 yourself."

Cold call opener, life (r/InsuranceAgent 35, proposed): say plainly that you sell life insurance, ask for two minutes to quote, offer to bring paperwork if they like the price, promise no pressure, and move on if they decline. Never call yourself a "benefits coordinator".

Every insurance script must match the actual coverage: a state-minimum policy may not cover the "Ferrari" example (r/InsuranceAgent 95).

## 12. Advisors: markets and retention

Downturn call (r/CFP 63): open with "How are things? Good weekend?" and let them raise the market. If they do: "We are long-term investors, not short-term traders. Here's how your plan already accounts for a year like this." (r/CFP 97)

Crash rehearsal (r/CFP 193, OP's hypothetical; CCO must approve any performance figures): a folded chart of a 2007 retiree's $1M 60/40 portfolio, ask "What do you think they were feeling at that time?" at the 2008 low, then reveal the recovery. Close:
> The market will go down. It's going to feel like it's different this time. We are here to help you stay the course.

## 13. Former-client and announcement mail (law)

New firm announcement to former clients (r/LawFirm 177, structure): letter explaining the move, sent to former clients only, active clients offered the choice separately; the lawyer added a $5 co-branded coffee card. Check state rules on client notification when leaving a firm and on gifts.
