# By sub-model

Each section: how it grows, the numbers that matter, the first three moves, and traps. Shared mechanics live in the other references.

## Bookkeeping and CAS

- **Engine:** CPA and tax-preparer referrals (reciprocal meetings outside tax season filled one practice to the point of hiring 3 part-timers, r/Bookkeeping 260), niche communities, Upwork and Indeed job posts as prospect lists, client add-ons (new locations, new businesses).
- **Numbers:** $250 to $499/month most common fee (Ignition 2025); CAS net client fees $156,250 per professional (CPA.com/AICPA 2024, possibly outdated); practitioners at $600 to $4,000/month; a two-person practice at about 30 clients and $275,000; a 13-month-old all-in firm at 10 clients and $90,000 (r/Bookkeeping 216).
- **First moves:** (1) books-access quote process with transaction-composition pricing and a diagnostic video; (2) cleanup priced separately with prepaid hours; (3) three CPA or EA referral partners met in the off-season.
- **Position:** bookkeeping as the step that makes tax compliance cheaper; prospects pay for compliance more readily than for books (r/Bookkeeping 61). A $9,500 year-end CPA bill vs a few hundred a month of bookkeeping can open a trade conversation, though commenters argued that bill was fair (r/Bookkeeping 194, 57 upvotes against).
- **Best-fit clients:** businesses underwritten by banks (r/Bookkeeping 105), multi-location owners priced per location, serial entrepreneurs who sell businesses. Properly priced books hurt businesses under about $30,000 monthly revenue (r/Bookkeeping 105).
- **Traps:** $100 to $350/month clients, unlimited Slack access, weekly reconciliations sold as monthly, cleanup discounted to "win" monthly work.

## Tax preparation (individual and small business)

- **Engine:** retention of the annual book, referrals, Google profile, local groups (BNI, Facebook "recommend a CPA" threads), inherited and acquired books, seasonal Yelp or Google ads.
- **Numbers:** individual return $400 to $599 most common, business $1,000 to $1,499 (Ignition 2025); practitioners' 1040 minimums $500 to $1,000+; 2026 season about 164M returns (IRS).
- **First moves:** (1) minimums by form type and new-client premium; (2) payment gates (deposit, retainer for extensions, payment before e-file); (3) December price-setting and fall engagement letters.
- **Growth patterns:** a new firm earned $80,000 in year one from cold calls to bookkeepers and advisors plus card drops (r/taxpros 175); an ex-IRS solo CPA sells $400 to $600/month packages and $1,000+ returns (r/taxpros 387); a seasonal walk-in senior segment paid for a cheap office (r/taxpros 69).
- **Traps:** volume at $250; free "quick questions"; drop-off exceptions; taking over a cheap retiree's clients without stating minimums on the first call (r/taxpros 103); 2,000-return firms with no advisory.

## CPA firm moving into advisory

- **Engine:** existing compliance clients plus niche COIs; CE teaching inside the niche (about 300 clients in two years, r/taxpros 61); partnerships with wealth managers (a CPA inside a wealth manager reported 40 to 50% client growth, r/taxpros 75).
- **Numbers:** CAS hourly as primary method only 10%; niche-heavy CAS +51% net revenue per client; formal CAS plan about +$10,000 per client (CPA.com/AICPA 2024).
- **First moves:** (1) three-tier packages with explicit advisory cadence; (2) `next_best_service` triggers from monthly closes and returns; (3) one niche package and one niche case study.
- **Traps:** advisory given away in emails; tiers that leave unsold time slots (one CPA moved to a single all-inclusive package, r/taxpros 119); 7216 consent missing when cross-selling planning.

## Law: consumer practices

| Practice | Engine | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Personal injury | Google profile, LSA + Search, medical and attorney referrals, client referrals, paid social, lead gen | One shingle ranked channels: Google, referrals, paid social, unbranded lead gen; about 50 cases a month (r/LawFirm 121). Keep the $10,000 to $1M band, refer out the rest. Middle cases (about 30% of volume between soft tissue and catastrophic) made most of one seven-figure practitioner's money (r/LawFirm 113) |
| Family | Google Ads, LSA, Thumbtack, mediators and attorneys winding down | $7,000/month Google Ads and 13 engagements in a month (r/LawFirm 69); immediate callbacks and next-day consults (r/LawFirm 126) |
| Criminal and traffic | Direct mail to arrestees, Google Ads with case results, directories, assigned counsel panels, legal plans | 8 of 10 callers cannot afford counsel (r/LawFirm 548): qualify on budget early; traffic at $375 a ticket with coverage attorneys at $100 to $125 per appearance (r/LawFirm 135) |
| Estate planning and probate | SEO with lawyer-written articles, Search + LSA, financial-advisor neighbors, funeral homes, ARAG | Lawyer-written content plus about $3,000/month SEO for 60% of business (r/LawFirm 91); ARAG 3 to 5 clients a week (r/LawFirm 119) |
| Employment (employee side) | Google + Facebook ads at launch, underserved geography, plaintiff-bar referrals | Only 5 to 10% of inquiries viable; hourly severance work while contingency cases mature (r/LawFirm 89) |
| Immigration, bankruptcy | Search, LSA, community partners | Bankruptcy leads signed at 11.7% vs criminal 6.4% in one paid sample (Pareto Legal 2026) |

- **First moves:** (1) 5-minute intake with after-hours coverage; (2) separate LSA and Search reporting to signed matters; (3) former-client and referral-source program.
- **Traps:** agencies owning the site; reps adding campaigns; PI at under $10,000 to $15,000 a month in Search (r/PPC 118); scaling cases past staff capacity.

## Law: business and transactional

- **Engine:** referral sources called daily, other lawyers' overflow, prior-career networks, LinkedIn, content and speaking, satellite offices where search demand exists.
- **Numbers:** $362/hour average, $484 corporate litigation (Clio Oct 2026). A real estate, mortgage and corporate firm reported $1.2M to $1.5M revenue and $675,000 to $900,000 profit in year six on referral-source calls (r/LawFirm 236).
- **First moves:** (1) list of 30 referral sources ranked by origination; (2) one niche authority asset; (3) client feedback interviews with cross-practice introductions.
- **Traps:** generic newsletters; partners who hoard origination with no system; office space far from referrers.

## Insurance: personal lines, life, Medicare, ACA, final expense

- **Engine:** bought leads and live calls, referrals, bundling, renewals; marketplace referrals (one Kentucky ACA agent received 3 referrals in an hour, r/InsuranceAgent 134).
- **Numbers:** 58% of independent-agency clients come via family or friends (Vertafore 2025); internet P&C leads $4 to $7 at 1.5% close (r/InsuranceAgent 42); $34 CPA in one ACA live-call operation (r/InsuranceAgent 65); budget 25% chargebacks (r/InsuranceAgent 134).
- **First moves:** (1) consent and scrub process, vendor scorecard and small tests; (2) 7-call plus 14-email cadence; (3) post-sale service for referrals and retention.
- **Traps:** stale "exclusive" leads, duplicate vendors, aged lists with 25% wrong numbers (r/InsuranceAgent 25), commission-maximizing coverage that charges back, captive carriers priced out locally (zero sales in a month until a move to a competitively priced office, r/InsuranceAgent 79).

## Insurance: commercial and benefits

- **Engine:** niche plus market access, COIs (realtors, lenders, CPAs, door-to-door B2B partners), networking daily, employer enrollment firms for supplemental lines.
- **Numbers:** $20,600 average agency marketing budget, 9.9 staff (Big "I" 2026); one commercial niche agent at $20,000 a month (r/InsuranceAgent 29).
- **First moves:** (1) pick one niche with carrier access; (2) win commercial accounts, then cross-sell personal lines to owners; (3) payment-before-binding rule.
- **Traps:** saturated territories, employer accounts that never pay the first invoice (commission chargebacks, r/InsuranceAgent 81).

## Financial advisors and planners

- **Engine:** client and COI referrals, CPA partnerships, seminars and classes, advisor directories, long-form YouTube, Meta VSL funnels, buying books, workplace planning.
- **Numbers:** median CAC $2,551, listings $1,366, referrals $3,198, SEO $3,020, COIs $7,371, seminars $15,447, social $48,836 (Kitces 2026); Schwab top 20% grew clients 12.4% vs 4.6% (2026).
- **First moves:** (1) written ICP and value proposition (Schwab 2025: +67% new clients for firms with them); (2) three CPA relationships via clients' CPAs; (3) one repeatable acquisition channel with booked-appointment tracking.
- **Paths that worked:** SmartVestor overflow for a split, then own market: 100 households and $20M in 5 years (r/CFP 190); university classes: $22M from 17 households in a year (r/CFP 111); flat-fee solo RIA for $2M to $7M retirees, 35 households, $320,000 (r/CFP 190); $1,500/month Facebook video ads, 30 to 40 appointments, 2 to 4 clients a month at a $3,500 planning fee plus about $500,000 assets each (r/CFP 71); rural sponsorships plus a $15M book purchase to $100M at 27 (r/CFP 392).
- **Traps:** social as direct response, lead-gated videos, unfunded "ghost" prospects, hourly exceptions, selling side products early, compliance-unapproved automation.

## Outside the US, quick notes

- **UK:** SRA bans unsolicited approaches to the public by solicitors; FCA rules on financial promotions and finfluencers; Self Assessment peaks December to January; "accountant" is unprotected, "chartered" is protected.
- **Canada:** law societies by province; CIRO and CSA for investment advice (finfluencer guidance Dec 2025); provincial insurance regulators.
- **Australia:** ASIC RG 234 (June 2026) and REP 845 lead-generation review (Nov 2026); TPB registration for tax and BAS agents; lodgment programs differ from the US season.
- **EU:** MiFID II Article 24 and IDD for marketing; GDPR for prospect data (`b2b-outbound`).
