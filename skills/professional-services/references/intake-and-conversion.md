# Intake and conversion

## The competitive bar

- 1,333 US law firms tested by web form in 2025: 25% replied personally in under 5 minutes, 33% within 10, 56% within an hour, 39% took over 2 hours or never, 26% never replied within 7 days; median 13 minutes (Hennessey Digital).
- Clio's 2024 secret shop (possibly outdated): 40% answered the phone, 33% answered email; only 18% of email replies explained next steps or cost and 2% mentioned similar matters.
- Do not repeat "contact in 5 minutes = 21x / 100x conversions". Those figures come from older cross-industry sales-software studies of contact rates, not signed clients (Caseflow 2026 review). Five minutes is a competitive standard you can hit, not a promised multiplier.
- Speed without substance fails. The reply must answer fit, next step and cost range.

## Build the intake system

1. **Instant acknowledgment** (text and email) naming the matter and when a person will call, with a confidentiality line. Template in [scripts.md](scripts.md).
2. **Human within 5 minutes in staffed hours.** Call first, then text, then email. Calls roll to a backup person or answering service, never straight to voicemail.
3. **After hours:** a 24/7 answering service for overflow and nights. One transactional solo pays about $500/month after a new website lifted leads (r/LawFirm 43). Test the service: one solo cancelled Smith.ai at $240/month because many calls went to its voicemail (r/LawFirm 169). A tax solo added a virtual receptionist (POSH) before tax season to answer FAQs and book Calendly calls and called it life changing (r/taxpros 387).
4. **Cheap dedicated intake:** a solo estate-planning lawyer hired an assistant through online-jobs.ph at $7.25/hour to answer every call, book and follow up, removing the bottleneck (r/LawFirm 117). A small firm routes voicemails to an associate who becomes "managing attorney" on the file for 5% of receipts (r/LawFirm 284).
5. **Self-booking with screening.** Calendly routing forms ask asset level, matter type or entity count before showing slots; route under-minimum prospects to a polite decline page (r/CFP 158). Clio Grow self-booking of free consultations plus immediate callbacks got a new family firm to 25 clients in 3.5 months (r/LawFirm 126).
6. **Lead states.** Every inquiry ends: booked, nurturing, disqualified (reason), lost to competitor, unreachable, duplicate, conflict, spam. Report no-contact and no-show by source and by intake person.
7. **Measure time to first human action,** not the autoresponder timestamp.
8. **Throttle.** When median response passes the target or the calendar has no slot within 7 days, pause the most expensive paid source first.

Watch automation costs: auto-creating a TaxDome account for every Calendly booking means paying $15 a year per prospect above the 150-account allowance, ghosts included (r/taxpros 67). Collect prospect documents in permissioned folders under one firm account instead.

## Qualification

Capture: the event that prompted the call, deadline, jurisdiction, entity or household type, opposing party for conflicts, budget or asset or revenue band where legitimate, decision-makers, next committed step.

Red flags practitioners screen for:
- Tax: cannot produce prior returns, cannot explain why they are leaving their accountant, odd story, first contact in February (r/taxpros 130); leaving only over price ("we will not be cheaper") (r/taxpros 97).
- Bookkeeping: asks price before understanding the service, wants a bar tab written off, "Doesn't QuickBooks do the bookkeeping automatically?" Add a difficult-client factor to the quote (r/Bookkeeping 39).
- PI: "I'm not the suing type", "it's the principle of the thing" (r/LawFirm 173).
- Advisors: repeated cancellations and interruptions during onboarding. An 8-month $1M pursuit with four planning meetings ended unfunded (r/CFP 78).
- Insurance: persistently hostile state-minimum buyers who consume service time ("we won't be a good fit for their needs") where the agency allows declining (r/InsuranceAgent 41).
- Any: wants detailed advice before engaging. Quote a fee for second opinions or allocations; non-serious prospects back away at once (r/CFP 59).

## The consultation

**Free vs paid.** Free 30 minutes works when the calendar is empty (a new family firm added 10 to 15 minutes of value and used flat fees and payment plans to close its first 10 clients, r/LawFirm 89). As capacity fills, charge ($250 non-client consult, r/taxpros 123; $250 non-refundable setup and document-review fee to stop ghosting, r/taxpros 67).

**Time box.** Cap unbilled calls at 5 to 15 minutes: "Okay, let's schedule a time to talk about this more in depth. I have a meeting coming up so I can't talk any longer." (r/taxpros 123, 121 upvotes). An estate lawyer limited a mismatched consult to a 10-minute needs assessment and quoted afterward instead of reviewing DIY documents on the spot (r/LawFirm 90).

**Structure.**
1. Open: what prompted the call today.
2. Diagnose with feeling questions (advisors): "What are your plans for retirement?" then "What could throw a monkey wrench in those plans?", "How do you feel your plan would hold up if history repeats itself?" (r/CFP 58). Sandler-style pain funnel on 2 or 3 concerns; one advisor reported 5 new clients in 60 days after starting (r/CFP 58).
3. Check commitment: "Do you need help? Do you want help? Do you want OUR help? What do you perceive our role to be in this?" (r/CFP 58).
4. Translate into the decision they want made: "we'll figure out what you can actually withdraw without jeopardizing your future goals", not cash-flow mechanics (r/CFP 46).
5. Show a one-page plan or scope with prices before asking for commitment (advisors: before the AUM fee, r/CFP 41).
6. Book the next step before they leave (r/CFP 41). If they want time, agree that they call with a yes or no within days, and you call at the same time next week if not; typical answers come in 2 to 3 days (r/CFP 41).

**Do it alone.** A young advisor believes bringing a senior advisor to look experienced lost him the business (r/CFP 190). Pre-empt age: "Obviously I am a younger advisor. I believe that is a strength because it tells my clients that I will be around for a long time." (r/CFP 190)

**Make paperwork the hook.** "Bring it in and I'll sort and make sense of it" turned a client's envelope pile into a dozen accounts consolidated to five, about $750,000 (r/CFP 83). A 90-minute file-cleanup session sealed a consolidation of 8 old retirement accounts (r/CFP 83).

**Diagnostic video.** Bookkeepers: preliminary range on the call, then a few-minute screen recording of the books showing what needs work, sent with a one-page proposal (r/Bookkeeping 105).

**Discovery notes to proposal.** One firm feeds meeting-notetaker output into an enterprise LLM to draft the proposal, research fee ranges, list required filings and generate follow-up questions (r/taxpros 173). Use only a tool covered by the firm's confidentiality and data-processing terms.

## Seminar and event conversion

- Sticky notes prefilled with appointment slots in the next two weeks, the appointment framed as a "lab session or third class"; about 80% book (r/CFP 41, 111).
- Lab session qualifies (about 40% move on), then a one-page plan meeting closes about 70% (r/CFP 111).
- Event leads: call Monday, retry Wednesday and Friday, continue until they meet or ask you to stop (r/CFP 169).

## Phone sales cadence (insurance and lead-driven advisors)

- **Metrics daily:** pickup rate, conversations over 2 minutes, policies per presentation. Low pickup: check spam labeling and dialer (Convoso, Kixie). Short calls: practice openers until fluent (r/InsuranceAgent 84).
- **Call cadence:** 60% of eventual quotes by call 4, 80% by call 7, 99% by call 14; after 6 calls in 3 to 4 days, wait 4 days (competitors have stopped) (r/InsuranceAgent 38). Alternative for solicited life leads: one call a week, no voicemail, warm opener (r/InsuranceAgent 29).
- **Email:** 14 short, mobile-friendly emails on days 1, 2, 4, 7, 10, 14, 21, 25, 30, 37, 45, 52, 58, 60, aiming for a reply or a site visit that feeds retargeting (r/InsuranceAgent 38). About 90% of sold leads originate on mobile (same source).
- **Text:** day 1 after the first call, then days 2, 3, 5, 8, 12, then every 8 days to day 90, where carrier rules and consent allow; some carriers ban texting before you have spoken (r/InsuranceAgent 38).
- **Day 30 to 35 re-dial** of aged internet leads by telemarketers; renewal shopping is the suspected reason (r/InsuranceAgent 38).
- **Routing:** best agents on newest, highest-value leads; aged and cheap leads to newer staff (r/InsuranceAgent 38).
- **Branded caller ID** plus separate tracking numbers for Google and the website: bought leads often call back via Google after searching the agency (r/InsuranceAgent 38).
- **Shared leads (SmartAsset, Ramsey):** call after the initial rush and say why: "I waited a while to call you because I'm sure you got bombarded with calls and texts and emails, but I just wanted to see what questions you had and how I can help." Contact ratio rose sharply (r/CFP 55).
- **Discovery call:** what prompted the request (a friend without coverage, a funeral GoFundMe) decides whether to book an appointment or just send a brochure (r/InsuranceAgent 29).
- **Live calls:** route only when free, with instant pause and caps (r/InsuranceAgent 65). Call flow: verify basics, solve the real problem, set a specific next action. Present 1 or 2 plans, never a menu (r/InsuranceAgent 65).
- **10 minutes a day of call recordings** found leaks the spreadsheet never showed (r/InsuranceAgent 65).
- **Before any vendor:** 2 to 3 weeks building scripts, routing, notes template and manual follow-up schedule (r/InsuranceAgent 65). Excel and Google Calendar first; expensive software nobody uses is waste.

## Proposals and engagement letters

- One page: deliverables, price, timing, client responsibilities, exclusions, what triggers a change order.
- Fee highlighted and initialed on the engagement letter, with a cover letter for increases. It did not stop one client from disputing later, so pair it with payment gates (r/taxpros 157).
- E-signature correlated with about 10% higher conversion (Clio 2025, correlational).
- Client handbook: scope, monthly timelines, billing, documentation rules, with a one-page owner summary of client duties and exact dates ("by the 5th", not "after 15") and a section on "habits that will increase the bookkeeping cost" (r/Bookkeeping 103).
- Portal-only document intake with no drop-off exceptions made a virtual tax season calmer (r/taxpros 185). Exception worth keeping: one remote firm's cheap downtown office paid for itself through seniors who needed in-office uploads (r/taxpros 69).
- Accept only the software stack you support (QuickBooks Online not Desktop, r/taxpros 185; required Gusto payroll, r/taxpros 119). An established bookkeeper warns long-time paper clients would leave if forced digital (r/Bookkeeping 42): apply to new clients first.

## Expectation management (law and advisors)

- Never anchor a client to a net payout before liens and expenses are final: a $650,000 settlement estimated at "around $335,000 net" ended at $331,000 and the client refused to sign (r/LawFirm 173).
- Advisors: rehearse a crash before it happens (folded-chart exercise, [scripts.md](scripts.md)) so clients stay invested.
