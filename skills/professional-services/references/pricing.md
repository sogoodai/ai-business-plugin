# Pricing

Psychology and generic increase mechanics live in `offers-pricing`; productized-retainer patterns in `agency-freelance`. This file holds what is specific to professional fees. The licensed owner signs every fee, scope and engagement letter.

## Choose the model by scope predictability

| Work | Best model | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Recurring bookkeeping, payroll, compliance | Monthly flat fee by package | Predictable; owners hate variable hourly invoices (lost first client, r/Bookkeeping 105) |
| Individual and business returns | Fixed fee from a minimum plus complexity add-ons | Only 3% bill tax prep hourly (Ignition 2025) |
| Tax planning, CFO, CAS | Monthly retainer, tiered | Advisory paid, not given away in "quick questions" |
| Cleanup, catch-up, IRS resolution | Fixed project fee from a scoped review, or prepaid hours | Unknown volume; never sight unseen |
| Routine legal (estate docs, uncontested divorce, formations, traffic, misdemeanors, immigration filings) | Flat fee | 75% of solos offer flat fees (Clio 2025); 71% of consumers prefer them (Clio 2026, vendor) |
| Litigation, open-ended matters | Hourly with retainer replenishment, or phased flat fees | Scope unknowable |
| PI, employment plaintiff | Contingency, with hourly options for pre-suit work | One employment lawyer sold hourly severance negotiation to high earners resisting a 35% cut (r/LawFirm 89) |
| Advisory with AUM | Blended tiered AUM, or flat fee by complexity | Kitces 2026 notes flat-fee growth; AUM still dominant |
| Planning without assets | Subscription or project | $4,500/year median subscription (Kitces, 2024 data); $4,950 median project fee (Form ADV data, Nov 2026) |
| Insurance | Carrier premium; agency fees only where state law allows | Grow policies per household, retention, commission mix |

Two-person firm that dropped all hourly work grew from $650,000 to $850,000; charging per call teaches clients not to call, and then you miss the decisions that matter for tax planning (r/taxpros 75).

## Minimums: the most underused lever

- Publish or state minimums before the first meeting. A bookkeeper who put the minimum "front and center" on the website saw tire kickers dry up (r/Bookkeeping 39). A new CPA states $1,500 personal / $2,000 business minimums when asking the network for referrals (r/taxpros 61).
- Charge new clients more than existing ones: $500 existing vs $800 new minimum with about 50% acceptance (r/taxpros 103); corporate returns $1,500 existing vs $1,900 new.
- Raise the acceptance floor as the book fills: $750 / $2,000 when busy; $1,500 planned new-client minimum (46 upvotes); a $2,500 floor for non-outsourced 1040s at one firm (r/taxpros 92).
- Advisors: raise the asset minimum as capacity fills ($500k to $3M, r/CFP 55); route under-minimum prospects with a Calendly routing form to a "not a good fit" page (r/CFP 158).
- Tell simple-return prospects your minimum is poor value for them (r/taxpros 97, 41 upvotes). It protects the brand and the calendar.

## Deposits, retainers and payment gates

- 50% deposit with a quoted range: about 5% objected, all paid (r/taxpros 117). 100% upfront plus signed engagement for new clients' extensions: minimal write-offs (r/taxpros 114, 88 upvotes).
- Extension retainer for clients who filed an extension and never finished last year: $100; ten more callers paid it the same day (r/taxpros 114). $500 retainer for last-two-weeks arrivals.
- Payment before e-sign or before release. TaxDome: link the return to the invoice to lock it. Send a summary approval letter with the key figures first so clients can review before paying (r/taxpros 128).
- Fall engagement letters for next season, ACH in January (r/taxpros 114).
- Bookkeeping cleanup: 8 hours prepaid ("my rate is $75/hr, must pay 8 hours up front") and stop when prepaid hours run out (r/Bookkeeping 198).
- Law: retainer before work, always. A cannabis lawyer who started LLC formations without signed, paid retainers had to sue to collect $2,000 fees and dropped the channel (r/LawFirm 110). Trust accounting rules apply to retainers; the lawyer decides how fees are held.
- Insurance: collect and process the down payment before binding agency-billed policies. One agent bound a $90,000 policy without the $19,000 down payment; the client vanished, leaving up to $22,500 in minimum earned premium exposure (r/InsuranceAgent 41). Script: "Sorry, the carrier doesn't allow us to bind coverage without confirmation of payment."
- Collect before filing when a client hints they cannot pay their tax bill (r/taxpros 114).
- Never use an inflated retainer to scare off a bad prospect. A boss asked $30,000 instead of $5,000 upfront; several paid, and every one had to be fired within a year (r/Lawyertalk 2,537). Decline directly.

## Quoting rules

- Review before quoting. Bookkeeping: accountant-user access or two months of statements for every account, count transactions and accounts (r/Bookkeeping 105). Tax: the prior returns, and for returning former clients the other preparer's returns ("send me the 2022 returns done by the other guy, I will take a look and send you a quote"), which in one case uncovered a $446,000 capital discrepancy (r/taxpros 90).
- Price transaction composition, not count: a 100-transaction restaurant can take more work than a 300-transaction designer (POS clearing, loan splits, payroll, personal charges, fixed assets) (r/Bookkeeping 114).
- Requote when the work changes: a dozen extra K-1s moved $600 to $1,200, client said "no problem I understand" (r/taxpros 111). Undisclosed FBAR and Form 8938 obligations justify an updated quote before work begins (r/taxpros 111).
- Give a range on the first call, final price after review, with a short diagnostic video of the books (r/Bookkeeping 105).
- Build legitimate contingencies into estimates and announce reductions: "I was able to reduce this for you" (r/taxpros 87).
- Add a screening premium when a prospect announces complexity and unwillingness to pay; one added $200 and the prospect vanished, as hoped (r/taxpros 111). Quote high for difficult estate-planning prospects and have a paralegal deliver it (r/LawFirm 90).
- Never quote to end a long call. A 1.5-hour call ended with $250/year to review and amend, far too low (r/taxpros 123).
- Price year-round access explicitly or exclude it. A $300 return for a $300,000-income client with RSUs and foreign accounts came with unpaid year-round "quick questions" (r/taxpros 111).
- Flat criminal fees must cover a trial: $2,500 misdemeanor, about $10,000 of trial work (r/LawFirm 182).
- Hourly exceptions to win a big account backfire: $900/hour plus a $25,000 retainer for a $16M prospect led to disputes over every call and an unimplemented plan (r/CFP 191). Refer prospects who want a different model.

## Packages

Three levels by outcome and cadence, not feature count (research report 2026):
- **Core:** compliance, bookkeeping or the return, defined response SLA, no proactive advisory.
- **Growth:** Core + quarterly review, forecast, tax planning or policy review.
- **Strategic:** Growth + monthly decision support, scenarios, CFO, wealth or owner coordination.

Each package states: entities and returns included, transaction or account volume, meetings per year, response time, cleanup and change-order rules, notice period, what is out of scope. "Unlimited support" needs written exclusions: a medical-practice CPA had clients abuse it, expecting AR and AP work (r/taxpros 119). One all-inclusive package can beat tiers when demand is strong: tiers created wasted time slots for that CPA.

Bookkeeping specifics: start hourly for 3 months after cleanup, then set the flat fee from real hours, with an annual review clause (r/Bookkeeping 86). Offer an annual prepaid price at a lower monthly equivalent; most logistics clients switched to annual after 2 to 4 months (r/Bookkeeping 105). Price per location so revenue grows with the client (r/Bookkeeping 51). Hold a price for 6 months and state that continued mixing of personal and business spending triggers an increase (r/Bookkeeping 34).

## Raising fees

- **Data:** 80% of accounting firms planned 2026 increases, mostly 5 to 10%; 28% hesitate from fear of churn; two-thirds of past raisers lost nobody or kept profit stable (Ignition 2025).
- **Practitioners go further:** +20% then +10% with no objections from existing clients (r/taxpros 266); 15 to 40% by situation, little resistance (r/taxpros 117); target average 25% allocated by realization (r/taxpros 111); a 900-return firm raising three years running gets about 10 complaints a year and rising income on fewer returns (r/taxpros 157).
- **Legacy and inherited clients:** set a target per client, close half the gap this year and half next ($300 to $550 to $800) (r/taxpros 157); reset inherited fees in the re-engagement letter (doubled, accepted, r/taxpros 97); clients of 10+ years leave more after doubling than newer ones (r/taxpros 97); successors to a father's practice heard "your dad never charged me" and lost clients who would not pay (r/taxpros 262). Expect that and replace them.
- **Timing:** one line at the client's first contact of the year or at the bottom of the document request (scripts in [scripts.md](scripts.md)); no explanation unless asked (r/taxpros 111). Or notify everyone early in the year with tailored increases (r/taxpros 117). Set the average each December from complaints, satisfaction and demand vs capacity (r/taxpros 111).
- **Surprise objections:** offer last year's price once, reset expectations for next year; clients usually pay the invoice anyway (r/taxpros 266).
- **Concessions do not buy loyalty.** A $255 bill cut to $190 after complaints; the client asked for $50 more and left (r/taxpros 262).
- **Unwanted work:** price it at a fee you would happily accept, because most clients stay (r/taxpros 155). Difficult clients: double the fee; all left, the owner slept better (r/Bookkeeping 39).
- **Competitor anchors:** TurboTax advertising a $1,499 business return (discounted to $999) supported increases when shown next to the firm's review, adjusting entries, planning and year-round advice (r/taxpros 103, 49 upvotes).
- **Hardship:** payment plans over 2 to 3 months instead of discounts (r/taxpros 142); payment-timing flexibility for discounted legacy clients (r/taxpros 88).
- **Win-back price wars:** keep your full fee and remind clients of what you fixed; do not criticize the other preparer (r/taxpros 89).

## AUM vs flat fee (advisors)

| Question | AUM (blended tiers) | Flat or subscription |
|---|---|---|
| Who it fits | Clients with investable assets you manage | Planning-heavy, asset-light, HNW who dislike percentage fees, young professionals |
| Revenue moves with | Markets and withdrawals (assets deplete in retirement; one $300M advisor prospects 2 hours a week to replace them, r/CFP 50) | Your pricing decisions; must reprice for complexity |
| Competitive risk | 40 bps on a $29M portfolio was reportedly well above competing quotes (r/CFP 43) | Hard to compare, easier to justify with a scope list |
| Consolidation lever | Family-level breakpoints, e.g. 0.50% at $10M across relatives (r/CFP 392) | Household fee covers everyone |
| Scaling trap | Low-revenue clients on the same service | Complexity creep without repricing (Kitces, Aug 2026) |

Present a one-page plan (goals, income, protection, taxes, investments) before the fee; showing only investment management invites comparison with cheap managers (r/CFP 41). Disclosure of fees follows Form ADV and the SEC Marketing Rule; the CCO signs.

## Insurance: what you can actually move

Premium is carrier-priced. Levers: carrier appointments and markets (an agent moved from zero Allstate policies in a month to $30,000 to $42,000 monthly premium at an AAA office with competitive local rates, r/InsuranceAgent 79), niche and overseas market access for commercial, bundling and account rounding, retention, and advocacy services where allowed (a Medicare broker packages an advocate for every plan sold, r/InsuranceAgent 49). Check state law before any broker fee.
