# Professional services: answer fast, price the work, engineer referrals

For solo practitioners to firms of about 20 staff: accountants, bookkeepers and CAS firms, tax preparers (CPA, EA, unlicensed preparers), lawyers and small law firms, insurance agents and agencies (P&C, life, health, Medicare, commercial), and financial advisors and planners (RIA, hybrid, broker-dealer reps, flat-fee planners).

Built from 538 practitioner threads (r/taxpros, r/CFP, r/InsuranceAgent, r/LawFirm, r/Lawyertalk, r/Bookkeeping, plus professional-services threads from r/PPC, r/googleads and r/EntrepreneurRideAlong), a November 2026 research pass (Clio, Hennessey Digital, Pareto Legal, Kitces, Schwab, CPA.com/AICPA, Ignition, TaxDome, Big "I", Vertafore, Invoca, Pew, Google, SEC, FINRA, ABA, NYSBA, SRA, FCA, ASIC, CIRO, IRS, HMRC) and the coreyhaines31/marketingskills legal teardown pack (MIT). "Operators report" marks a practitioner anecdote with its upvotes; everything else carries its source and year. Sources: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md). The owner's decisions win over this guide, except on the compliance lines in "Never".

This skill does marketing, pricing and intake. It never gives legal, tax, investment or insurance advice, and it never decides whether an ad complies. It drafts, flags the rule, and routes to the licensed owner or compliance officer.

## How these firms actually grow

- **Referrals are the base layer, everywhere.** 59% of solo and small law firms name referrals as their top source (Clio 2025). 58% of independent-agency clients came via family or friends; only 12% via online search (Vertafore 2025). Client referrals cost advisers $3,198 per client vs a $16,925 all-tactic average (Kitces 2026).
- **The leak is intake, not demand.** Only 25% of 1,333 law firms replied personally to a web form within 5 minutes; 26% never replied within 7 days; median 13 minutes (Hennessey 2025). Buying leads into that is buying waste.
- **Price is set by scope and minimums, not by hours.** Only 3% of accounting firms bill tax prep hourly (Ignition 2025). Practitioners who raised 20 to 30% a year report income up while return counts fell (operators report, r/taxpros 111 to 157).
- **Niche changes economics.** CAS practices with over half of revenue in defined industries had 38% higher median CAS revenue and 51% higher net revenue per client (CPA.com/AICPA 2024, 2023 data). Buyers say they would pay 25% more for a specialist (TaxDome 2025, vendor survey).
- **Existing clients are the cheapest growth.** Top RIAs got 4.2x more assets from existing relationships (Schwab 2026). Insurance clients with 5+ policies retain at 84.7% vs 77.1% for single-policy (MarshBerry via Relay 2025, association only).
- **Capacity caps everything.** A PI firm that hit 50 cases a month "broke staff and systems" and throttled to 30 to 40 (operators report, r/LawFirm 109, 121). Tax firms that market in March buy chaos. Grow capacity and price before volume.
- **Compliance is part of the funnel.** Every regulator in scope treats AI-written marketing like human marketing. "The AI wrote it" is no defense (FINRA 2026).

## First session: ask, then compute

Ask the owner:

1. Profession, license (CPA, EA, attorney and bar states, insurance lines and states, RIA / BD / IAR, FCA, AFSL), solo or team, country and states served.
2. Practice areas or services, with revenue and hours by service for the last 12 months.
3. Where the last 30 clients came from, by person or partner, not "referral".
4. Who answers phones and forms, hours covered, and median reply time this month.
5. Current fee schedule, minimums, deposits, payment-before-release rules, and last increase date.
6. Capacity: hours available, season peaks, waitlist or turning work away.
7. Paid channels, agencies and their contracts (who owns the website, domain and ad accounts).
8. Compliance owner: bar rules state, CCO, principal, compliance vendor, or "me".
9. Offers or claims they will never make; client data they must not use.

Then compute ([references/economics.md](references/economics.md)):

```
signed-client CAC = channel spend (incl. agency fees) / signed or paid clients from that channel
lead-to-client funnel = inquiry > contacted > qualified > consult > engaged > paid (by source and by intake person)
first-human-response time = median minutes to a person (not the autoresponder)
first-year value = first-year fees collected x gross margin; payback = CAC / monthly gross margin
realization = fees collected / (hours x internal standard rate)        (per client)
effective hourly = fee / all hours incl. unbilled calls, rework and fee disputes
CPL ceiling = revenue per sale x lead-to-sale rate x margin share you will spend
insurance net commission = premium x commission rate x (1 - chargeback rate) - lead cost
advisor CAC tolerance = first-year fee x expected years retained x margin / 3
```

Report the three biggest gaps in money. Benchmarks: [references/benchmarks.md](references/benchmarks.md).

## Decision rules, in order

1. **Fix intake before spending.** Instant acknowledgment, a human within 5 minutes in staffed hours, calls roll to a person or answering service, every lead ends in a recorded state. 87% of responding law firms used the phone (Hennessey 2025): call, then text and email.
2. **Make referrals attributable.** Source field = `referral/client/<name>` or `referral/CPA/<firm>`. Ask every new client "who introduced you?" even when analytics says Google. Rank referrers by signed revenue and reciprocate on that list.
3. **Set minimums and gate payment before buying volume.** Minimum fee by form or matter type, deposit or retainer before work, payment before release or e-file. Then raise prices every year on a fixed date, allocated by client realization.
4. **Own the Google profile.** Correct primary category, every service listed, tracked links, neutral review request to everyone. GBP phone leads converted at 38% vs 33% for paid search in financial services (Invoca 2026, vendor observational).
5. **Lawyers on paid search: two ledgers.** LSA cost $2,354 per signed case vs $3,812 for Search in a 12-firm sample, though LSA CPL was higher ($192 vs $92) (Pareto Legal 2026, agency data). Judge by signed matter, never CPL.
6. **Niche when you can prove it.** Pick a niche where the firm has proof, a referral ecosystem and repeatable workflow; test 3 to 5 early, narrow when full (operators report, r/CFP 142).
7. **Cross-sell from triggers, not calendars.** Each client gets a `next_best_service` field driven by observed events (payroll growth, entity change, liquidity event, renewal, new property).
8. **Paid acquisition last, one channel at a time,** with a CAC cap the owner signs and offline conversion import to signed or funded clients.
9. **Capacity throttle.** Pause acquisition when first response time or service SLAs slip; in tax season sell only high-fit work unless the workflow shows spare capacity.

## Pricing in one screen

- **Benchmarks (Ignition 2025, 219 US firms, vendor cohort):** individual return most often $400 to $599; business return $1,000 to $1,499; bookkeeping $250 to $499/month; CFO/controller over $2,500/month. Lawyer average $362/hour (Clio, Oct 2026). Advisor subscription median $4,500/year (Kitces, 2024 data); median published hourly $296 and project fee $4,950 (Form ADV data, Nov 2026).
- **Minimums filter clients.** Practitioners report $750 to $1,000 for a 1040 and $1,500 to $2,000 for entity returns, with new-client minimums above existing ($500 existing, $800 new, about 50% acceptance) (operators report, r/taxpros 92 to 387).
- **Annual increases by realization:** target average 25%; clients under 70% realization get 30%, over 150% get 20%, nobody under 20% (operators report, r/taxpros 111). 80% of firms planned 2026 increases, mostly 5 to 10% (Ignition 2025).
- **Fewer clients survive bigger raises than owners fear.** One tripled prices and lost 3 of 100; another raised 50% expecting most of 80 clients to leave, and only a handful did (r/taxpros 155).
- **Bookkeeping:** quote from read-only books access and transaction composition, not count; target about 2% of client revenue (operators report); cleanup = 12 months of fees + 10 to 25%.
- **Law:** flat fees where scope is predictable (75% of solos offer them, Clio 2025); price flat criminal fees for the trial, not the plea ($2,500 fee, $10,000 of work, r/LawFirm 182).
- **Advisors:** AUM blended tiers or flat fee by complexity; family-level breakpoints consolidate households. Referral partners with a different billing model beat one-off exceptions.
- **Insurance:** carrier sets premium. Grow commission per household, policies per household and retention, not price.

Detail and the AUM vs flat-fee decision: [references/pricing.md](references/pricing.md).

## Intake and the consultation

- **Acknowledge instantly, without inviting confidences:** "Please do not send confidential information until we confirm we can assist" (conflicts first for lawyers).
- **Quote a range early.** Clio's 2024 secret shop found only 18% of email replies explained next steps or cost; the reply must reduce uncertainty, not just arrive.
- **Cap free calls at 10 to 15 minutes,** then book a paid or scheduled consult (r/taxpros 123). A $250 consult fee for non-clients filters tire kickers.
- **Screen:** why they are leaving their current provider, missing prior returns, February arrivals, "I'm not the suing type", "it's the principle of the thing" (r/taxpros 130, r/LawFirm 173).
- **Book the next step before they leave.** Seminar sticky notes with slots booked about 80% of attendees into a lab session; 70% of plan-meeting attendees signed (operators report, r/CFP 111).

Full funnel, states and scripts: [references/intake-and-conversion.md](references/intake-and-conversion.md).

## Compliance gates (route every regulated asset)

- **Green, auto-publish:** hours, office news, approved templates, neutral review requests.
- **Amber, licensed reviewer approves:** claims, comparisons, case results, testimonials, performance, rates, promotions, ads, AI-generated content, anything with tax or legal information.
- **Red, never from the agent:** individualized advice, guarantees, "specialist/expert" claims without certification, live solicitation, performance projections, content outside licensed jurisdictions.

Key rules: ABA Model Rules 7.1 to 7.3 as adopted by each state (New York dropped its "Attorney Advertising" label and 30-day PI blackout from 1 June 2026, NYSBA); SEC Marketing Rule testimonials need client status, compensation and conflict disclosures, and a written agreement above $1,000 a year of promoter pay; FINRA 2210 retail communications need principal approval before use; insurance advertising and rebating are state law; AICPA bars commissions and contingent fees with attest clients and contingent fees on original returns; IRC 7216 needs written consent before tax-return data drives non-tax marketing. Full matrix: [references/compliance.md](references/compliance.md).

## When practitioners and the data disagree

| Topic | Practitioners say | Data says | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Five-minute response | "9x / 21x / 100x more conversions" | Those multipliers trace to old sales-software studies of contact rates, not signed clients (Caseflow 2026) | Use 5 minutes as the competitive bar (fastest 25%, Hennessey 2025), not a promised multiplier |
| LSA vs Search for lawyers | Search is cheaper per lead | LSA cheaper per signed case in one agency sample | Run both, judge on signed CAC per practice area |
| Social media | YouTube built a book (190 clients, r/CFP 71) | Adviser social CAC $48,836 on average (Kitces 2026) | Long-form education with 6+ months of patience; never judge social as direct response in month 2 |
| Seminars | Dinner seminars are dead money (r/CFP 50) | 75% of users report success, $15,447 CAC (Kitces 2026); one advisor $12k to $15k per class for $45k ARR (r/CFP 111) | Paid-entry, tightly targeted classes with booked lab sessions; track revenue per class |
| Raising fees | Clients will leave | 2/3 of raisers lost none or kept profit stable (Ignition 2025) | Raise yearly, allocate by realization, replace leavers at current rates |
| Bought insurance leads | Leads are a scam | Some agencies run $4 to $7 leads at 1.5% close and $265 to $465 per sale profitably with bundling (r/InsuranceAgent 42) | Small-batch tests, vendor scorecards, net-of-chargeback math |

## Never

- Never let the agent publish amber or red content, or send AI-written outreach in finance, insurance or law, without the named approver signing it.
- Never gate or incentivize reviews. For lawyers a review gift can also breach Rule 7.2(b); for insurers it can be rebating; for advisers a featured review becomes a testimonial.
- Never pay or promise anything for a referral to a lawyer, CPA or adviser before checking Rule 7.2 / 5.4, state board rules (California bans fees solely for referral), SEC promoter rules or state insurance referral-fee law.
- Never confirm someone is a client in a public reply; never disclose matter or return details.
- Never use tax return information to market non-tax services (financial planning, insurance) without IRC 7216 consent.
- Never call or text bought leads without consent records, DNC and litigator scrubs; a single live transfer produced a $25,000 demand (r/InsuranceAgent 160).
- Never claim "specialist", "expert", "certified" or "best" without the licensing basis; never promise outcomes, refunds, verdicts or returns.
- Never let an agency own the firm's domain, website or ad accounts.
- Never invent a benchmark, a case result, a testimonial or a fee the owner did not approve.
