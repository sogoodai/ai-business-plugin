# Production and sourcing as a marketing constraint

Marketing promises (drop dates, restocks, preorder ship dates, "heavyweight", "won't crack", "sustainable") are only as good as production. The agent checks this file before scheduling a drop, scaling ads on a SKU, or writing a quality or green claim.

## Lead time is a marketing variable

- **Average production process: 17 to 18 weeks** in McKinsey's 2026 fashion sample. A brand with three weeks of stock and a three-month reorder cannot scale ads on that SKU without preorders, backorders or a quick-turn local supplier.
- **Local and nearshore capacity as a hedge.** 50% of UK brands surveyed use UK factories for test-and-react orders (UKFT 2025). A higher unit cost can be cheaper than weeks of lost sales or emergency air freight.
- **Season windows.** A jacket run started in June for November arrived late, leaving 50 units to sell before warm weather (r/streetwearstartup, 374-pt thread). "Fashion is seasonal, miss the window and you sit on stock" (r/ClothingStartups list post). Work backward from the selling window: selling start - freight - QC - production - sample approval - sampling.
- **Chinese New Year.** POD and cut-and-sew orders routed through Chinese factories can stall for a month around the holiday; one Etsy store was "nearly ruined" when orders sat in production (r/printondemand, 24-pt thread). Confirm shutdown dates in writing and lengthen processing times in advance.
- **Reorders are planned before they are needed** (r/ClothingStartups list post). The reorder point is per size ([economics.md](economics.md) section 8).

## MOQs and run size

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| UKFT 2025 | 60% of UK average orders are 500 to 1,000 units, 20% are 1 to 500 |
| USFIA 2025 | China is the most competitive major country for low MOQs and flexibility; lower-cost countries favor larger runs |
| Epic Sourcing 2026 (commercial estimate) | China MOQs about 300 to 500 basic tees, 300 to 600 hoodies, 500 to 1,000 jeans; production 30 to 60 days |
| Operators | First runs of 25, 50 or 100 units per style (r/ClothingStartups, 41-pt); makers' first runs of 40 and 50 units after months of sampling (r/streetwearstartup) |

- **MOQs are negotiable.** Accepting the factory's own packaging cut one quote from about 500 to about 200 units (r/ClothingStartups, 15-pt thread). A Turkish factory courting startups advertises 50-unit MOQs and credits the sample fee against production (r/ClothingStartups, 18-pt thread, factory self-promotion).
- **Start with 3 to 5 strong styles, not 20; hero products scale brands** (r/ClothingStartups list post).
- **Bulk before demand is the classic loss:** 1,200 hoodies, 744 unsold (r/ClothingStartups, 27-pt thread).

## Finding and vetting manufacturers

- **Find a comparable brand's factory on ImportYeti** (US import records) and contact it directly. One founder tried Alibaba samples about six times before switching and found more professional, cheaper suppliers; a brand-owner commenter called the tool extremely helpful (r/shopify, 806-pt thread).
- **Alibaba is full of agents and resellers marking up 3 to 4x.** A basic sample should not cost more than $30 to $40; one commenter was quoted $300 for a dress sample (r/ClothingStartups, 87-pt thread).
- **A founder who contacted 50+ Alibaba suppliers paid one and never received goods;** the fix was factory visits and trade fairs (r/ClothingStartups, 15-pt thread).
- **Factories prioritize clarity, not your story:** clear specs, realistic quantities and a believable chance of repeat orders got "faster replies, better samples" (r/ClothingStartups, 15-pt thread).
- **"If a factory says yes to everything, that's a red flag"** (r/ClothingStartups list post).
- **Vet several factories, even ones you will not use,** to learn what is possible and what things cost (r/ClothingStartups, 41-pt thread).

Questions to answer in writing before paying a deposit (r/ClothingStartups, 17-pt thread, workshop promoter; the list holds regardless):
1. What does the sample cost include?
2. What is the real MOQ per style, per color and per size?
3. Who owns the pattern and the tech pack?
4. What happens if the first sample is wrong, and what does a second sample cost?
5. Are trims, labels, packaging and grading included?
6. What is the timeline after sample approval?
7. How are defects handled and credited?
8. What shrinkage and stretch should be expected, and how is it tested?

## Specs, samples and tolerances

- **Write tolerances on the spec sheet,** for example plus or minus 1.5 cm on key measurements. "If a manufacturer tells you 'zero variance,' they are playing with you"; without limits agreed before the deposit, "the medium fits like a small" (r/ClothingStartups, 45-pt thread).
- **The first sample will probably be wrong, and a great sample does not mean great bulk.** Approve lab dips, strike-offs and trims before bulk; fabric rolls vary even from the same mill; run independent tests on bulk; fix small issues in bulk rather than paying for endless samples; budget freight and duties (r/ClothingStartups list post).
- **Tech packs: operators disagree.** "You don't need full tech packs, most manufacturers on Alibaba or in Asia can walk you through" (r/ClothingStartups, 41-pt) vs "you can't cut corners on tech packs and expect clean results" (list post). A manufacturer complains about AI-generated tech packs from clients who do not know GSM or single vs double knit (15-pt). Rule: cut and sew needs a real tech pack with measurements, tolerances and materials; a printed blank needs a print spec (placement, size, colors, method).
- **Learn enough to negotiate.** FIT non-credit courses at $100 to $500 each, many online: "You can't negotiate a fair deal if you have no idea how they are making your clothes" (r/ClothingStartups, 183-pt thread).
- **Embroidery across seams:** have the factory line up stripes or embroider after the piece is sewn so lines meet (r/ClothingStartups, 47-pt thread).
- **Use the samples twice:** inspect quality, then shoot launch photos and an unboxing with them (r/printondemand, 16-pt thread).

## Decoration methods: what to sell on

| Method | Strength | Watch |
|---|---|---|
| Screen print, plastisol | Cheapest per unit at volume, durable, vivid | Setup per color and location; 20% stretch additive on polyester to avoid cracking (r/SCREENPRINTING, 595-pt) |
| Screen print, water-based or discharge | Soft hand | Weak white on dark garments; must be fully cured or it washes out (r/SCREENPRINTING, 447-pt) |
| Puff and specialty inks | Texture, 3D look from few colors | Puff "cracks quicker"; order specialty ink samples first (thermochromic arrived light grey instead of black, 1,140-pt) |
| DTG | No setup, any colors, POD-friendly | Print feel and wash life vary by provider; blends print worse |
| DTF | No setup, works on more fabrics | Hand feel; operators blend DTF with spot colors |
| Embroidery | Premium perception | Not every audience wants embroidered tees ("let us know if you ever print some", 953-pt) |

Blanks, as practitioners see them (opinions, not tests):
- Carhartt is the go-to premium blank for indie screen printers: "the garment holds up as well as the print does" (r/SCREENPRINTING, 724 and 1,389-pt threads).
- Comfort Colors: customers keep requesting it while a shop complains quality has dropped (r/SCREENPRINTING, 143-pt thread).
- Bella+Canvas: thin, soft, drapey; split opinion (r/streetwearstartup, 5,663-pt thread).
- Gildan 18000 through Printify's provider: print show-through made the sweatshirt feel cheap (r/printondemand, 16-pt thread).
- Cotton Heritage MC1087 box tee with Printful DTFlex was one brand's best result after many samples (r/ClothingStartups, 247-pt thread).
- Stanley/Stella blanks from a European provider supported a $65 hoodie (see `ecommerce-brand` pod-handmade.md).

A generic logo on a Gildan or Fruit of the Loom blank at a premium price is the most mocked pattern on r/streetwearstartup (3,519 and 1,006-pt threads). Differentiate with cut, fabric weight, print technique, or details (embossed buttons instead of a chest logo, 62-pt thread).

## Briefing a print shop

Shops read vague, hyped requests as buyers who disappear: "they describe the quantity as a 'bulk order' and that everyone has been DMing them asking when it's going to drop. It's gonna 'blow up'! You never see them again" (r/SCREENPRINTING, 814-pt thread, 12-up comment). A good brief states:
- Exact quantity by size and garment color, and the blank style number.
- Print locations with size and placement measured from the collar or seam; label front and back. One shop printed back art on the front of about 50 shirts before anyone caught it (same thread, 104-up comment).
- Ink colors (Pantone where it matters) and method; whether the shop separates the art.
- Art at final print size, 300 dpi or vector. "Size your art to the size you'd like to print it before bitmapping" (r/SCREENPRINTING, 324-pt thread).
- Deadline and delivery method; proof or first-pull approval before the run.
- Design notes for speed: loose registration art prints faster and safer on big runs (r/SCREENPRINTING, 760-pt thread, 18-up comment); coarse halftones for small shops.

Quote request template: [scripts.md](scripts.md).

Placement merchandising (operators split): big rectangular art works better on the back because it is flat, older buyers like a small front and big back, and one seller "saw a major change" after moving the print to the back (r/streetwearstartup, 3,634-pt thread). Others say back prints vanish under jackets, zip hoodies and hair (r/SCREENPRINTING, 814-pt thread). Rule: small front plus big back on hoodies; test placement on tees with two colorways; always photograph both sides.

## POD and outsourced printing: quality controls

- **Sample every product and color before launch.** Skipping samples to save a week: a forest-green hoodie printed almost black and white text printed off-grey, discovered after 12 customer orders (r/printondemand, 16-pt thread). Printful offered sample discounts up to 25% (same thread).
- **Wash-test before charging premium prices.** A 280gsm tee brand selling at €40 to €45 trialled two POD providers: print particles detached after four washes and prints were ruined by wash six, matching complaints after 3 to 5 washes. It went back to in-house heat-press DTF; those prints still looked new after two years and drove repeat purchases (r/printondemand, 25-pt thread).
- **Avoid known weak combinations:** bright red on black Gildan hoodies at one provider led to $100+ in refunds on about $500 of sales (r/printondemand, 30-pt thread).
- **Budget the complaint rate:** 5 to 7 order-issue emails a week at 50 to 100 sales a week (r/printondemand, 43-pt thread).
- **Check the parcel:** one provider's package showed the $9 wholesale price (r/printondemand, 29-pt thread).
- **Brand the unboxing without inventory:** providers that sew woven neck labels and ship custom mailers; one 7-figure POD operator reports a 32% repeat rate after switching to them (r/printondemand, 35-pt thread). Made-to-order custom bags and hangtags with no prepayment exist too (17-pt thread).

## Printing in-house

- **A tiny shop is cheap to start:** used 6-platen press $150 at auction, conveyor dryer $1,300, inks and emulsion $700, tools $300, rent $65 a month in rural Sweden (r/SCREENPRINTING, 896-pt thread).
- **Halfway step:** print in-house but outsource screen burning and reclaiming (r/SCREENPRINTING, 226-pt thread).
- **Micro runs:** Cricut vinyl stencils instead of burning screens, about 10 prints a stencil at most, water-based ink only (r/SCREENPRINTING, 832-pt thread).
- **Capacity is a body limit on a manual press:** 4,500 pieces in two weeks on a 6-color manual, 1,600 pieces in 2.5 weeks "and my body broke" (r/SCREENPRINTING, 760-pt thread). Commenters advise subcontracting big runs to an automatic press.
- **Shops that fail price too low and miss deliveries;** a brand choosing the cheapest shop risks it folding mid-order (r/SCREENPRINTING, 268-pt thread).

## Sustainability and quality claims: legal limits since 2026

- **EU:** Directive 2024/825 applies from **27 September 2026**. Generic environmental claims ("green", "eco", "ecological", "environmentally friendly") are prohibited unless recognized excellent environmental performance is shown; product claims based on offsetting ("climate neutral") and self-made sustainability labels are targeted (European Commission, 2026).
- **UK:** CMA fashion guidance warns against "green", "sustainable" and "eco-friendly" unless the overall impression is substantiated, and wants qualifying information next to the claim, not behind a link or QR code (CMA, Green Claims Code).
- **US:** FTC Green Guides (pre-2024, possibly outdated; check for updates): broad unqualified claims are hard or impossible to substantiate.

Safe: "Body fabric contains 72% GRS-certified recycled polyester by weight." Risky: "Our sustainable hoodie is good for the planet." (research report, 2026). The agent never writes "eco", "planet friendly", "carbon neutral", "sustainable" or "ethical" without the specific attribute and the certificate or evidence the owner provides.

Handmade and origin claims must be literally true: "No dropshipping, no outsourcing, every shirt cured and QC'd by hand" works for a one-person shop because it is (r/SCREENPRINTING, 724-pt thread).

## IP and compliance traps

- **Trademarks in graphics:** a launch mixing a Mercedes car, the DMV logo and The Atlantic drew warnings (r/streetwearstartup, 972-pt thread); transit signage, band and artist names, characters and brand parodies carry the same risk. Commenters argue a few dozen tees risk "at worst a cease and desist"; the agent flags the risk and lets the owner decide, and never puts protected names in listing titles or tags.
- **Amazon apparel listings need GS1 barcodes,** not cheap resold UPCs; Amazon pulled a seller's entire store after it used a code that belonged to another brand, who supplied proof (r/Entrepreneur, 6,623-pt thread, 553-up warning).
- **Hazmat in the box:** lighters shipped as merch must go ground only, never air or international (r/streetwearstartup, 362-pt thread).
