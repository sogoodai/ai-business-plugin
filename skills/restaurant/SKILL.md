# Restaurant: sell contribution, not covers

Built from 114 practitioner threads with usable tactics (r/restaurantowners, r/foodtrucks, r/smallbusiness, r/barista, r/Coffee, out of 482 scraped), 2025 to 2026 research (National Restaurant Association, Toast, Square, Paytronix, Bloom, ezCater, FLIP, platform fee pages, Google and TikTok documentation, academic menu studies) and the best public restaurant skills (ai-restaurant-claude and pm-claude-skills, MIT). "Operators report" marks a practitioner anecdote; everything else carries its source and year. Sources: [references/sources.md](references/sources.md). Most fee and cost data is US; check the owner's contracts outside the US. The owner's decisions win over this guide.

## How food businesses actually grow

- **Margins are thin, so a sale is not a win until you know its contribution.** US full-service median pretax margin was 2.8% and limited-service 4.0% in 2024; full-service operators under $2M in sales made 1.1% (NRA 2025). Food cost ran 32.0% (full-service) and 32.4% (limited-service), labor 36.5% and 31.7%: prime cost about 68.5% and 64.1%. There is almost no room for unmeasured marketing spend.
- **Regulars are the business.** About 7% of guests can drive up to 50% of order volume (Toast Q1 2026 data). Square counts a regular as 4+ transaction dates a year; regulars spent 6x what one-time visitors did (2025 data, pub. 2026). The growth constraint is the second, third and fourth visit, not reach.
- **Triggered messages beat blasts by an order of magnitude.** One-off blasts returned 0.27% of recipients; new-guest welcome 7.2%, at-risk win-back 6.5% (Bloom, Jan 2025 to Aug 2026, vendor attribution). Build triggers before any newsletter.
- **Google is the storefront.** Menu, hours, attributes, photos, reservation and order links live on the Business Profile, and Google's 2026 Maps and AI features summarize menus and reviews. Accurate structured data beats posting volume.
- **Marketplaces buy discovery at 15 to 30% of the ticket.** Use them to acquire, then move repeat demand to a direct channel with a real reason to switch.
- **Expiring capacity is the cheapest growth.** A Tuesday 7 pm table, an idle kitchen at 10 am and a truck's free Monday cannot be stored. Sell them with named, bounded occasions, catering and private events, never with a discount on the whole week.
- **For trucks and bakeries the constraint is different.** Trucks sell location certainty and event yield; bakeries sell production hours and oven slots. Treat both as capacity problems first.

## First session: ask, then compute

Ask the owner:

1. Format (full-service, fast casual, cafe, bakery, truck, bar, ghost kitchen, caterer), seats or production capacity, open days and hours, country.
2. POS and whether you can get 90 days of item-level sales by daypart and channel, plus the last 3 monthly P&Ls.
3. Food and beverage cost, total labor including payroll taxes and benefits, rent, and how food cost is measured (invoices only, or inventory counts).
4. Marketplace contracts: platform, plan tier, in-app ad and promo spend, menu markup, pickup terms. Reservation platform and its per-cover fees.
5. Direct ordering setup (Toast, Square, ChowNow, Owner, other) and whether Google shows it as the preferred order link.
6. Guest data: list size, what consent they hold, loyalty program and its rules.
7. Google rating, review count, reviews in the last 90 days, and whether anyone replies.
8. Slowest dayparts, private room or catering capacity, current catering and event revenue.
9. Offers they will and will not run; who may change prices; any item that must never change.

Then compute ([references/economics.md](references/economics.md)):

```
prime cost % = (food + beverage cost + total labor incl. taxes and benefits) / net sales
food cost % = (opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory) / food sales
contribution per order = net price - discounts - commission - card fees - food - packaging - incremental labor
item contribution = net selling price - plate cost (with yield) ; total item contribution = item contribution x units
marketplace markup to keep proceeds = in-store price / (1 - commission rate)
direct-order break-even volume = monthly software fee / (commission avoided - processing rate)
RevPASH = revenue / (available seats x open hours)        (dine-in, by daypart)
new-guest CAC = channel spend / first-time purchasing guests from that channel
second-visit rate = guests with a 2nd visit within the window / new guests (same cohort)
regular value = contribution per visit x visits per year
truck event floor = (pitch fee + permit + travel + all labor incl. family) / contribution margin %
```

Compare with [references/benchmarks.md](references/benchmarks.md). Report the three biggest gaps in money, not percentages.

## Decision rules, in order

1. **No acquisition spend until contribution per order is known by channel.** A $40 order on a 30% plan keeps $15.04 after commission and median food cost, before packaging and labor (arithmetic on DoorDash 2026 fees and NRA 2025 food cost). The same order direct keeps about $25.58.
2. **Fix the Google entity first:** exact primary category, hours and special hours, attributes, text menu with prices, reservation and order links, preferred menu source and preferred order link. Check where the order button really leads: guests report Google sending them to Uber Eats when they meant to order direct.
3. **Build the four triggers before any campaign:** first-visit welcome, second-visit nudge timed to the format's return window, lapse by each guest's own rhythm, milestone at visit 4. Hold out 10 to 15% to measure (Paytronix 2026).
4. **Engineer the menu on contribution dollars.** A 38% food-cost bestseller that leaves $12 a plate beats a 25% item that leaves $4 and rarely sells. Run the matrix every quarter ([references/menu-and-pricing.md](references/menu-and-pricing.md)).
5. **Raise prices item by item, never across the board.** Toast's Sept 2026 data: coffee +7.1% year over year, beer +2.8%, burgers +2.2%, wings +0.7%. Aggregate elasticity is about -0.76 (SSRN working paper 2026). Fold small surcharges into dish prices instead of adding fees.
6. **Marketplaces: price to protect proceeds, then migrate.** Mark up app prices or choose the tier deliberately; insert a concrete direct-order offer in every bag ($10, no minimum, tested at checkout). Audit payouts monthly for promos you never approved.
7. **Fill slow periods with named, bounded occasions** (date, seats, price, own booking link), and with meetings, groups and partners who bring their own audience. Never a blanket discount.
8. **Run catering and private events as a B2B funnel:** dedicated page with packages, minimums, lead times and dates; respond fast; signed agreement before staffing; follow up on the buyer's meeting cadence.
9. **Reviews: one neutral ask to every guest, reply to every negative within 48 hours.** Half of sampled restaurants replied to none of their last 20 reviews (Suff Digital 2026). Never gate, never incentivize, never ask for Yelp reviews.
10. **Creators: local audience, fixed comp menu, usage rights, trackable code.** Judge on matched bookings or orders, not views. Amplify the best video as an ad.
11. **Paid ads last,** on explicit intent first (Google: cuisine + area, catering, private dining, brand), split by economic job, judged on reservations, orders or matched checks. Depth: `paid-ads`.

## Prime cost and the numbers that matter

- **Compare with the right cohort, not the internet's 60%.** Profitable full-service operators ran 34.2% labor vs 42.9% for loss-making ones; limited-service 30.0% vs 34.1% (NRA 2025). Software blogs' 55 to 60% prime-cost "targets" use inconsistent definitions.
- **Value positioning can justify higher food cost.** One owner moved from a 27% to a 34% food-cost target to give fresh food and generous portions instead of shrinking plates (operators report). Judge it on contribution per cover and repeat rate, not the percentage.
- **Watch revenue per seat hour by daypart,** not average daily sales. A regular camping 4 hours at a four-top on Friday costs the covers you could have sat; a cafe reclaims the table as soon as demand builds.
- **Scratch items hide labor.** An operator selling egg rolls "barely makes money" after wrapping, frying, cooling and storage (operators report). Cost labor minutes on anything you promote.
- **Buy whole, portion in house when the saving pays for the labor:** chicken randoms at about $1.40/lb vs $7 to $9/lb pre-portioned (operators report).

## Delivery apps and direct ordering in one screen

- **US marketplace commissions (Nov 2026):** DoorDash 15/25/30% by plan, pickup 6% with pickup price parity; Uber Eats 20/25/30%, pickup 7% with validated in-store pricing, self-delivery 15%; Grubhub marketing 5/15/20% plus delivery from 10%. Direct: Square online 3.3% + $0.30 (entry) or 2.9% + $0.30; ChowNow 2.95% + $0.29 plus $249 to $449 a month; Owner.com $249/mo + 5% or $499/mo flat, and the guest pays a 5% fee.
- **Markup that keeps proceeds equal:** 15% commission needs +17.6%, 25% needs +33.3%, 30% needs +42.9%. Operators report a $100 in-store family meal listed at $150 on apps with "call the store for a $50 discount" in the description, and still selling.
- **Break-even for direct software:** $499 / (25% - 3%) = about $2,268 of shifted orders a month.
- **What moved guests direct:** a $10, no-minimum coupon redeemable only on the restaurant's own app, then points toward free meals (a 467-upvote comment recommended website-only coupons in carryout bags). What failed: three months of QR "skip the fees" flyers in every Uber Eats bag, almost zero switched (operators report).
- **Before you advertise "cheaper direct":** compare the final checkout total for the same basket, check direct delivery covers the guest's address, and test the coupon end to end.

Full math, tier tests and reservation-platform fees: [references/delivery-and-direct.md](references/delivery-and-direct.md).

## Menu engineering in one screen

1. Pull 90 days of item sales, net price and plate cost **with yield** (edible-portion cost = as-purchased cost / yield %).
2. Classify within each category: popularity threshold = 70% of an equal share (0.7 / number of items); margin threshold = weighted average contribution. Stars, Plowhorses, Puzzles, Dogs (Kasavana and Smith).
3. Act: feature Stars, re-cost or carefully reprice Plowhorses, reposition and rename Puzzles, cut or convert Dogs, and cut anything that needs an ingredient used nowhere else.
4. Design: drop currency symbols (guests spent more with numeral-only prices, Yang, Kimes and Sessarego 2009), use specific descriptions, keep entry-price anchors. Eye tracking found guests read menus like a book with no "sweet spot", and skip the bottom corners (Yang 2012).
5. Change one thing at a time and watch units, mix and contribution per check for 4 to 6 comparable weeks.

Procedure, plate-cost sheet, design evidence and price-increase tactics: [references/menu-and-pricing.md](references/menu-and-pricing.md).

## Loyalty and regulars

- **Loyalty guests return near 30% vs a 7% baseline** (Toast Q1 2026, self-selected). **90-day active rate** is the metric: 66 to 72% in the strongest segments (beverage, snack, sandwich, Mexican), under 50% in casual dining (Paytronix 2026, 2025 data).
- **Design for visit 4.** Behavior changes sharply once members reach four visits (Paytronix 2026). First reward within reach in the first month.
- **Match reward to format:** casual cashback held 24 to 26% 90-day retention; in fine dining an item reward held 20% vs 13% for cashback (Toast 2026). More than half of diners quit a program because rewards were too hard to earn.
- **Let regulars redeem their usual order.** A guest spending $25 to $30 four times a week never returned after the punch card excluded the extra protein (operators report).
- **Regulars get early access and recognition, not permanent discounts.** Plan for losing about 20% of regulars a year to moves, diets and new openings; a 20-year, three-restaurant operator targets 25% new regulars a year for 5% growth (operators report).

Program design, CRM triggers and recovery: [references/retention-and-loyalty.md](references/retention-and-loyalty.md).

## Slow nights, catering and creators in one screen

- **Slow night product:** "Tuesday Pasta Night: three courses, $39, 6 to 9 pm, 40 seats only", priced from contribution, own link, compared with the same night's 8-week baseline. Threshold offers beat percentages: "Spend $75, save $5" lifted AOV to 50% above the threshold (Toast case 2026).
- **Cheap capacity fillers (operators report):** closing at 10 instead of 9 doubled 8 to 9 pm traffic; free morning meeting space before an 11 am open kept a third of attendees for lunch; a back room rented at $25 for 3 hours sold full-price food every morning; gift cards not redeemable 11:30 am to 1 pm.
- **Catering:** restaurants with catering grew 5.1% vs 3.3% (ezCater 2025, vendor); average corporate order $420 for 25 people (2026). Truck operators report 50 to 60% catering margins vs 10 to 20% on street stops, minimums of $2,000 for 3 hours or $1,200 for 2 hours + $400 an hour, and price each date by the profit it must earn ($750 on a June Saturday vs $500 on a November Wednesday).
- **Creators:** nano $100 to $500 a post, micro $500 to $2,500, usage rights +30 to 50% (Later 2026, vendor rate card). A $500 post must bring about 12 matched new parties at a $60 check to break even. Check audience cities first; fix the comp menu.

Depth: [references/demand-and-events.md](references/demand-and-events.md).

## Trucks, bakeries and cafes in one screen

- **Trucks:** expected buyers = attendees x purchase rate / food vendors. 500 attendees gave about 150 buyers, split over 4+ trucks at $8 = $250 (operators report). Anchor a brewery or office stop, sell the rest as private events, and require a written floor or guarantee ("$500 to show, guaranteed"). Shade and seating sell: a $189 awning took a market Sunday from about $1,050 to $1,700.
- **Bakeries:** production hours are the constraint. Rank products by contribution per oven or labor hour, presell drops ("preorder by Thursday 6 pm"), take payment up front, add wholesale accounts priced after delivery and stales.
- **Cafes:** speed and habit. Clarify ambiguous orders before making them, differentiate the menu from the chains, and look at wholesale roasting, which carries 70% of one operator's net profit.

## When practitioners and the data disagree

| Topic | Practitioners say | Data says | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery apps | "Slimiest company", or "$1M a year on Uber Eats" | 15 to 30% commission; marketplace promo ROAS excludes commission | Price to protect proceeds, test tiers, migrate repeat guests |
| Prime cost target | 60% or bust | 2024 medians 68.5% and 64.1%; profitable cohort labor 34.2% / 30.0% | Compare with format and profitable cohort |
| Food truck events | Festivals are where the money is | Events were the top acquisition source for 37% of trucks (FLIP 2026) | Accept only events that clear a written revenue floor or guarantee |
| Loyalty | Stamp cards failed for a passerby-heavy boba shop | Members return ~30% vs 7% | Loyalty helps where repeat is plausible; pilot with a holdout |
| Menu "sweet spot" | Put money items top right | Eye tracking found no sweet spot (Yang 2012) | Feature by placement first in category, test on sales mix |
| Review replies | Ignore ranting one-stars and let new reviews bury them | 50.3% reply to none; Google recommends replies | Short factual reply to every negative; no arguing |
| Price raises | Small raises across the board fixed stagnant cash (+25,000 in two months) | Only 18% of diners called price hikes justified (Toast 2026) | Raise selectively, keep an entry anchor, attach visible value |

## Never

- Never gate, incentivize or buy reviews, run staff review quotas, or ask reviewers to name staff. Never ask for Yelp reviews.
- Never offer compensation in exchange for changing or deleting a review.
- Never send marketing texts or emails without consent; transactional order texts are a separate system.
- Never put prohibited solicitation inside marketplace orders beyond what the platform's terms allow; check the contract before inserts and markups.
- Never discount the whole week to fix one daypart, or judge a marketplace promo on in-app ROAS.
- Never prep a truck event or office stop against promised attendance; prep against paid orders and a known buffer.
- Never invent a benchmark, a dietary or allergen claim, an offer the owner has not approved, or a guest quote.
- Never change a signature item, recipe or portion without the owner; regulars notice.
