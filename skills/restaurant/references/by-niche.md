# By niche

What changes by format. Shared rules live in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md); numbers and sources in [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md).

## Food trucks, carts and stands

**The model:** sell location certainty and event yield. Events were the top acquisition source for 37% of trucks; 58.5% prefer food festivals, 51.2% music festivals, 47.6% farmers markets; peaks 5 to 8 pm (63.6%) and lunch (55.7%) (FLIP 2026, vendor survey). Operators who make money build a base of recurring stops and private bookings, then cherry-pick events.

**Schedule mix that works (operators report):**

- **Anchor + private:** a brewery seven days a week plus private events and about five chosen festivals, about $30,000 a month gross with two employees; a beer garden truck that never moved did $400,000+ a year with four staff; one weekly brewery plus private catering; three or four proven gigs a week for $15,000 a month and almost double margins, leaving room for lucrative last-minute bookings.
- **Breweries and taprooms without kitchens:** same truck, same weekday, so guests know. One pizza truck's busiest day is Monday and slowest Saturday. Make the partner's promo graphics yourself so they match your brand. Never cancel a recurring brewery date on the day for a better gig; breweries drop trucks for it.
- **Office lunch:** the organizer circulates a paid preorder link the afternoon before, three or four meals with pickup windows, orders close around 9 am; paid orders decide go, cancel or guaranteed minimum. Book office stops on your weakest lunch day (about $1,000 downtown in Chicago) and add a condo stop on the way home.
- **Uncatered crews:** film-studio construction crews eat at noon and get $10 to $12 for lunch; flyers committing to "every Friday in May" plus advance orders (suggestion).
- **Follow a group:** trucks that follow a moving customer group (a unit, a crew, a circuit) keep queues; regulars tell you where they will be next.
- **School family events:** carnivals, Halloween and back-to-school nights where families stay through a meal; one operator reports 8 to 10% outreach-to-booking and $800 to $1,500 an hour, another sold 14 meals at a 200-staff school.
- **Join rotation lists:** find businesses that rotate trucks (local truck Facebook groups, places trucks park regularly) and ask for their waiting list.
- **No-fee recurring events** in exchange for working all opening hours: one truck sold out twice on the first day, more than a usual week.

**Events to refuse or protect against:**

- Ask: expected attendance, dwell time, seating, number of food vendors and last year's count, placement on the walking route, fees and permits, power and trash. One block from the main action, or behind 10 to 15 trucks on a five-minute walk, kills sales.
- Apartment and HOA events: promised 100 attendees, delivered a handful, 100% disappointment for one commenter. Require a guarantee or prepaid catering.
- Withdraw when organizers add competing trucks late. Operators say so plainly ([scripts.md](scripts.md)).
- Match the menu to dwell time: stays of 1 to 3 hours buy drinks, sweets, sides and handhelds, not full meals; without seating sell what eats standing.
- Prep against paid orders plus a known walk-up buffer, never against promised headcount.

**Menu and line:** 2 to 3 mains and a few sides keep the line moving (a BBQ truck does 100 to 120 orders an event, 250 at busy ones); end-of-month premium specials (beef ribs, oxtail); a menu one or two people can run with frozen stock that keeps beats a four-person scratch operation on bad days; slicing visible at the window with samples handed out there. A tea truck's customizable menu at $3 / $4 / $5 for 16 / 24 / 32 oz with free fruit garnish and a named off-menu "peachy palmer" sold best.

**Line comfort and throughput:** a $189 awning took a market Sunday from $1,000 to $1,100 to $1,700; a fan tent added just under 50% for another stand; a KDS lets groups order fast and wait away from the order line; text guests when food is ready ("Hey Siri, text [number], your food is ready"); card tipping on the POS paid back within a season.

**Equipment choices:** a tent setup at about $10,000 vs a $50,000+ truck adds capacity for overlapping catering (disputed); a pizza trailer built for 200 people an hour at a headquarters skips the POS and delivers in bulk.

**Truck parks** need an anchor bar with beer and wine, truck commitments before construction, and an audience bigger than the trucks' own; launch with a guaranteed minimum, then free, then a percentage (about 10%) a year later (operators report).

**Before buying a concept:** study which concepts are for sale locally; recurring ones signal weak demand, not bad cooks.

## Bakeries

**The model:** production hours and oven slots are the constraint. Pull demand forward with preorders, stabilize with wholesale, and sell walk-in from a planned batch. No credible 2025 to 2026 benchmark for the channel split exists; do not invent one (research report).

- **Preorder drops:** "Saturday croissant box: 6 pastries, preorder by Thursday 6 pm, pickup Saturday 8 to 11 am. We make only the preordered quantity plus a limited walk-in batch." It is an inventory tool and a measurable marketing event at once.
- **Take payment up front on every advance order.** A $210 unpaid cookie and brownie order was cancelled at 1:55 pm for a 1 pm pickup, then reviewed badly (813 upvotes).
- **Wholesale:** price by contribution per production hour after the wholesale discount, delivery, packaging and stales. Broaden past restaurants: pizza dough, ethnic breads and bulk pastry dough to pizzerias, smaller bakeries, ethnic grocers, nursing homes, county jails and assisted living (one bakery: restaurants were only 20 to 25% of customers). Re-pitch good prospects every 12 weeks; don't let one account dominate before you diversify.
- **Side revenue from the same kitchen:** renting commercial kitchen time 24/7 was "the real money maker", booked 18 months ahead; compound butters and freeze-dried foods brought $5,000 to $8,000 a month in cash sales (one former owner).
- **Seasonal gift sellers:** presell holiday orders in summer, or land one wholesale account that covers rent.
- **Leftovers:** route unsold food through a nightly shelter pickup partner; giving it to guests at close trained them to wait for free bread.
- **Aroma and samples:** one loaf always in the oven, warm samples on the tray.
- **Custom and event work:** grazing tables look abundant when packed tightly with height (salami roses, a meat "river", folded cheese, crackers and nuts in the gaps): about $60 of food fed a 30-person shower (23,599 upvotes). Price the labor, not the ingredients.
- **Reviews:** say only what you know. Disclose a reviewer's real relationship (rejected applicant) calmly; never claim you have no record of a cash customer.

## Cafes and coffee shops

**The model:** habit and speed. Visit frequency is weekly or daily, tickets are small, and the counter queue is the conversion funnel. Coffee prices rose 7.1% in a year (Toast Sept 2026), more than most items, so local competitors have likely moved too. Check their prices before assuming a raise is out of line.

- **Differentiate from the chains on the menu:** a New Orleans-themed shop sells chicory coffee and cafe au lait that Starbucks and Tim Hortons don't; boba added to an established coffee shop was "a huge hit"; tasting flights in a science theme; premium bottle upgrades for boba.
- **Speed:** a self-serve drip station with honor payment (cups, lids, a cash mug, a notepad to log sizes) won guests escaping the Tim Hortons queue for two years and outweighed underpayment; one shop used a card reader instead of cash.
- **Ordering scripts prevent remakes and bad reviews:** macchiato and americano clarifications ([scripts.md](scripts.md)); a "Starbucks" POS modifier on a caramel latte so staff call out "macchiato". Avoid ambiguous cup labels: "whole" read as "whale" produced a fat-shaming Yelp review.
- **Seating policy:** laptop guests and outside drinks need a written, polite policy; strict enforcement of an outside-coffee ban lost a party of nine. Test a disclosed carry-in fee ($1 sign wording in scripts) or a receipt-sharing deal with the neighboring coffee truck before banning. Measure guest count and check before and after, not just coffee sales.
- **Wholesale roasting** is where many coffee businesses make their profit: one operator's roastery wholesale is 70% of net profit; another cafe plus roastery with 8 accounts runs $1.8M at 19% profit.
- **Location:** a 10-year shop chose its highest-rent site because 3 million tourists a year pass and vacationers care less about price.
- **Recruiting first guests:** business cards with a code ("this one's on me"), a filtered street handout ("Do you like coffee?" then a free-coffee coupon and ten seconds on what is different), boutique receipt deals, community and fandom events, hot-day iced offers.

## Bars and pubs

- **Beverage is the margin:** 70 to 85% beverage margin vs 60 to 72% food (uncited); raise drinks first. Resist reps pushing $30,000 of back-bar bottles nobody drinks.
- **Keep the town beer cheap,** $3 Lone Star in a Texas town bar.
- **Events need rules:** trivia charge when teams sit on seats with one drink; distributor-supported tastings; bar-only food item.
- **Loyalty is weak here:** bar and grill loyalty active rate fell 13% and nearly 3 in 4 new members did not return in 90 days (Paytronix 2026). Lean on events and regular nights.
- **Counter service for pub menus:** one operator converted three pub-style businesses to counter service in 2014 to 2019, servers became counter staff and bartenders, sales grew (disputed by others).
- **Ads:** alcohol ads target 21+ in the US; check each platform's alcohol policy and local law.
- **Aroma sells across the room:** a garlic pizza walked the long way through a busy bar, or an empty pizza box of sauteed garlic carried through a partner bar, brought orders within minutes.

## Ghost kitchens and delivery-first concepts

- **Marketplace visibility is the business,** so tier tests, menu photos, ratings and in-app promo math matter most ([delivery-and-direct.md](delivery-and-direct.md)).
- **Build a direct channel from day one:** a direct site linked from Google, a concrete first-order code in every bag, reorder in one tap.
- **Guard the brand:** search platforms for unauthorized listings and price errors.
- **Slot caps** protect quality on rush nights; ratings fall faster than they rise.

## Caterers and event food businesses

- Two funnels: corporate (repeat, cadence, reliability) and social (weddings, showers, birthdays; referral and venue driven). Separate pages, packages and CRM.
- Price by the date's required profit; rental fee plus per-person with a minimum; signed agreement before staffing ([demand-and-events.md](demand-and-events.md)).
- At-home services: a seafood truck with 40 to 45% food costs was advised to sell home seafood boils at ingredients plus a service fee.
- Cook aromatics on arrival so guests smell the food (private chef).

## Full-service restaurants

- **Capacity:** RevPASH by daypart, last seating posted, reservations on the cheapest Google-linked platform.
- **Tableside tech can backfire:** guests read servers on iPods and iPhones as texting; a resort-town restaurant dropped handhelds within a month, and a diner traced falling covers to Toast handhelds and lost paper receipts with handwritten tips. Present an itemized paper check before the card machine. A rural restaurant succeeded with handhelds by programming menus for the fewest taps.
- **QR menus as overflow only:** physical menus by default; QR for the fast-changing beer list with a paper backup.
- **Off-menu requests:** print the policy ("We're pleased to offer modifications but we are unable to do off-menu items due to limited availability of ingredients. Let us know if you have any allergies."), train redirect scripts, or warn that off-setup dishes add at least 25 minutes (most guests then decline). A poached-egg favor "utterly destroyed the flow of the kitchen".
- **Splitting fees** feel fair when they include something: $7 to share a schnitzel with an extra soup or salad.
- **Secret shoppers:** friends or family as unannounced guests to see service the owner never sees.
- **Changing neighborhoods:** when local demographics stop fitting the core product, a new concept can beat added menu items. A beef-burger franchise owner converted to fried chicken for $250,000 and sold for $750,000 (operators report).
- **Selling the business:** a genuine closure announcement that mentioned the sale reached buyers through local news within 24 hours after years of failed listings.
