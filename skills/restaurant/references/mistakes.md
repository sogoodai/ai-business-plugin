# Expensive mistakes

Two tables: what the data shows (measured, platform terms, or arithmetic on them) and what practitioners report losing.

## From the data

| Mistake | What it costs | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Celebrating marketplace sales growth | A $40 order on a 30% plan keeps $15.04 after commission and median food cost vs about $25.58 direct (DoorDash 2026, NRA 2025) | Report contribution by channel; migrate repeat guests direct |
| Judging in-app promos on platform ROAS | ROAS excludes commission and fees; a "2.13 ROAS" left one operator owing Uber money | Recompute every promo after commission, food and packaging |
| Copying a 60% prime-cost target | 2024 medians were about 68.5% (full-service) and 64.1% (limited-service); targets online use other definitions (NRA 2025) | Compare with your format and the profitable cohort (labor 34.2% / 30.0%) |
| Weekly newsletter as the main CRM | One-off blasts returned 0.27% vs 7.2% for welcome and 6.5% for win-back (Bloom 2025 to 2026) | Build the four triggers first |
| Counting attributed visits as caused visits | Members return ~30% vs 7% baseline, but members self-select (Toast 2026) | 10 to 15% holdout (Paytronix 2026) |
| Measuring loyalty by enrollment | Casual dining 90-day active under 50%; bar and grill -13%, nearly 3 in 4 new members gone in 90 days (Paytronix 2026) | Track 90-day active rate and visits vs holdout |
| Rewards too hard to earn | More than half of diners quit a program for that reason (Toast 2026) | First reward within one normal visit cycle; design for visit 4 |
| Uniform price increases | Item price moves ranged from +0.7% (wings) to +7.1% (coffee) in a year (Toast 2026); only 18% of diners call hikes justified | Item-by-item raises, keep an entry anchor, attach visible value |
| Deleting a high food-cost bestseller | A 38% item leaving $12 a plate beats a 25% item leaving $4 that barely sells (research report) | Rank on total contribution dollars |
| Gated or incentivized reviews, staff quotas, "mention my name" | Google can remove reviews, block new ones, unpublish the rating or show a public warning; Tripadvisor bans the same | One neutral ask to everyone |
| One "leave us a review" automation for every platform | Yelp says businesses should not ask for reviews (published guidance, possibly outdated) | Ask on Google and Tripadvisor; passive "Find us on Yelp" only |
| Ignoring reviews | 50.3% of sampled restaurants replied to none of their last 20 (Suff Digital 2026) | Reply to every negative within 48 hours |
| Menu only as an image or PDF | Google's AI menu extraction needs review; AI search uses indexable text (Google 2026) | Text menu on the site and in GBP, preferred menu source set |
| Using $2.05 CPC / 8.05% CVR as CAC | "Conversion" may be a call or a menu click (WordStream 2026) | Track first-time purchasing guests per channel |
| Discounting the whole week for one slow daypart | Subsidizes guests who would have paid full price | Named, dated, capped offer on the slow block only |
| Paying OpenTable for your own Google traffic | About $1 per Google-referred cover in some agreements (operators report) | Read the agreement; set the cheapest provider as Google's reservation link |
| Waiting until opening day to build demand | Launch cases built 1,155 covers or 1,200 signups before opening (agency cases 2025 to 2026) | Start 4 to 6 weeks out with a list and previews |

## From practitioners (operators report)

| Mistake | What it cost | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| QR "skip the fees" flyers in every Uber Eats bag with no concrete offer | Almost zero switched in three months | $10 no-minimum direct-only code, tested at checkout |
| Untested direct coupon | Guest tried a $10 website coupon and it failed | Test every code end to end before printing |
| Promising cheaper direct delivery outside the delivery radius | Guest could not switch | Check coverage and final checkout totals first |
| Not checking the marketplace payout detail | Two campaigns the owner said they never approved ("$0 delivery fee", "Sponsored Listing") cut payouts | Monthly audit of Marketing > Campaigns |
| Never searching marketplaces for yourself | An unauthorized Uber Eats listing took orders at 20% below menu prices | Search all apps quarterly; demand removal |
| Paying a revenue share for "missed calls" | Pitch said hundreds missed; reality 4 a week, all while closed; fee 4.85% of every order | Audit call logs first |
| Taking a free event on an organizer's promise of a "huge line" | Lost $180 with an extra hire instead of making a couple of thousand at the usual spot | Written floor or guarantee; count displaced revenue |
| Accepting apartment and HOA events on promised headcount | Wasted food, fuel, propane and travel; 100% disappointing for one operator | $500 guarantee or prepaid catering |
| Not asking how many food vendors are booked | 500 attendees split across 4+ trucks gave about $250 in sales; wages alone were twice that | Ask the vendor count and last year's; withdraw if it grows late |
| Ignoring family labor in event math | $300 profit for 70 to 80 family hours; the business closed | Cost every hour at a wage |
| Buying a $1,500 festival spot without the break-even | Needs about $4,500 sales; rain or heat adds waste | Compute the floor before applying |
| Picking a truck spot on passing traffic | 2,500 cars a day, about 0.5 customers a day | Test visibility and parking; trial before committing |
| Canceling recurring brewery dates for a better gig | Brewery dropped both trucks before their slow season | Treat anchors as contracts |
| Unpaid advance orders | $210 bakery order cancelled at pickup time, then a bad review | Payment up front on every preorder |
| Bought reviews | 20 Google reviews vanished in two months and rankings fell below the start | Ask every real guest |
| "We have no record of you being a customer" in a reply | Readers know cash guests leave no record | State the known relationship calmly and report |
| Server complaining about a regular within earshot | Regular spending about $8,580 a year walked | Manager serves him; fix tip-out instead |
| Punch card that excludes the usual order | Lost a guest spending $25 to $30 four times a week | Redeem the usual order |
| Charging for expected condiments | Lost a monthly Reuben regular over a $2 dressing | Fold extras into the dish price |
| Pricing components below the dish | $25 fajitas rebuilt for $14 from sides | Price custom builds at or above the dish |
| Accommodating any off-menu request | One poached-egg favor "destroyed the flow of the kitchen" | Printed policy, redirect scripts, 25-minute warning |
| Replacing signature scratch items with frozen | Neighborhood favorite became a "ghost town" | Protect signatures; cut elsewhere |
| Raising staple prices in a tourist town | Locals could no longer come 2 to 3 times a week | Staples for locals, showpieces for tourists |
| Strictly enforcing an outside-drink ban | Lost a party of nine and others in a weekend | Measure guest count and check first; carry-in fee or two-options script |
| Tableside handhelds that look like phones | Guests left before being seated; dropped within a month | Paper menus and an itemized paper check by default |
| Undated downloadable menus | Daily calls for items gone 2+ years; guests expecting 2014 prices | Date every menu; one permanent URL |
| Giving leftovers to guests at closing | Guests waited around for free bread | Nightly shelter pickup partner |
| Complaining to a tour guide in front of the group | Lost 24 to 60 guests a day | Handle logistics privately |
| Letting a third party run events in your space for free | Guests spent about $3 each; staff burden and angry calls | Paid venue agreement |
| Inviting influencers with an open tab and no location check | Warning of a $500 order; non-local followers | Fixed comp menu, audience-city screenshot, code |
| Paying for social "promotions" judged by likes | $150, 3,500 likes, zero visitors | Pay only for trackable local outcomes |
| Building a truck park before signing trucks | Restrooms, seating and hookups built; no trucks, no customers | Commitments and an anchor bar first |
| Taking beverage reps' advice on stock | Warning of $30,000 in bottles nobody drinks | Stock on sales evidence |
