# Getting guests: slow nights, events, catering, partners, creators, Google

Restaurant-specific angles only. Generic GBP, citations and review policy depth: `local-seo`. Email, SMS and loyalty mechanics: `email-sms-loyalty`. Ad account structure: `paid-ads`.

## 1. Slow nights and dayparts

**Find the hole first.** RevPASH or sales by weekday x hour for 8 weeks ([economics.md](economics.md)). Fill the worst two blocks, not "weekdays".

**Rules for a slow-night product:**

- Fixed date, fixed proposition, bounded capacity, own booking link and code, a reason to bring someone. "Tuesday Pasta Night: three courses, $39, 6 to 9 pm, 40 seats only." "Thursday Wine Table: four pours, four small plates, one seating at 7 pm, reservations required." Set the price from contribution, not from these examples (research report 2026).
- Count cannibalization: guests who would have come anyway at full price. Compare the night's covers and contribution with the same night's 8-week baseline.
- Recurring beats one-off. A repeated named night becomes an occasion, gets cheaper to produce, and builds a retargeting audience.
- Discount only the slow inventory. A threshold offer works better than a percentage: "Spend $75, save $5" brought almost 200 orders in a month with AOV 50% above the threshold (Toast case, 2026). Set the threshold above current AOV.
- Event-specific creative and booking paths produced the cleanest attribution in 2025 to 2026 hospitality cases; there is no reliable "events add X%" benchmark (research report).

## 2. Catering and private events

Catering is a B2B sale with its own buyer, cadence and economics. Restaurants with catering grew revenue 5.1% vs 3.3% for the market (ezCater 2025, correlation, vendor). Corporate orders averaged $420 for 25 people in 2026, and 43% of organizations run recurring meal programs (ezCater).

**The catering page** (research report 2026): headcount ranges, 2 to 4 named packages, per-person or tray prices, dietary handling and labels, pickup and delivery radius, minimum order, lead time, cancellation policy, invoicing and payment terms, short inquiry form. Private events: seated and standing capacity, minimum spend or room fee, sample menus, AV, accessibility, bookable dates. Make rooms and table minimums bookable online if your platform allows (Tock users report more direct event bookings).

**Get found:** a truck whose catering is 75% of revenue credits being searchable on Google and Yelp; walk-up guests rarely book catering. Put "We Cater!" on the order side of the truck. Pages for "[cuisine] catering [city]" and "private dining [area]". Use Google Ads on catering and private-dining terms with their own conversion event.

**Find buyers:**

- Offices, event venues, schools, hotels, coworking, wedding planners, studios within delivery range. Short pitch with two package examples and a sample order ([scripts.md](scripts.md)).
- Conference organizers advertising nearby events on Facebook: several private events from simple outreach (operators report).
- A regional business association with weekly owner meetings: the fee paid back in company events (operators report).
- "Book us for private parties" posted in the corporate holiday season brought several corporate and birthday bookings at once; it does not always work (operators report).
- Wedding venues that refer you: one truck booked 31 weddings for 2026 mostly from venue referrals plus a regular Friday night at a wine store.
- Corporate clients: build a fixed-price lunch menu around their budget with advance orders.

**Sell it:**

- Respond fast; planners hire the first credible answer. Speed-to-lead depth: `local-services-marketing`.
- Price by the profit the date must earn, then add costs: one truck targets $750 profit on a June Saturday evening vs $500 on a November Wednesday lunch, then adds food and labor by headcount and hours, plus about $4 a mile round trip. Charge a rental fee plus a per-meal price with a minimum; a $1,000 birthday on a date that could have held a wedding at two to three times that is a loss (operators report).
- Minimums operators use: $2,000 for up to three hours (50%+ margin); $1,200 for two hours plus $400 each extra hour; "$500 to show, guaranteed", sales credited against it.
- Host-paid events: agree headcount, per-head price and menu, prep only that. Guest-paid: narrow menu chosen with the host, aim to sell out, host signs for any shortfall with a card on file.
- Price objections: compare per person with delivered fast food including fees and tip. One truck quotes $24 a head for 100 guests against about $23.39 a head for McDonald's delivery and says "It is a licensed restaurant that comes to you."
- Paperwork: "Here is the LOA for you to review. Once it's signed and returned, I can get the event staffed." Calendar status PLACEHOLDER until signed, then CONFIRMED (operators report).

**Keep the buyer:** the person who orders is not the person who eats. They value on-time delivery, labeled dietary options, simple invoices and packaging. Put them in a separate CRM segment with a "next meeting" reminder on their own cadence (research report). Follow-up wording: [scripts.md](scripts.md).

**Mobile bars and add-ons:** a mobile beer trailer added a certified bartender and a premium cocktail package when bookers asked for cocktails and was booked almost every weekend within months (1,641 upvotes). Popcorn and boba shops sell "party bars" for showers, weddings and birthdays.

## 3. Partnerships that bring their own audience

- **Neighbor receipts:** a cafe gave a free drink with a food order to shoppers showing a same-day receipt from nearby boutiques, which put promo cards in their bags; Fridays filled and boutique staff came too. A truck with no drinks gave $20+ buyers a coupon for a free drink at the neighboring liquor store; 9 in 10 redeemed and bought more there (operators report).
- **Reciprocal coupons with caps:** "Spend $10 or more here, get 10% off at [partner], up to $6."
- **Real estate welcome packs** for new renters and buyers; **campsites and RV parks** for remote cafes ("with your half-off latte, want a breakfast sandwich?").
- **Community audiences:** K-pop cupsleeve events at boba shops (goodies only with a drink); local artists and vendors hosted for 3 years built enough regulars that traffic held without posting.
- **Word-of-mouth hubs:** invite hairdressers, dog walkers and taxi drivers to try the menu; give DoorDash drivers a small sample (one driver became a fan, reviewed and brought friends).
- **Other owners:** a Thai restaurant owner posts meals at other local restaurants on his closed days, with their owners, and holds a 500-meal community giveaway every 3 to 4 months that neighbors now contribute to.
- **Manufacturer reps:** ask suppliers to feature you on their social channels and site.
- **Tour guides:** handle logistics privately. A manager who complained in front of a 12-person group lost a tour company worth 24 to 60 guests a day.
- **Third-party events in your space** need a paid venue agreement. An inherited monthly wedding arrangement paid no fee and guests spent about $3 each.
- **Sponsorships:** start at $25 instead of $500, ask the school or team to film in your shop and post it, and judge before renewing. Route other donation requests through a form with lead time and relevance rules (operators report the requests stopped).
- **Press:** a critic's visit turned into a second story when the owner showed $3.10 of a $10 order going to DoorDash.

## 4. Creators and food influencers

**Economics:** rate cards put nano creators (1K to 10K) at $100 to $500 a post and micro (10K to 100K) at $500 to $2,500, Reels about 20 to 30% above feed posts, usage rights or whitelisting +30 to 50% (Later 2026, vendor, no methodology). Many local food creators work for a comped meal. Break-even: fee / value per new party ([economics.md](economics.md): a $500 post needs about 12 matched new parties at a $60 check).

**Screen:**

- Audience location first: ask for an insights screenshot of top cities. Followers outside the trade area are worth nothing to a restaurant.
- Engagement quality: real comments from locals, not emoji walls. Paid LinkedIn "promotions" from 10K to 100K profiles drew 3,500 likes and 500 comments and zero website visitors (operators report).
- Fit: do they film food the way your guests order it?

**Terms:**

- Fixed comp menu you choose, not an open tab; one owner warns of a $500 order (operators report).
- One hero dish, one location cue, one reason to visit now, one call to action, a trackable link or code per creator (research report 2026).
- Paid usage rights in writing from the start, so a winning video can run as a Spark Ad or Meta partnership ad within a few miles. The Botanist's Edinburgh opening combined local creators, staff content and Spark Ads: 700+ bookings at GBP 1.83 each (TikTok case 2026, platform-selected).
- Disclosure: creators must label paid or gifted content (FTC in the US, CMA in the UK).

**Judge on** matched bookings, codes redeemed and new guests, not views. Rank creatives; pay to amplify winners. TikTok's local feeds (Nearby in the UK, France, Germany and Italy from late 2025; US Local Feed from 11 Feb 2026) reward location cues.

## 5. Google, reviews and AI search for restaurants

- **Structured facts:** most specific primary category, attributes (dine-in, takeout, delivery, outdoor seating, dietary), text menu with sections, descriptions and prices, preferred menu source, preferred order and reservation links. Google can build a menu from a photo or PDF with AI; review it before publishing (Google 2026).
- **Write facts in text, not in images:** "gluten-free sourdough pizza", "private room for 20", "kitchen open until midnight" must appear in website HTML, the GBP menu and attributes, not only in an Instagram graphic. Google's AI features need nothing beyond normal indexable text (Google 2026).
- **AI answers lean on directories:** Yelp appears in about 80% of ChatGPT local answers; OpenTable and Tripadvisor recur in AI Overviews and AI Mode (BrightLocal 2026). Keep those profiles complete and consistent.
- **Monthly intent check:** search the 5 to 10 money queries from real locations ("coffee near [district]", "private dining [city]", "birthday dinner [city]", "bakery near me", "catering [neighborhood]") and fix what Google misunderstands at the source.
- **Hours are trust:** a second trip to an unexpectedly closed place ends many guests' willingness to return (operators report). Post special hours before holidays; post last seating and kitchen close separately ("Last seating 9:30, kitchen closes 9:30, we close at 10").
- **Trucks:** publish the next location and window as text on a stable web page, not only in Stories.
- **Reviews:** a neutral ask to every guest after payment or next morning, plus table QR or tap stands. The owner pair who talks two minutes with every table and has a tap stand on each reached 4.9 stars by month 4 and nearly 1,000 reviews in 15 months (operators report). Google and Tripadvisor allow neutral asks; Yelp says not to ask. No incentives, no gating, no staff quotas, no "mention my name".
- **Reply to every negative within 48 hours,** short and factual, offline route by name. Replying with the bakery's products named ("our organic bakery... fresh baked pastries") is an SEO claim with no evidence; harmless if natural.
- **Retaliatory reviews** (rejected job applicants, removed shoplifters): reply calmly, report with evidence (screenshots of messages, police report), appeal through the review management tool. Never claim "we have no record of you"; cash guests leave no record.
- **Bought reviews disappear:** 20 bought Google reviews vanished within two months and rankings fell below the start; bought Facebook reviews arrived as duplicated text from fake profiles (operators report).
- **Ratings matter economically:** one Yelp star was worth 5 to 9% revenue for independents (Luca, 2011 to 2016 data, possibly outdated).

## 6. Physical marketing that still works (operators report)

- **Selfie wall:** a plain deep-turquoise exterior wall drew a group selfie about every five minutes and noticeable walk-ins (2,608 upvotes). A town-themed mural with the logo in a corner and no tables in front got every group photo; a $5,000 mural visible from two streets was "the best $5,000" one owner spent, and the visitor bureau reused it.
- **Street sign:** a diner's daily joke plus "$10/10 min specials" was its main marketing while sales went from under $200,000 to $1.2M.
- **Aroma:** onions and garlic in butter on the truck flat-top; a pan of onions and garlic in bacon fat walked around a deli at 6 am; a loaf always in the oven at a bakery for warm samples; sizzling fajitas carried the long way through the dining room (one set off the fire alarm).
- **Sampling:** a sandwich shop sample day cutting each 12-inch sub into six 2-inch pieces with return cards (warning: tiny portions draw complaints); a business card with a code, handed over with "Here, this one's on me whenever you get a chance" (a coffee roaster's best tactic in five years).
- **Newspaper ad with a dotted border at full menu price:** 23 "coupons" in 35 minutes, nobody complained about paying full price.
- **Measure printed offers:** 100 flyers per offer, guests surrender the flyer, count redemptions.
- **Family signals:** crayons and good coloring pages turned a neighborhood place visibly kid-friendly.
- **Local Facebook groups:** a takeout owner credited groups and community giveaways as online orders tripled (pandemic period, attribution unclear). Ask the group about friction too: a diner learned guests hated tableside handhelds and lost paper receipts, fixed it, and announced it there.

## 7. Content and photos that convert

Show a concrete dish, drink or occasion, never generic mood. A signature-dish video got 1.42% CTR vs 0.42 to 0.45% for carousels in one hospitality account (agency case 2026). There is no evidence a restaurant needs to post daily; one local hero-dish video with a trackable booking link can beat ten generic posts (research report 2026). Cadence advice in public skills (Instagram 4 to 7 posts a week, Stories 3 to 5 a day, TikTok 3 to 5 a week) is uncited: use it only as a ceiling for a team with time.

**Idea bank** (ai-restaurant-claude, MIT): "why I quit my 9-to-5 to open this", "what $20 gets you here", "things I'd order if I were you", "the cheapest great order here", plating speed-run, first-bite reactions filmed with permission, "day before service", tonight's open tables at 6 pm, staff and suppliers by name.

**Shot angle by dish** (ai-restaurant-claude, MIT): pizza, salad, charcuterie and sushi overhead; burgers and sandwiches three-quarter at 45 degrees; pasta 45 degrees or overhead; soup 45 degrees with steam; steak a low 30 degrees; cocktails at eye level, backlit; layered desserts in cross-section; tacos lined up at 45 degrees.

**Light and styling:** soft window light between 10 am and 2 pm with a white reflector opposite; never dining-room light or flash; wipe rims; fresh herbs for color; a kettle blast for steam; one fork, one napkin, one drink; leave an empty corner for text. Shoot Stars first, then Puzzles (photos help them most), then new and seasonal items. A one-day professional shoot of 25 to 30 finals runs $1,500 to $3,000 (uncited). Never use AI-generated food images as if they were the real dish.

**Review mining for content and fixes** (ai-restaurant-claude, unifapi, MIT): read the last 90 days of reviews per platform, tally the top 5 praises and complaints with share and one quote each. A dish praised again and again is your next hook and ad; a complaint that recurs for you but not the leader is a conversion leak to fix before buying traffic. Investigate cross-platform gaps (4.4 on Google, 3.6 on Yelp).

## 8. Competitors and search coverage

- **Competitor set** (ai-restaurant-claude, MIT): within 3 miles or 10 minutes, same cuisine, one price tier either side, same daypart, 50+ reviews. Plan as defend (match), attack (amplify where you lead) or differentiate (own a gap: cuisine niche, price tier, late night or weekday brunch, occasion such as date night or large party, dietary, service style).
- **Rank check set:** "best [cuisine] in [city]", "best [cuisine] [neighborhood]", "[cuisine] near me", "[cuisine] [zip]", "[cuisine] delivery [city]", "[signature dish] [city]", plus "catering [area]" and "private dining [area]". Record map-pack and organic position from real locations each month.
- **Website pages** (ai-restaurant-claude, MIT): home "[cuisine] restaurant [city]", menu as text, catering, private dining, reservations, one page per signature dish, a location page "near [landmark]". Restaurant schema with servesCuisine, priceRange, openingHoursSpecification, hasMenu, acceptsReservations; Event markup for wine dinners and live music. Self-served review stars do not show in Google; never promise them.
- **Review gap math** (unifapi, MIT): new 5-star reviews needed to reach a target rating = ceil(count x (target - rating) / (5 - target)); below about 4.3 stars fix the rating before chasing volume. Depth: `local-seo`.

## 9. Paid media, restaurant settings only

Split campaigns by economic job ("new local diner", "fill Tuesday", "private event lead", "catering buyer", "opening waitlist", "lapsed regular"), each with its own conversion event and target (research report 2026).

- **Google first for explicit intent:** brand, cuisine + area, "open now", order and reservation, catering and private dining. Dining Local Services Ads charge per contact and can show reserve, call and directions actions where available (Google 2026).
- **Meta and TikTok for a concrete object:** hero dish, launch, prix fixe, event, catering package, private room. Retarget menu and reservation page visitors.
- **Radius and timing defaults** (ai-restaurant-claude, uncited): weekday lunch 1 mile, 10 am to 2 pm; first visit 3 miles; weekend brunch 5 miles; catering 10 miles. Budget by revenue: under $1M a year, $300 to $800 a month.
- **Hooks** (research report 2026): "Tuesday only: [dish/experience], [price], [neighborhood]. 40 seats. Book here." / "Hosting 12 to 30 people in [area]? Private room + family-style menu from [price/person]. Check dates." / "We bake [hero item] at 6 am; the Saturday batch usually ends by [realistic time]. Preorder by Thursday."
- **Franchisees:** name your location in every ad, or neighbors in the same brand get your demand.
- **Compliance:** alcohol ads to 21+ in the US; no "best in the city" without proof; real photos of the real dish; avoid ads that assert personal data ("it's been 90 days since your visit").
