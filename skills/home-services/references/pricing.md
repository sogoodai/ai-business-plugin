# Pricing

Price follows the cost structure, the owner's capacity and the competition's weakness, in that order. The customer-facing unit does not need to match the internal costing unit.

## Choose the method

| Method | Use when | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Flat price per job, from an internal rate | Production rate is repeatable | House wash, driveway, gutter clean, standard clean by bedroom count |
| Per unit (sq ft, linear ft, window, item, dog) | A size driver dominates and is easy to measure | Post-construction cleaning, fences, polymeric sand, knife sharpening, pet waste per dog |
| Hourly | Scope is genuinely unknown | Handyman punch lists, junk sorting, troubleshooting |
| Flat monthly | Recurring commercial or seasonal plans | Office cleaning, lawn seasons, maintenance plans |
| Trip or diagnostic fee plus repair | Diagnosis time varies | HVAC, appliance, plumbing |
| Session plus product | Value is in what the client buys after | Portrait photography |

Operator examples:

- Commercial cleaning: flat monthly price on a 1-year auto-renewing contract 98% of the time, built in a spreadsheet from service costs plus markup (r/sweatystartup 1,990).
- Residential cleaning: published flat rates by bedroom count, no haggling; lost a couple of jobs early, then people stopped asking (r/smallbusiness 276). A two-person team charges $160 a biweekly visit for a small apartment that takes them one hour (r/sweatystartup 197).
- Knife sharpening: switched from $1 per inch to $6 per knife and $6 to $8 per tool; orders of 4 to 10 items at $24 to $60 (r/sweatystartup 622). Simple beats precise.
- Post-construction cleaning by phase: rough $0.10 to $0.15/sq ft, detail $0.15 to $0.25, final touch-up $0.05 to $0.10; about $10,000 to $17,000 for 35,000 sq ft (r/sweatystartup 22). Price dealership glass and high dusting as separate lines after walking the site.
- Appliance repair: $110 service call covering diagnosis and the first 30 minutes in the home; skip microwaves and disposals, where repair nearly always costs more than replacement; decline repairs near half the appliance's value (r/sweatystartup 254).
- Floor care: strip and wax $1.20/sq ft twice a year, monthly scrub and recoat $0.35, weekly burnish $0.10, daily mopping $45 to $70 a day (r/sweatystartup 351).
- Polymeric sand lift-and-replace: $2.00/sq ft in one market (r/pressurewashing 101).
- Pressure washing: about $1,200 for a 2,250 sq ft house softwash plus 880 sq ft of driveway and sidewalk and a 156 sq ft patio, plus or minus 20% by market (r/pressurewashing 167); $399 for driveway, sidewalk and a modest pool-deck border in about 1.5 hours (r/pressurewashing 2,164); $500 per gas station overnight (r/pressurewashing 183).
- Dog waste: about 100 a month per weekly customer in one market (r/sweatystartup 37).
- Mobile car wash: test $100 first wash, $80 after, on new customers only (r/sweatystartup 956); RV trailer washes $299 to $399 in 1 to 2 hours (check park water rules first).
- Portrait photography: $1,490 session fee, then images and wall art sold after an in-home planning visit, with sales from $1,490 to $10k to $20k (r/sweatystartup 383).

## Floors and minimums

- Minimum charge per visit so short jobs pay for the drive: $250 with a $200 per working hour target (r/pressurewashing 2,164); $130 rising to $150 per property (r/sweatystartup 200); $250 even for an inspection-only roof visit (r/pressurewashing 581); carpet cleaning £80 minimum with average jobs £150 to £250 (r/sweatystartup 82).
- Handyman: focus on quick, visibly needed repairs that are hard to DIY (roof and attic access), minimum a couple of hundred dollars; one reports $200 to $400 per hour per job (r/smallbusiness 1,419).
- A photographer moved to 8-hour minimum wedding coverage after a 4-hour booking collapsed when the ceremony ran 45 minutes late (r/WeddingPhotography 208); another removed the smallest package because it displaced peak-Saturday full days (r/WeddingPhotography 48).
- Separate trip and diagnosis from repairs (HVAC, r/smallbusiness 1,175).
- Out-of-route and travel: price remote stops separately; destination photographers quote travel plus a fee for time (one suggested travel plus $1,000), never expenses only (r/WeddingPhotography 223).

## Good-better-best

- Options must differ in scope, warranty, speed or equipment, not just price (research pass): Good = repair and restore function with standard warranty; Better = repair plus prevention with longer warranty; Best = full replacement, premium warranty, priority service.
- 54% of thriving contractors present three options on at least half of jobs vs under 10% of struggling ones (ServiceTitan 2025, correlation, not proof).
- Track close rate by tier. If nobody buys Best and Good dominates, the middle is not anchoring; rebuild the tiers.
- Recurring services: one-off, monthly, biweekly, weekly with falling price per visit.
- Labor guarantees as the "Best" lever: an HVAC owner offered 10 years of labor coverage against a 1 to 2 year norm and stopped competing on price (r/smallbusiness 481; several commenters doubted the post, treat as an idea).
- Premium tier from customer requests: a mobile bar added a hired certified bartender as a higher package when customers kept asking for cocktails and booked almost every weekend (r/smallbusiness 1,637). Listen for repeated requests.

## Raising prices

- 65% of owners raised prices in the prior year; 80% of $500K+ businesses; cleaning lagged at 55% (Jobber 2026). Raising is normal.
- Signal to raise: win rate persistently above about 60 to 70% with a full calendar. Jobber warns that close rates near 100% mean underpricing (vendor benchmark).
- Test 5 to 10% on a subset of new quotes first (research-pass testing band, not a benchmark), then roll to existing customers at renewal.
- Exterior cleaner: low prices made leads too easy to close, so he set a $130 minimum and planned $150 for spring (r/sweatystartup 200).
- When overloaded, raise hard: a wedding photographer went from 15 weddings a year to 5 for the same money after repeated raises (r/WeddingPhotography 203). The opposite also happens: another lost 50%+ of sales at higher prices and moved to volume in a $2,000-budget market (r/WeddingPhotography 69). Check local budgets before copying either.
- Announcement that worked (agency-style client base, 22 clients, 40% increase, three months' notice): "Hey [name], I wanted to give you a heads up that starting January my rates will be [new rate]. This reflects the growth in my skills, the results I've been delivering for your business, and an adjustment to market rates. I completely understand if this doesn't fit your budget and I'm happy to recommend someone else if needed. If you'd like to continue working together at the new rate, no action needed on your end." Result: 7 left (the most demanding), 12% more monthly revenue, 35% less work (r/smallbusiness 1,239). A commenter warned 40% is large; for homeowners, use smaller annual steps.
- Prepay or deposit keeps the old rate: raise the price, then offer the old price to clients who prepay or leave a non-refundable deposit (r/smallbusiness 571).
- Commodity spikes: florists and anyone with fixed contracts should adjust new quotes immediately and add material escalation clauses (r/smallbusiness 1,146).

## Discounts without damage

- Show the normal price, then the discount and its reason as a separate line, with an end date. Never zero out services (r/pressurewashing 2,164; r/smallbusiness 612, three practitioners agree).
- Label intro prices out loud: "This is our new client pricing." Fast turnaround too: "Right now I'm not too busy as we're just starting out, so I can get to this right away." Owners who skipped this were stuck with 20 to 30% below-market prices (r/smallbusiness 2,215).
- Premium list price, discount early jobs for reviews, shrink the discount as reviews land: a cleaner priced 20% above competitors and went from break-even to profit as discounts stopped (r/sweatystartup 252). Never tie a discount to the review itself.
- Package discount when services are booked together, shown against separate prices ($900 separately, $840 together). Have a floor and walk away when pushed below it (r/sweatystartup 191).
- Portfolio discount conditional on marketing use: "The use of your photos for marketing offsets the $900 I discounted from my normal fees. If you wish to deny the use of the photos for marketing purposes, I will have to charge my normal rate." The client chose to pay full price (r/WeddingPhotography 213).
- Hardship discounts as a visible line ("low income / government assistance x% discount") or a smaller scope at the full rate (r/smallbusiness 361).
- Hold the line on "my friend got it cheaper": the repair shop that said its lowest-ever price was $120 for multiple items got paid $150 three days later (r/smallbusiness 1,175). People asking $10 to $20 off: explain; people asking 50% off: end the call.
- Do not pre-pay for promised volume ("I have 1,000 houses"); charge normal price (r/pressurewashing 338).
- Make a one-time concession explicit as one-time, or it becomes the reference price and can cost the referrer too (r/smallbusiness 612).

## Tricks most owners miss

- Leave negotiating room by design: one pressure washer quotes $350 for a driveway he would do for $250 (r/pressurewashing 581). Better: three margin tiers decided before quoting.
- Compute the price the job demands before checking competitors; competitor prices are a sanity check. Call 3 to 5 competitors casually before launch, some will share supplier tips (r/pressurewashing 38).
- Commercial bids: test-clean one unit first. A storage facility with 93 oxidized doors needed about $600 of chemical alone; the $4,400 bid lost to $900 and $1,500 bids, which is fine if your costs were real (r/pressurewashing 23).
- Count travel, setup and editing time: a "friendship rate" $150 detail took 5 to 6 hours ($25 to $30 an hour before supplies, r/smallbusiness 288); a $1,000 paint correction ran 40 hours plus 4 hours of daily driving (r/AutoDetailing 2,813).
- Build occasional costs (parking, tolls) into the package rather than invoicing after the event (r/WeddingPhotography 333).
- Corrective work after a cheap competitor is a new job at full price, not a partial (r/pressurewashing 2,734).
