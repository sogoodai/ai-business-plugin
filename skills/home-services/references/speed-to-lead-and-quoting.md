# Speed to lead, quoting and unsold-quote follow-up

The cheapest growth in this segment: answer every inquiry fast, quote fast and clearly, and follow up every quote until it is won or closed. Triage and reply structure are adapted from anthropics/knowledge-work-plugins small-business speed-to-lead and proposal-builder (Apache-2.0, changes noted in [sources.md](sources.md)).

## What the data says

- Only 52% of inbound home-service calls reach a person (Invoca 2026). Of answered calls from marketing, 38% are real leads and 45% of those book on the call.
- 20% of pros reply within an hour, 60% the same day; over half of customers expect a reply within an hour (Jobber, Dec 2025 survey). In a 2026 consumer study, 79% said they chose the fastest-responding business.
- The famous "reply in 5 minutes = 21x more likely to qualify" and "78% buy from the first responder" figures come from 2007 to 2011 lead studies on contact and qualification, not contractor close rates. Directionally uncontested, but cite the year or do not cite them.
- Operator measurements: replying by text within about 5 minutes won about 70% of jobs; after 30+ minutes the prospect had usually called someone else; close rate went from about 40% to 75% once an app answered real questions while the owner was on jobs (r/pressurewashing 28). A cleaning founder called all five local competitors as a prospect; none called back (r/sweatystartup 578). Being reachable is a positioning advantage on its own.
- One cleaner answered calls 5 pm to 8 am when rivals stopped: "It sucks to be woken up, but it's 200 bucks, right?" (r/sweatystartup 252).

## The response system

1. **Missed-call text back** within seconds, staffed hours and after hours. It is a reply to the customer's own call: keep it service-only, no promotion (consent rules in `email-sms-loyalty`).
2. **Form-to-text:** forms trigger an immediate text from a real number, plus the email. A wedding photographer's "hey received your inquiry, sending pricing to your email now!" text made couples check spam for the quote and lifted replies sharply (r/WeddingPhotography 66).
3. **Answering service or AI receptionist** trained on services, prices, areas and booking rules, answering in the business name (r/sweatystartup 578). Hot jobs route to a human immediately.
4. **Online booking with reassurance:** a date picker plus "Yes, it really is this easy!" stopped confused customers calling to ask what else to do (r/sweatystartup 578). An appliance tech whose homepage validates the request and books a slot rarely answers the phone and stays booked five days a week (r/sweatystartup 254).
5. **Owner alerts:** any lead not answered in 5 minutes escalates (owner or rep), 15 minutes (backup), 30 minutes (manager) (harvest: marketingskills revops, MIT).
6. **After-hours rule:** only list hours you answer. Extending GBP hours to evening or 24/7 is an edge for emergency trades if covered; uncovered, it earned 1-star reviews twice (r/localseo 95, 80).

KPIs: median minutes inquiry to useful reply, age of the oldest unanswered lead, answer rate by hour of day, and win rate by reply-time bucket (under 5, 5 to 30, 30 to 120, over 120 minutes).

## Triage every inquiry into four buckets

| Bucket | Signs | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Hot | Fits plus urgency: "today", "no heat", "leak", named budget, start date, repeat customer, named referrer | Reply now and alert a human with one instruction: "Call 503-555-0142 now." |
| Qualified | Fits, no urgency | Answer their question, confirm you can help, offer 2 to 3 real slots from the live calendar (respect drive time between jobs) |
| Unclear | Missing what, where or when | Ask the one question that resolves it |
| Out of scope | Wrong service or area | Still reply and refer by name: "That's not something we're certified for. [Company] handles it, ask for [name], tell her I sent you." Owners report these come back as referrals |

Rules for every first reply (adapted from anthropics, Apache-2.0):

- Under 100 words. Answer their actual question first.
- Name the specific job and ask one clear question; do not demand phone or email first (60.5% vs 50.3% 24-hour reply rate, LeadTruffle Sept 2026).
- Never quote a firm price, date or crew commitment in a fast automated reply; give direction ("for a home that size it usually lands between X and Y; I confirm when I see it").
- Never say "this is an automated response" and never claim to be a person if it is not. No invented familiarity.
- Flag for owner review any draft containing a price, a date promise, a complaint or contact after a bad job.
- Volume anomaly (dozens of near-identical overnight forms) = bot or form loop: stop and ask.

## Quoting

### Qualify before you drive

- Give a range by phone tied to the size driver: "most houses your size land between X and Y, I confirm the exact number when I measure" (Christmas lights, r/sweatystartup 18).
- State the per-foot rate or the minimum first: a fence installer stopped hours of diagramming for ghosts by confirming the per-foot price before any estimate (r/sweatystartup 164); a lighting installer states a $1,500 minimum on the first call (r/sweatystartup 18).
- Ask the decision-maker question before presenting: "If you were comfortable moving forward today, are you the only person who needs to be involved in the decision?" (r/sweatystartup 187).
- Remote quotes for repeatable work: customers enter home details, you check square footage online and siding, access and height on Google Maps; one pressure washer almost never adjusts the generated price (r/sweatystartup 477). Adjust for stories and height, not just square feet: a 2,000 sq ft two-story can take longer than a 3,500 sq ft ranch (r/sweatystartup 477).
- Paid estimates for heavy consults: charge an initial meeting fee credited to the job if they buy (r/smallbusiness 361). An HVAC contractor keeps the trip charge separate from repairs because diagnosis can take 10 minutes to 2 hours (r/smallbusiness 1,175).
- Always inspect when the property is unseen: a $40 lawn quote for a referral's parents became a 3-hour job (r/sweatystartup 43); a $269 house wash turned into discoloration and rework after skipping a test patch (r/pressurewashing 976).

### Build the quote

- Price from 2 to 3 comparable past jobs and show the arithmetic ("4 units at $14,200 = $3,550 per unit; this job 5 units = $17,750 + $900 access = $18,650"). Flag anything more than about 15% off the rate card. Leave a blank with a specific question when no comparable exists (anthropics proposal-builder, Apache-2.0).
- Never invent a labor rate, material cost, lead time, or a quantity from a photo; a 30% square-footage miss sinks the job.
- Line items the customer sees as decisions (equipment, prep, access, permit, add-ons), not "materials / labor". A painter's $4,000 estimate: prep $400, siding, trim, windows and doors $2,500, materials $700, tax $400, with the warranty (about 3%) built into labor (r/sweatystartup 262).
- 2 to 3 options maximum; recommend one and say why; never offer a tier you would not want to deliver.
- Terms: deposit 30 to 50% on material-heavy work, 30-day validity (shorter when material prices move), written change orders.
- Scope by surface and exclusion: "driveway, front walkway, back patio; pool deck not included." Add expectation lines where results vary: "Cleaning does not constitute refinishing work"; "Power washing may reveal uneven wood tones due to long-term UV exposure" (r/pressurewashing 124). Written limits still did not prevent one 50% concession on a 20-year-old gas station lot, so take before photos too (r/pressurewashing 179).
- Send it within 30 minutes of the visit or call from templates (r/sweatystartup 17), by text with a link, not only by email.
- Log every quote as won, lost or no response, with the final price and reason ("lost at $18,650 to $16,000 competitor"). Every lost estimate needs a reason code.

### Present and negotiate

- State the price, then stop talking.
- Deferral ("I'll think about it", "getting other quotes"): "I completely understand. Normally when my customers tell me this, it has to do with price. May I ask, what was your expectation?" (r/sweatystartup 222).
- Ask during qualification, while relaxed: "When you were looking for a painter, why did you pick me?" and "You've lived here 20 years, what made you want to paint now?" Use the answers when you defend the price (r/sweatystartup 262).
- Three margin tiers (for example 50%, 45%, 40%) set before the visit. Concede one tier only for a decision today: "The good news is, we're not that far away. I'll tell you what, I'll lower my price to $3,636 if you allow me to earn your business today." Try the last tier once, then walk (r/sweatystartup 262). Pick the floor by schedule load: hold the top when booked out.
- Never cut the same scope twice. A pest rep who dropped $100 in two or three steps made the buyer distrust the first price (r/sweatystartup 222). Each concession removes scope.
- Budget objection: "What budget range are you working with? Sometimes I can adjust the scope to fit." Then: "At $X, I'd recommend we focus on [piece]. You'd get [outcome] without [removed part]." (r/smallbusiness 321).
- Cheaper competitor: "Does their quote include [specific thing]? What's their warranty?" then "I'm confident in my pricing because [one reason]. But if they're a better fit for your budget, I completely understand." (r/smallbusiness 321).
- Close the door kindly: "If you change your mind or ever need something else, please give us a call." Operators report re-inquiries months later after people shop around (r/smallbusiness 276).
- Preventive framing for small repairs: fix the siding hole for $200 before the next big rain, versus far larger damage later (handyman, r/smallbusiness 1,419). HVAC: "Your capacitor is at about 70%, it'll probably last another season but I'd plan on replacing it next spring." Photos plus a timeline, no pressure; very high follow-up close rate reported (r/smallbusiness 535).
- Financing on $500+ jobs, introduced with the options, not after an objection (Housecall Pro 2026: 62% more likely to proceed).

## Unsold-quote follow-up cadence

The pool of open quotes is an owned remarketing list. ServiceTitan's 2025 survey links follow-up to 1 to 15% of revenue (self-reported).

| When | Channel | Message job |
|---|---|---|
| Within 1 hour of sending | Text | Confirm it arrived, one-line summary, "any questions?" |
| Day 1 | Text or call | Answer the question they did not ask (timing, what is included) |
| Day 3 | Text | "Is the main question price, timing, or what's included? Reply with one and I'll clarify it." |
| Day 7 | Text | Two named slots: "We have Tuesday AM or Thursday PM open in [area]. Reply TUE or THU to hold one." |
| Day 14 | Text or email | Close the loop politely; leave the door open |
| Day 30 (big projects) | Call or email | Reactivate: new season, price change, crew availability |
| Next season | Text | Seasonal reason to revisit (pre-spring wash, pre-summer tune-up) |

- Fast-moving residential services can compress it: one cleaner followed up at 1 hour, then days 1, 2, 3 and 4 (r/sweatystartup 252). A knife sharpener texts a digital business card (Blinq) and follows up every few days until book or decline (r/sweatystartup 188).
- Stop on any reply, including out-of-office until it expires; space touches at least a day apart after the first-hour check; never follow up after they already answered (the clearest automation tell).
- Track recovered revenue as its own source.

## Tricks most owners miss

- Alert the owner when a channel produces leads faster than the team can answer; more spend then lowers booked-job economics.
- Mystery-shop competitors' response time before choosing positioning: if nobody calls back, "we answer" is the pitch.
- A quote template per service, prefilled with photos of that job type, cuts quote time and raises acceptance.
- Use the customer's own channel: text replies to texts.
