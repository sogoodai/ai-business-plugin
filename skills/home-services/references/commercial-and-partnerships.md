# Commercial accounts, partnerships and referrals

Commercial and partner work trades a lower price for volume, predictability and density. Decide per account whether it adds route density or destroys it.

## Commercial cleaning and janitorial

What the best-documented operator did (101 facilities, $277,000 invoiced in one month, about $10,000 a month of new recurring business, two clients lost in a year; r/sweatystartup 1,990, 428):

- Started by buying a book of business: three contracts grossing about $7,000 a month for $50,000 on BizBuySell.
- One salesperson owns prospecting through signature: personal calls, emails, building walk-ins and persistent follow-up. No social media, no automated blasts. Google Ads, lead-gen firms and cold email (apart from cold email) were "almost nonexistent" in effectiveness and growth stalled at $7,000 to $9,000 a month for three years until the salesperson was hired.
- Targets single-tenant offices so the buyer is the occupant, not a property manager. Two of the top three clients are single-tenant headquarters; the top three (a school and two HQs) pay about $23,000 a month.
- Materials, dress and speech that look like a $100 million company when bidding against nationals.
- Flat monthly price, 1-year auto-renew.
- Retention: inspect buildings often, reply to any client email within an hour, visit the same day for critical issues.

Small-route version: two restaurants and a small office close together, about four hours a night six days a week, netting about $5,000 per person a month; scope fixed to restrooms, floors, vacuuming, trash, windows weekly (r/sweatystartup 1,045).

Prospecting moves:

- **Walk-ins that produce a name and a renewal month:** "Hey, I own a local office cleaning company. Who usually handles janitorial here?" If they are out, get their name, best email and roughly when they review vendors. A real name plus a renewal month is a successful visit. One short leave-behind, avoid lunch rush and opening time, log the visit the same day (r/sweatystartup 17).
- **Survey opener:** "Hey, my name is Matt. How often do the cleaners come by?" (never "do you use a cleaning service?", which invites a no), then "I own a cleaning company and I'm doing a survey on what businesses like about their current cleaning companies and what they wish they did better." (r/sweatystartup 17).
- **Permit records:** use the city's open-data portal (Socrata where available) to find office, medical, gym or restaurant build-outs weeks before occupancy, with address and square footage. Offer post-construction cleaning to the property manager or GC, then convert to recurring. Have a quote ready within two days (Monday permit, Wednesday quote) before lead-service batches arrive Friday. Run it as a 30-day test in one city, tracking quote rate and close rate against normal outbound (r/sweatystartup 60).
- **Bid lists:** ask every construction company in the market to put you on its post-construction cleaning bid list (r/sweatystartup 60). A cleanup company visited every builder within 20 miles ("I'm looking for work and I'm hungry. Let me help you out anyway I can.") and grew to 15 employees and 55 dumpsters in ten years (r/sweatystartup 20).
- **Niche verticals:** daycares (8 cleans at $175, heavy deep cleaning in the first months, r/sweatystartup 197), medical offices for painters via medical association meetings (r/sweatystartup 16), restaurants for add-ons such as hood, grease trap and equipment cleaning (get a maintenance bid from a qualified tech for the installed brand before selling it, r/sweatystartup 1,045).

## Property managers, apartments and HOAs

- Property managers rank cost, then responsiveness, then quality (former carpet-cleaning owner, r/sweatystartup 22). Lead with turnaround and reporting.
- Approach: search "apartment complex near me", walk into the leasing office and ask for the maintenance supervisor or property manager; offer to wait (r/sweatystartup 22). Or ride along with a complementary vendor: a painter joined his wife's flooring sales route, spotted a unit a painter had damaged by not masking, asked what the manager paid that painter, and offered better work. He needed an LLC and liability insurance and started two weeks later, reaching $10,000 booked by month three on weekends (r/sweatystartup 249).
- Free demo cleaning, then "what price would win your business?" Rarely converts because of existing vendor contracts, but timed to a contract end it yields steady work (r/sweatystartup 22).
- Low-price entry, high-margin follow-on: carpet cleaning as the loss leader for approved-vendor status, then ducts, odor fogging, 24/7 water extraction and dryer vents (r/sweatystartup 22). Insurance ran $30,000 to $40,000 a year for three vans: price it in.
- Apartment complexes pay for annual pressure washing (former maintenance manager, r/pressurewashing 338). HOA common-area jobs: budget machine time honestly; a large HOA lot was estimated at three days with a 4 GPM machine, and rushing reproduces stripes (r/pressurewashing 708).
- The research pass's operational offer beats a referral commission: "We reserve two priority slots each week for your move-ins and turnovers. You get a single contact, photo completion report, invoice by property and a response within [X] business hours."
- Measure partner economics: partner CAC including reciprocal discounts, turnaround compliance by partner, margin and route fit. Fire partners whose jobs are consistently low margin or far away.

## Realtors, stagers, movers and banks

- Cleanout package for realtors and banks: empty abandoned or foreclosed homes, clean, quick paint touch-ups and potted plants before staging; hazardous conditions cost extra (former owner, r/smallbusiness 535).
- Move-in and move-out cleaning, junk removal, handyman punch lists from inspection reports, and window and pressure washing before listing photos all hang off realtor timelines.
- Offer realtors speed and proof: listing-ready by a date, before/after photos they can use.
- Referral fees can raise licensing, disclosure or anti-kickback issues in regulated trades and some jurisdictions (research pass). Default to reciprocal introductions, co-marketing or client benefits unless local rules are checked.

## Venues, planners and event partners (photographers, detailers, rentals)

- Wedding planners are gatekeepers: one luxury photographer's second-largest referral source after couples (r/WeddingPhotography 276). Serve them with next-day teasers chosen for what planners need, gifts, and free coverage of their own events (r/WeddingPhotography 124).
- Venue reviews: leave a Google review with your photos for every venue you shoot, and update it with new photos after repeat visits; one photographer attributes 30+ bookings over five years to this (r/WeddingPhotography 201).
- When a planner uses your photos uncredited, turn it into a relationship: ask for a tag, offer more photos, propose coffee about their preferred vendor list; a referral and backlink beat a $200 to $300 usage fee (r/WeddingPhotography 59).
- Tier licensing by relationship: free for small vendors who credit and refer, a low fee for new ones, regular rates for corporates (r/WeddingPhotography 497).
- Event rentals: become one venue's dance-floor or uplighting provider (r/sweatystartup 18, 184).

## Trade-to-trade and manufacturer channels

- Complementary competitors send overflow: a repair shop befriended a microsoldering specialist who later texted him a camera install lead that became a $3,000 job paid within an hour, his biggest sale (r/smallbusiness 1,055).
- Home-maintenance subscriptions: pass documented leads to repair partners in exchange for waived call-out fees or discounts for your members, which justifies a higher plan price (r/sweatystartup 16).
- Wholesale through adjacent installers: pool builders sell a maker's loungers at $850 wholesale vs $1,450 retail (r/sweatystartup 254).
- Manufacturer and retailer dispatch (appliance warranty jobs, Geek Squad installs) for a new tech's first customers (r/smallbusiness 507).
- Restroom-trailer manufacturer sends a service business dozens of new clients a year (r/sweatystartup 18).

## Local institutions

- Walk into local businesses and ask for referrals: a moving company walked into 500 businesses and reported $30,000 a month more in sales (r/sweatystartup 274).
- Churches (one plumber: 90% of new and repeat business, r/sweatystartup 38), vets and shelters (pet services), schools and sports clubs.
- Sponsorships: start at $25 instead of a $500 tier, and ask for a short video inside your business shared on the organization's social accounts; scale only if it pays (r/smallbusiness 1,092).

## Customer referral programs

- Referrals and repeat work are each a top source for 59% of owners (Jobber 2026). Referral rate and conversion data for contractors are weak: Housecall Pro quotes 2 to 10% referral rates and 4 to 6% conversion from third parties (2026, not their data).
- Ask with a specific opening and cards in hand: "I have a couple of openings next week if you have any friends who need [service], here's a few cards, most of my best customers come from referrals so pass on my info." (r/pressurewashing 338).
- Ask social customers to share before/after photos with friends; one pressure washer got many leads this way after Facebook ads gave none (r/pressurewashing 94).
- Rewards: one free hour of service per referred customer (repair shop, r/smallbusiness 2,215); 20% off for both the customer and the referred neighbor (weeding, r/sweatystartup 514); distance-weighted rewards that pay most for the house next door and more for consecutive houses on a street (lawn, r/sweatystartup 445).
- Size the reward from CAC: max combined reward = target CAC minus other acquisition cost per customer (coreyhaines31/marketingskills, MIT).
- Thank referrers by handwritten note, about 10 minutes with a stamp; one customer replied with a gift (r/smallbusiness 2,215).
- Discounts in exchange for promised referrals rarely pay: referred clients expect the same discount and are often the same kind of problem client (r/smallbusiness 361).
- When a referred client goes wrong, call the referrer early and ask for advice before ending it; one owner who skipped this lost the referrer worth $1,000 to $1,200 a month (r/smallbusiness 828). Another declined a hostile referral, called the referrer to explain, and kept getting referrals.

## Tricks most owners miss

- Give each partner a code or landing page so their jobs can be counted.
- Corporate accounts poach easily on price; homeowners stay. Keep commercial share deliberate (r/sweatystartup 453).
- Check permit data, vendor renewal months and bid lists on a calendar; commercial wins come from timing.
- A trades business can open where rivals neglect local SEO: one plumbing and HVAC firm scans maps for weak competitors and opens shops there (r/GoogleMyBusiness 28). Only with a real, staffed location.
