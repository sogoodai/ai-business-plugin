# Offline and neighbor marketing

The pattern across 286 threads and the research: offline works when it is **specific to the house or the street and tied to a crew already nearby**. Blanket drops across a city mostly return nothing. Every piece gets a tracked number, QR code with UTM, or a street-level offer code, or it cannot be judged.

## Neighbor saturation (do this first, it is nearly free)

- While working at an address, offer the 20 to 100 homes around it a slot "while the crew is already here." The research script: "We're working for one of your neighbors on [street] today and have room for one more [service] stop in this area on [day]. If you want a quote while the crew is already nearby, text [number] with '[street]'." Never name or imply endorsement by the customer without permission.
- Ask in person with a price: a gutter contractor working on the same road asked "Need your gutters cleaned? I'll do it for $XX price right now." The homeowner said yes on the spot after other companies never showed up (r/sweatystartup 578).
- Bring cards and flyers to every job. One new pressure washer had two neighbors walk over on his first paid job and no cards to give (r/pressurewashing 52).
- Big lettering on the trailer: neighbors called during a first job and the operator spent the next week washing the street (r/sweatystartup 477).
- Use the visible contrast. A pressure washer who accidentally cleaned part of a pool deck showed the customer the difference and sold the whole deck (r/pressurewashing 344). Same logic: a clean driveway next to a dirty one sells the neighbor.
- Real social proof only: "I'm taking care of your neighbor Linda down the street tomorrow" worked better than company facts for a pest seller, but a commenter checked names and caught reps lying (r/sweatystartup 188). Use a real name only with that customer's permission; otherwise "a neighbor on [street]".
- Tag offers by street: a unique code per street, and log the originating job ID so new jobs can be credited to the job that created them.

## Door knocking

What works:

- **Problem-specific houses only.** A handyman knocks only where he can see a specific exterior fault he can fix today, explains the cost of waiting, and leaves a magnetic card if no sale: $22,000 gross in two weeks with no ads, flyers or website (r/smallbusiness 1,419). The card tip got 251 upvotes on its own.
- **Handwritten, priced, addressed.** For weedy front yards at $700k+ homes: an index card with name and phone plus a sticky note with the exact address, a price and "Front weeds only." 30 cards, $4 of supplies, 3 callbacks and 5 days of work (r/sweatystartup 514). Test handwritten vs printed cards, both with the handwritten price note.
- **Recently sold homes on small lots** (filter Zillow or Realtor.com, 1/4 acre or less), in a branded polo with door hangers, for lawn care (r/smallbusiness 919).
- **Botched DIY jobs:** leave a before/after card at homes with striped, half-done driveways (r/pressurewashing 299). Check when the surface was last washed first: mildew regrows in surface-cleaner stripes even after a good job (r/pressurewashing 708).
- **New developments** for fences (avoid rocky ground) (r/sweatystartup 164).
- **Winter pre-selling:** door knock in the off-season, book an in-person estimate for the weekend, take a 25% deposit and a signed contract for spring. About $90,000 booked before spring, deposits funded startup costs; seasonal staff kept on payroll by paying them hourly to knock (r/sweatystartup 453).
- **Offer the walkthrough on the spot** if someone answers and needs the service (r/sweatystartup 30). Ask those who do not need it to pass the flyer to a friend; one resident phoned a friend in front of the owner (r/sweatystartup 30).

What the numbers say:

- A D2D trainer: about 1 in 5 people you actually talk to want the offer; about 60% of a market will never buy (r/smallbusiness 1,419). Measure per conversation, not per door.
- A commission pest seller: 16,000 doors, 82 contracts, $800 net after rent and food (r/sweatystartup 188). Measure net per door before committing a season.
- Exhaustion is real: 370 to 470 pieces over three days gave one leaf-cleanup inquiry, which haggled $150 down to $100 (r/sweatystartup 27).

## Door hangers, flyers and cards

- Evidence is weak and publication-biased. A seller's case: 10,000 HVAC hangers, 1.8% response by day 14, 4.5% by day 60, $67 CPA vs $140 for concurrent Google Ads (Think Flyers 2025). Operators: 300 hangers in under 15 seconds per door gave zero leads and one complaint call; 150 of 250 gave zero calls (r/pressurewashing 338).
- Cards with an expiring offer and a QR code: 5,000 Vistaprint cards for about $200, 20% off the first clean, distributed over a month at five hours a day. Reported 3 to 5 bookings per 500 cards (figures inconsistent) and an email list from a "save this offer for later" option on the landing page (r/sweatystartup 201).
- One commenter reports an 8% callback rate from flyers, with calls still coming from saved flyers later (r/sweatystartup 201). Track beyond the first two weeks.
- Pay per result, not per hour: a solo cleaner paid her daughter $30 per acquired client; one hour of distribution gave three clients, two became weekly (r/sweatystartup 90).
- Deep discounts backfire: 100 flyers offering 50% off or 4 free hours gave 2 leads, both ghosted after the quote (r/sweatystartup 25).
- Offer design from operators: lead with one priced job and a strong before/after ("Make your home look like new for under $399", then upsell add-ons, r/pressurewashing 581). Batch-test offers in runs of 100 and collect the flyer at redemption to count response (r/smallbusiness 258).
- Test the design on real people before printing 1,000: one mascot card read as aggressive and suggestive (r/pressurewashing 278). Keep the mascot under a third of the card, name large.
- Dig into adjacent targets: thermal-imaging flyers at homes for sale ("Understand your home utility bills BEFORE you buy") to reach sellers buying their next home (r/smallbusiness 919).

## Yard signs

- Some businesses live on them: a gutter cleaner got 50+ clients and $6,500 to $7,000 in the first season entirely from yard signs (r/sweatystartup 200); yard signs were the main source for a junk removal business that went from $5,500 a month to $10,000+ (r/sweatystartup 227); a pet-waste route reached 20 clients in nine months on signs, word of mouth and free posts (r/smallbusiness 535).
- Others get nothing: 100 signs, no calls (r/pressurewashing 94).
- What separates them: placement at red lights and stop signs where drivers can photograph the number ($300 budget, Vistaprint or UZmarketing signs, H-stakes from Amazon; r/sweatystartup 477, r/pressurewashing 101); a phone number readable at a glance (8.5 x 12 signs with small numbers get ignored, r/pressurewashing 278); and one service named.
- Signs in customers' yards after a job convert best because they are proof on the street. Ask permission; offer to collect them after 7 to 14 days.
- Check local sign ordinances before staking signs on public land.

## Direct mail and EDDM

- EDDM Retail postage $0.260 per piece in 2026; a 2,000-piece test is $520 postage before print (USPS). Home-services response 3.75% in a vendor compilation (Postalytics 2025).
- One send is not a test. A $200 EDDM drop returned $0; the advice was 3 to 4 rounds to the same 400 houses (r/sweatystartup 200). A high-ticket flooring operator mails 10,000 a month via Valpak for three years; customers bring postcards kept for 18 months, and a few jobs pay the year (r/sweatystartup 405).
- Pest control: about $0.70 per address, calls from 3 to 5% of 1,000 homes mailed, about 10% of callers staying several years; four years of mailers grew the business 65% (r/pressurewashing 338). Mail suits recurring services where one customer is worth years.
- Co-op postcards: sell eight category-exclusive slots on the back at $0.05 to $0.10 per household ($500 per advertiser per 10,000) to cover your side. One operator reports 50,000 postcards, about 13 flooring jobs and $40,000; another spent two weeks pitching 500 businesses for 3 tentative yeses (r/sweatystartup 405). Only if you can sell ads quickly.
- Personal touch: a tree-service franchise flyer with a printed "handwritten" section on heavy stock read as personal and trusted (r/sweatystartup 295).
- Open the call with "What about the flyer made you contact us today?" to learn what worked (r/sweatystartup 187).
- Stop mailing a ZIP when its booked-job CAC passes the same threshold used for digital.

## Vehicles

- A wrapped, clean truck changes price perception: a plumber who wrapped two unmarked trucks got calls from neighbors seeing them parked and says homeowners stopped haggling on $4,500 tankless installs (r/sweatystartup 122).
- Parking is a channel: a trash-bin cleaning truck parked at Target brought a good share of 400 to 500 customers (r/sweatystartup 360).
- Two elements dominate: the business name in huge letters and the phone number, readable from 50 feet at 35 mph. Name the service ("PLUMBING SERVICE", not "CONTRACTOR") and add one differentiator ("24/7 EMERGENCY", "NO TRIP CHARGE", "4.9 stars on Google") (r/sweatystartup 28).
- Use a separate phone number and a QR code to a dedicated page on the vehicle to attribute those calls (r/sweatystartup 360).
- Magnetic signs and card holders work on steel doors; check first, magnets do not stick to an aluminum F-150 body (r/sweatystartup 28).
- Do not buy the big rig before customers ask: one operator served 100+ customers from a hatchback with one snarky comment (r/sweatystartup 477).

## Other cheap local channels

- Workplaces: mobile detailers and car washers schedule wash days at offices (doctors, dentists, CPAs: 5 to 10 cars at $50 to $75 each; an office route of 9 detailing customers at $400 every two weeks, r/AutoDetailing 1,901, r/sweatystartup 956).
- Apartment resident packets and flyers (small buy-in) for home repair and cleaning (r/smallbusiness 2,215).
- Vets, shelters, groomers and pet stores for pet services (r/sweatystartup 431).
- Churches: one plumber reportedly gets 90% of new and repeat business through church connections (r/sweatystartup 38); church bulletin boards for seasonal services.
- Farmer's markets: about 30 cards a week for 1 to 2 appointments (r/sweatystartup 622). Low yield; count it.
- Sidewalk chalk with service and number in high-traffic areas (suggested, 339 upvotes, untested, r/smallbusiness 919). Check local rules.

## Tricks most owners miss

- Offline is a route-density tool: target the streets next to existing stops, not new towns.
- Every piece needs a tracking method or it is a donation.
- Count delayed responses for 60 to 90 days; hangers and postcards get kept.
- Judge per conversation (door knocking) or per household reached 3+ times (mail), never per piece after one drop.
