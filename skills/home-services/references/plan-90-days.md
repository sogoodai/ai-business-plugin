# 90-day plan

For a new or stalled home-service business. Order matters: stop the leaks, then convert, then buy acquisition, then densify and scale what works. Each phase ends with a short owner report: what changed, the scorecard, what the owner must decide.

## Brand-new business: the first 30 days instead

1. Google Business Profile on day one (verification can take weeks), real address hidden if customers do not visit. Hand to `local-seo`.
2. Five to ten full-price jobs from friends, family and neighbors, each asked for an honest Google review (LSA calls started after 5 reviews for one cleaner).
3. Before/after portfolio: your own home and two friends, matched angles (one carpet cleaner left his stairs unvacuumed a month for contrast; the white-bucket wastewater shot is a proven social format, r/sweatystartup 45).
4. A cheap site (one operator paid $450 and got a job on launch day, r/sweatystartup 1,014) with services, areas, hours, price ranges, FAQ and a booking or quote form.
5. Missed-call text and quote templates before the first ad.
6. Neighbor saturation and one batch of 30 handwritten, priced cards on target streets.
7. Call 3 to 5 competitors as a prospect: response time and prices tell you the opening.

Then join the plan below at day 15.

## Days 1 to 14: measure and stop the leaks

Owner supplies: services and margins, real service area and towns worth driving to, weekly capacity, licenses and insurance, logins (GBP, LSA, ads, software), and a 12-month export.

Agent builds one source-of-truth funnel ([economics.md](economics.md)): source, service, ZIP, timestamps, first reply, answered, qualified, quote, won/lost reason, completed, collected, gross profit, recurring flag, review status.

Fix:

- Missed-call text back, form-to-text, answering service or AI receptionist, escalation at 5, 15 and 30 minutes.
- Targets: 90%+ of qualified calls answered or returned promptly; median reply under 15 minutes in staffed hours (an operating target, not a benchmark; aim for 5).
- GBP pass with `local-seo`: real name, most specific primary category, service areas (up to 20), hours you answer, no virtual office.
- Review request after every completed job, same neutral text, no Yelp in the automation.
- Truck and cards: name and phone readable at 50 feet; cards on every job.

Exit check: answer rate and reply time measured for 7 days; every lead has a source.

## Days 15 to 30: convert before acquiring

- Quote templates per service with photos; Good-better-best where scope truly differs; quotes out within 30 minutes; deposits on material-heavy jobs; financing on $500+.
- Price audit: loaded hourly cost, drive time, minimum charge, material markup. If win rate is over about 70% and the calendar is full, test 5 to 10% higher on new quotes.
- Launch the unsold-quote cadence (1 hour, day 1, 3, 7, 14, 30) and work the last 90 days of open quotes. Track recovered revenue as its own source.
- Plan offer on every eligible job; offer rate target 90%+. Draft the three tiers and the plan margin.
- Win-back list by each customer's rhythm: top 15 by past gross profit, call anyone who complained.

Exit check: quote win rate, median quote time, and recovered revenue reported; plan offer live.

## Days 31 to 60: controlled acquisition

- **Neighbor and route work:** doorstep offer around every job; street-coded offers; yard signs where permitted.
- **One paid channel**, chosen by trade:
  - Urgent trades (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, pest, junk): LSA on profitable job types and tight areas; weekly budget for 20 to 30 leads; weekly reconciliation. Search as second channel at the LocaliQ CPL sanity check.
  - Recurring and visual (cleaning, lawn, pressure washing, windows, detailing, pet): Nextdoor (organic first, $5+ a day ads) or Meta with real before/after and a qualifier question.
  - Considered purchases (painting, landscaping projects, photography): Google profile, portfolio pages and partner outreach before ads.
- **Offline test:** 500 to 2,000 homes around existing clusters, mailed or hung 3 times, tracked number or code; EDDM postage $0.260 a piece, so 2,000 pieces start at $520 before print.
- **One marketplace at most**, only if answer speed is proven.
- Decision rule: scale only when booked CAC is at or under the target from first-job GP plus realistic repeat value. Kill after the sample if the best plausible case still misses.

Exit check: booked and completed CAC by channel; capacity used; no channel generating leads faster than the team answers.

## Days 61 to 90: densify and scale the winners

- Heat map recurring customers by ZIP and time between stops. Stop marketing to remote micro-clusters; price outliers separately; push referral rewards for adjacent houses.
- Partnerships: the two partner types with the best fit (property managers, realtors, builders, venues, vets) get the operational offer: priority slots, photo completion reports, invoicing by property.
- Commercial: if the trade fits, start the walk-in and permit routine with a name and renewal month as the goal of each visit.
- Seasonal plan for the next 6 months: pre-sell with deposits before the peak; trough offers ready.
- Budget moves to channels with known GP CAC and capacity to serve; raise prices where the calendar is overfull.

## Day 90 review: seven numbers

1. Qualified-lead rate by source
2. Median reply time and answer rate
3. Booking rate
4. Quote win rate (and by tier)
5. Completed-job CAC by channel
6. First-job GP CAC by channel
7. 30, 60 and 90-day repeat or plan revenue by source

Then: kill channels that cannot meet GP CAC, raise prices where capacity is full, deepen routes where drive time is high, and add budget only where downstream economics are known.

## Weekly cadence after day 90

- Monday: unanswered leads, open quotes over 3 days, LSA reconciliation and disputes.
- Wednesday: route map and next week's gaps; neighbor offers for streets with jobs.
- Friday: reviews received vs requested; win-back calls; one-line owner report with the seven numbers.

## Questions to ask the owner at each phase end

- Which jobs do you want more of, and which would you happily lose?
- How many more jobs a week can the crew take without hiring?
- Which towns are you willing to drive to, and at what minimum price?
- What offers are you willing to run (intro price, plan discount, referral reward)?
- Who may change prices and send messages in your name?
