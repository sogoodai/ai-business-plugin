## Creator outreach

**Cold DM or email to a nano or micro creator (template, built from the gifting model in r/Entrepreneur 2,730-pt thread and TikTok sample rules):**

> Hi {{first name}}, I make {{product}} for {{audience}}. Your {{specific video or post}} on {{topic}} is exactly how our customers use it. Can I send you one, free, no strings? If you like it and want to show it, great. If not, keep it. If you'd rather do a paid video, tell me your rate for a 30 to 45 second piece and whether you'd allow us to run it as an ad for 60 days.

**Follow-up after 5 days (template):**

> Following up on the {{product}}. Happy to ship this week if you want one. {{one line of proof: "Our {{color}} version sold out twice this month"}}.

**TikTok Shop sample approval message (template):**

> Approved! Ships within 2 days. Here is a 60-second video on how people use it and what they ask about: {{link}}. The videos that sell open on {{problem}} and show {{demo}} in the first 3 seconds. Posting window is 14 days after it arrives. Your commission is {{X}}%; reply if you want a higher rate on a second video.

**Creator brief (verbatim, research report 2026):**

> "The goal is not a polished commercial. Show the product naturally in {{specific situation}}. Open on the problem or surprising result in the first beat. Demonstrate {{mechanism/proof}} visibly. Please do not make claims outside the approved claim sheet. Required: product shown in use, one clear reason to believe, one objection answered, and a direct CTA. Avoid scripted brand-history intros."

**Brief for creators you trust (verbatim, backpack brand):** "Just make stuff your audience will watch & enjoy."

**Usage-rights ask (template):**

> For this one we'd like the raw file and the right to run it as a partnership ad (or Spark code) for 60 days, with edits to captions and length. What would you add to your rate for that? We'll ask before renewing.

## Wholesale

**First pitch (verbatim template, research report 2026):**

> "Hi {{name}}, I noticed {{store}} carries {{specific adjacent brand/category}}. Our {{product}} is designed for {{customer/use case}}, retails at {{MSRP}}, and gives retailers {{gross-margin or merchandising fact}}. I can send the line sheet plus our top three opening SKUs and reorder data. Worth a look?"

**Sample drop note (template, from the cafe-roaster method):**

> Thanks for trying {{product}}. Wholesale is {{price}} per unit, suggested retail {{MSRP}}, minimum order {{min}}, and we deliver free next day within {{area}}. The three that sell first in shops like yours: {{SKU 1}}, {{SKU 2}}, {{SKU 3}}. I'll check in Thursday.

**Reorder prompt, day 30 to 40 (template, from Faire's reorder timing):**

> Hi {{name}}, it's been {{N}} weeks since your first order. Most shops reorder when they're down to about a quarter of the shelf. How's {{top SKU}} moving? I can hold {{qty}} for delivery {{date}}.

**Countering risky terms (template, from r/smallbusiness 925-pt thread):**

> We'd love to be in your stores. For a first order we work prepaid, standard labels and scents, no returns of unsold stock. If it sells through, we're happy to talk terms and custom options on the second order. Can we start with {{smaller qty}} on that basis?

**Supplier-question deflection when a customer asks where you buy materials (verbatim, r/EtsySellers):** "I get my supplies wholesale, and there's a $5k minimum purchase. Give me your resale certificate number and I'll pass it on to my sales rep to reach out to you."

## Press

**Gift-guide pitch (verbatim, r/ecommerce 270-pt thread):**

> "Saw your post on ___ sending a tip for any Valentine's Day Gift Guides! If included I'll have the social team re-share it!"

Attach one photo and the price. Send Thursday or Friday, never Monday. After 3 days, reply with only: "Following up!" (credited with over half of placements).

## Review requests that stay inside the rules

General rule: ask everyone, the same way, never conditioned on a positive review, never with a reward tied to the review, never asking anyone to change or remove a review. Amazon bans incentives and positive-only requests outright; the FTC rule bans incentives conditioned on sentiment and requires disclosure of any incentive.

**Amazon insert (verbatim, research report 2026):**

> "Thank you for your order. We hope the product performs as expected. If you'd like to share your experience, please leave an honest product review through Amazon. Your feedback helps other customers make an informed decision."

Plus the Request a Review button on every eligible order. No website links, discount codes or "contact us first if unhappy" lines on Amazon inserts.

**Etsy insert about Etsy's automatic reminders (verbatim, r/EtsySellers):**

> "Etsy may send you unsolicited reminders asking for a review after your purchase. Please know these messages are automated by Etsy and are not sent or requested by me. I truly appreciate your order!"

**Own store, after delivery plus the time it takes to see a result (template):**

> Subject: How is your {{product}}?
> Hi {{first name}}, your {{product}} arrived {{N}} days ago. If you have a minute, tell other buyers what it's like: {{review link}}. A photo helps most. If anything isn't right, reply to this email and I'll fix it.

Ask reviewers to name the product or problem ("feel free to mention which {{product}} you got"), and reply naming it back; reviews that name the item rank and convert better (r/smallbusiness, 320-pt thread).

## Customer service

**Someone asks how you make it (verbatim, 939 up):** "It's cool you're interested in [craft]! I've been perfecting my skills for [x] years. I'm not much of a teacher though - here's a link to help you get started."

**Hostile complaint, written as evidence (verbatim, 222 up):** "Thanks for reaching out. As described in the listing, this item is made of [...]. Our refund policy is [...]." Attach a listing screenshot with the relevant lines circled.

**Unclear 3-star, private message (verbatim, 17 up):** "Hi there, thank you for your feedback on X item. I would love to know if there is anything that could have improved your experience. Thank you for supporting my shop!"

**Public reply to a vague negative review (verbatim, 28 up):** "So sorry you weren't fully satisfied! Please reach out to us so we can make it right!"

**Public reply to a size complaint (verbatim):** "Yes, the box is indeed small because, as stated in the description, it is made specifically for rings. Complete measurements of the box are listed in the description and are always available prior to purchase."

**Public reply when a buyer saw your photos on other sites (verbatim, 205 up):** "I'm glad you liked them! Unfortunately, the reason you are seeing my products on other websites is because my photos have been stolen by scammers. Rest assured that you have purchased the original product and that these are my photos."

**False "never arrived" when the buyer posted the item (verbatim, r/EtsySellers 720-pt thread):** "upon further investigation I can confidently say that you definitely have my one-of-a-kind, handmade item in your possession. I want to thank you for supporting my shop and my passion. I was honoured to see it make an appearance on your Instagram account. If you could please tag my shop in the future I would be so grateful!"

**"Delivered but missing" (verbatim sequence, r/ecommerce; package turns up about 75% of the time):** First confirm the address (wallets autofill old ones). Then: "I'll put in a missing mail request with the carrier -- occasionally they'll prematurely mark an order as delivered..." / "call your local post office and give them the tracking number" / "If they're insisting ... I'll put in a mail theft claim with the carrier. After that, we can get the ball rolling on sending a replacement shipment."

**Chargeback filed while the parcel is in transit (verbatim, r/shopify):** "hey what's up saw you filed a chargeback??" then a second email once delivery is confirmed. The buyer apologized and withdrew it.

**Separate shipping explained (verbatim):** "We prefer to be transparent and separate the cost of the item and the shipping. Since we sell items that are usually bought in multiples, this lets us adjust the shipping so that you are not paying too much"

**Personalization field (verbatim):** "Type name exactly as you would like it printed. Double check for spelling errors and grammatical mistakes. To keep production flowing as quickly as possible a mockup will not be sent prior to production."

**Unusual measurements on a made-to-measure order (verbatim):** "I noticed these are not quite what I usually see, I usually expect a range of x-x, just double checking that measurement was correct"

## Past buyers and referrals

**Founder email to past customers (verbatim, r/ecommerce; about 4x the conversion of automation for that store):** "Hi Steve! We're not nominated for an Oscar tonight but we still have great ___ ... We're offering 10% off to past customers because we loved them and really want Michigan to beat Ohio State this weekend. I'm the founder if you need anything or want to tear apart the product so I can learn."

**One-year subscriber referral (verbatim, 154-pt thread):** "Hi ___, I'm a founder, thank you for being a subscriber for a year, if there is anyone you know who would love [Your Company], I would be happy to send them a [Your Product] (on us of course)." Put a subscription coupon in the gifted box.

**Asking a well-connected customer to share (verbatim line):** "no worries whatsoever if you can't we're just trying to grow the old-fashioned way"

**Slump email to past buyers (template, from r/ecommerce):** plain text, from the founder, subject "what's new", three lines on what changed and one link.
