# Wholesale playbook

For brands selling to independent shops, cafes, galleries, gift stores, subscription boxes and, later, chains. Wholesale is a reorder engine, not a prospect list: the money is in the second to fifth order.

## The reorder facts (Faire, 2026; marketplace operator, so biased, but operational)

From active brands with 200+ lifetime orders on Faire:

- **70% of reorders happen within 90 days** of the previous order.
- **About 60% of reorders are placed when the retailer's stock is at 25% or less.**
- **Average reorder cadence is about 45 days.**
- **A retailer reaching a 5th order is about 2.5x likelier to become a long-term account.**

Agent rule: start a reorder timer on every first order. Check in at the expected sell-through window, prompt a reorder before day 45, and push each account toward order five. Cross-sell new SKUs only after the opening SKUs show sell-through.

## Building the account list

- **Google Maps list:** independent cafes, gift shops or boutiques within your delivery radius. One coffee roaster built 300+ contacts within 40 miles, sent free samples with a Canva catalog (photo, unit wholesale price, suggested retail price, minimum order) and reported 20 to 30 accounts and about $40k a month within 4 months (r/Entrepreneur, 1,876-pt thread; margins challenged by commenters, so copy the method, not the numbers).
- **Beat distributors on service, not price:** a $100 minimum order with free next-day delivery on tight routes (about 10 stops within 5 to 10 miles; drivers paid mileage plus $5 a stop).
- **Find stores that already stock adjacent brands** (search the brand on Google Maps, Instagram tags and Faire) and pitch with that reference; the research pitch template is in [scripts.md](scripts.md).
- **Subscription boxes:** find them on Cratejoy and pitch directly. One brand got a 5,000-piece order in 30 days at a deep discount (r/ecommerce, 150-pt thread). Price the labor before you say yes.
- **Galleries and makers:** 30% off retail with a $500 minimum order worked for years for one Etsy seller and got the work into galleries (r/EtsySellers).
- **Complementary makers:** sell components or put partner cards in each other's orders with a tracked code.
- **Repeat Etsy resellers:** when someone keeps clearing out your shop, make one custom listing for their next batch and raise public prices.
- **Local government and offices:** one woodworker sold a single $10k conference table to a county court.
- **Teach the technique that needs your product.** The Bob Ross line was stocked at a national craft chain after the show taught a method that required its stiff paint (r/Entrepreneur, 572 up).

Do not invent wholesale reply rates for the brand. Track its own denominator: delivered emails, replies, samples sent, opening orders, second orders, fifth orders.

## Terms

- **Get paid upfront from new accounts.** A boutique chain asked a candle maker for custom labels, custom scents, net 60 and returns of unsold stock within 30 days; the maker countered with a smaller prepaid trial order and walked away when refused (r/smallbusiness, 925-pt thread).
- **Fund big orders with deposits.** One founder took a 50% deposit on a large order and used it to buy the inventory (r/Entrepreneur, 1,914-pt thread).
- **Digital catalog, not a printed lookbook.** A printed lookbook cost "thousands" and had 5 price changes and a stockout before it printed; a catalog built from the product spreadsheet cut order errors to "near zero" (r/ecommerce, 80-pt thread).
- **Seasonal businesses:** if 60%+ of revenue lands in November to January, aim for one recurring wholesale account that covers rent, or presell holiday orders to stores in summer.

## Big retail: read before saying yes

One DTC brand's Target program (r/ecommerce, 115-pt thread) is the cautionary case:

- $45 wholesale on an $80 retail item, 1,800 stores at 3 to 5 units each.
- Returns: under 1% on Shopify, under 3.5% on Amazon, 6% on QVC, about 16% at Target, from the same batch.
- Return-to-vendor rights were not negotiated; 60 to 80% end-of-program markdowns were on the table.
- A surprise Labor Day discount at Target removed the brand's Amazon Buy Box and cost "six figures" in a weekend.
- They cancelled a 10,000-unit reorder when sell-through missed.

Rules: negotiate returns, markdown funding and promo approval in writing; use separate SKUs, packaging or GS1 barcodes per channel so one retailer's price does not suppress your Amazon listing; model sell-through, not the opening order.

## Protect price across channels

- **Screen wholesale accounts and enforce MAP.** About 10 unauthorized sellers dropped one brand's $79.99 item to about $65 on Amazon; Buy Box share tanked and stockists complained (r/ecommerce).
- **Find diverted stock** with test purchases and lot numbers.
- **When a reseller marks you up 3x online,** raise your own retail price; the arbitrage disappears.

## 90-day plan: wholesale

**Days 1 to 14:** wholesale price list with suggested retail and minimums; contribution per case after samples and delivery; digital catalog; sample kit; terms (prepaid first order, minimum, delivery promise).
**Days 15 to 45:** a fixed weekly batch of prospects from Maps and adjacent-brand stockists (the owner sets the number the samples budget allows); samples only to the best-fit stores; pitch from [scripts.md](scripts.md); log every step of the funnel.
**Days 46 to 75:** opening orders shipped with merchandising assets (shelf card, photos, a one-line staff pitch); reorder timer at day 30 and a prompt before day 45.
**Days 76 to 90:** second-order rate by account; push top accounts toward order five with new SKUs only where the core sells through; exit check: every account past day 45 contacted, second-order rate measured and rising, contribution per account positive after samples and delivery.
