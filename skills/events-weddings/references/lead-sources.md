# Lead sources: playbooks by channel

Order of work for a small vendor: referrals and preferred lists, Google and owned pages, Instagram as a portfolio, then paid channels. Marketplaces and shows are judged on cohort contribution, never on lead count. Platform depth lives in `local-seo`, `organic-social` and `paid-ads`; this file keeps the wedding-specific angles.

## Attribution first

- Ask two questions on every form and call: "How did you first hear about us?" and "What made you reach out today?" A couple finds you on Instagram, Googles your name, reads reviews, then fills the website form; last click credits the website alone (research pass, 2026).
- Store the named referrer ("Jess at Barn Hill"), never a generic "referral" bucket.
- Venue-review and word-of-mouth bookings can't carry tracking. Ask on the call where they saw your work; that is how one photographer counted 30+ bookings from venue reviews (r/WeddingPhotography 197).
- Keep inquiry forms short: date, venue or location, guest count or scope, budget band, how they heard. Extra fields make couples second-guess contacting you (r/weddingvideography 62).

## Referrals and preferred vendor lists

Highest intent and cheapest. Referrals averaged 40.2% of bookings in a 2026 survey of 81 vendors; 66% of pros were on at least one preferred list in 2022 (WeddingPro, possibly outdated).

Build it:

1. List the 20 to 30 venues, planners and vendors who touch your couples. Rank by overlap and past jobs together.
2. Give first. Send each partner what helps them sell: a vendor gallery of their setup, tables, florals or space within days of the event, with free web and social use and a credit line.
3. Ask for their list criteria, then send a one-page partner sheet: price range, service area, ideal client, insurance, three recent galleries at their venue.
4. Refer suitable clients to them before asking for reciprocity. Track sent and received by partner.
5. Show up at their open days and tastings. A couple met a $1,200 photographer at a venue open house and catering tasting, switched from a $950 photographer and forfeited a $200 deposit because they connected in person (r/Weddingsunder10k 1136, buyer report).

Planners are the luxury gate. A 20-year vendor: luxury work comes from "service for the planners": next-day teasers with the images the planner needs first, communication with the planner, gifts, and free photos at the planner's personal events (r/WeddingPhotography 123). A luxury photographer names planners as the second-largest referral source after past couples and bridal parties (r/WeddingPhotography 276).

Venue bundles: a videographer reportedly went from 10 to 15 weddings to 80 to 100 a year by getting venues to add him to their quotes as the only recommended supplier for a 500 per wedding cut (secondhand, r/weddingvideography 16). Run the partner math in [economics.md](economics.md) first.

What didn't work: emailing 30+ preferred vendors offering free off-season headshots got 3 takers and no photo bookings, though one DJ connection led to MC work paying twice a second-shooting fee (r/WeddingPhotography 119). Cold freebies to strangers underperform service to people you already worked with.

Turning conflict into a relationship:

- A planner used your photos uncredited: ask for a tag and offer more images relevant to their work, then propose coffee about their preferred list (92-upvote script in [scripts.md](scripts.md)). Commenters were split; never let it cost the couple's privacy terms.
- A venue used your photo with AI-altered faces in an ad: one photographer explained the risk to the couple and the venue, and negotiated recommended-supplier status plus a display at open days; that weekend's open day produced one booking (r/WeddingPhotography 177). Others objected to rewarding unauthorized use (35 upvotes). The owner decides.
- Venue sends a "photographer agreement" claiming copyright: decline the ownership terms (they conflict with the couple's contract), loop in the couple or planner, and offer the venue free use through your vendor gallery policy. The 183-upvote commenter reports no pushback across several venues.

## Google and owned pages

Google is the main owned channel for photo, video, DJ, venue and florist searches. Google's local ranking factors are relevance, distance and prominence; AI Overviews and AI Mode use the same index with no special markup (Google, 2025 to 2026). GBP setup, categories, reviews and AI visibility depth: `local-seo`.

Site structure that ranks (wedding vendor who grew a business past $700K on SEO alone, r/weddingvideography 62 and 23):

- **Homepage = primary city page.** Title: "[City] Wedding Videographer | [Brand]" with the service term people search ("videographer", "wedding videos") before the brand. Only that city, work shot there, 2 to 3 inquiry buttons.
- **Service pages** (/wedding-photography) explain the service for conversion; don't geo-target them, or they compete with the homepage.
- **Location pages** for secondary markets you actually serve (/houston-wedding-videographer), each with unique content and local work.
- **Venue pages:** one evergreen page per venue with search demand where you've worked, combining all weddings there with useful facts (history, capacity, nearby hotels, prices, where the light is). Not six posts about six couples at the same venue.
- **Pricing page:** short, with "starting at" at minimum. It filters bad leads.
- **One /reviews page,** not one page per testimonial.
- **Contact page:** minimum fields.

Niche pages win in crowded markets: courthouse-style weddings brought nearly 40% of one Melbourne photographer's enquiries (r/WeddingPhotography 445); an LGBTQ+ and alternative niche in a rural conservative market ranked first in city and state and made 2024 bookings mostly Google-sourced (r/WeddingPhotography 119). Candidates: courthouse, elopement, micro wedding, LGBTQ+, cultural and religious weddings (South Asian, Jewish, Catholic church), destination, venue types (barn, winery, rooftop).

Blogging conflict: one photographer gets inquiries within a day or two of every Thursday post (10 to 15 images, written text; r/WeddingPhotography 75), often for other venues. The SEO vendor calls per-couple posts link-equity waste. Rule: put SEO effort into venue and niche pages; keep couple posts only as proof to send clients, noindexed if they target nothing searched; judge a blog by total inquiries in the following week, not by which venue it named.

Venue reviews (two practitioners): after posting Instagram sneak peeks, leave a Google review on the venue's listing with 1 to 2 wedding photos and your Instagram handle at the end; after the next wedding there, edit the same review and add new photos (claimed to move it back to most recent). One credits 30+ bookings over five years and a preferred-list spot; another got 4.3M photo views and no traceable bookings (r/WeddingPhotography 197). Low cost, uneven return: do it for venues where your ideal couples marry.

AI search for corporate buyers: 75% of planners now use AI in venue sourcing (Cvent 2026). Put capacity, layouts, parking, AV, power, catering rules and indicative rates in plain text on the page, not inside image PDFs.

Never use a fake or generic street address on a GBP; a wedding photographer reports a suspension after five years of reviews and top-three ranking (r/GoogleMyBusiness 18). Service-area businesses hide the address. See `local-seo`.

## Instagram and social

Instagram ranks on watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach, and favors original content (Buffer summary of Meta, 2025 to 2026). Couples use it as a portfolio and a shortlist.

- **Carousel after every reel.** A former bride screened 50+ vendor profiles and skipped reels-only ones before reaching the website (r/WeddingPhotography 221). Reel for reach, carousel of finished stills right after so the grid shows the style.
- **Send-worthy hooks:** "Send this to whoever is choosing your venue: the three costs that aren't in the headline rental fee." "Before you hire a photographer, ask to see a full gallery from a rainy wedding." "Three questions to ask your DJ before you sign." KPI is sends per reach and watch time, not followers.
- **Bands and DJs:** post clips with the actual live audio at a real wedding; couples can't judge a band from reels with overlaid songs (r/WeddingPhotography thread, buyer report).
- **Tag the venue.** Couples browse a venue's tagged photos to find vendors who already know the space (bride, 10 upvotes).
- **Post the couple the day before the wedding** (reuse an engagement image: "Can't wait to photograph these two tomorrow"), so they can share it when guests ask who the photographer is (r/WeddingPhotography 69).
- **Destination and multi-day weddings:** release a few rehearsal or welcome-party previews before the wedding day while guests are gathered and watching the couple's stories; guests recognized and cooperated with the photographer (r/WeddingPhotography 69).
- **Get highlights out fast.** Guests who shoot only highlights post and tag the couple, venue and vendors while the hired photographer culls thousands of images; their posts get the reshares (r/WeddingPhotography 58-upvote comment). Send a teaser within days.
- **Don't use Instagram Collaborations on posts you need to keep.** If the invited collaborator deletes their account, your post is deleted permanently (r/WeddingPhotography 90).
- **Post the work you want more of.** Photographers debate posting every wedding; post enough real weddings to prove consistency, weighted toward your target couples and venues (r/WeddingPhotography 48).
- Opening shots matter: a videographer's reel opened with a bride standing in water; a commenter read it as "I ain't doing that" (r/weddingvideography 55). Open on moments couples want, not stunts they fear.

Facebook groups: a 13-year videographer never booked a wedding from Facebook wedding groups where 80+ vendors answer every request (r/weddingvideography 18; Colorado photographer, r/WeddingPhotography 85). Publish your own posts that invite inquiries instead of competing in comment pile-ons.

Content cadence, formats and DM funnels: `organic-social`.

## Marketplaces (The Knot, WeddingWire, Zola, Easy Weddings, Hitched, Bridebook)

- **Intent is lower.** Knot leads tour at 10%, WeddingWire 7.4%, Zola 4.0%, against 25.4% for direct email (Mikla 2026). 66% of Knot leads never reply after the first response.
- **Pricing is opaque.** No public rate card; varies by market and category; a 2025 product mixes a lower base fee with performance spend (The Knot Worldwide).
- **Contract risk is real.** Operators report pressure sales, cancellation refused 5 days after signing, debt collection after a card was cancelled (r/weddingvideography 19, 2026), and a requirement to prove the business closed before a listing came down (r/WeddingPhotography 91). Former employees alleged fake leads and inflated rates; Senator Grassley asked the FTC to investigate in October 2025 (r/WeddingPhotography 171, 113). These are allegations, not findings. One photographer reports getting a termination notice and pro rata refund offer after replacing the storefront content with criticism of the platform (r/WeddingPhotography 188, single unverified case; do not recommend as a tactic).
- **Before signing:** get the term, auto-renew, cancellation window and refund rules in writing; prefer month-to-month; check the category's local saturation (how many vendors pay for the same city page).
- **While listed:** answer within the hour (marketplace couples contact several vendors at once), include price and a booking link in the first reply, move the conversation to text or email, and get reviews there too (5+ from the last 2 years).
- **Keep or cut** on the cohort CMER in [economics.md](economics.md) after a full booking cycle. One commenter booked year-round on The Knot plus a website alone; another paid $350 a month for four months and zero bookings after moving markets.

## Styled shoots

No credible 2025 to 2026 data links styled shoots to bookings or CAC (research pass). Treat them as asset production and relationship building.

- Approve one only if it produces at least two of: a venue relationship, a planner or photographer relationship, images for a venue or niche page, publication-worthy work, or portfolio coverage in a missing segment (luxury venue, beach, mountain elopement).
- Label styled images on your site (39-upvote commenter) and replace them with real weddings as they come. Never send a styled gallery as if it were a real wedding (r/WeddingPhotography 109).
- Check the venue's styled imagery against real-day layout: one photographer found every promoted portrait spot blocked by tables and guests (r/WeddingPhotography 109). Promise only what a real day allows.
- Write a usage agreement: who can use which images, credits, submissions.

## Bridal shows and expos

Weak public data. Adapt the trade-show discipline from marketingskills `events` (MIT):

- **Pick by audience, not size.** One photographer booked 5 weddings at an alternative and LGBTQ+ show and 0 at a general January show (r/WeddingPhotography 119).
- **Pre-book:** announce your booth to past inquiries and engaged followers with a reason to visit (date check, price guide, a live demo for DJs and bands).
- **Qualify in 30 seconds:** "When and where is the wedding?" Then date availability on the spot. Capture date, venue, guest count and one note per real conversation.
- **Follow up within 24 to 48 hours,** tiered: hot (date open, budget fits) get a personal message and a call link; warm get one useful asset; scan-only get one light touch.
- **Judge on collected contribution after 6 to 12 months, over two editions.** Include staff time, samples and follow-up hours in cost.

## Paid ads

Only after the first-reply system and price page work. Account mechanics: `paid-ads`.

- **Google Search** for photo, video, DJ and venue intent: geo plus service terms, venue-name terms, niche terms. Count only submitted inquiry forms as conversions (not clicks, page views or phone taps). Negatives for jobs, courses, cheap, free, school, travel. Arch Wedding Studio moved click-to-inquiry from 0.99% to 2.43% and inquiries +53% at the same CPL this way (Live PPC Ads, 2026, agency case).
- **Meta for visual categories** (venues, florals, decor, rentals): ad -> cost calculator or brochure -> instant WhatsApp, SMS or email follow-up -> self-scheduled tour. The Hidden Oak case reported 6 bookings worth £112,624 from £1,300 (outlier; copy the architecture).
- **Copy filters budget.** A photographer getting price objections was told to remove "affordable" from ad copy (r/WeddingPhotography 50). Words attract the budget they name.
- **New videographers:** shoot 2 to 4 weddings at a low rate to build a reel, then spend a little on Facebook ads (r/weddingvideography 18). Never run ads without a real-wedding reel.
- Ticketed public events: $50 a week of Eventbrite ads surfaced a $20 tech event inside Eventbrite and sold about 100 tickets a week (r/EventProduction 16, incrementality unknown).

## Marketing at the wedding itself

Guests are next year's couples. Operators report:

- **Same-day highlight on loop:** 30 to 40 predictable clips, one redundant card to an iPad, a basic LUT, 1.5 to 2 seconds per clip, logo at the end, no audio; about 30 minutes of editing at dinner; plays on loop all night (r/weddingvideography 87). Some guests find screens distracting: ask the couple.
- **Wi-Fi frame near the bar or the bathroom route** showing in-camera starred images (r/weddingvideography 16-upvote comment).
- **Framed print on the couple's table:** Canon Selphy CP1500 (about $120) plus a $5 frame, 7 to 8 minutes during the meal, not promised in the contract (r/WeddingPhotography 199).
- **Sneak-peek slideshow beside gallery-link cards with QR codes** where departing guests pass (r/WeddingPhotography 7-upvote comment; one reply called it tacky).
- **Don't force a reveal.** A videographer spent her meal break on a quick edit; the couple declined to watch until the hotel (r/WeddingPhotography 64). Leave a mini album in the card box or email the preview next day.
- **Carry an emergency kit** (lighter, sewing kit, stain wipes). A photographer who lit candles and sewed a button got a $500 bonus from the planner (r/WeddingPhotography 581). Planners remember who solved problems.
- **Be helpful to other vendors.** An event photographer lent a backup camera to a rival whose lens broke, then gave a card and shared his footage (r/photography 1494). Vendor goodwill is the referral graph.

## Spec and PR plays (event producers, DJs, florists)

- A drone photographer shot a public watch party on spec and posted an aerial image; the sports commission hired him for the next game and the victory parade (r/photography 1643).
- A florist gifted a themed arrangement to a local bookstore to get the work seen (r/florists 9257); a high-end florist built a name by gifting bouquets to businesses where target customers shop, plus small events (r/florists 100-upvote comment). Commenters suggest golf courses and caterers.
- A DJ left a card at a venue open three months and got a booking call within days, then learned from the house DJ that three previous DJs were never paid (r/DJs 139). Ask the current act about payment before accepting.
- An unusual public event (adult book fair) earned TV, radio, newspaper and magazine coverage estimated at $10,000 and drew 8,100 people (r/EventProduction 25).
