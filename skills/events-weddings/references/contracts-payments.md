# Contracts and payments

The contract protects the date, the cash and the scope. Everything here is an operating brief for the owner and a local lawyer, not legal advice. Consumer law on deposits, cancellations and force majeure differs by country and US state; the label "non-refundable" does not decide enforceability (Sprintlaw UK, US and Australia guidance, 2026). Sources in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. Payment schedule

**Data:** average first payment 38% of the contract; 35 to 50% the most common first payment in two-payment schedules (That's The One, 21,802 wedding payments, 2026, private platform data). 63.5% of US photographers use 2 or 3 payment milestones (HoneyBook, 425 photographers, 2026).

**Default to propose (owner decides):**

| Payment | When | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Booking payment, 30 to 50% | At signing, with the contract | Reserving the date, initial planning work |
| Midpoint (optional) | Halfway, or 6 months out for long lead times | Cash flow for florists, caterers, rentals buying stock |
| Balance | 14 to 30 days before the event | Everything else |
| Extras | Invoiced before delivery | Overtime, add-ons, travel |

Rules operators learned the hard way:

- **No exceptions, including friends and planners' relatives.** A photographer let a friend pay "before arrival"; one-third arrived the day before and the rest was still unpaid days after (r/WeddingPhotography 98). Another made one exception for a planner's niece and was ghosted for a week before payment (r/WeddingPhotography 110): "never ever make exceptions to your terms."
- **A small retainer invites cancellation.** A $250 retainer: four cancellations in one year, couples haggling for 2 to 3 hour coverage instead (r/WeddingPhotography 128). Retainers should hurt to walk away from.
- **Payment after delivery is a red flag.** A parent who demanded to pay after images arrived had already been declined by four other photographers (r/WeddingPhotography 339).
- **Nothing ships before the balance lands.** Day after the wedding, a warm message plus "sneak peeks and editing begin once payment reaches the account" (r/WeddingPhotography 98). Don't deliver within a week on a promise to pay later (r/photography 1440).
- **Don't spend unearned money.** "Not a dime of my clients' money gets spent until my services are complete" (18 upvotes). Track upcoming vs completed contract money; treat only deposits and completed-event money as safe (r/weddingvideography 33). A couple paid $3,000 in full a year early, then cancelled and wanted it back minus the deposit.
- **Card fees:** about 3%. Price them in or offer ACH and bank transfer; check local surcharge rules before adding a card fee line.

## 2. The booking rule

"The first couple to sign the contract and pay the booking payment gets the date." Say it in the first reply, on the call, and in the contract email. Footer for every pre-booking email: "A booking fee of 30% is required to secure your date. Email correspondence does not guarantee a booking; dates are not held without a booking fee." (r/WeddingPhotography 88). Contract links expire (operators use 4 days, 10 days, 2 weeks). Full closing process in [inquiry-to-booking.md](inquiry-to-booking.md).

A couple who lost a date to a faster couple may leave a bad review. Reply with the facts, politely: consultation happened, neither contract nor retainer arrived, another couple completed both ([scripts.md](scripts.md); 178-upvote advice, r/WeddingPhotography 252).

## 3. What the contract must define

Checklist from the research pass (The Knot 2025 lawyer-reviewed guide; Sprintlaw 2026) plus the disputes operators report:

| Clause | Write it so | Dispute it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| Parties | Both partners sign and are the contact; parents may pay but don't direct | Parent-controlled bookings (r/WeddingPhotography 339) |
| Date, every address, start and end time | Full venue addresses and both partners' phone numbers at signing | Wrong venue, refund (r/WeddingPhotography 241); 11-hour, 3-location itinerary 72 hours out (r/WeddingPhotography 1150) |
| Coverage time | Consecutive; setup and travel between locations inside coverage; meal break terms | 4-hour couple expecting arrival 2 hours early (r/weddingvideography 19); hike-in coverage (r/WeddingPhotography 209) |
| Deliverables | Count range, film lengths min and max, formats, web vs print files, delivery window | Film length dispute (r/weddingvideography 18); couple printing 800px web files and complaining of grain (r/photography 1440) |
| What editing excludes | Body reshaping, heavy retouching, style changes from the portfolio | Refund demand (r/WeddingPhotography 80); 3-day-out style change request (r/WeddingPhotography 67) |
| RAW and unselected files | Not included; price for release stated | "Send all photos" (r/WeddingPhotography 274) |
| Overtime | Hourly rate; extension agreed with the couple on the day | Associate staying 2 extra unpaid hours (r/WeddingPhotography 333) |
| Travel, parking, accommodation | Included, capped, or reimbursed at cost with notice | $75 hotel parking surprise (r/WeddingPhotography 333) |
| Communication duty | Couple returns planning details by a date; without them the vendor leads the timeline and can't guarantee shots | Non-responsive bride (r/WeddingPhotography 186, 523) |
| Restrictions | Couple obtains officiant and venue rules | Church rules changing on the day (r/WeddingPhotography 392) |
| Image use | Portfolio and social use, opt-out with price consequence, privacy lines (no children, no intimate moments) | Marketing refusal after a discount (r/WeddingPhotography 211) |
| Copyright | Vendor retains copyright; couple gets a personal license; vendor galleries for other vendors under a credit rule | Venue "photographer agreement" claiming ownership (r/WeddingPhotography 501) |
| Price changes | Substitution rights and cost-escalation terms for perishable or wholesale goods | Florist losing money on pre-signed contracts (r/smallbusiness 175 upvotes) |
| Rescheduling | Separate from cancellation; repricing if the new date is in a higher season, weekday or new calendar year | Couples moving a winter date into June at the winter price |
| Cancellation | In writing; staged consequences tied to timing, work done, committed third-party costs and loss of the date | Refund fights (section 4) |
| Vendor illness or replacement | Named replacement standard, approval process, refund rule | Associate disclosure dispute (r/WeddingPhotography 49) |
| Force majeure | Defined events, suspension or rescheduling, termination timing, prepaid costs | Pandemic-era cancellations |
| Dispute and jurisdiction | Where and how disputes are handled | |
| Contract edits | Changes reviewed by the vendor's lawyer at the client's cost | Endless redlines |

Contract-edit script some operators use: "Any edits to our contract will be presented to our attorney at a rate of $400 per hour. That fee is paid upfront so that our attorney can properly advise us, but it does not guarantee acceptance of the suggested changes." The commenter claims 99% back down (3 upvotes, unverified). Use only if the owner is comfortable with the tone.

## 4. Cancellation and postponement

**Counsel briefs** (research pass, 2026; for a local attorney, not plug-in clauses):

> "Client cancellation must be in writing and is effective when received. The booking payment compensates Vendor for reserving the event date and initial work, subject to applicable law. Any further cancellation charge will correspond to the timing of cancellation, work already performed, nonrecoverable third-party commitments, and reasonable loss from the reserved date. If Vendor rebooks the same date, any amount otherwise due will be adjusted where required by applicable law."

> "A requested date change is subject to availability. Existing payments will be applied to the replacement event to the extent stated here and permitted by law. Changes in season, weekday, scope, supplier cost or calendar year may require repricing under the then-current schedule."

**Jurisdiction notes** (verify locally):
- UK: consumer terms must be fair and transparent; retained sums should match the reserved date, work done and likely loss (Sprintlaw UK 2026).
- US: state exactly what the retainer is for, how cancellation happens, consequences by time window and how rebooking affects damages (Sprintlaw US 2026). Operators note that keeping money beyond the retainer "varies state by state" (r/WeddingPhotography 125).
- Australia: avoid generic "no refund" wording; define force majeure, suspension and rescheduling, termination timing and prepaid costs (Sprintlaw Australia 2026).
- France: Civil Code Article 1218 defines force majeure (outside the debtor's control, not reasonably foreseeable, effects unavoidable); a temporary impediment suspends performance, a permanent one can end the contract.

**How operators handle real cancellations** (common cases in 2024 to 2026 threads: illness with a doctor's note 4 months out, infidelity, divorce, "low on funds", a wedding called off four days before):

1. Get the cancellation in writing with the reason.
2. Read the contract and the local rule; don't improvise from sympathy or anger.
3. Compute what you can justify keeping: the booking payment, plus documented work done (meetings, engagement session, timelines and shot lists written, items ordered), plus third-party costs you can't recover, plus the loss if the date can't be resold. One photographer cancelled 4 days out kept the retainer and was working out how to bill timelines, shot lists and meetings (r/WeddingPhotography 125).
4. Refund the rest promptly, with a short cancellation addendum both sign. "Refund the balance and keep the deposit" was the top advice for a couple who cancelled and hired someone else (r/weddingvideography 33, 55 upvotes).
5. If the date is resold, adjust as the contract and law require.
6. Goodwill above the contract is the owner's call (one photographer considered a 75% refund on a 50% deposit, r/WeddingPhotography 194). Write it as a one-time gesture.

Some operators carve out "death in the family" as the only full-refund reason (r/WeddingPhotography 90; r/weddingvideography 33, 6 upvotes). Decide the list before it happens.

## 5. When the vendor fails

- **Late delivery:** if you're very late, deliver with an apology and an invoice that shows a 100% discount on the outstanding balance so nobody wonders whether payment is expected (a year-late video with $750 owed on $2,500; waiving supported by 125 and 59 upvotes, invoice format 7 upvotes, r/weddingvideography 22).
- **Missed shot:** apologize, offer what the couple actually wants. A missing family group became a replacement photo at the next family gathering; the couple called a discount excessive (r/WeddingPhotography 410). Compositing via a retouch service works for some couples, not this one.
- **Missed wedding:** a full refund plus a free bridal session didn't stop a bad review because the couple wanted photos of the actual day (r/WeddingPhotography 1641). Prevent it: final-month meeting, reconfirmation two days out, call the venue if the couple goes silent.
- **Double booking discovered:** tell the couple immediately. A photographer who sat on a known conflict for months lost a referral partner for good (r/WeddingPhotography 1641 thread, 6 upvotes).

## 6. Vendor, venue and planner agreements

- **Marketplaces (The Knot, WeddingWire and others):** get term, auto-renew, cancellation window and refund in writing before signing; prefer month-to-month. Operators report cancellation refused 5 days after signing and debt collection (r/weddingvideography 19), and a demand to prove the business had closed before removing a listing (r/WeddingPhotography 91). Former employees alleged fake leads; a US senator asked federal regulators to investigate in October 2025 (r/WeddingPhotography 171, 113). Allegations, not findings.
- **Venue "photographer agreements" claiming copyright:** decline the ownership terms, explain they conflict with the couple's contract, loop in the couple or planner, offer the vendor gallery with free use and credit (183 upvotes, no pushback across several venues, r/WeddingPhotography 501).
- **Image licensing to vendors:** tiered by relationship. Free to small vendors who reciprocate, low fee to similar vendors without a relationship, free or low-fee digital to small venues that credit 100% of the time and refer, normal digital licensing rates to corporate vendors that print and submit to editorial (r/WeddingPhotography 501, 7 upvotes).
- **Commission and bundle deals with venues:** a per-wedding cut to be the bundled supplier (500 per wedding, secondhand report of 10 to 15 weddings growing to 80 to 100, r/weddingvideography 16). Put it in writing, check disclosure rules, and run the partner math in [economics.md](economics.md).
- **Subcontractors and freelancers:** written brief with clock times, deliverables, payment date and image rights. Freelance florists report unpaid and month-late payments from big shops (r/florists 960).
- **Event producers on profit-share:** negotiate a share (the producer suggests at least 10% of profit) before running a bigger repeat of a successful event; asking afterward got nothing (r/EventProduction 25).
- **Corporate proposals:** state proposal validity, provisional hold expiry and what converts a hold to a contract, so an RFP that never signs doesn't block a date (research pass, 2026).

## 7. Collections

1. Friendly reminder with the invoice link on the due date.
2. Firm email citing the contract clause and a date 7 days out.
3. Pause services (no planning calls, no sneak peeks, no delivery).
4. Final notice, then the local small-claims route. Keep the tone factual; reviews follow tone.

A firm deadline tied to the contract's communication clause got a silent bride to answer within minutes (r/WeddingPhotography 186). The same works for money.

## 8. Tricks most owners miss

- Separate "booking payment for the date" from "advance payment for deliverables" in the contract wording; courts and couples treat them differently.
- Write repricing for postponements into a higher season or a later calendar year before anyone asks.
- Treat parents as payers, not clients.
- The communication clause is a sales tool: it gets replies.
- Hold unearned cash in a separate account; spend it after the event.
