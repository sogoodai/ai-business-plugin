# Seasonality, off-season products and corporate events

Off-season is a product problem, not a price problem. The date calendar is lopsided, so the agent's job is to protect peak dates at full price and sell different, defined products into the empty ones. Sources in [sources.md](sources.md).

## 1. The calendar

US weddings by month (The Knot 2026, 2025 weddings): June 16%, October 16%, May 14%, September 13%, August 10%; 76% fall May to October; December is 1%.

```
peak share       = bookings on the top 20 dates / all bookings
open-date value  = contribution a date normally attracts (near zero for a January Tuesday)
fill target      = open weekday and off-month dates you can serve x realistic fill rate
```

What the calendar means for the agent:

- **Booking lag:** couples book venues first and other vendors later; a 2026 inquiry is often a 2027 wedding. Read cohorts by inquiry month, not by wedding month.
- **Peak dates sell out first.** Track peak dates still open for the next 12 and 24 months every month; that number, not inquiry volume, drives price decisions ([pricing-packages.md](pricing-packages.md)).
- **Inquiry season is not wedding season.** Plot the owner's inquiries by month of arrival, not event month. Fix the reply system and price page before the heaviest inquiry month, which is usually months before the heaviest wedding month. One photographer's January bridal show booked nothing while an August niche show booked 5 (r/WeddingPhotography 119): timing and audience both matter.
- **Weekend vs weekday:** intimate weddings and elopements often run on weekdays; book them there to keep weekends for big weddings (r/WeddingPhotography 445).
- **Local calendars differ.** Southern hemisphere, destination markets and religious calendars shift peaks. Use the owner's own 24 months of bookings, not the US curve, once it exists.

## 2. Micro weddings, elopements and courthouse weddings

**Demand:** 48% of couples considered a micro wedding, 57% of engaged respondents (The Knot Worldwide 2025 trends study). Definitions: elopement up to about 15 guests, micro wedding up to about 50. The Knot's own Simply Eloped brand sells packages from $450 to $5,500 (commercial interest: trust the demand more than the prices).

**Operators report:**
- Courthouse-style weddings are nearly 40% of enquiries for a 17-year Melbourne photographer who ranks for them on Google (r/WeddingPhotography 445).
- 1-hour ceremony-plus-portraits in one hometown location priced like an engagement session with more delivered photos; 90-minute weddings are common outside peak season (r/WeddingPhotography 82, 9 and 5 upvotes).
- A Monday town-square elopement with 5 hours of coverage (r/WeddingPhotography 445, 53 upvotes).
- Two short weddings in one day or several over a weekend for a 2 to 4 hour package; less per wedding, done by evening (r/WeddingPhotography 71, secondhand).
- Short events have strong hourly earnings and little editing; full-day weddings pay more in total (r/WeddingPhotography 203, 9 vs 64 upvotes).

**Package template** (research format, 2026):

> "Micro Wedding Collection: up to 40 guests, Monday to Thursday, 4 hours, [defined deliverables], from $X."

Rules: fixed scope, fixed days, own page and own inquiry form, never available on peak Saturdays, priced so effective hourly matches or beats a full wedding ([economics.md](economics.md) "off-season product test"). Build a courthouse, elopement or small-weddings page for search and link it from the "just a few photos" reply ([scripts.md](scripts.md)).

**Budget elopement buyers spend on photography first.** A $6,000 Colorado elopement where the photographer was the biggest expense (r/Weddingsunder10k 2637); parents spending $5,000 on a photographer who hiked with the couple at 2 am (r/Weddingsunder10k 2185). Adventure elopement is a premium niche, not a discount.

## 3. Off-season products by vendor

| Vendor | Products for empty dates | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Photographer | Courthouse + dinner, weekday elopement, proposals, engagement, second shooting, corporate events and headshots, family sessions | $300 second-shooting Saturdays and $25 to $125 an hour corporate work kept one photographer afloat in a bad year (r/WeddingPhotography 119) |
| Videographer | Same-day edits, corporate, social content retainers | Gym content contract: $3,000 a month for about 20 hours a week of stills and video (r/photography 1436) |
| DJ / band | Corporate parties, galas, schools, alumni, MC work, uplighting rentals | MC work paid 2x second shooting for one photographer-turned-MC (r/WeddingPhotography 119) |
| Florist | Corporate holiday installs, office and restaurant weekly vases, funerals, Valentine's and Mother's Day, proposals | Fixed designer's-choice menu from $100 (r/florists 886) |
| Planner | Corporate retreats, proposals, destination, milestone parties | 26% of proposers hired a vendor (The Knot 2026) |
| Venue | Weekday offsites, private dining, showers, memorials, launches, workshops | 61.3% of wedding-first venues already host other events (HoneyBook 2026) |
| Caterer | Office catering, holiday parties, executive dinners | See `restaurant` for catering ops |
| Rentals / AV | Corporate launches, conferences, festivals, school events | Rent printed backdrops alongside LED (by-vendor) |

**Other ways photographers fill weekdays** (r/photography 1436): email local antique shops, pawn shops and car dealerships offering inventory photography; about two stores a day; $25 to $75 an hour suggested for beginners. A hometown photo series turned into annual calendars (several hundred a year) and a Kickstarter coffee-table book that sold 3,000 copies across two printings (r/photography 1314). Off-season products can also be products, not only services.

**What not to do:** "20% off winter weddings." It trains couples to haggle and leaks into peak dates. Price the off-season product on its own page, with its own name.

## 4. Corporate and private events: the second market

**Data:** Cvent 2026 Global Planner Sourcing Report (1,650 planners, surveyed July 2025):

- 48% actively sourcing non-hotel or special-event venues, up 30+ points since 2023.
- 75% use AI in venue sourcing (North America 78%, Europe 68%, Australia 85%): searching and selecting venues 43%, attendee data 41%, comparing bids 40%.
- 60% use digital RFP tools for most or all meetings.
- What most drives an RFP: meeting-room specs 50%, images and video 48%, floor plans 46%.
- Where venues should improve: proposal transparency 22%, answers to custom questions 21%.
- Expected RFP reply in 1 to 4 days: 51% for 50 or fewer attendees, 49% for 51 to 100, 40% for 101 to 250.

**Who should chase it:** vendors with reusable capacity on weekdays: venues, caterers, rentals, florals and decor, DJs and AV, bands, planners. Photographers and videographers can sell event coverage, headshots and content days.

**Corporate page (not the wedding homepage with "company" swapped for "couple"):**

```
capacity by layout (theater, classroom, banquet, reception) | room dimensions and ceiling height
floor plans (downloadable) | load-in and loading dock | parking | power | Wi-Fi bandwidth
in-house AV list and outside-AV policy and fees | F&B minimums and menus | dietary handling
accessibility | insurance | lead time | indicative rates by day part | RFP contact and reply SLA
```

All as text, not inside image PDFs. AI sourcing tools and Google's AI features read text; Google needs no special markup for AI Overviews (Google Search Central 2025 to 2026). Depth: `local-seo`, `website-conversion`.

**First RFP reply** (research pass, 2026):

> "Thanks, [Name]. We can currently accommodate [date or range] for [attendee count]. I've attached a one-page option matching the layout and F&B requirements in your brief. The estimated range is [range], subject to [two variables]. Floor plan, AV specification and load-in details are here: [links]. If these parameters work, I can hold [date] provisionally until [specific date] while we finalize scope."

Faster than 1 to 4 days and complete beats either alone. Every provisional hold has an expiry date and a conversion rule ([contracts-payments.md](contracts-payments.md)).

**What corporate buyers push back on** (useful for quoting):
- AV service charges and small-room gear: one buyer got service charges waived at several properties and a 25-person projector-and-screen quote cut from $1,000 to $250 a day (r/EventProduction 17, 3 upvotes).
- Outside-AV fees: buyers now ask for power, rigging, internet and liaison fees during shortlisting (15 upvotes). Quote them up front.
- F&B lines: cake from $9 to $4 a slice after asking (r/EventProduction 17). Keep pre-approved concessions instead of cutting the per-head.
- Static LED walls: buyers swap a $50,000 LED wall for a printed backdrop at about $20 per square foot (r/EventProduction 21). Offer both options yourself.
- Destination cost: secondary cities at one-third to one-half the price of major ones, plus convention bureau rebates (r/EventProduction 17). Venues in secondary cities should say so on the corporate page.
- Drinking less: offer a real zero-proof menu (r/EventProduction 17).

**Corporate DJ and entertainment brief questions:** guest count and age mix, drinking or not, how long the event runs before you start, whether a dance segment is planned, seated or standing, brand music rules. A 3-hour set after a 3-hour seated dinner for 30 people lost the floor (r/DJs 102). Sell what the room will do.

**Corporate event marketing measures** (from an event-marketing lead, r/EventProduction 15): before the event, invite clicks and registration drop-off; during, session attendance and dwell; after, recap-page visits, deck downloads and shared photos. Offer this reporting to corporate clients; it helps them justify the next budget, which is your rebooking.

## 5. Public and ticketed events

For producers, venues running their own events and vendors hosting open days:

- Pilot small, scale on proof: a 17-vendor brewery book fair with lines around the block in 20°F weather became a 150-exhibitor, 8,100-attendee event netting $80K (r/EventProduction 25).
- Waitlist landing page and private invitations, then ticket sales on Eventbrite; $50 a week of Eventbrite ads for visibility inside the platform (r/EventProduction 16).
- Free events: register 2x capacity; charge $10 to $20 with food or a drink to cut no-shows; email registration plus reminders with parking and schedule (r/EventProduction 17).
- Sponsors: sell a named meal function or table; profit usually depends on big-ticket sponsors (r/EventProduction 17, 11 upvotes).
- PR: an unusual concept pitched to TV, radio, newspapers and magazines earned coverage valued at $10,000 (r/EventProduction 25).

## 6. Tricks most owners miss

- The weekday micro-wedding page is also an SEO page (courthouse, elopement, micro wedding + city) and a filter for "just a few photos" inquiries.
- Second-shooting and corporate gigs keep a photographer's cash and network alive in a slow year; the network produced the MC work.
- Corporate buyers reward completeness: one page with every spec beats a beautiful brochure with none.
- Report open peak dates monthly; raise or hold price on those, sell products into the rest.
