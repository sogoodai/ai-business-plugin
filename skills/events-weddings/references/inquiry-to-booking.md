# Inquiry to booking

The leak in most wedding businesses is between the inquiry and the contract, not before it. In 2026 operators describe "qualified leads, love of my work, excitement to talk... and then I never hear from them again" (r/WeddingPhotography 50; no phone consult since the previous October) and a $300K studio getting "a lot of inquiries, but they all say they went with someone else" (r/WeddingPhotography 71). Diagnose the stage before buying more leads. General speed-to-lead and quote follow-up mechanics: `home-services`.

## 1. Response system

Data: 42% of private-event inquiries arrive outside weekday hours and get a human reply after about 17 hours vs 3 in the workday; replies under an hour booked 30% vs 23% after 24 hours (Reunion 2026, correlation). 64% of venue tours are booked within 24 hours of the first message (Mikla 2026). Inquiries come by email 27%, personal text 27%, Instagram DM 13%, business texting 11%, marketplace messages 5%, phone 4% (HoneyBook 2026).

Set up:

1. **One inbox.** Route website forms, marketplace messages, Instagram DMs and texts into one CRM or shared inbox with a timestamp. Count the scattered ones you missed last month.
2. **Instant auto-reply that is useful:** availability for their date if the CRM can check it, starting price and typical spend, the booking link. Never "Thanks, we'll be in touch."
3. **Instant plain SMS** when the form has a phone number: "Hey [name], got your inquiry, sending pricing to your email now! Check Promotions or spam if you don't see it." One photographer's branded PDF went to Gmail Promotions and the bride thought she'd been ignored; adding the text made response "night and day" (r/WeddingPhotography 65). Transactional replies to an inquiry are fine; marketing texts need consent (`email-sms-loyalty`).
4. **Human reply inside one hour in waking hours,** 7 days a week in season. For DJs and planners, personality decides (53% and 50%, The Knot 2026): the human voice is the sample.
5. **Phone-first tools.** 63.8% of photographers run client work from their phone (HoneyBook 2026): templates and a scheduling link must work from a phone.
6. **Busy weekends:** "Got your message, I'm working an event right now. Can I call you Monday at 10?" (bride's suggestion, r/WeddingPhotography 90). 30 seconds beats 30 hours.

## 2. The first reply

It must answer three things without another round trip: is the date open, does the price fit, what's next.

- Subject line: "Yes, we're available for [date] at [venue]".
- One specific line from their inquiry (venue, guest count, something they said).
- Starting price and typical spend: "Full wedding coverage starts at $3,800; most couples invest $4,600 to $5,500 depending on hours, second shooter and album." (format from research pass, 2026)
- Two or three concrete inclusions.
- One next step with a self-booking link, plus an email-only path for couples who don't want a call.
- The booking rule in one line: "Dates are held only with a signed contract and retainer."

Wording to avoid: "investment" for price (one bride booked no vendor who used it on their site, r/WeddingPhotography 90), a 14-option price sheet, a long PDF as the only content. Make sample pricing findable on the site in under a minute; that bride left sites where she couldn't.

Privacy note: one photographer received a fake inquiry from a firm harvesting vendor replies for an AI research project (r/WeddingPhotography 63, 2026). Put only what's on your website in the first reply (prices, packages); send a la carte menus and full galleries after a two-way exchange.

## 3. Qualification

Ask, in the form or first reply: date, venue or town, guest count (venue, caterer, florist, rentals), hours or scope, budget band.

- **"We just need a few photos" or "something simple":** ask the budget before gathering details. One photographer found these prospects consistently at about $100 for elaborate shoots (r/WeddingPhotography 82). State the minimum: "This is my minimum fee to reserve a date" (66 upvotes). The minimum covers gear, travel, setup and the date itself regardless of image count. Most won't reply, which is the point. Link a small-weddings page for the ones who fit.
- **Mismatched budget, desirable couple:** offer fewer hours, not a lower rate: "5 hours of great coverage instead of 10 of rushed coverage, built around the moments you care about most." Couples are often surprised this is an option (r/WeddingPhotography 263).
- **Florists:** show a minimum spend or typical range and ask guest count, table count, venue and style before any design work (research pass). An open "tell us your floral vision" form books design hours with couples who can't afford you.
- **Rentals:** date availability, delivery zone, minimum order, install and strike needs, quantities. Inventory conflicts are the real constraint; instant quotes beat more traffic.
- **Red flags:** a parent booking and insisting all communication goes through them (operators require the couple to book and communicate directly, r/WeddingPhotography 339); payment only after delivery; "guaranteed photos of every guest". One photographer checked with peers and found four others had already declined the same family (r/WeddingPhotography 339).
- **Check the buyer's reliability too:** DJs ask the current act at a new venue whether they get paid (r/DJs 139).

## 4. Consultation script

Diagnose before presenting (research pass, 2026):

1. "Before I walk through options, what made you reach out to us specifically?"
2. "What has been hardest to figure out so far?"
3. "When you picture the day going perfectly, what matters most: [category options, e.g. candid moments, family portraits, the dance floor]?"
4. "Have you set a working range for [category], or would it help if I show what changes at each price point?"
5. "Who besides you needs to feel comfortable before you book?"
6. "If the fit is right today, is there anything that would stop you from reserving the date?"

Then present two packages and the recommended one. Set expectations that prevent disputes later:

- Photographers: "I take about 2,000 frames and deliver about 500. I don't throw away good pictures. You get every good shot I take." Requests for all unselected images then stop (r/WeddingPhotography 274).
- Photographers: body reshaping is not standard editing and costs extra (lesson from a refund, r/WeddingPhotography 80).
- Videographers: show film lengths per package (minimum and maximum) and how music is chosen (offer a playlist of 4 or 5 licensed tracks and let them pick 1 or 2; a bride assumed reception audio would be the soundtrack, r/weddingvideography 35).
- Everyone: what counts as coverage time (consecutive, setup included), meal break, travel and parking.
- Marketing use: if the couple worries about privacy, ask what concerns them, explain you never share children or intimate moments, show past posts and ask which equivalent moments they'd be comfortable sharing (56-upvote practice).

## 5. Closing

- **The rule:** "The date stays open to everyone until the contract is signed and the retainer is paid." Say it in the first reply, on the call, in the email with the contract (r/WeddingPhotography: 178-upvote and 9-upvote practitioners; no issues reported).
- **Footer on every pre-booking email:** "A booking fee of 30% is required to secure your date. Email correspondence does not guarantee a booking; dates are not held without a booking fee." (r/WeddingPhotography 88)
- **Contract links expire.** Operators use 4 days, 10 days (link stops accepting signatures) or 2 weeks. Tell the couple the expiry when you send it.
- **Competing inquiry:** at contract time, say another couple may ask about the date and you'll try to tell them first; when it happens, send the courtesy note. It moves slow prospects (r/WeddingPhotography 34-upvote and 5-upvote commenters). Never invent one.
- **Don't wait on one maybe.** A photographer waited on a first couple while two others wanted the date; the first chose a cheaper cousin and the other two had booked elsewhere (r/WeddingPhotography 88).
- **Price objections:** hold the price, change scope. "Rejection is protection" (33 upvotes). One father pushed for $1,000 off and no second shooter on a 200+ guest August Saturday; the photographer explained the season and the work, held, lost the booking and was relieved (r/WeddingPhotography 137). If you want the couple and they need more coverage, offer value instead: "Book the 10-hour collection and I'll add the second extra hour free" (buy one, get one hour).
- **When a couple leaves for another vendor,** thank them and ask why in one line; it clears your pipeline and feeds lost-reason data. Some couples send your contract to a rival to undercut you (r/WeddingPhotography 56-upvote story).

## 6. Follow-up cadence

About a third of venue tours came only after a follow-up (Mikla 2026). Test this default and measure two-way rate per touch:

| When | Channel | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Minute 0 | email + SMS | auto-reply with price and link; SMS pointing to the email |
| Under 1 hour | email or text | personal reply (first-reply structure) |
| Day 1 | text or email | one useful decision aid (timeline sample, what's included at the venue, a full gallery at their venue) |
| Day 3 | email | a short question that is easy to answer ("Is [date] still the plan?") |
| Day 7 | email | date status and the next step, with two call times |
| Day 14 | email | close the loop politely; offer to keep their date on watch |
| Monthly | email | useful planning content or date-availability changes until the date passes (nurture flows: `email-sms-loyalty`) |

After a contract is sent: reminder when the expiry nears, then the closing email two days later ([scripts.md](scripts.md)): "I can only assume you are no longer interested... if I don't hear from you by [date], I will open your date to other couples and consider the contract void." (103 upvotes; OP and others endorsed it.)

## 7. When inquiries stop converting

Diagnose by stage with the owner's data:

- **Two-way rate falling:** deliverability (test the reply to a Gmail account), reply speed, a first reply that doesn't answer price, inquiries coming from marketplaces that blast many vendors.
- **Calls happen, contracts don't:** price vs the couple's band (2026 couples in many markets want about $2,000 for photo or video, r/WeddingPhotography 13-upvote comment), weak differentiation, slow proposals, a competitor bundling with the venue.
- **"Went with someone else" everywhere:** look at the time between inquiry and proposal, and at what the winners include; in one 2026 thread commenters said someone with nearly the same quality at half the price is winning (28 upvotes).
- **Last-minute and lower-budget inquiries rising:** add a defined small-wedding product rather than discounting the main one ([seasonality-and-corporate.md](seasonality-and-corporate.md)).

## 8. From signed to delivered (operations that protect referrals)

- **At signing, collect:** every venue address, coverage hours, start time, both partners' phone numbers. A photographer who took a booking 6 days out with only a verbal venue name went to the wrong venue and refunded (r/WeddingPhotography 241). Another got an 11-hour, three-location itinerary 72 hours before the wedding (r/WeddingPhotography 1150).
- **Final-month meeting and a reconfirmation a couple of days before.** An unanswered itinerary preceded a missed wedding and a full refund (r/WeddingPhotography 1641).
- **Unresponsive couple before the wedding:** a firm email citing the contract's communication clause with a deadline got a reply within minutes (r/WeddingPhotography 186). If still silent, call the venue to confirm the event is happening (a 20-year photographer learned this way that a wedding was cancelled).
- **Missing details:** send a written note that without planning information you will lead the timeline and can't guarantee specific shots; a bride who ran 40 minutes late later complained about missing grandma photos (r/WeddingPhotography 523).
- **Church restrictions:** have the couple get the rules from the officiant and pass them on; on the day, concede a known boundary and ask for the angle the couple needs ("I know I can't enter the sanctuary, but I want to see their faces; is there a way?").
- **Associates:** give clock start and end times and an extension rule; one associate stayed 8 hours on a 6-hour booking at the owner's cost (r/WeddingPhotography 333).
- **Coverage end:** warn the couple 30 minutes before the end, again at 10, offer a paid extension, leave on time.
- **Delivery:** quote a window longer than your real turnaround (3 to 6 weeks quoted, under 2 delivered; r/WeddingPhotography 124) or publish an editing queue with ETAs updated nightly so nobody asks (34-upvote commenter). "Just checking in" messages from about half of clients halfway through the window are normal in 2026 (r/WeddingPhotography 57).
- **Never deliver before the balance is paid.** Hold sneak peeks and editing until the money lands.
