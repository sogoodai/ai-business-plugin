# Wholesale, consignment, galleries and open studios

The reorder engine (Faire reorder timing, Google Maps account lists, big-retail terms, MAP) is in `ecommerce-brand` (wholesale.md). Jewelry consignment terms and the full consignment agreement template are in `jewelry-accessories`. This file keeps the art and craft decisions.

## Which products can wholesale

Wholesale works when a SKU can be reordered without eating all the owner's making hours: candles, soap, small ceramics, cards and prints, small wood goods, repeatable textiles (research report 2026). One-off originals and big commissions rarely can.

```
wholesale-ready  = MSRP the market accepts x 0.5 - unit cost - samples, freight and fees per unit >= owner's floor
hourly check     = (wholesale price - materials - packaging) / hands-on hours per unit >= owner's rate
```

A couple who filled a 5,000-piece subscription-box order netted $2 to $3 an hour (`ecommerce-brand`). Run the hourly check before any large order. Worked examples (candle passes at $28 MSRP; decorated mug fails, simplified mug passes) are in [pricing.md](pricing.md).

## Wholesale terms for a small maker

- **Keystone:** shops expect wholesale at about 50% of retail. Candle makers plan wholesale at roughly half of online retail before pitching niche boutiques (r/candlemaking, 256 pts).
- **Galleries and gift shops:** one Etsy maker has sold for years at 30% off retail with a $500 minimum order, and it got the work into galleries (r/EtsySellers, 23-pt comment).
- **Prepay first orders.** A boutique chain asked a candle maker for custom labels, custom fragrances, net 60 and 30-day returns of unsold goods on an order that would have tripled her revenue. She offered a smaller prepaid trial; they declined; she walked away (r/smallbusiness, 925 pts).
- **Faire math (North America, 2026):** 15% commission on every marketplace order plus a $10 new-customer fee on opening orders; 0% commission on qualifying Faire Direct orders from retailers you bring; processing 3.5%, 2.4% or 1.9% + $0.30 for next-day, 30-day or 60-day payout (Faire brand terms and pricing update). Older notes and blogs still quote 25% on first orders; quote the current Faire page.
- **Never just say "we're on Faire" to a shop owner you met in person.** Ask for their email and send your line sheet plus your own Faire Direct link ([scripts.md](scripts.md)).
- **Quote all terms on one line:** wholesale per unit, MSRP, opening minimum, reorder minimum, lead time, payment terms, shipping charged separately (research report 2026). Do not hide wholesale prices behind a long application unless exclusivity requires it.
- **Digital line sheet from the product spreadsheet,** not a printed lookbook: a printed one went stale with five price changes and a stockout before it shipped (r/ecommerce, 80 pts).
- **Free next-day delivery on a tight route** beat bigger suppliers for one local wholesaler: $100 minimum, about 10 stops in 5 to 10 miles (r/Entrepreneur, 1,876 pts; profit claims disputed).
- **Screen resellers.** When an eBay buyer kept ordering to different addresses, Google Lens showed he resold the maker's work on Amazon at 3x; the maker raised prices and the arbitrage vanished (operators report). When a repeat buyer clears out an Etsy shop, offer a custom wholesale listing and raise public prices (r/EtsySellers, 817 pts).
- **Shelf kit with the first order:** display card with materials and care line, a one-line staff pitch, three photos for the shop's social posts (same as `jewelry-accessories`).
- **Start a reorder timer** at day 30 to 40 after delivery (`ecommerce-brand`).

## Consignment

Use consignment to enter a shop that will not buy yet, then convert to wholesale.

- **Splits:** 60/40 maker/shop is the common shop split, 70/30 happens, 80/20 is rare. At 50/50 ask the shop to buy at wholesale instead (practitioner norm, same rule as `jewelry-accessories`).
- **Some local spaces charge a small booth fee plus a low commission.** An artist's best gallery results came from Asheville spaces with a small fee and low percentage, some requiring one staffed day a week (operators report).
- **Agreement contents:** each work or SKU with retail price, commission, who carries damage and theft risk and insurance, discount authority, payment timing and statements, display commitment, return date and condition, termination notice (CAA guidance, pre-2024, consistent with current European guidance). Template: `jewelry-accessories` scripts.
- **Photograph inventory at drop-off and at each count.**
- **Check the work is out.** A shop reported zero sales for a month; a friend had bought a piece there, and the maker found the stock had never been shelved (r/Entrepreneur, 1,115 pts). A gallery that promised 16 framed oils for a 50,000-visitor festival weekend left them piled in the back on Saturday; the artist took them home and sold one to a waitress while loading the car (r/ArtBusiness, 103 pts).

## Galleries

- **Dealer commission is typically 50%** (College Art Association guidance, pre-2024, possibly outdated); the Danish artists' association recommends 25% to 50% and deducting substantial production costs before the split (BKF, current).
- **Price parity:** studio sales must not undercut the representing gallery. European model agreements commonly require it (Netherlands gallery association model agreement).
- **Discount clause:** one model agreement allows gallery discounts up to 15% with the cost shared. Without a clause, the artist learns of the discount after the sale (research report 2026). Write who absorbs any discount.
- **Prestige is not income.** An artist's prestigious St. Augustine gallery took a high percentage and required paid travel, lodging and food for shows; it produced very little money and contributed to debt. Direct social sales gave her the highest income at the lowest cost (r/ArtBusiness, 168-pt thread).
- **Pitch a coherent body of work** with price range and story, not single pieces. Ask for commission, display period, shipping both ways and insurance on premises in writing. Pitch script in [scripts.md](scripts.md).
- **Exhibitions need an audience you can verify.** A first solo show at a bonsai studio ran four Friday evenings, paintings came down between weeks, and invitations reached only about 100 course participants: nobody came (r/ArtBusiness, 84 pts). Ask the venue how many people receive the invitation and from which list.

## Art agencies and pay-to-show offers

An artist paid $2,000 for "representation" and participation in four major art fairs (packages $2,000 to $6,000). The agency did not appear on Miami art week or London Art Fair exhibitor lists; the artist received five purported sale contracts and no proceeds. A registered LLC, a clean BBB record and six weeks of calls did not reveal it. Contacting other artists on the agency site exposed the same buyers and no payments (r/ArtBusiness, 89 pts).

Rule: before paying anyone to show work, check the fair's official exhibitor list, call two artists the agency lists, and ask them whether they were paid for any sale.

## Open studios

- **Regional trails sell.** Artmap Argyll 2025: 4,215 visits across 34 reporting studios (124 per studio) and £63,415 of sales across 29 studios, about £2,190 each (regional sample).
- **Run it as a release:** preview to the list first, a timed opening, a signup sheet, and a follow-up email to everyone who signed within 48 hours.
- **Private previews matter to collectors:** 62% value exclusive previews and 54% behind-the-scenes access (Artsy 2025, 1,236 collectors).

## Trade shows

No defensible 2026 trade-show ROI benchmark for small makers exists in the inputs. Judge a show on 12-month account contribution, book buyer meetings before buying space, and use emerging-maker programs to lower entry cost (`jewelry-accessories` covers the jewelry shows).
