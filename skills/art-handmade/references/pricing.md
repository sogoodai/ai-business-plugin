# Pricing art and handmade goods

All worked examples use illustrative inputs unless a source is named. Replace every input with the owner's real numbers before recommending a price. Pricing psychology, tiers and increase notices in general live in `offers-pricing`; Etsy fee mechanics in `ecommerce-brand`.

## 1. The formulas

```
hands-on labor     = minutes per unit (timed over a real batch) / 60 x owner hourly rate
unit cost          = materials + packaging + hands-on labor + overhead per unit
channel price      = (unit cost + fixed fees per order) / (1 - percentage fees - target margin)
contribution       = price - unit cost - all channel fees           (owner labor is already paid inside unit cost)
convention check   = (materials + labor + overhead) x 2 = wholesale; wholesale x 2 = retail
wholesale price    = unit cost / (1 - maker margin on wholesale)
MSRP               = wholesale x 2                                   (keystone, what shops expect)
wholesale-ready    = the market will pay MSRP at retail
price-raise check  = units you can lose before contribution falls = p / (m + p)
                     (p = raise as % of price, m = current contribution margin)
```

- **Time a batch, not a single piece.** A jeweler with decades of experience found his estimates badly wrong once he logged each step; one ring took 16 hours (operators report, `jewelry-accessories`). Include sanding, curing checks, labeling, photographing and packing.
- **Overhead per unit** = annual tools, booth gear, insurance, software, testing and studio costs / units sold last year. New makers: start with 10% of materials plus labor and correct after six months.
- **The x2 x2 convention** (Dummies handmade pricing; repeated across Etsy pricing guides) is a sanity check, not the rule. The margin formula is the rule because it includes channel fees.
- **Owner hourly rate** is the owner's decision. Ask for it. If they refuse to name one, show what their current prices imply per hour.

## 2. Worked example: 8 oz soy candle

Inputs (illustrative): wax $1.10, fragrance 0.6 oz at $1.60/oz = $0.96, wick and sticker $0.20, vessel and lid $2.40, label $0.30, box and tissue $0.50. Materials and packaging = $5.46. A batch of 24 takes 3 hours from prep to packed: 7.5 minutes each at $25/hour = $3.13. Overhead $0.80.

```
unit cost = 5.46 + 3.13 + 0.80 = $9.39
convention check: 9.39 x 2 = $18.78 wholesale, $37.56 retail
```

Market check: one maker sold 6.5 oz at $16 and 10 oz at $24; another met resistance at $38 (operators report). $37.56 is above most local markets, so set MSRP from the margin formula instead:

```
wholesale at 30% maker margin = 9.39 / 0.70 = $13.41  -> $14
MSRP = 14 x 2 = $28   (wholesale-ready at $28)
```

Contribution per candle at $28 by channel:

| Channel | Fees on this order | Contribution | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Own site | card 2.9% + $0.30 = $1.11 | $17.50 | Shipping charged at cost |
| Market booth | card about 3% = $0.84 | $17.77 | Before booth fee share |
| Etsy, organic, $7 shipping charged | 6.5% of $35 + 3% + $0.25 + $0.20 listing = $3.78 | $14.83 | Etsy fees apply to item plus shipping |
| Etsy with Offsite Ads 15% | + $5.25 | $9.58 | Price must survive this case |
| Faire, marketplace opening order of 12 at $14 | 15% + $10 + 2.4% + $0.30 (30-day payout) on $168 | $1.32 a unit | First order barely pays |
| Faire reorder | 15% + processing | $2.15 a unit | |
| Faire Direct (shop you brought) | processing only | $4.25 a unit | Send your own link |

Fees: Etsy Help 2026 (6.5% transaction, $0.20 listing, Offsite Ads 15% under $10,000 trailing sales, 12% above and mandatory, $100 cap); Faire North America pricing update 2026 (15%, $10 new-customer fee on opening orders, 0% Faire Direct; processing 3.5%, 2.4% or 1.9% + $0.30 for next-day, 30-day or 60-day payout). US Etsy processing 3% + $0.25.

Tricks:
- **Choose the 60-day Faire payout when cash allows:** 1.9% instead of 3.5% is 1.6 points back on every order.
- **Refills separate the vessel from the repeat buy:** $10 refills offered against a $38 candle (operators report, planned). Refill contribution is high because the vessel cost disappears.
- **Price the validation batch to fund the next one.** One maker priced the first run so selling out funded a batch five times larger, accepting thin early profit (operators report).
- **8 to 10 oz sells best; 10 SKUs total including seasonal; vanilla or similar as a staple; niche florals position the brand but do not carry sales** (perfumer advice relayed by a maker, operators report).

## 3. Worked example: thrown and painted mug

Inputs from the research report's illustration (Nov 2026): clay, glaze, firing and packaging $8; hands-on labor $14 (35 minutes at $24/hour). Etsy fixed fees $0.45 ($0.20 listing, $0.25 processing), percentage fees 9.5% (6.5% + 3%). Target margin 30%.

```
organic Etsy price  = (8 + 14 + 0.45) / (1 - 0.095 - 0.30) = 22.45 / 0.605 = $37.11  -> $38
with Offsite Ads    = 22.45 / (1 - 0.245 - 0.30)           = 22.45 / 0.455 = $49.34  -> $49
at $35 (the report's example): contribution $9.22 organic (26%), $3.97 with Offsite Ads (11%)
```

Wholesale: MSRP $38 means wholesale $19, below the $22 cost. This mug is not wholesale-ready. Build a wholesale edit instead: same clay, simpler glaze, 15 minutes hands-on ($6 labor):

```
cost $14 -> wholesale at 30% margin = $20 -> MSRP $40
```

The shop line is a simpler mug at a similar shelf price; the hand-painted mug stays DTC and at markets. Potters who are told "your prices are too low" usually have this problem: their decorated work (1.5 to 8 hours of decoration per piece in one LA potter's post) is priced like production ware (r/Pottery, 4,116 pts).

## 4. Worked example: hardwood cutting board

Operators report: four boards sold in a row at $35, none after a raise to $45 that weekend (OP said this may be coincidence); another seller moved bulk rubberwood boards from $45 to $75+ by design size and always charges $10 for on-site engraving; exotic woods, especially purpleheart, sold first (r/woodworking, 6,155 pts).

Inputs (illustrative): lumber $12, oil and wax $1.50, abrasives $1, 1.5 hours finishing at $25 = $37.50, overhead $2.

```
unit cost = $54.00
at $45 the owner pays himself about $28.50 for 1.5 hours instead of $37.50
at $75: contribution after about 3% card fee = 75 - 54 - 2.25 = $18.75
engraving add-on $10: nearly all contribution once the tool is paid for
```

Rules: lead with the unusual wood and the largest size, offer engraving as a paid add-on, and do not buy a laser until volume justifies it (same seller's warning). Let shoppers touch an unfinished board, then a finished one, to make the finishing hours visible.

## 5. Worked example: original paintings

There is no universal rate per inch. Pick one method, apply it to the whole body of work, and raise only after sales.

Anchor from one painter's floors: 8x10 watercolor $150, 8x10 oil $200, 10% off at most (operators report).

```
square-inch method: rate = 200 / 80 sq in = $2.50 per sq in
   16x20 = 320 sq in = $800      24x36 = 864 sq in = $2,160
linear-inch method: rate = 200 / (8 + 10) = $11.11 per linear inch
   16x20 = 36 in = $400          24x36 = 60 in = $667
```

Square-inch pricing climbs fast on large work; linear-inch climbs slowly. Many artists sit between them. A sanity check: one artist's 24x36 acrylic commission at $900 took about four weeks (operators report), so the linear-inch $667 would underpay labor for that painter.

- **Gallery parity:** if a gallery takes 50%, the $800 painting nets $400. The artist's floor per piece must hold at the gallery split, because the studio price must equal the gallery price (European model agreements, research report 2026).
- **Framing** is a separate line priced at cost plus the owner's markup, so the art price stays consistent across framed and unframed sales.
- **Small originals as entry points:** 4x6 or 5x7 still lifes in vintage frames sold as Instagram drops; small 7x9 paintings tested at $100 to $150 and raised if they sell (operators report).
- **Price ladder by demand:** an artist's agent reports prices moving from $300 to $1,000 to $2,000 to $20,000 over 10 years at about 100 paintings a year with about 10% unsold (operators report). Raise when most of a series sells within its show window.

## 6. Worked example: prints, cards and stickers at a market

From one artist's second market, six days over two weekends: 562 sales, $6,500 revenue, $5,000 profit (operators report, r/ArtBusiness, 561 pts). Bestsellers: stickers, single 8x10 prints, the two-print 8x10 bundle.

| Item | 1 | 2 | 3 or 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8x10 print | $20 | $35 | 3 for $50 |
| 5x7 print | $10 | $17 | 3 for $23 |
| Greeting card | $5 | 2 for $8 | 4 for $15 |
| Postcard | $4 | 3 for $10 | 4 for $12 |
| Sticker | $2.50 | | |
| 3x9 bumper sticker | $10 | | |

Cost side: 8x10 cardstock prints for about $1.80 each from a local reprographics shop (operators report, Asheville). With a sleeve and backing board at about $0.60 (illustrative), a 3-for-$50 sale earns about $42.80 before the booth fee.

- **Match the print to the venue:** home giclee (Epson ET-8550, fine art paper, 300 DPI RGB) for handmade shows; cheaper outsourced CMYK prints on 100 lb uncoated paper for convention artist alleys (operators report, 10+ years).
- **Prints need new art; finished books keep selling.** A con artist found prints the highest-profit product, but they required fresh artwork; a completed manga sold without new work (operators report).

## 7. Worked example: crochet and big fiber pieces

The hour ceiling is real. A commenter priced a crocheted wedding dress at 3 months of 40-hour weeks at $30 an hour: over $14,000 before materials, against about $3,000 for most listings found online (r/crochet). A 90x100 chunky blanket took 48+ hours and then cost $460 to ship (r/EtsySellers, 1,744 pts).

```
finished price = hours x rate + materials + packed shipping quote
if finished price > what buyers in that niche pay:
    sell it as art (state fair, gallery, commission at full rate), or
    sell the pattern or kit (see digital-products), or
    make the smaller version (amigurumi, coasters, hats) and keep the big piece as the hero photo
```

- Patterns reached 10% of one embroidery artist's income next to 50% from physical art (operators report). A $6 crochet pattern that took 6 months to write generated constant "where are my booties" messages because buyers thought it was the finished item (operators report): label digital items in the first photo, not only the title.

## 8. Testing and raising prices

- **One change per event or per two weeks online.** Track units sold per hour open (markets) or per 100 visits (online), plus contribution per hour.
- **Raise the most labor-heavy bestseller first.** Etsy sellers report doubling a cumbersome item's price and selling more, and a 46% unit and 99% revenue lift year over year after broad raises (operators report, uncontrolled, refreshed listings may explain part).
- **Overbooked means underpriced.** A woodworker with a waitlist into the next year was told "raise your prices: fewer sales, same money, less stress" (r/woodworking, 38,138 pts). A waitlist or sell-out in hours is the signal to raise.
- **Buyers at markets are often not price-sensitive.** A potter raised prices for her second market and buyers did not look at prices; one overpaid by $5 (r/Pottery, 2,204 pts).
- **Discounts at checkout tip hesitant online buyers** (an Etsy shopper spending $200 to $1,000 a month says a shop discount converts her "every time"). Use abandoned-cart coupons, not sitewide sales.
- **Include small extras in the price instead of nickel-and-diming.** Pet-portrait buyers objected to $10 for glasses or a bone; one seller includes accessories and outfits in one price and reports customers like it (operators report).
