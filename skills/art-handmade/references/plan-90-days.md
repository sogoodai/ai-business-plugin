# 90-day plan

For a new or stalled artist or maker business. Order matters: economics, then conversion, then retention, then one new channel, then cut and scale (research report 2026 sequence, adapted). Each phase ends with a short owner report: what changed, the scorecard, what the owner must decide.

## Days 1 to 15: economics and offer

- **Time five products** over a real batch. Build the SKU sheet: materials, packaging, minutes, overhead, unit cost ([pricing.md](pricing.md)).
- **Model every channel per SKU:** own site, market, Etsy organic, Etsy with Offsite Ads, TikTok Shop by market, Faire marketplace, Faire Direct, consignment. Flag SKUs that fail the Offsite Ads case or fail keystone.
- **Reprice** the failures. Raise the most labor-heavy bestseller first. Write the reason for each change.
- **Commissions:** publish a starting-price menu with an example per tier, and written terms: deposit, revisions, timeline, shipping, cancellation, rights ([commissions-and-licensing.md](commissions-and-licensing.md)).
- **Art:** make price, dimensions, medium, framing and shipping visible on every work.
- **Compliance pass:** labels, claims, CPSIA status, EU GPSR data, edition disclosures ([compliance-and-labeling.md](compliance-and-labeling.md)).

Owner questions: hourly rate, minimum profit per order, which SKUs they refuse to reprice, which countries they will ship to.

Exit check: every live SKU clears the owner's floor in its worst channel case, or is marked DTC-only.

## Days 16 to 30: conversion surfaces

- **Etsy:** list the listings with the most impressions and weakest purchases. Replace the first photo (real item, real surface, scale in hand), show the price clearly, expose shipping and returns, fill attributes, add a 10-second making clip with the shop name visible, fill the About section. Titles last.
- **Own site:** Merchant Center feed and Product structured data for every SKU; shipping and return policy in the feed; maker and material facts on each page.
- **Instagram:** keyword and location in the Name field, three-line bio, link to shop or signup.
- **Booth kit** for the next event: card reader with products preloaded, signup sheet or QR with consent wording, custom-order notes card, price on every item, one touch or sniff demo ([fairs-and-markets.md](fairs-and-markets.md)).

Exit check: top-impression listings rebuilt; feed approved; booth kit ready.

## Days 31 to 45: retention

- **Repeatable goods:** welcome, abandoned cart, post-purchase care and review, replenishment (candle and soap timing from burn or use life), win-back (`email-sms-loyalty` for setup).
- **Artists:** collector preview flow, new-work alert, follow-up to people who clicked a specific piece. Cap broadcasts at two a month.
- **Every in-person contact goes into the list** with consent and a source tag (fair name, open studio, commission inquiry).
- **Commission follow-through:** finished-piece photos before shipping, a care card, a referral line and contact details on the back of any print or card file.

Exit check: flows live; list growing from every event; source tags in place.

## Days 46 to 60: content laboratory

- **Batch-film every making session:** raw transformation, failure or problem, material close-up, finished piece in hand.
- **Publish one recording three ways** (Reels, TikTok, Shorts). Test 6 to 12 second process clips against tutorials; run hook variants as Trial Reels.
- **Leave slow posts up to day four** before judging. Boost only posts that already beat the account's normal reach or shop clicks.
- **Set order caps and processing windows** before any promoted or likely-viral post.
- **One community test per week:** a local or subject subreddit, a fandom, a daily-sketch community, a state fair entry.

Exit check: two content formats identified that produce clicks or signups, not only likes.

## Days 61 to 75: add one second revenue channel

Pick one by sub-model, not five:

| Sub-model | Second channel | First step |
|---|---|---|
| Candle, soap, small ceramics, cards, small wood | Wholesale to independent shops | Line sheet, wholesale edit, 10 target shops that stock adjacent brands, Faire Direct link, prepaid first orders ([wholesale-consignment-galleries.md](wholesale-consignment-galleries.md)) |
| Fine artist | Private preview release or open studio | Preview email to list, timed opening, signup sheet |
| Illustrator | Licensing prospect list | 20 product companies whose categories fit the style; inquiry reply ready |
| Fiber artist | Patterns or kits | One pattern from the bestseller with a writing-style preview image (`digital-products`) |
| Woodworker | Plans or institutional orders | Plans for the most-asked build; one pitch to a local government or office buyer |

Exit check: the channel has a first paying customer or a clear no with a reason.

## Days 76 to 90: cut losers, scale evidence

- **Dashboard in contribution dollars,** not revenue, by SKU, channel and event.
- **Drop events** below the owner's contribution-per-hour threshold unless they produced list signups or custom orders worth the gap. Rebook the best one or two event types.
- **Stop paid promotion** on creative that cannot reach acceptable contribution after fees.
- **Expand** the top two content formats, the top one or two event types and the highest revenue-per-recipient email flows.
- **Price step** on anything with a waitlist, a sell-out or a backlog.
- **Shopify diagnostic only:** median store converts 1.3%, top 20% 2.8% (Littledata Nov 2026); category and price point matter more than crossing the line.

Decision rule: scale what makes repeatable contribution, keep channels that create first-party customer relationships, stop anything whose success can only be described in followers, views or gross revenue (research report 2026).

## Brand-new maker: first 30 days instead of days 1 to 30

1. Time and price five products; launch with 5 to 6 variants, not 20.
2. Book three different local events in the first six weeks (flea market that welcomes artists, studio or atelier market, holiday market) and log sales per hour.
3. Open Etsy with real photos and a full About section; one making clip per listing.
4. Start the list at the first event.
5. Post finished work weekly in one niche or local community where buyers already gather, and announce open commission slots occasionally.
6. Keep the day job until slow months are covered; reinvest early profits (a festival seller reinvested 100% for two years before going full time, operators report).
