# Craft fairs, art markets, festivals and cons

Fairs and events were among the majority in-person sales sources for 34.6% of UK makers (Crafts Council 2025). They are also where makers lose the most money on a single bad day. Jewelry-specific booth tactics (mirrors, ring sizers, stone guides) are in `jewelry-accessories`; a shop's own in-store events are in `local-retail`.

## Booth economics

```
event contribution  = sales - COGS - booth and application fees - card fees - travel and lodging
                      - helper pay - damaged display stock
per owner-hour      = event contribution / (setup + selling + teardown + travel hours)
event multiple      = sales / booth fee            floor 3x to 5x, strong 10x
list value          = consented signups x revenue per subscriber in the next 90 days
repeat rule         = repeat if contribution per owner-hour >= owner's rate, or list value closes the gap
```

The two databases disagree on the median because coverage differs. Use the owner's own region: ask past vendors.

## Picking events

- **Score in advance, in writing.** Decide the contribution that makes an event worth repeating before you go. Record sales by SKU and by hour at every event.
- **Ask past vendors three questions:** what did you sell, what was the crowd buying, would you return. Organizer attendance numbers are not sales.
- **Check foot traffic, sightlines and obligations.** An artist paid $75 to sell in a near-empty cafe, made $40, and learned at the last minute that vendors had to honor organizer coupons (r/ArtBusiness, 93 pts). A jewelry maker's spot faced a pillar behind two vendors next to an aggressive perfume sampler (`jewelry-accessories`).
- **Flea markets that welcome artists can beat art markets.** Two friends made $2,500 across their first two flea markets (r/ArtBusiness, 338 pts).
- **Test many markets in the first season.** A candle maker who launched 1 May went to as many markets as possible, then kept the ones that sold: about $25,000 and 675 units in five months, 98% at markets (operators report). Another candle brand's first year: 207 units, just over $4,000, nearly half at four in-person events (r/candlemaking, 614 pts).
- **Studio and atelier markets are warm crowds.** A first-year potter sold out in 3 hours at her atelier's winter market and left with an Instagram waitlist (r/Pottery, 3,842 pts).
- **Know when markets are not the channel.** A potter selling nothing over $35 (two vases at $59, a sculpture at $89) barely covered the table fee and started questioning markets (r/Pottery, 3,104 pts). Low price points need high volume or a cheaper table.
- **Regional seasons.** A 15-year festival veteran sells in Florida from fall to spring, does a few Northeast shows in summer, and uses summer to make new work. Single shows ranged from $20,000 to barely covering costs (operators report).
- **Gallery-partnered markets build your resume.** A market co-run by a farmers market and a gallery offered each vendor one piece in the gallery, which met two recurring eligibility criteria for more selective calls (r/ArtBusiness, 139 pts).
- **Fine-art fairs may forbid prints.** Check the rules; if allowed, a QR code to the print store captures buyers who will not pay for an original (planned tactic, permission unconfirmed).

## Display that converts

- **Let them touch the work you charge for.** Keep one unfinished board on the table; shoppers rub it, then a finished one, and understand the labor (r/woodworking, 13-pt comment from a long-time seller).
- **Glass cloches over scent testers** keep dust out and the scent concentrated until lifted (r/candlemaking, 66 pts). Uncovered testers collect dust (73 pts).
- **Empty space signals premium.** Study local boutique merchandising, highlight a few pieces, do not cover the table with stock (r/candlemaking, 9 pts).
- **Soap:** each bar on a thrifted soap dish or tea towel; Goodwill baskets at $1 for gift sets (r/soapmaking, 66 and 21 pts). Wrap the bottom of display bars in paper so shoppers can pick them up to smell without residue; shoppers were seen wiping hands on their pants (same thread).
- **Frames sell prints and portraits as gifts.** Show the same piece in several frame styles (r/ArtBusiness).
- **Lead with what looks unusual.** Purpleheart and other exotic-wood boards sold first; the largest coin racks sold out at full price while discounted small ones did not (r/woodworking, 6,155 pts). Compliments are not demand: headphone stands drew pointing and comments and sold one unit in two days (same thread).
- **Labels must read at arm's length.** Shoppers mistook squeezable wax melts for dog treats, shampoo, syrup and drink mix despite back-label instructions. A packaging designer's fix: put "squeezable wax melt" on the front at the size the scent name used, shrink the scent name, move the dog art to the back (r/candlemaking, 575 pts, 105-pt comment). Novel formats can outsell the core: those melts sold much better than candles (same thread).
- **Tag the price on everything.** Hidden prices lose online art buyers (43% cite it, Artsy 2025); at a table they mean every shopper has to ask.

## Checkout

- **Take cards.** Card payments were about half of one convention artist's income and card buyers bought more items (r/ArtBusiness, 59 pts).
- **Preload every product into the POS** and ring cash sales through it too, so it totals multi-item baskets and tax; keep change in a server apron instead of running to a cash box (r/candlemaking, 6-pt comment confirmed by OP).
- **Give buyers a way to buy later:** business cards or a QR to the shop. One con artist says every web sale came from someone met at a con or on stream (operators report).
- **Collect contacts for custom orders.** A potter: sales at the market are not the most valuable part; have a way for people to write their email or phone and notes about custom ideas, because you contacting them converts far better than them contacting you (r/Pottery, 2,204 pts).
- **Consent wording at the sheet:** "Want first access to new work and event dates? Add your email; I send [frequency], unsubscribe anytime." (research report 2026).
- **Holiday pre-orders and next-day pickup** extend a sold-out day; take payment upfront.

## Booth conversation

- **Greet, then give space.** A shopper said even "let me know if you have questions" felt pushy and walked away from a vendor who pointed at every item and recited its price (r/candlemaking, 10 pts).
- **Tell the story when they engage:** what the piece means, how long it took, why it mattered. One seller nearly sold out half way through a first market this way; shoppers "rarely leave empty-handed" after hearing the stories (r/ArtBusiness, 349 pts).
- **Use the market as product research.** In October, a candle maker asked everyone who stopped to smell holiday test scents and vote; the winner, Evergreen and Apple, became a top holiday seller (r/candlemaking, 614 pts).
- **Handle lurkers so buyers can reach the table.** Scripts in [scripts.md](scripts.md): step-aside request, product cue then turn to your neighbor, checkout cue, purchase-or-leave line for flirting. Get to know neighboring vendors before you need them.
- **Craft-secret askers:** redirect kindly to a beginner resource, or "some day I will write a book; want to join my email list?" (r/EtsySellers, 38 pts).

## Vendor network

- **Trade and cross-post with neighbors;** they also tell you which other markets pay (r/candlemaking, 30 pts).
- **Introduce yourself at close** with a 2 to 3 oz sample candle and a card (planned tactic).
- **Donate to the event's door prize** for exposure (r/candlemaking, 9 pts).
- **Partner booths:** a baker launched candles through her bakery with a Valentine's cinnamon-roll-and-candle bundle and took 7 orders in four days (r/candlemaking, 205 pts). A honey vendor bartered 10 lb of burned honey for soap bars to put in his gift baskets (r/soapmaking, 358 pts).

## Unusual venues that sell

- **Bars:** portrait sketching (above); a father built dioramas inside thrifted kerosene lamps each winter and sold them at a bar with older regulars, "Christmas money for many years" (operators report).
- **Specialty grocery sampling tables:** a scrub maker greeted every shopper ("All natural sugar scrubs today if you'd like to test them"), sold almost everything including stock a local shop had failed to sell, and booked every Saturday (r/Entrepreneur, 1,115 pts).
- **Private showings:** an artist spent a day in an arts district with an interested colleague, then hosted him at an apartment hung with about two dozen large framed photographs, with music, wine and food: three pieces sold for $4,500 (r/ArtBusiness, 81 pts).
- **Live markets where you make while selling** offset shipping losses for a former Etsy seller and cleared stockpiled work (operators report).

## Supply risk before a fixed date

Order event stock with slack. An artist ordered with paid fast shipping 35 days before a market; a missing commercial invoice froze tracking for two weeks and the stock missed the date (r/ArtBusiness, 85 pts). Bring 2x to 3x the stock you expect to sell (`jewelry-accessories` fair rule).
