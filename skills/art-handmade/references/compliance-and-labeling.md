# Compliance that changes what you can say and sell

Claims and safety information on a listing are part of the regulated product presentation (research report 2026). An agent writing listings, labels or ads must apply this file. It is a marketing checklist, not legal advice: when a product sits near a line (kids, skin, food contact), tell the owner to confirm with the regulator or a specialist before launch.

## Candles and wax melts

**US**
- CPSC points candle makers to ASTM F2058 (fire-safety cautionary labeling) and ASTM F2417 (fire safety). Candles that qualify as hazardous under the Federal Hazardous Substances Act need cautionary labeling: signal word, principal hazard, precautions, first aid where needed, "keep out of reach of children" where relevant, and the maker's identity (CPSC candles guidance and FHSA labeling FAQ).
- Net quantity and maker identity on the label are standard retail expectations; most shops will ask for them before stocking.

**UK and EU**
- Indoor candles carry EN 15494 safety information (the 2019 edition reduced the mandatory pictograms from five to four; confirm the current set with BSI before printing). This is a separate obligation from CLP (British Candlemakers and Trading Standards advice pack 2024).
- If the fragrance blend makes the candle or melt hazardous, CLP labeling also applies: pictograms, hazard and precautionary statements, and for EU and Northern Ireland a UFI code with poison-centre notification; GB notifications go through HSE and the National Poisons Information Service (trade guides 2024 to 2026). Ask the fragrance supplier for the CLP calculation at the load you use.
- EU GPSR (applies since 13 December 2024): every online offer must show the manufacturer's name and postal and electronic contact, product identification and warnings; a non-EU maker must also show the EU responsible person (EU Commission guidance 2025). Some UK makers stopped selling to the EU because of it (Crafts Council 2025).

**Marketing claims**
- Do not call a fragrance candle "non-toxic", "safe around pets" or therapeutic because the raw materials sound benign (research report 2026).
- Novel formats need the format on the front: shoppers mistook squeezable wax melts in pouches for dog treats and drink mix despite a "not edible" back label (r/candlemaking, 575 pts). Add a non-edible warning near any food-like scent name or image.

## Soap and cosmetics

**US**
- **The claim decides the category.** A product marketed to cleanse or beautify can be a cosmetic; one marketed to treat or prevent disease or affect body function is a drug (FDA small business and homemade cosmetics fact sheet). Words that cross the line: "treats eczema", "antibacterial", "heals acne", "reduces inflammation", "antifungal".
- **True soap** (mostly alkali salts of fatty acids, sold and labeled only for cleaning) is outside FDA cosmetics rules; once it is marketed as moisturizing, deodorizing or skin-improving it is regulated as a cosmetic (FDA; maker guides summarize the three-part test). Keep "true soap" copy to cleaning, scent and look.
- **Cosmetics labeling:** ingredient declaration in descending order of predominance, identity, net quantity, business name and place (FDA cosmetics labeling regulations).
- **MoCRA:** businesses under $1 million average annual US cosmetics sales over the prior three years are exempt from facility registration and product listing, but not if they make eye-area, injected, internal or long-lasting non-removable products; the exemption is self-assessed and rolling, so keep sales records (FDA fact sheet; maker guides 2025). Adverse-event and safety obligations still apply.

**EU, GB, Canada**
- EU: safety assessment, an EU Responsible Person and CPNP notification before placing on the market (Regulation EC 1223/2009; CPNP is free).
- GB: notification to OPSS (SCPN) and a GB Responsible Person; labels show identity, quantity, responsible person, batch, durability, precautions, ingredients (GOV.UK 2025).
- Canada: notify Health Canada within 10 days after first sale; bilingual identity and cautions, INCI ingredients, metric net quantity (Health Canada).

## Children's products (US CPSIA)

Applies to items designed or intended primarily for children 12 and under: crochet and knit toys, wooden toys, nursery goods, kids' clothing, children's jewelry.

- **Children's Product Certificate** based on testing where required, plus **permanent tracking information** (maker, location and date of production, batch) on the product and packaging where practicable. CPSC says there is no small-business or handmade exemption from tracking labels (CPSC tracking label FAQ).
- **Small Batch Manufacturer registration** is free and renewed every calendar year; it covers products made in runs under 7,500 units a year and also has a revenue threshold (check the current figure on cpsc.gov/FAQ/Small-Batch). It removes third-party testing only for Group B rules; Group A rules still need a CPSC-accepted lab, and products must still comply with every rule. Put the registration number on the CPC.
- **Material determinations save testing:** natural and manufactured fiber textiles (dyed or undyed) and certain untreated, unfinished wood are determined not to exceed lead limits and need no third-party lead-content testing; they still must comply, and paints, finishes, buttons, safety eyes and metal parts are separate (16 CFR 1500.91; CPSC lead FAQ).
- **Toys for under-3s** raise small-parts and choking issues (safety eyes, buttons, pompoms). Treat any amigurumi marketed for babies as a toy that needs the full review, or label and market it as decor for older children and adults only when that is genuinely the intended use.
- **Marketing implication:** the listing's words, photos (a baby holding it) and category choose "children's product" for you. Do not show infants with a toy that has not been certified.

## Ceramics and food contact

- **Decorative pieces that look usable for food** must carry a conspicuous stick-on label and a permanent statement such as "Not for Food Use. May Poison Food" or "Not for Food Use. Glaze contains lead", unless they meet food-use limits (21 CFR 109.16). FDA's compliance policy sets extractable lead action levels for ceramic tableware (CPG 545.450).
- **Lead-free glazes can still pick up lead** from kilns previously used for lead glazes (FDA 2010 guidance). "Lead-free" and "food safe" are claims that need testing at a lab, not assumptions from the glaze label.
- **California sales** may also trigger Proposition 65 warnings for lead or cadmium; check before wholesaling into California shops.
- **Wood:** describe finishes factually ("finished with food-grade mineral oil and beeswax") rather than claiming the board is "food safe" or "antibacterial".

## Art and editions

- **California Print Dealers Act** (Civil Code 1740 to 1745): fine-art multiples offered at $100 or more, excluding the frame, need written pre-sale disclosure (artist, signature, medium, whether a reproduction, date, edition size). A stated edition size is an express warranty that no more multiples were made, proofs included. Several other US states have similar print disclosure laws; check before selling editions into them.
- **Never call a print limited** if substantially identical prints can be made indefinitely (research report 2026).
- **Copyright:** a commission does not transfer copyright unless the contract says so (Illustrators Australia; AOI). State the rights granted on the invoice.

## Marketplace traps

- **Artist and brand names in titles or tags.** A seller accepted Etsy's AI suggestion to retitle "Graffiti Art" wearables as "Basquiat-Style", then got three policy violations, a letter from the estate's lawyers and every listing removed (r/EtsySellers, 346 pts).
- **Stolen photos can get your own listing removed.** Etsy deactivated a seasonal bestseller because its stolen photos appeared elsewhere; reinstatement took months and missed the season (r/Etsy, 2,434 pts). Keep dated originals and making-of footage, and recheck for copies before peak season.
- **AI images:** Etsy requires at least one unedited photo of the actual product against a real background (October 2026).
- **Account links:** a 12-year six-figure Etsy shop was suspended, likely for buying with a card owned by a suspended account. A second appeal explaining the specific innocent mistake restored it after a template rejection of the first (r/Etsy, 581 pts).
- **Unsolicited religious inserts** in unrelated products cost one seller a positive review and repeat buyer (r/Etsy, 1,362 pts). Inserts should match what the shop sells.
