# Boring businesses: small, proven, unglamorous

A boring business sells one plain thing to people who already search for it: a printable, a spreadsheet, a template, a guide, a fixed-scope service. No app, no investors, no audience required on day one. This skill helps an agent pick one, check the money, and launch it in weeks.

Built from platform fee pages and ad-network rules checked November 2026, plus public examples of small online businesses. Depth for selling files lives in `digital-products`; for search traffic in `seo-content`; for services in `agency-freelance` and `offers-pricing`. Every number has a source in [references/sources.md](references/sources.md).

## The rules that decide most outcomes

- **Pick demand you can see.** A boring business works when people already search or browse for the thing (a "budget spreadsheet", "date night ideas printable", "thank you message for teacher"). Check search volume, marketplace best-seller pages and the top results before building. No visible demand, no build.
- **Ship one product, not a catalog.** Start from a narrow offer: one date-night PDF pack, one budget sheet, one editing service. Widen only after the first one sells.
- **Mass AI content is the fastest way to fail.** Google's March 2024 update cut low-quality, unoriginal results by 45% and made scaled content abuse spam "whether automation, people, or both" made it (Google, 2024). A content site needs first-hand proof: real tests, original photos, named expertise, sourced facts.
- **Know the fee floor before pricing.** Fixed fees (about $0.45 on Etsy, $0.80 on Gumroad per order) eat 14 to 29% of a product under $9. Price files at $9 or more, or bundle (see `digital-products`).
- **Ads need traffic first.** Display ads only pay once a site has traffic: Journey by Mediavine needs 1,000 monthly sessions from tier-1 countries, Raptive 25,000 monthly pageviews (2025 to 2026 rules). Plan 6 to 12 months of content before ad income on a new site.
- **Services make money first, files and sites make it last.** A productized service can bill in week one. A content site can take a year. Most founders should start with the model whose first dollar comes soonest and build the slower asset alongside.

## First session: ask, then pick

Ask the founder:

1. What they know better than most people (a job, a hobby, a place, a skill). Expertise is the moat for content and services.
2. Hours per week, money they can spend, and when they need the first dollar.
3. Whether they want to sell files, sell their time, or build traffic.
4. Any audience already (email list, followers, a community, past clients).
5. Tools they can use well (Excel, Notion, Canva, Figma, Framer, a camera).

Then match with this table and open [references/models.md](references/models.md) for the chosen model:

| If they... | Start with | First dollar | Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need money this month, have a skill | Productized service | Days to weeks | 7 |
| Are good with Excel, Notion, Canva, Framer | Templates | Weeks | 2, 3 |
| Teach, parent, plan events | Printables and PDF packs | Weeks | 1 |
| Know a niche deeply and can write | Niche guide or review site | 6 to 12 months | 4, 5 |
| Can maintain a data set | Comparison, directory or job board | Months | 6, 9 |
| Have an audience | Newsletter or small course | Weeks to months | 8, 11 |
| Can build a small tool | Calculator or generator site | Months | 10 |

Then look up two or three live businesses with the same shape (each model lists public examples) and study their offer, prices and pages.

## Size it before building

```
net per sale = price - platform % - fixed fee - payment processing
sales needed  = monthly goal / net per sale
traffic needed = sales needed / conversion rate     (assume 1 to 2% for a cold product page until measured)
ad income     = monthly pageviews / 1,000 x RPM      (RPM only once accepted by a network; never forecast with a guess)
service cap   = hours available / hours per job x price per job
```

Say the result plainly: "At $12 a file you net about $9.65 on Gumroad direct (10% + $0.50, plus 2.9% + $0.30 card processing), so $1,000 a month is about 104 sales, or 5,200 to 10,400 product page visits at 1 to 2%."

## What to never do

- Copy an example business's content, design or name. Copy the shape only.
- Publish hundreds of AI pages, fake reviews, invented tests or invented quotes.
- Sell templates that were not tested in the real tool (Excel and Google Sheets, live Notion duplication, an editable Framer project).
- Promise income to the founder. These are examples, not forecasts.
- Give medical, legal or financial advice in a content site without a qualified reviewer.
