# The 11 boring business models

Each model: how it makes money, the numbers that matter, launch steps, a launch checklist, the usual pitfalls, and public examples (named for their shape only, revenue not claimed). Sources in [sources.md](sources.md). Numbers are platform rules or published data with a date; anything else is marked as an estimate.

## 1. Printables and PDF packs

Date-night cards, kids' activities, classroom worksheets, Montessori materials, planners, party games.

**Money:** one-off file sales and bundles. Set the price from the 10 best-selling listings for the exact search, not from a guess. Free printables can instead drive email signups or ad traffic.

**Numbers:**
- Etsy: $0.20 listing, 6.5% transaction fee, 3% + $0.25 payment processing (US), plus 12 to 15% Offsite Ads on orders it attributes (Etsy fees page, Nov 2026).
- Gumroad direct: 10% + $0.50 plus card processing; Lemon Squeezy 5% + $0.50; Payhip free plan 5% (fee pages, 2026).
- Under about $9, fixed fees take 14 to 29% of the price. Bundle low-price items.

**Launch steps:**
1. Pick one buyer and one moment ("couples who want a cheap date night at home", "kindergarten teachers before Valentine's Day").
2. Read the 20 best-selling listings for that search on Etsy and Pinterest. Note price, page count, format, what reviews praise and complain about.
3. Make one pack of 10 to 30 pages, original text and graphics, A4 and US Letter.
4. Print every page on a home printer. Check margins, ink use, answer keys, age fit.
5. Sell on your own site with instant download, and list the same pack on Etsy for discovery.
6. Make 5 to 10 Pinterest pins per pack (Pinterest is the main free traffic source for printables; see `digital-products`).

**Checklist:** both paper sizes; file names readable; a preview image of every page type; license line (personal use vs classroom use); instant delivery tested with a real purchase; refund rule stated.

**Pitfalls:** selling clip art or fonts you have no commercial license for; classroom material no teacher checked; 50 thin listings instead of 5 strong ones; ignoring seasonality (Valentine's, back to school and Christmas packs sell in narrow windows).

**Examples:** A Twist Of Date (date-night PDFs), PJSandPAINT (free printable games), Mrs. Jump Class (classroom games), Montessori Tools.

## 2. Spreadsheet and finance templates

Budgets, trackers, small-business planners, financial models.

**Money:** one-off template sales; specialist financial models sell for more than simple trackers. Price from the current top sellers for the same job.

**Launch steps:**
1. Pick one job ("monthly budget for couples", "cleaning business job tracker", "3-year model for a coffee shop").
2. Build in Google Sheets and Excel. Test every formula with real-looking data, empty rows, negative numbers and a new year.
3. Lock formula cells, add an instruction tab, add a filled-in example tab.
4. Record a 60-second walkthrough video for the product page.
5. Sell on your own site; a Google Sheets copy link plus an .xlsx file.
6. Write 3 to 5 tutorial articles around the problem (see model 4) that link to the template.

**Checklist:** works in Excel and Sheets (and Numbers if claimed); no broken references after copying; currency and date formats switchable; a version number; support email.

**Pitfalls:** formulas that break when a user inserts a row; Excel-only functions sold as Sheets-compatible; financial models with assumptions nobody qualified checked; no support plan (spreadsheet buyers email questions).

**Examples:** Spreadsheets Crafter, SimplyOrganizedPro, ChartSheets, Financial Aha, ProjectionHub (financial models), TrumpExcel (tutorials that sell templates).

## 3. Notion, Framer, UI, slide templates and asset packs

**Money:** one-off sales, on the tool's own marketplace and your own checkout. Price from that marketplace's top sellers for the same use case.

**Numbers:**
- Notion Marketplace: 8% + $0.40 per sale, biweekly payouts, $20 minimum; you can instead link to your own checkout (Notion help, 2026).
- Gumroad Discover and Lemon Squeezy Marketplace take 30% on marketplace-found sales (fee pages, 2026).

**Launch steps:**
1. Build the template in the real tool, for one use case ("freelance client portal in Notion", "Framer site for a dentist").
2. Test duplication from a clean account. Check relations, views, permissions (Notion); responsive breakpoints and CMS (Framer); masters and fonts (slides).
3. Make a demo: a live preview link plus a 60-second video.
4. List on the tool's own marketplace for discovery and sell from your site for margin.
5. Ship an update every month or two and email past buyers.

**Checklist:** clean duplicate link; no personal data in the template; fonts are free or licensed for redistribution; setup guide inside the template; changelog.

**Pitfalls:** a template that only works in the creator's workspace; generic "ultimate life planner" in a saturated market; music or image assets without clear rights (licensing and copyright-claim support is a business in itself).

**Examples:** Pathpages, Pascio, Notionway, Ultimate Brain, Second Brain (Notion), Templifica (Framer), CreativeTim (UI kits), Slides Carnival (slides), Free To Use (music).

## 4. Niche information publication

A site that answers one kind of question very well: thank-you message wording, slang meanings, pool care, relocation steps for one city, puzzle tutorials.

**Money:** display ads, then affiliate links and a small product (model 1 to 3) once traffic exists.

**Numbers:**
- Journey by Mediavine: 1,000 monthly sessions from tier-1 countries (US, Canada, UK, Australia) in 30 days. Mediavine main network: about $5,000 a year in ad revenue (Mediavine, 2026).
- Raptive: 25,000 monthly pageviews, mostly US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand (Raptive, since Oct 2025).
- AdSense: publishers keep about 68% when Google Ads buys the ad, 80% after third-party buyer fees (Google, 2024).
- Google cut low-quality, unoriginal content by 45% in the March 2024 update and treats mass-produced pages made to rank as spam (Google, 2024).

**Launch steps:**
1. List 100 real questions in the niche (Google "People also ask", Reddit, forums, AI answers). Keep the ones with clear search demand and weak current answers.
2. Write the 20 best answers first, each with something only you can add: a tested method, a real photo, a sourced table, a local fact.
3. One clear template per page type (a definition page, a how-to, a list of examples).
4. Publish 2 to 4 strong pages a week for 3 to 6 months. Track impressions in Search Console from week one.
5. Apply to an ad network at its threshold; add affiliate links only where they help the reader.

**Checklist:** author page with real expertise; sources linked on every factual page; original images; Search Console and analytics set up; privacy and cookie notice before ads.

**Pitfalls:** AI-written pages at scale; no first-hand angle; health, legal or money advice without review; waiting for ads instead of adding an email list or product.

**Examples:** Tons of Thanks, LetsLearnSlang, Tiny Buddha, Routines, Swim University, All About Berlin, J Perm.

## 5. Review, gift and buying-guide sites

**Money:** affiliate commissions, then ads.

**Numbers:**
- Amazon Associates: many categories pay 1 to 4%, a few up to 10% (Amazon compensation page, 2026); one 2026 report says rates were cut by up to 50% in some categories. Check the live table for your category before modelling income.
- Direct brand programs often pay more; compare before choosing.

**Launch steps:**
1. Pick a product niche where you can test or have real experience (golf gear, keyboards, footwear, tablets, gifts for one occasion).
2. Build a testing method and publish it. Buy or borrow products; take original photos.
3. Start with "best X for Y" pages where you can be specific (budget, use case, size).
4. Keep prices and availability current; date every guide.
5. Disclose affiliate links clearly at the top.

**Checklist:** test notes and photos for every recommended product; FTC-style disclosure; update dates; no fake star ratings.

**Pitfalls:** desk-research reviews presented as tests; dead products and wrong prices; depending on one retailer's commission table.

**Examples:** Giftlab, Living Cozy, Frump Fighters, NoShameIncome, Stridewise, BreakingEighty, KeebFinder, WorldofTablet, Tooltester, SiteBuilderReport, GetDeploying.

## 6. Comparison, price and deal sites

Price tables (hard drives), deal lists (hotels), provider comparisons (moving, TV and internet).

**Money:** affiliate and lead fees from providers.

**Launch steps:**
1. Find a data source you can legally use and refresh (an affiliate product feed, a public price list, provider sign-ups).
2. Start tiny: one product type or one city.
3. Automate refresh and flag stale rows; show "last checked" on every row.
4. Add the guide content that ranks (model 4) around the table.

**Pitfalls:** scraping data you have no right to use; stale prices (the fastest way to lose trust); lead partners that do not pay or need contracts.

**Examples:** Disk Prices, VolHotels, MoveBuddha, CableTV. The data upkeep is the business.

## 7. Productized services

One fixed-scope service, fixed price, clear turnaround: editing, resumes, design subscriptions, photo retouching, marketing for one trade, Notion setup.

**Money:** fixed packages or monthly subscriptions.

**Numbers:**
- Designjoy, the best-known design subscription, lists one plan at $4,995 a month with one request at a time (Designjoy, Nov 2026). A Q4 2026 index of 15 design subscriptions puts most small-business plans at $449 to $1,700 a month (ManyPixels, a competitor; treat as indicative).
- Marketplaces for finding first clients: Fiverr keeps 20% of each order; Upwork charges freelancers 0 to 15% (help centers, 2026).

**Launch steps:**
1. Name the service by outcome and buyer ("resume rewrite for nurses, 3 days").
2. Fix scope, price, turnaround and revisions. Write what is not included.
3. Show 3 before-and-after examples (your own work or free pilots with permission).
4. Intake form, payment up front (or first month), delivery by email or shared folder.
5. First 5 clients from people you know, communities and one marketplace profile; ask each for a review.

**Checklist:** written scope; intake form; turnaround promise you can keep at 2x volume; revision limit; confidentiality and data handling for personal documents.

**Pitfalls:** "unlimited" without a request queue; underpricing; using AI output without human review on work clients pay for; resumes with invented experience.

**Examples:** EditorNinja, NoDegree, DesignJoy, Fixthephoto, PlumbElec, Notionology. Depth: `agency-freelance`, `offers-pricing`.

## 8. Newsletters

**Money:** sponsorships, paid subscriptions, affiliate links.

**Launch steps:** pick one reader and one cadence; publish 8 issues before selling anything; grow with a lead magnet (a model 1 or 2 product as a free download) and swaps with similar newsletters; sell sponsors on clicks, not open rates. Depth and platform fees: `creator`.

**Pitfalls:** no niche; counting opens (privacy features inflate them); buying lists.


## 9. Directories and job boards

**Money:** paid listings, featured spots, job posts.

**Launch steps:** seed 100+ real, verified listings before launch; charge for featured placement, not basic listing; one niche (remote jobs in one field, suppliers in one industry); refresh or remove dead entries monthly.

**Pitfalls:** empty directory on launch day; listings scraped without permission; job posts with no applicants (charge only once traffic exists).


## 10. Simple web tools

Calculators, generators, converters, checkers.

**Money:** ads, a paid tier, or a lead into a product or service.

**Launch steps:** pick one task people search for with a clear input and output; build the smallest version that works on a phone; add a useful explanation page around it (tools rank better with content); track use per tool and keep the ones people use.

**Pitfalls:** tools that need constant API spend; copying a free tool with no better angle.


## 11. Small courses and training

**Money:** one-off course price or cohort fee.

**Launch steps:** pre-sell before building (a waitlist or a paid pilot cohort); teach live once, record it, then sell the recording; one transformation, one buyer. Depth: `education-coaching`.

**Pitfalls:** building 40 lessons before one sale; no community or support; claims of income results.
