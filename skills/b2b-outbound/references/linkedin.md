# LinkedIn outreach

LinkedIn is the second channel for most small B2B sellers and the first for some buyers (wealth management, founders, people who ignore cold email). It also carries the highest account risk: the founder's profile is a business asset. Sources: [sources.md](sources.md).

## Official mechanics (LinkedIn help center and User Agreement, current 2026)

- **There is no published universal invitation cap.** Restrictions come from sending many invitations in a short time, a high share of ignored, pending or "I don't know this person" invitations, and suspected automation. Most invitation restrictions clear in about one week. Withdrawing invitations does not lift an active restriction. A withdrawn invitee cannot be re-invited for up to 3 weeks.
- **Automation is prohibited.** The User Agreement bans bots, browser extensions and other unauthorized software that scrape, add contacts, send messages or create engagement. LinkedIn enforces against vendors too: it sued Proxycurl (shut down July 2025) and ProAPIs (October 2025), and removed company pages for Apollo, Seamless and HeyReach (coreyhaines31 compliance reference, MIT).
- **Personalized notes:** Basic (free) accounts can add a note to about 3 connection requests a month, 200 characters max; Premium removes the note quota. The wording has changed across help pages, so check the account's UI.
- **InMail credits:** Premium Career 5 a month, Premium Business 15, Sales Navigator Core 50, Recruiter Lite 30. A reply, accept or decline within 90 days returns the credit. You cannot send a second InMail to someone until they respond to the first. **Messages to Open Profiles cost no credit** (r/LeadGeneration 23).
- **Sales Navigator** search caps at 2,500 results and has no native export. Use it to define accounts and personas; pull contacts from a licensed database.

Practitioner limits, not policy: about 100 invitations a week, 15 to 25 a day, 20 to 30 a week on new or quiet accounts (coreyhaines31). One operator ties capacity to acceptance: above 40% acceptance allows about 200 a week, below 25% about 50 or fewer; check the last 30 days before scaling and spread requests across the week (r/b2bmarketing 31). Keep acceptance above 25 to 30% by sending fewer, better-targeted requests.

**The economic metric is meetings per invitation sent:** acceptance x reply x qualified-meeting rate. A high acceptance rate with no conversations means targeting works and the first message does not.

## Note or no note

The data splits, and the rule follows the objective:

- **No note maximizes acceptance:** 36% vs 29% over 100K+ requests (r/b2bmarketing 42); about 15% better across 100+ accounts (r/b2bmarketing 102); Belkins agrees on acceptance.
- **A note maximizes replies:** Belkins 8.2% vs 5.3% reply after acceptance.
- **Rule:** blank request for network building and Tier 3; a short specific note for Tier 1 and 2 when you have a real reason. Free accounts get about 3 notes a month, so spend them on the best prospects.

## The sequence

1. **Make the profile the landing page first.** Headline says who you help and how (all 220 characters); About opens with the problem, then proof, then next step; Featured holds one case study or demo; recent activity in the last few weeks. A founder with 2 demo videos in Featured and free product-made research reports got about half of all clients from LinkedIn (r/startups 728).
2. **Engage before asking.** View the profile, react or leave a real comment (3 to 4 lines, one concrete point) on a recent post. Turn on notifications for about 20 target founders and comment within 30 minutes of their posts (r/LeadGeneration 395, which produced a $900 client by same-day DM). An opinionated comment on an executive's post led to about $60K of work (r/b2bmarketing 326).
3. **Request 1 to 2 days later.**
4. **After acceptance: ask about their process, not your product.** First message 150 to 200 characters.
5. **Message 2, 3 to 5 days later: value** (insight, resource, relevant example). This is statistically the strongest step.
6. **Message 3, 5 to 7 days later: light ask or a voice note.** Then stop.
7. **Not accepted after a week:** withdraw, then send a short email that mentions the LinkedIn attempt (r/b2bmarketing 38). Withdraw invites older than 2 to 3 weeks; a pile of 2,000+ pending invites suppressed one account until withdrawn (r/b2bmarketing 42).

Funnel from one operator using this warm-then-connect sequence: 2,000 requests, 353 accepted (17.65%), 123 replies (35% of accepted), about 25% of replies positive, 25 to 30 calls, 10 clients, $16K to $18K (r/b2bmarketing 68). Of the 123 replies, 40 replied once and went quiet: track "replied then silent" as a stage.

## Messages that reported results

Connection note (research pattern):
> {{First}}, saw {{genuine shared context or trigger}}. I work with {{peer type}} on {{problem}}. Thought it made sense to connect.

First DM after acceptance (research pattern):
> Thanks, {{First}}. The reason I reached out: {{trigger}} often creates {{specific problem}}. We recently helped {{peer}} get {{result}}. Is that on your radar, or am I off?

Problem DM, 24% replies vs 4% for a long intro plus a 15-minute meeting ask (r/LeadGeneration 28):
> Your post about struggling to get demos from cold outreach, still dealing with that?

Reply rate by DM length in the same thread: under 20 words 28%, 20 to 40 words 22%, 40 to 60 words 14%, 60 to 80 words 9%, 80+ words 4%. Routine: about 5 minutes per DM, 15 to 20 a day (about 90 minutes), giving 4 to 5 conversations a day, 1 to 2 calls a week and 2 to 3 customers a month.

Process question, above 30% replies and 2 sales from 21 DMs (targeting also changed, r/salestechniques 16):
> how are you getting users right now?

Text first, then a voice note after about 2 days (opening with a voice note did worse, r/salestechniques 24):
> Hey [name], how long does it usually take a new rep to handle calls without manager help? ... Open to a quick look?
> Voice note: "Hey [name]! I sent you something a couple days ago, and I'm curious what you think. Open to a look?"
Deliver it unscripted, slightly puzzled, under 45 seconds.

Event attendees (search a keyword, Events tab, register for the most popular events; juniors reply more), 15 responses per 25 accepted (r/b2bmarketing 42):
> Hi [first name], noticed we're both in the [industry] space, would be great to connect!

Then wait 24 hours, comment on their post if they have one, then 1 to 2 lines on what you build and a question about their daily challenges.

Pre-event DM (r/b2bmarketing 43):
> I saw you're attending the event on DTC retention. We're working on something that helps Shopify brands recover 20 percent more abandoned carts through WhatsApp. Happy to share more if you're exploring this.

Founder validation interview, about 20 conversations from 250 requests and the first 2 customers (r/startups 371):
> Hi X, please pardon the out of the blue message. I'm one of the founders of X... I'm interviewing executives in your space to help us validate our product roadmap and I would be beyond grateful for 5-10 minutes of your time.

Competitor engager (operator on 47 automated accounts, 22% average reply; the automation breaks LinkedIn's terms, the wording does not):
> Hey {{firstname}} I saw your comment on {{competitor's}} post. We're actually a competitor of theirs. I put together a playbook showing how you can {{achieve outcome}}. Mind if I share it?

Missed-call note (see [cold-calling.md](cold-calling.md)): a connection request referencing the call within 5 minutes of no answer got 14.1% responses vs 2.3% voicemail callbacks (r/Entrepreneur 480).

## Volume and routine

- Manual: 20 minutes a day, 20 to 30 requests and 20 to 30 problem-discovery messages (r/b2bmarketing 98). 10 researched messages a day beat 1,000 generic for one designer: 3 clients in one niche (r/freelance 188).
- Sales Navigator at 25 to 35 contacts a day with videos and tailored messages: about 3 to 5% became opportunities (r/startups 371).
- Seed a new founder network: join relevant groups and invite 10 to 20 members a day, no note, until LinkedIn's own suggestions become relevant; personal message only to the best after acceptance (r/socialmedia 149; r/b2bmarketing 20).
- Who to message: CEOs and owners of small companies accept fast (about half within 8 hours, r/freelanceWriters 131). For writers and creatives, the editors who assign work, not the top title (r/freelanceWriters 113).
- Channel by role: LinkedIn in the morning for C-level, email for mid-level, phone for sales ops (r/DigitalMarketing 245).
- Only worth heavy LinkedIn effort above about $200 a month ACV for SaaS (r/b2bmarketing 36).

## The automation decision

| Option | Risk | Fits |
|---|---|---|
| Manual (Sales Navigator, a daily routine) | Normal limits only | Founders, Tier 1, any profile that is a business asset |
| Assisted (tools that queue tasks for a human to click, CRM reminders) | Low | Most small teams |
| Automated (Expandi, HeyReach, Dripify, Waalaxy, La Growth Machine, Unipile-based) | Restriction, permanent ban, vendor can vanish | Never on the founder's profile; the agent recommends against it |

The agent's role on LinkedIn: research, list building from permitted sources, drafting notes and DMs, and reminders. The human sends. Expandi's useful benchmarks do not make its automation compliant.

## Tricks most owners miss

- Acceptance and replies are different goals. Pick one per campaign and judge on meetings per invitation.
- Morning requests are accepted more; replies do not care about time of day. Do not optimize send time before targeting.
- Message 2 is the strongest step. Most people stop after message 1.
- Check a prospect's last 60 days of activity. Inactive profiles go to email instead (r/LeadGeneration 26).
- LinkedIn messages and InMails are still personal-data processing under GDPR and PECR. The legal rules in [legal.md](legal.md) apply to DMs as they do to email.
- Founder content plus outreach compounds: 3 posts a week plus 4 to 5 comments a day on ICP posts; judge content by repeat ICP profile visits, not reactions (r/SaaS 752). Content mechanics belong to `organic-social`.
