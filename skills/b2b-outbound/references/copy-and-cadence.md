# Copy, offers, follow-up and multichannel cadence

Default: **one screen, one problem, one proof point, one soft ask.** Personalization changes why this person should care now, not the greeting. Sources: [sources.md](sources.md). Templates marked "(r/sub, score)" are practitioner wording with the reply rate they reported; templates marked "pattern" are syntheses from the research.

## What moves replies (data)

| Lever | Finding | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Personalization depth | 2 custom attributes: 5.6% replies vs 3.6% with none | Hunter 2026, 31M emails sent in 2025 |
| Human editing | Manually edited emails 5.2% vs fully automated 4.4% | Hunter 2026 |
| Length | First emails under 50 words: 1.47% reply, best band; 100 to 149 words: about 1 reply per 97 contacts | Smartlead 2026, 850M emails |
| Length | Better Instantly campaigns are usually under 80 words | Instantly 2026 |
| Length, conflicting | Hunter sees good results at 61 to 80 and at 181 to 200 words | Hunter 2026 |
| Pitching in email 1 | Pitching cut replies up to 57%; 344 sends per meeting on average | Gong, 28M+ emails, 2025 |
| Sequence length | 3 emails: 6.8% replies vs 1 email 3.3% | Hunter 2026 |
| Sequence decay | Email 1: 1.18% reply; email 7: 0.33% | Smartlead 2026 |
| Reply timing | 88.5% of replies arrive within 24 hours of a send | Smartlead 2026 |
| AI prose | 69% of US decision-makers are bothered when they think AI wrote the email | Hunter 2026 buyer survey |

Rule when they conflict: default short (under 80 words for email 1), test longer only for analytical buyers or markets that reward insight (UK, DACH), and judge on positive replies.

## Personalization that counts

Good personalization changes one of four things: the problem hypothesis, the reason for timing, the proof, or the offer. Store it as:

```
trigger -> inferred business consequence -> evidence (source URL, date) -> why our offer is relevant
```

- **The removal test:** delete the personalized line. If the email still makes sense, the line was decoration.
- **Hard to automate and relevant:** name their clients or competitors, not your company (r/sales 901).
- **Banned openers** that now read as automation: "I noticed", "I saw you're hiring", "congrats on", "hope this finds you well", "I came across your profile", fake title personalization ("saw you lead ops at company") (r/salestechniques 18; r/b2bmarketing 23). If the angle could apply to any company in the industry this quarter, drop it.
- **Allow "no hook found".** When research finds nothing specific, send the segment-level version. Never invent context.
- **One reusable asset beats per-prospect Loom** at volume: spend 1 to 2 days on a lead magnet or teardown template (r/LeadGeneration 31). Save personal video for Tier 1 and for people who replied.
- **Video data is vendor and anecdote only:** Vidyard reports 1.5% to 6% replies with video for one customer; operators report 3% to 18 to 20% with 15 to 30 second videos (r/b2bmarketing 21, 128, both suspect). Test on Tier 1, never as the first touch to a cold list without permission.

## Templates that reported results

Commercial cleaning, 51 words, 9 to 11% replies (r/b2bmarketing 41):
> hey {name}, do you have a cleaning vendor locked in for your properties in {city}? we work exclusively with commercial property managers here and we have a few openings this quarter. would love to give you a free walkthrough quote on one of your buildings.

Ecommerce agency, 6 to 8%, about 3x the generic "we help ecommerce brands scale" (same thread): the proof line "we helped a similar brand reduce CAC by 19% in 60 days".

Free deliverable by permission, claimed 14 to 18% replies on about 40,000 emails a month (r/b2bmarketing 25):
> Hey Ed. Saw that you run 2 companies at the same time. Seriously, where do you get an extra 24hrs? I wrote out a google doc going over how you can increase your revenue by 30% and increase customer LTV with segmented email campaigns without increasing your ad spend. Can I send it over?

Its day-1 follow-up after sending the doc, the OP's top source of positive replies:
> Hey Ed, did you have the time to go over the doc I sent? Quick question, are you currently doing anything email related?

Guide by permission, then call the same day; booked meetings +56% (several changes at once, r/sales 321):
> Mind if I send over a guide on [problem they actually care about]?

Competitor intent (r/LeadGeneration 65, vendor plug):
> hi [name], noticed you're researching [competitor]. we help companies like [company] get [specific benefit] without [competitor's main drawback]. for example, we helped [client] [specific result], saving them [quantifiable outcome]. mind if i share a quick video showing what this could look like for you?

Their own data as the email (SEO agency grown from EUR 74K to EUR 1.6M a year, mostly cold email, r/EntrepreneurRideAlong 89). Subject "About [their domain] results":
> [service] + [city]: #9 (1,900/mo) ... As a rough guide, position 1 gets over 30% of the clicks, position 10 under 3%. Would this be worth a look for you?

Its one-line nudge a week later was forwarded to the marketing director 87 minutes after sending and became a multi-year client.

Audit as the whole email, 2% to 18% replies on the same business types (r/digital_marketing 78): show the real searches where they are missing or a competitor appears; no services, no credentials; close with "thought you'd want to see this, happy to walk through what I'd fix first if you're curious."

Speculative redesign, built only after a yes: 120 manual emails, 25 replies, 10 wanted it, 5 calls, 3 clients (r/agency 84). Subject "We redesigned one of your pages. Want to see it?"

Binary qualifier (r/LeadGeneration 16):
> Hey [Prospect], quick q: Is [specific problem] a priority for your team rn? Reply with a number: 1. Yes, it is 2. No, not a focus

Trigger-to-problem pattern (research synthesis):
> Subject: {{trigger}} at {{company}}
> {{First}}, saw {{specific trigger}} at {{Company}}. Teams at that point often run into {{specific operational problem}}.
> We helped {{peer type}} {{quantified outcome}} by {{one-sentence mechanism}}.
> Worth sending the three-line breakdown?

Executive routing ask when the first emails die (enterprise, EVP replied on day 18, r/sales 831):
> Who would be the best person to chat with for 4-6 minutes on your team about this?

Second decision-maker subject line: "You or [X Name]?" (r/LeadGeneration 169).

## Frameworks to choose from

Adapted from coreyhaines31 cold-email skill (MIT), which credits the named authors:

| Framework | Shape | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Observation, problem, proof, ask | Noticed X, usually means Y, helped Z, interested? | Default for signal-led sends |
| PAS | Pain, consequence, solution plus soft CTA | Problem-aware buyers |
| Mouse Trap (Will Allred) | One observation plus a binary value question, 1 to 2 sentences | Busy executives |
| 3C (Alex Berman) | Compliment, case study, CTA | Agencies with a recognizable case |
| SCQ | Situation, complication, a question they answer | Consultative sales |
| Justin Michael | Trigger or pain, solution hint, binary CTA, 1 to 3 sentences, no intro | High volume, mobile reading |
| Poke the Bear (Josh Braun) | Neutral question exposing an invisible problem | When they do not know they have the problem |
| Breakup, 1-2-3 | "Reply with a number: 1 interested, 2 not now, 3 stop" | Last touch |

## Subject lines and CTAs

- 2 to 4 words, lowercase, looks internal ("controller search", "inventory counts", "question about {Company}"). A two-line email under "question about {Company}" matched heavily researched emails for local businesses (r/freelance 194). Lowercase subjects won about 80% of one agency's tests (r/b2bmarketing 173).
- Context beats curiosity. Hunter's data does not support "Quick question" as a universal winner. First name in the subject is associated with fewer replies (Lavender data via coreyhaines31, MIT).
- **Interest CTA, never a calendar link in email 1** (r/sales 901): "Is this a relevant topic for you?", "Would you like to explore?", "Is this subject top of mind?", "Would you like to run this by your team?" Replacing "would you like to hop on a quick call to see how we can help" with an outcome plus a free deliverable took one client from 0.9% to 3.4% replies in 2 weeks, same infrastructure (r/b2bmarketing 170). "Is this something your team is currently looking into solving" replaced a 15-minute meeting ask in a rewrite reported at about 4x replies (r/b2bmarketing 23).
- "You" about 10 times as often as "I", at most 5 sentences, no formatting (r/sales 901). Write as if typed in 45 seconds; "would this be useful?" beats "i think this would be useful" (r/b2bmarketing 173).
- Strip jargon by pitching to someone outside the market first (r/b2bmarketing 208).
- No AI tells: no contrast reveals ("it's not X, it's Y"), no self-answered questions, no stock phrases ("Here's the thing", "Unlock"), no long dashes. Calibrate tone: US short and blunt; UK more insight and politeness; DACH precise and data-led.

## Offers that get replies

Offer strength was the best predictor of results across one agency's 49 clients (r/b2bmarketing 173): a number plus a deadline ("we help ecommerce brands reduce CAC by 30-40% in 90 days") or risk reversal ("first month is performance based"). Ladder, smallest first:

1. **Permission to send something useful** (doc, guide, teardown, sample list, 100 free leads). No link until they say yes; protects deliverability and filters interest.
2. **A finished piece of work they can use** ("Look, I wrote this email you can test right away. If it drives traffic, you can pay my normal rate", r/copywriting 279).
3. **Free sample of the product** (physical samples, a few free inboxes, a scan of their app; jerky samples reported above 20% replies, r/LeadGeneration 15).
4. **Free tier over free trial:** about 50 emails per signup with a free tier vs about 600 with a trial (12x); money-back and "no card" wording barely moved results (r/LeadGeneration 17).
5. **Small paid pilot** with gates and production pricing fixed before the test (a $75K paid evaluation ahead of a $500K to $750K a year contract, r/sales 831; two buckets at about $400 grew into about $1.2M over 6 years, r/LeadGeneration 27).

Guarantees that depend on a platform you do not control are dangerous: a cold-email agency with full refunds paid back 20 clients ($52K) when Google filtering changed (r/startups 270). Pricing psychology and guarantee design belong to `offers-pricing`.

## Follow-up cadence

**Default: 3 emails, then stop.** Day 0, day 3, day 8 (operational default from the research, not a proven optimum).

| Step | Angle | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Email 1 | Trigger, problem, one proof, interest CTA | Above |
| Email 2 (same thread) | New information: a peer result, a consequence of the trigger, the resource | "One reason I flagged this: {{peer result}}. If {{problem}} is relevant, I can send the exact workflow." |
| Email 3 | Close the loop or a different ask | "Should I close the loop, or is {{problem}} something you expect to revisit this quarter?" |

- Cutting a 4-email sequence to 3 kept the same reply rate and freed 25% of sends for fresh leads (r/b2bmarketing 170). Each step can carry a different ask: demo, then free resource, then an open question about their business (r/b2bmarketing 35).
- Never "just checking in", "bumping this", "did you see my last email". Gong data: "I never heard back" is associated with a 12% drop in meetings booked.
- Compress gaps when the signal goes stale fast; give every sequence an explicit stop date (r/salestechniques 18).
- Conflicts: one thread wants 8+ touches (r/sales 901), others 2 to 3. Rule: 3 emails per channel, more touches only by adding a channel with its own job.
- After the sequence: a "not now" gets the date they named; interested-then-silent prospects get a useful reply in the original thread every 90 days with no ask (r/salestechniques 69). Ghosted late-stage deals: "Have you given up on this project?" (r/sales 489).
- Breakup that a HubSpot partner AE reports brings back most "thinking about it" prospects (claimed 90%, unverified, r/salestechniques 19):
> Hi Carlos, You had expressed interest in learning more about HubSpot and a potential relationship with Sales Titans, but I've struggled to get in touch with you. This is my last time reaching out. When timing might be better to connect, feel free to schedule some time directly.

If you send a breakup, honor it.

## Multichannel: each channel has a job

| Tier | Channels |
|---|---|
| 1 | Email, LinkedIn, phone, personal video; mail after engagement |
| 2 | Email and LinkedIn; phone for best-fit accounts |
| 3 | Email only |

A 21-day Tier 1 cadence (coreyhaines31 cold-email skill, MIT): day 1 view profile and engage with a post, email 1; day 3 connection request; day 4 email 2 in thread; day 6 call 1, voicemail plus recap email; day 8 LinkedIn message or short video; day 11 email 3 new angle; day 13 call 2; day 15 voice note or comment; day 18 breakup or referral ask; day 21 call 3, then nurture.

Operator cadences with results: day 1 pain email, day 2 call or LinkedIn, day 3 personal video: about 2x lead-to-opportunity over 1.5 years (r/agency 39). Missed call to LinkedIn within 5 minutes, email the next day (see [cold-calling.md](cold-calling.md)). Light LinkedIn touch (view, like, small comment) before the email, with volume cut from 1,000 to 150 to 200 emails a week, gave more real replies (r/b2bmarketing 19).

Rules that hold any cadence together:
1. **A reply on any channel stops every channel** for that person until a human classifies it.
2. **An opt-out on one channel is an opt-out on all.**
3. **One sender per account at a time.**
4. **Never two channels the same day,** except a voicemail and its recap email.
5. **Warm the account with ads** where budget allows: upload the prospect list to LinkedIn Matched Audiences or Meta and run light awareness ads for about 2 weeks before outreach (operator report, no numbers). Turn off the LinkedIn Audience Network and Audience Expansion. Ad mechanics belong to `paid-ads`.

## Reply handling

| Reply | Action | Agent alone? |
|---|---|---|
| Positive | Answer within minutes in business hours, two specific times plus a link | No, drafts until accuracy is proven over 100+ replies |
| Info request ("what does it cost?") | Answer the question briefly, then offer a call | Draft |
| Objection | One short reply (see [discovery-and-booking.md](discovery-and-booking.md)) | Draft |
| Not now | Thank, set a dated reminder, stop the sequence | Yes |
| Referral | Thank, contact the named person citing the referral | Draft |
| Out of office | Pause, resume after return date; note any stand-in | Yes |
| Unsubscribe or hostile | Suppress on every channel immediately, never argue | Yes |
| Bounce | Remove; check for a job change (a new signal) | Yes |

Keep two ready-made reply sequences ("more info" and "interested") and enroll from a phone at once (r/LeadGeneration 169). Add everyone who replied on LinkedIn, including those who said no (r/b2bmarketing 35).

## Tricks most owners miss

- Test the offer and the list before the subject line. Positive replies per prospect is the metric; opens are noise.
- Ask for permission before sending the link or the asset; one exception reported 60% replies by sending a 90-second fix video to 40 dream accounts with "Made this for you. It is yours, no strings, no meeting needed." (r/b2bmarketing 70). That is a Tier 1 move, not a list move.
- Write a separate template per role at the target company, at least 5 per product (r/DigitalMarketing 245).
- Healthcare buyers: cite a documented operational problem at that hospital and acknowledge HIPAA; specifics beat fear (3 to 5%, r/b2bmarketing 41). Wealth managers: email failed; LinkedIn worked better (same thread).
