# By type of seller

Outbound mechanics are shared; the offer, list and channel mix change by what you sell and to whom. Segment depth lives in sibling skills; this file holds the outbound angle. Sources: [sources.md](sources.md).

| Seller | Best first play | Channel mix | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agency, freelancer, consultant | Value-first: one verified gap, teardown after a yes | Email + LinkedIn; calls for local buyers | Free work without qualification; see `agency-freelance` |
| B2B SaaS, self-serve | Product-led outbound on usage signals; problem-led for the rest | Email from a founder, LinkedIn | Speed: usage signals decay within a day; see `saas-apps` |
| B2B SaaS, sales-led ($5K+ ACV) | Signal-led Tier 1 and 2, founder writes | Email, LinkedIn, phone, video | Single-threaded champions |
| Services to local SMBs (web, marketing, IT, cleaning) | Visible-defect or free-quote offer | Phone first, then email | Budgets that cannot buy results |
| Industrial and manufacturing suppliers | Catalog plus a sample or trial order | Phone, email, trade shows, mail | Procurement gatekeeping; see `b2b-manufacturing` |
| Professional services (law, accounting, finance) | Expertise asks and content | LinkedIn, referrals, events | Regulated promotion rules; see `professional-services` |
| High-ticket enterprise | Researched multi-month account play | Email, phone, physical mail, events | Long silence is normal |

## Agencies and freelancers

- Offer something they can see: the audit is the email (2% to 18% replies, r/digital_marketing 78), a redesign built only after a yes (120 emails, 3 clients, r/agency 84), three sentences naming a broken booking page with an offer of a 2-minute fix video (r/agency 61).
- Each service gets its own campaign and ideally its own sending infrastructure and landing page (r/LeadGeneration 169).
- Qualify ability to pay: roofing and solar budgets of $500 to $700 a month cannot buy appointments that cost $250+ (r/agency 154).
- Time-box outbound while delivering: Tuesday and Thursday 08:00 to 09:30, research batched Monday (r/agency 61).

## SaaS

- Under 10 customers, outreach is the whole day; 10 to 25, half; hire sales after the founder closed 10 (r/startups 280).
- Product-led triggers by strength: plan or usage limit hit, a big import, team invites, company-domain signup, repeat pricing-page visits. Email within minutes from a named founder; talk about the job they are doing, never the tracked events.
- Free tier needed about 50 emails per signup vs 600 for a free trial (r/LeadGeneration 17).
- Price routing: $20 to $50 a month goes signup then card trial; $100+ a month goes signup then demo (r/SaaS 574). Instant demos and onboarding on the call took demo-to-close from 20% to 50%+ (r/microsaas 89).
- Validation interviews double as first sales: about 250 LinkedIn requests, about 20 calls, first 2 customers (r/startups 371).

## Selling to local and small businesses

- Phone beats email for owners who live on their phone. 50 calls a day in a 4-hour block took a solo developer to about $120K a year (r/Entrepreneur 1838).
- Lists from Google Maps (Places API) and Instagram; filter by missing website, broken bio link, DM-only ordering, review count.
- Short and casual works: "question about {Company}" with two lines matched researched emails (r/freelance 194). Commercial cleaning's free walkthrough quote got 9 to 11% (r/b2bmarketing 41).
- Instagram DM that won 2 clients (r/LeadGeneration 395): "hey, love your products. noticed you take all orders through dm, i build simple ordering websites for small brands like yours, i made one for a cafe recently. want me to send a quick example?"
- Lunch works for local owners: "I'm sure you eat lunch. Let me bring you lunch tomorrow. How about noon at X place?" (r/salestechniques 74).

## Industrial, manufacturing and distribution

- Start with engineering or the function owner; procurement follows their recommendation. Call the front desk for routing (r/salestechniques 76).
- Attach the real catalog; buyers file it for future needs (same thread). A procurement manager says cold calls never landed a supplier for his team: lead with a sample, a trial order or a site visit.
- Physical signals: job sites passed by drivers, consumption visible at the site, new permits and licenses (r/sales 426, 227).
- Small paid trials grow: 2 buckets at about $400 became about $1.2M over 6 years (r/LeadGeneration 27).

## High-ticket and enterprise

- A 113-day example (r/sales 831): days 1 to 4 on the 10-K, investor decks and executive talks; emails on days 5, 7, 12, 13 to CEO and EVP; rewritten on day 16 around peer adoption in their sector with 2 actionable strategies; EVP replied day 18 with the routing ask; $75K paid evaluation with production pricing fixed before the test.
- Physical mail for short, valuable lists: a box with a plastic foot ("It's my way of putting my foot in the door if you will") and a lunch invitation, about $450 each, reported 40% reply and about $31.5K a year per customer (r/microsaas 770). A one-page pitch by FedEx got replies in 2 to 3 days; a themed $21 gift after many opens got callbacks within 4 days (r/sales 656).
- Conference office hours: overlap prospect meetings so they meet each other, then a bar from 15:30 to 17:30 (r/sales 831). Dinners of 6 to 8 people: "I'm organizing dinner tomorrow at 7pm, want to join?" (r/salestechniques 15).

## By buyer role

- C-level: ultra-brief, peer tone, LinkedIn in the morning; executives reply at higher rates once a message passes their filter (vendor data, coreyhaines31).
- Mid-level managers: more specific value, email.
- Technical buyers: precise, no fluff, proof over adjectives.
- Sales ops and operations: phone works (r/DigitalMarketing 245).
- Finance: frame repair cost and lost production in money (r/salestechniques 19).
