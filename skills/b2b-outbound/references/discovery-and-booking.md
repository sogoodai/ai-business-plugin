# Appointment setting, discovery and qualification

A reply is not a meeting, a meeting is not a held meeting, and a held meeting is not an opportunity. This file covers the hour after a positive reply, booking and show rates, discovery, qualification, objections and the post-call follow-up. Sources: [sources.md](sources.md).

## Speed and booking

- **Answer positive replies within minutes** in business hours. The famous speed-to-lead multipliers come from 2007 to 2011 studies (direction holds, numbers are stale). Smartlead 2026: 88.5% of replies arrive within 24 hours of a send, so someone must own the inbox every working hour. Acknowledge at once even if the full answer comes later (r/salestechniques 18).
- **Propose two specific times in their time zone plus a link as fallback.** A bare link feels like homework.
- **Remove every delay** (SaaS founder, demo-to-close from 20% to 50%+, several changes at once, r/microsaas 89 and r/salestechniques 72): keep 2 to 3 open slots a day; offer "Are you free right now? I can show you in 5 minutes."; set up on the call ("Perfect, let me set you up right now while we're talking." / "You'll be using it in the next 10 minutes."); take payment during setup with a Stripe link; show only one week of calendar availability.
- **Book it yourself for interested prospects:** ask what time works, send the invite, then SMS and email reminders where they agreed to texts (r/b2bmarketing 36).
- Send warm leads a direct booking link, never the homepage. A hostile booking-page line ("DON'T BOOK IF YOU'RE GOING TO WASTE MY TIME WITH WINDOW SHOPPING") lost one qualified buyer (r/LeadGeneration 32).
- Offer 15 to 20 minutes, block 30; research 1 to 2 hours before morning calls (r/salestechniques 54).
- Confirm the day before with something useful (agenda, a relevant case, the teardown you will discuss); remind an hour before. Reschedule a no-show the same day, warmly, once; a second no-show goes back to nurture.
- **Opt-ins who do not book get a call at once;** bookers get automated reminders (r/LeadGeneration 15).
- **Pre-call nurture for higher tickets:** 3 to 5 days of a custom Loom, founder story and case studies, with rough pricing before the call (r/b2bmarketing 40). An industry demo video sent before the call makes the call work like onboarding (r/agency 53).
- **Show rates:** one report puts fully AI-booked meetings at 52% attendance vs 71% for human-booked (r/b2bmarketing 24, unsourced). Rule: a human takes over at the positive reply.

## Discovery: what you need to leave with

The objective is evidence for seven questions: Why change? Why now? Who cares? What happens if nothing changes? Who decides and how? What does success look like? What are they using or considering instead?

Gong (326K calls, March 2025): sellers average 60% talk; won deals 57% seller talk vs lost 62%. That is correlation, and the gap is modest. Older Gong work on "11 to 14 questions" predates 2024; treat it as possibly outdated. The goal is balanced two-way dialogue, not silence.

Operator versions:
- Reps who let the prospect describe the problem closed 44% vs 18% when the rep talked most (r/salestechniques 69, observational, possibly vendor content). Another adopted 70% prospect / 30% seller after 30 calls with zero closes (r/b2bmarketing 18).
- Barely talk for 20 minutes: how do you do it now, what hurts, why isn't it fixed, what have you tried, what happens if nothing changes (r/salestechniques 109).
- Past behavior over opinions: "Can you walk me through your process of...", and have them stack-rank their problems. "Let me know when you launch" is not commitment; a pre-order or a team intro is (r/startups 281).
- Buyers who have bought before: "What have you used before? What did you like about it? What didn't you like? What are you trying to accomplish?" Experienced buyers get specs and price and go straight to a close (r/salestechniques 15).
- Plan the call: "What must be true for this person to buy?", "What usually stops people from buying?", "What belief shift needs to happen?", with 2 to 3 questions each (r/salestechniques 24).

Openers that set the frame (r/Entrepreneur 1199, a freelancer claiming 200+ deals):
> Inbound: "So, to start, I'd love to hear what prompted you to book this call today? What's going on in your business?"
> Outbound: "When I reached out, something in my message must have clicked. What was it that made you decide to take this call?"

Then "Should we dive right in?", ask about past providers, and anchor: "I've done similar projects for around $4,000."

Trigger-based opener (r/salestechniques 54):
> Recently, I read that you raised funds, I want to congratulate you on that. I suppose that the company is looking now for fast growth. Can you tell me more about your growth objectives?

Take control after 1 to 2 minutes of small talk: "Okay let's focus, here's how it's going to go" (r/salestechniques 38). Tie the offer to how the team is measured: ask how they are reviewed and paid, then "if we can help you all achieve any of those let us know" (r/sales 831).

Do not repeat discovery when a setter booked the call: read the setter's notes or transcript, summarize them back, ask only what is missing (r/b2bmarketing 19).

## Qualification as a data schema

Use MEDDICC as fields, not an interrogation: Metrics, Economic buyer, Decision criteria, Decision process, Identified pain, Champion, Competition. Missing evidence stays "not discussed". An agent summarizing a transcript must never fill a field it did not hear.

**Budget question that gets a number** (claimed 90% of the time, r/freelance 172):
> I can craft a $5,000 solution or a $50,000 solution... So I need a ballpark idea of what resources you have before I can recommend an approach.

**Authority first:** confirm who decides at the start; if someone else decides, reschedule with them instead of pitching (r/sales 638).

**Single-threaded champion is the most common silent killer.** When you hear "I'm handling it internally", ask to be introduced to everyone who touches the decision. It was present in all of one seller's roughly 12 lost deals; won deals brought in security in week 2 and finance before the proposal (r/salestechniques 51). Before a group demo, the champion prep call:
> Hey, got 15 mins so I can walk you through my plan for the demo, just want to make sure it aligns with what you're thinking.
If they refuse, the deal is at risk (r/salestechniques 245).

**Disqualify out loud** when it is not a fit: "Hey we aren't a good fit for the situation so we won't be moving forward you can call X They are better for you" (r/sales 638). A DIY disqualifier won 2 clients in 2 months who credited the honesty (r/Entrepreneur 485).

**Gate "pick your brain" requests:** "Happy to help. My consulting rate is EUR 150/hour. First 15 minutes free to see if I can actually add value." 500+ earlier free sessions produced zero clients (r/Entrepreneur 190).

| Objection | Reply | Source |
|---|---|---|
| "I need to get other quotes" | "Ah, I completely understand. Normally, whenever my customers tell me this - it has to do with price. May I ask, what was your expectation?" | r/EntrepreneurRideAlong 260 |
| "Can you do it cheaper?" | "you absolutely could, the question is do you want this done cheaply or properly?" | r/sales 638 |
| Early price question | "Right to business, I like it. Pricing depends on just a few more pieces of information I need from you first and then we can get right to it" | r/salestechniques 85 |
| Late "I need to ask my partner" after claiming authority | "look at the start of the call you said you could make a decision by yourself... do you really need to talk to [them] or is there something else that's stopping you from saying yes or no?" | r/sales 638 |
| A flat reaction in a demo | "Sounds like that didn't impress you at all, Jim" / "Boy I really didn't explain that well did I" | r/sales 404 |
| After a no | "What about this doesn't work for you?" / "What would you need to make this work?" | r/sales 489 |
| "Not fair" | "OK, I apologize. Let's stop everything and go back to where I started treating you unfairly and we'll fix it" | r/sales 489 |
| Any answer you just gave | "Does that actually address what you were worried about?" before asking for the next step | r/salestechniques 30 |
| Stall at the end | Ask what their plan is once you leave, and name the next visit you are making to their competitors | r/sales 589 |

Negotiation habits from the same threads: mirror the last 3 words then pause at least 4 seconds; label ("It seems like...") then pause; run an accusation audit; drop "I understand" (r/sales 489). Turn final blockers into contract clauses while holding price (r/sales 4270). Price the contract terms: $8K a year on the standard agreement, $15K if they want to redline it (r/SaaS 350). Show the full price; one $50K quote held against 4 competing bids (r/salestechniques 16). Quote every fee up front; a missed $500 fee had to be absorbed (r/sales 890).

## After the call

- **Next-step discipline:** a 3-field CRM (opportunity, next step, next step due date). No next step means it was a bad meeting (r/salestechniques 98). Book the next meeting in the last 5 minutes.
- **One sentence on the buyer's stakes** after every call, in their words: "Needs to move before the school year or the whole thing falls apart" (r/salestechniques 109). Reuse their exact pain words in the follow-up, sparingly (r/salestechniques 38).
- **Follow-up matrix** (r/salestechniques 16): tag intent (hot, warm, cold, dead) against timeline (under 30 days, 1 to 3 months, 3+ months, unknown). Hot and under 30 days: meet again as soon as possible. Warm and 1 to 3 months: email day 1 and day 7, call day 15. Cold and unknown: ask permission to add them to the newsletter.
- **A mini build after the call:** "Hey I got curious about what xyz might look like so I did a quick little implementation and here's a screenshot/video/link." 2 of 3 won (r/Upwork 133).
- **Stalled champion:** a 60 to 90 second forwardable video for their boss (r/salestechniques 19).
- **Draft the follow-up from the transcript within 10 minutes,** then a human edits and sends.

## The handoff record (setter to closer, or agent to human)

Store, in the prospect's own words where possible: source and trigger, current situation, stated problem, consequence, urgency and timing, decision participants, next-step commitment, verbatim objections. A generated summary that launders "maybe" into "yes" costs more than no summary.

## Tricks most owners miss

- A booked meeting with the wrong person is worse than none: it eats a slot and trains the team to ignore setter bookings. Qualify title and authority in the reply thread before sending times.
- Year-end and quarter-end are real levers for the buyer too: "It would be personally meaningful to me if we can proceed before Jan 1st" closed a slipping deal (r/sales 649). Use it once, honestly.
- Closing line from a proposal-heavy seller: "May we proceed with this order/project" (r/salestechniques 43).
- Keep it in messages when the buyer prefers: ask "what questions do you have about working together?" and propose a call only when pricing or a complex demo comes up (r/salestechniques 17).
