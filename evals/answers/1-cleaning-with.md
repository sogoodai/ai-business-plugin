Your best bet is to turn the referrals you already get into a system, and only then pay for leads. You have a good rating and clients who already refer people. Most of the $1,000 should reward those referrals and reach the neighbors of homes you already clean. A smaller share goes to one paid channel.

**The timing works for you.** Pre-holiday deep cleans (now to Dec 20) and January "new year reset" cleans are when people book a first clean. Your job is to turn those one-off cleans into biweekly clients.

**The rough math.** If about 40–50% of first cleans become recurring, 10 new recurring clients takes roughly 20–25 first cleans. That's about 40–50 real inquiries. That conversion rate is my working guess, not an industry figure, so track your own. Before you start, check you have room: 10 biweekly clients is about 5 more cleans a week.

## Week by week

**Week 1 (Nov 24–30, Thanksgiving week): stop losing leads and set up tracking**
- Turn on a missed-call auto-text: *"Hi, this is [Business]. Sorry we missed your call. Are you looking for a one-time or recurring clean? Reply with your ZIP and home size and I'll send a price and the next opening."*
- Set a rule: every inquiry gets a reply within 15 minutes during work hours (aim for 5). Fast replies win most cleaning jobs.
- Start a simple sheet with these columns: date, source, reply time, quoted, booked, converted to recurring, price.
- Write down your offer, using your own prices:
  - The first visit is a "reset clean" priced separately.
  - Then weekly, biweekly and monthly, with a lower price per visit the more often they book.
  - "New-client pricing: book biweekly by Jan 31 and get $X off the reset clean." Give it a name and an end date, and never make it free.
- Apply for Google Local Services Ads now. Applying is free, but the background check and insurance verification can take weeks. Cleaning leads there cost about $33 each (2026 agency data, and rising).

**Week 2 (Dec 1–7): ask your current clients**
- Send every current client a short Google review request, the same neutral text to everyone. No rewards for reviews, and don't skip clients you think won't rate you 5 stars. The goal is to get from 23 reviews to 35 or more by mid-January. Reply to every review.
- Make the referral ask, in person or by text: *"We have 2 openings for recurring clients in January. Most of our best clients come from referrals. If a friend books, you both get $50 off after their second clean."* Hand over 3 cards.
- Update your Google profile: holiday hours, 10 or more real photos of your team and finished rooms, a post about the holiday deep clean, and a list of the neighborhoods you serve.

**Week 3 (Dec 8–14): work the streets you already clean**
- At every job, leave a printed note at the 15–20 nearest homes: *"We clean a home on your street every other [day]. Since we're already here, we have one opening on this route. [Offer, end date, number]."* Don't name the client unless they say it's OK.
- Text every past one-time client and anyone who got a quote and didn't book in the last 6 months. Offer pre-holiday slots and mention the recurring plan.
- If Google has approved your ads, start them: about $40–50 a week, only for ZIP codes near your current routes.

**Week 4 (Dec 15–21): holiday deep cleans**
- Every first clean ends with the recurring offer, made in person while the client is happy with the result: *"Want to keep it like this? Biweekly is $X a visit, and we can hold this same day for you."*
- If they don't book on the spot, text them on day 1, day 3 and day 7.
- Ask every new client for a review the day after the clean.

**Week 5 (Dec 22–Jan 4): slow weeks, so prepare January**
- Message everyone who got a quote or a one-time clean: *"Booking January recurring spots now. 3 left on [day]."*
- If Google still hasn't verified you, move the ad money to Nextdoor or Facebook ads. Use real before-and-after photos, target only your neighborhoods, and ask "home size and how often?" in the form.

**Weeks 6–7 (Jan 5–18): the new-year push**
- This is when people book "reset" cleans. Put the most ad money here and repeat the neighbor notes around every job.
- Make a second referral ask to your 5 happiest clients, by name and personally.
- Contact 3–5 local realtors or property managers about move-in cleans. A new homeowner who liked their move-in clean is an easy recurring client. Offer them speed and photos they can use, not a kickback.

**Week 8 (Jan 19–23): review and decide**
- From your sheet, work out the cost per **recurring** client by source. Keep what worked and drop what didn't.
- If you're filling up and winning more than about 70% of quotes, raise prices 5–10% on new quotes.

## Rules to keep
- Don't give a big discount up front in exchange for referrals you've been promised. People who come in on deep discounts tend to disappear before booking.
- Turn down clients who book less than monthly but expect a full reset every time.
- Only market to areas near your current routes. Clients far from your other homes cost you drive time on every visit.
- Don't use Angi or Thumbtack unless you can reliably reply within minutes.
