## Option B: Meta ads now
The most an order can cost you in ads before you lose money is **~$14**, your profit per order.

- Share of visitors who buy: ~1–3%
- **Ad cost per sale: ~$30–75**

There's also a practical problem. Etsy doesn't let you install Meta's tracking code (the pixel), so Meta can't learn which ads lead to purchases. Running Meta ads properly basically requires your own site, and as Option A shows, that isn't worth it yet.

## What I'd do instead
1. **Raise your average order to $50–60 or more.** Sell 3-packs, gift sets and seasonal bundles. A $60 bundle with ~$20 in materials and ~$6 in fees leaves ~$34 profit per order. That's more than double your current ad budget per sale, and it's what makes paid ads possible later.
2. **Leave Etsy Offsite Ads switched on.** Etsy already runs ads on Google and Meta for you and only charges 15% when one leads to a sale. On a $24 order that's $3.60, compared with $30+ through your own Meta ads. Shops under $10k a year can opt out, so make sure you haven't.
3. **Test Etsy Ads at $1–5/day.** They reach people already shopping for candles, and Etsy shows you exactly which sales they bring in.
4. **Start a customer list now.** Put a card in each package with a QR code to an email sign-up and a reorder discount. Candles get used up, so repeat buyers are what eventually make your own site worth it.

## When to switch
- **Shopify:** when 20–30% of your sales are repeat or direct customers you could bring over yourself, or once you're past ~$3–5k/month and Etsy's fees add up to real money.
- **Meta ads:** when your average order is $50+ and your profit per order clearly beats a realistic $30–40 ad cost per sale. That usually means your own site with tracking set up, and repeat purchases helping to cover the cost of winning each customer.
